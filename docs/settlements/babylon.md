## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Etemenanki, the ziggurat of Marduk. Its base was about 91 m square, and its height is often given as about 91 m in seven stages, after the Esagila Tablet [stated: Wikipedia "Etemenanki"]. Esagila, Marduk's temple, stood just south of it [stated: Wikipedia "Esagila"].
- **The Ishtar Gate:** the eighth gate of the inner city, built about 575 BC under Nebuchadnezzar II, of glazed brick in blue with rows of aurochs and dragons. The Processional Way through it was lined with walls of glazed lions [stated: Wikipedia "Ishtar Gate"].
- **The city:** the inner city had double walls. The Southern Palace stood west of the Processional Way near the gate, and the Euphrates ran through the city [stated: Wikipedia "Babylon"].
- **Moment shown:** about 570 BC.

## 3. Orientation

The Processional Way runs north–south; the ziggurat's great stair rises from the south [stated: Koldewey].

## 4. The style

Mud brick with baked-brick facings in bitumen; glazed blue brick with yellow-brown beasts on the gate and the Way's walls; crenellations; buttressed temple walls. [stated]

### 4.1 The Processional Way (L2–L3, built)

- **Paving:** the middle of the street paved with "mighty flags of limestone measuring 1.05 metres each way", the sides with slabs of red breccia 66 cm square; the bevelled joints filled with asphalt; the paving on a base of brick covered with asphalt; each slab inscribed on its edge [stated: Koldewey, *The Excavations at Babylon*, ch. on the Procession Street]. Large slabs of limestone and red breccia [stated: thearchaeologist.org]. Ours: a limestone lane seven flags wide (7.4 m) down the middle and breccia to the walls (the lane's width is [inferred]; Koldewey gives no figure); dark bitumen in the 3.5 cm joints; flags settled by a few millimetres; the same flags through the Ishtar Gate's passages. The breccia flags in the palette's red breccia veined white, the joints and the bed's coat in bitumen (both were stand-ins before: pink marble and road asphalt) [stated: Koldewey, "Over every course of brick is a thin layer of asphalt"; the throne-room pavement "laid in asphalt"].
- **Walls:** the flanking walls 7 m thick with slightly projecting towers at intervals equal to their breadth [stated: Koldewey]. Ours: towers 6 m broad every 12 m, projecting 0.45 m on both faces and 1.4 m higher (the walls themselves kept 2.4 m thick: the layout is compressed) [inferred: projection and height].
- **Lions:** on the east wall walking right, on the west wall walking left (both toward the gate, south), each about 2 m long; some white with yellow manes, others yellow with red manes (now turned green); the ground light or dark blue; black and white enamelled lines divide the wall; the plinth bore rows of broad-leaved rosettes; 60 lions a side [stated: Koldewey; Madain Project]. Ours: lions 2 m long and about 1 m high, white/yellow-maned and yellow/red-maned by turn, three to every 9 m; two rows of rosettes on the plinth, a white–black–white band under and over the frieze, a rosette row above, a band under the battlements [inferred: the composition of the bands]. The red of the yellow lions' manes in the palette's red glaze (it was a red lacquer): "In addition to the black outline and the dark blue ground, the colours employed are white, light blue, yellow, and red", the red, an opaque enamel hard to make, "now has everywhere the appearance of green"; "Black and white lines in flat enamel on the edges of the towers" [stated: Koldewey, *The Excavations at Babylon*, Project Gutenberg 71398]. The enamelled lines stay white–black–white and the throne room's band of squares yellow, black and white, as stated; no red band is attested in the sources read, so none is added. The black glaze is still the palette's black lacquer (no black glaze exists) [inferred].
- **Battlements:** stepped merlons, three steps narrowing upward, on the Way's walls, the gate, the city wall and the palace [inferred: the stepped Mesopotamian battlement, as in the Pergamon reconstruction; not stated in the sources read].

### 4.2 The Ishtar Gate (L2–L3, built)

Bulls and sirrush (dragons) in horizontal rows, a row of bulls then a row of sirrush; each figure 13 brick courses high with 11 plain courses between, 24 courses almost exactly 2 m; two projecting towers flank each entrance; at least 575 animals in all [stated: Koldewey]. In the Pergamon reconstruction dragons and bulls never share a row; rosette patterns and linear borders frame them [stated: Madain Project]. Ours: rows every 2 m up every face of the towers, bulls (yellow, a row of blue-green hair on the belly) and dragons (white, the scaled neck yellow: horned serpent head, forked tongue, feline forelegs, eagle hind legs, scorpion tail) by turn, walking toward the passage on the fronts and toward the city on the outer sides; a rosette band at the foot and under the battlements; round-arched passages with a white and a yellow archivolt [inferred: the arch form, colours of details, directions]. The corner towers no longer narrow the passage (they did before).

### 4.3 The Southern Palace (L2, built)

The throne room 17 × 52 m with a great central door and two side doors on the court; its facade toward the court: on a dark blue ground yellow columns with bright blue capitals, above them a frieze of white double palmettes bordered below by a band of squares, yellow, black and white; the principal court 55 × 60 m; gates with side chambers between the courts; the north front of Nabopolassar's palace in vertical steps, 80 of them on 80 m [stated: Koldewey]. Ours: one palace of baked brick round three courts (the east court behind the towered east gate, the throne court 46 × 54 m, a court of lodgings), gateways through the ranges between them, the outer faces stepped every metre, stepped battlements, brick-paved courts; on the throne court's south side the facade with three doors (cedar leaves bound in bronze in doorways rebated in three steps of glaze), columns with two tiers of volutes, the band of squares and the frieze of double palmettes [inferred: the court sizes compressed, the volute form, the rebated doors]. The palace's courts can be walked into from the east gate.

### 4.4 Etemenanki's stair (built)

"A colossal stairway led up from the south to the top of the immense mass of building", the ruins showing 90 m for a side [stated: Koldewey]; a great open stairway from the south, very steep [stated: globalsecurity.org]. Ours: the stair now rises from the south, toward Esagila (it faced north before); the approach down the Way reaches the tower's back.

### 4.5 Esagila, the house of Marduk (L2–L3, built 2026-11-06)

- **Plan [stated: Koldewey, *The Excavations at Babylon*, §XXXV]:** "The temple is almost square, with its northern front of 79.3 metres and its western front of 85.8 metres long. Inside it is a court 31.3 metres broad and 37.6 metres long." "On the west of this court, as we learn from the mighty-towered façade, there was the principal cella, that of Marduk." "Exactly in the middle of each side there is a great gateway elaborated with massive projecting towers." "Here every tower is placed between two flanking towers, thus forming a unit of three towers." "The enclosing walls, which, including the 2-metres-thick kisu, are 6 metres thick." The annexe to the east (89.4 × 116 m) is not built. An anteroom and the inner sanctum with the statues of Marduk [stated: Wikipedia "Esagil"].
- **The cella [stated]:** "I put bright gold (on the walls of) the cella, the seat of his lordship, as if it was šallaru-plaster" (Nebuchadnezzar II 019, i 29; the same in 023, i 33, naming the cella Eumuša) [ORACC RIBo]. Herodotus 1.183: in the lower temple "a great image of Zeus sitting, made of gold", by it "a large table of gold", "his footstool and seat are of gold also" — 800 talents of gold, the Chaldaeans said; outside the cell an altar of gold on which only sucklings may be offered, and a larger altar for full-grown animals, where a thousand talents of frankincense were burnt each year at the god's festival.
- **Ours:** `esagila` (src/styles/babylonian/temple.js) on 80 × 86 m: the outer walls 6 m thick, 12 m high, with stepped battlements; on every face corner towers, gate towers 7 m broad standing 3 m out round a 5 m passage, and between them units of three towers (a broad one 4.5 m wide and 1.4 m out between two narrow ones) [inferred: tower sizes, heights, spacing]; the north, east and south gates passable, with cedar leaves bound in bronze standing open; the west gate's doors shut, flush with the wall [inferred: in our plan the cella lies behind it]. The court 31.3 × 37.6 m paved in brick; the ranges of rooms round it built solid (their rooms left for later). On the court's west side the cella's facade between two great towers rising 3.5 m over the roofs, a band of blue glaze with white rosettes over its door [inferred]; before it the golden altar with horned corners, two bronze censers, and the great brick altar for full-grown sheep with its hearth blackened and steps up its east side [inferred: forms]. The antecella (6 m) plastered; the cella of Marduk 14.5 × 24 m, a broad room entered on its long side [inferred: the Babylonian broad-room cella; its size], its walls sheeted in gold in panels 1.5 m wide to a ceiling of cedar boards on cedar beams [inferred: the cedar], the niche of the god (6 × 2 × 8.5 m) in the back wall, sheeted too; the dais of brick faced with gold, three steps up; on it the golden box throne with panelled sides and back, the footstool, the seated image of gold (a figure, 3.3 m tall standing, seated, in a long robe with long hair and beard) with a horned crown of three pairs of horns and a feathered crest [inferred: the crown after the god's horned crown in Babylonian art]; the great golden table before the dais with vessels of gold; two bronze lampstands. Esagila is entered from the precinct through a new gate in its south wall on the stair's axis [inferred].

### 4.6 The temple on Etemenanki's summit (built 2026-11-06)

"I covered the top of Etemenanki with blue glazed burnt brick" [stated: Koldewey]; "on their summits, I resplendently built a holy shrine, a well-adorned bedroom, using baked bricks (colored with) shining blue glaze" [stated: Nebuchadnezzar II 023, i 38, ORACC]. Herodotus 1.181–182: on the top of the last tower a large cell with a large couch well covered and a golden table by it; no image; the god is said to rest there. Ours (`etemenanki`): the ziggurat as before, its summit temple (the Ur house plan, 24 × 21 × 15 m) in blue glaze, stepped battlements of blue glaze on its parapet; in the cella the couch (cedar frame on gold-shod feet, linen mattress, red coverlet hanging over its side, a bolster) and the golden table with a bowl and a jar; the Ur shrine's emblem of Nanna left out [inferred: furnishings' forms and colours]. Its door is still the Ur shrine's (1.6 × 2.9 m), small for the building — for later.

### 4.7 The Euphrates: the bridge, the quay wall, the boats (built 2026-11-06)

- **The bridge [stated: Koldewey §XXXI]:** "Seven river piers have been excavated." "The piers are 9 metres wide and are placed 9 metres apart"; "the pier lengths of 21 metres"; "They are built with a very marked batter"; "Their bricks are of the small size 31 × 31 centimetres and are unstamped"; "strengthening baulks of wood once lay 50 centimetres apart"; "The sides of the piers are convex and meet in a point in front facing the current on the north... the ground-plan of the pier follows the water-line of a ship"; "the brick piers were roofed over with stone, on which the rafters for the roadway were laid" (probable); the whole 123 m; Diodorus: 30 feet broad. Herodotus 1.186: the stones bound with iron and lead, squared timbers laid across on top. Ours (`bridge`): our river is 64 m between the bridgeheads, so three piers 9 m wide, 21 m long and 9.25 m apart, battered, pointed upstream (north), square downstream; courses of timber baulks every 2 m above the water; stone caps; nine squared beams along the bridge, planks across them; the roadway 9 m wide, 0.45 m above the banks, between brick bridgeheads with merlons, at z = 40 west of the tower [inferred: the number of piers, the beams, the height].
- **The quay wall [stated: Koldewey §XXXIII]:** the Nabonidus wall "7.67 metres thick, with its cavalier towers stands on the river bank"; "Towers, alternately broad and narrow, are placed at a distance of about 19 metres from each other. The broad ones are 7.3, the narrow ones 6.3 metres wide"; "Nabonidus for his Euphrates wall turned once more to asphalt"; Nebuchadnezzar: "quay walls of burnt brick". Ours (`quay`): along the east bank for 400 m, following the river's bend, its top a walk level with the bank, stepped merlons on its water edge, towers 7.3 and 6.3 m wide by turns every 19 m standing 3 m into the river and 2.6 m above the walk, a band of bitumen at the waterline [inferred: the parapet, the band, the towers' projection].
- **The boats [stated: Herodotus 1.194]:** round "like a shield", of hides stretched on frames of willow cut in Armenia, filled with reeds, carrying mostly palm-wood casks of wine; two men standing steer each, one pulling the paddle, the other pushing; the largest carry 5,000 talents, some a live ass; at Babylon the frames and reeds are sold and the hides taken back on the asses. Ours (`quffa`): round hide boats 1.6–2.6 m in radius with a rim of wicker, a floor of reeds, casks of palm wood or bundles of reeds; four moored at the quay, three on the stream with two boatmen standing with their poles, one with an ass [inferred: sizes; the paddles drawn as poles].

### 4.9 The dedication by the gate (L3, built 2026-11-07)

"The inscription is written in Akkadian cuneiform using white and blue glazed bricks", Nebuchadnezzar's dedication explaining the gate's purpose, "15 meters tall by 10 meters wide", 60 lines; "The roof and doors of the gate were made of cedar, according to the dedication plaque" [stated: Wikipedia "Ishtar Gate"; Madain Project "Ishtar Gate Inscription", which opens "Nebuchadnezzar, King of Babylon, the pious prince, appointed by the will of Marduk"]. Ours (`gate_inscription`): a panel 10 m wide of blue glazed brick on the city face of the inner wall just east of the gate, between its first two towers, where it is seen on coming out of the gate into the city (the outer face beside the gate lies hidden behind the Way's walls in our layout); 9.6 m high (the wall is lower than the stated 15 m); 60 ruled lines of signs in white glaze standing a little proud — horizontal, vertical and corner wedges in clusters — framed by white–black–white enamelled bands with a row of rosettes at the top [inferred: the place, the signs (generated, not the text), the frame].

### 4.10 Reed and bitumen in the walls (L3, built 2026-11-07)

Herodotus 1.179: they "made bricks of the earth which was carried out of the place they dug" and "baked them in ovens", "then using hot bitumen for cement and interposing layers of wattled reeds at every thirtieth course of bricks" [stated: Herodotus 1.179, Perseids]. Ours (`city_wall`): on every face of the double wall and its towers a band of reed ends standing 1.8 cm out of the face every thirty courses (30 × 8.5 cm, about 2.55 m), over a dark line of bitumen [inferred: the reed ends left showing on the face].

## 5. The town

About 800 courtyard houses. [inferred] Mud-brick houses "scarcely strong enough to support even one upper storey"; the ordinary house of one floor [stated: Koldewey]. The houses share Ur's house type (`docs/settlements/ur.md` §5): brick footings, plastered walls, a door with a lintel, gutters, a court with a stair to the roof [inferred].

### 5.1 Two houses to enter, furnished (L3, built 2026-11-07)

The two houses on the east side of the Processional Way south of the palace (the lots of seeds 757329 and 866290) are built hollow and furnished as Ur's (`docs/settlements/ur.md` §5.1: lobby, court with stair and drain, reception room with couch, table and lamp, domestic chapel with niche and altar, kitchen with hearth, bread oven and quern, storeroom, lavatory), their street doors on the Way [inferred: the Neo-Babylonian house kept the courtyard plan of the older city].

### 5.2 The market, the caravan, the scribes (L3, built 2026-11-07)

- **The market** at the east end of the bridge (`MARKET`, 64 × 58 m, no houses on it; a market room for the generic pass): two rows of sellers behind reed mats and under cloth awnings — dates and melons, fish, greens, bread, pots, baskets, bolts of cloth — buyers before them, two donkeys standing loaded [inferred].
- **The caravan:** four camels with their packs and two Arab drivers, bareheaded in long robes, and a man with a loaded donkey, coming down the road to the Ishtar Gate on its east side, clear of the arrival's view [inferred: the Arabs' dress after the Apadana's delegation (`docs/settlements/persepolis.md` §13); camels as Arabia's beasts of burden in the 6th century BC].
- **The scribes:** on the quay by the moored boats two scribes cross-legged on their mats record a cargo for a man with a staff — one on a clay tablet, the other on a leather scroll with ink and pen. "Scribes are shown in pairs, one writing in cuneiform on a board or a tablet", "the other writing in Aramaic on a leather scroll" [stated: ORACC, "Writing cuneiform on other media" (Ancient Kalhu), of the Assyrian palace reliefs] (`scribe_kit` with `scroll`).
