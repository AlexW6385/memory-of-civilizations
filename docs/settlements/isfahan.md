# Safavid Isfahan, c. 1630 — dossier

Settlement id: `isfahan` · Style: `safavid` · Region: Persia (the Isfahan plain) · Code: `src/settlements/isfahan/`
Status of this dossier: L2, parts toward L3 (the net of arabesques on both domes, the inscriptions and grilles of their drums; Ali Qapu's talar in detail; the muqarnas of the iwans; and Naqsh-e Jahan Square with its two-storey arcades, the canal and plane trees round the polo field and the stone goalposts; the Shah Mosque with its portal, court, four iwans, minarets and dome; the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque; Ali Qapu with its talar; the Qeysarieh portal and the first stretch of the vaulted bazaar; the royal garden behind Ali Qapu; the town of courtyard houses; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 recorded for later: the muqarnas of the portals, the tile patterns and inscriptions, the madrasas and side halls of the Shah Mosque, the tunnel under the square, the caravanserais and timchehs, the Chahar Bagh avenue, the river and its bridges, people, polo matches and the night lamps. Phase D (§16): a furnished courtyard house, the bazaar's shops and trades, caravan animals, water carriers and porters, door frames and weathered wall feet on every house, the Lotfollah's carpets, Qur'an stand and lamps; the caravanserais, the Shah Mosque's interior and the madrasas still for later.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Naqsh-e Jahan Square, laid out under Shah Abbas I, "160 metres wide by 560 metres long" [stated: Wikipedia]; the Shah Mosque on the south side, Ali Qapu on the west, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque on the east, the Qeysarieh gate into the Grand Bazaar on the north [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Moment shown:** about 1630 — the Shah Mosque begun 1611 and finished 1629 [stated: islamichistoryandtravel; Wikipedia], Lotfollah 1603–1619 [stated: Wikipedia 2008 text], the bazaar completed 1620 [stated: Wikipedia, Grand Bazaar]. Chehel Sotoun (1647) is not yet there.

## 3. Orientation

The square's long axis north–south, the Shah Mosque at the south end; the mosque's court and sanctuary turned 45° to face Mecca (south-west), the portal facing the square. Lotfollah's dome chamber is turned the same way. [stated: Wikipedia, Columbia]

## 4. The style

Fired brick, buff, for the arcades and the outer walls; tile mosaic and haft-rangi (seven-colour) tile for the portals, iwans and minarets — deep blue with turquoise [stated: iranontour]; the Shah Mosque's dome turquoise, double-shelled [stated]; Lotfollah's dome cream with blue arabesques [inferred from its well-known appearance]; pointed arches; wooden talar columns. Muqarnas left for L3.

**The court's arcades (2026-11-06).** The court is "surrounded by a two-story arcade on four sides with four iwans, one at the center of each side"; the walls "fully covered with a polychrome, mostly dark blue, glazed tile revetment above a continuous marble dado"; the portal iwan "surmounted by tiers of muqarnas forming a semi-dome" [stated: Archnet, Masjid-i Imam]. Ours: each bay of both storeys a rectangular recess 2.45 m deep (it was a flat panel at the back of the opening, with the space behind the face open to the sky) under a pointed half-dome of lapis tile with a net of turquoise ribs (karbandi) following the shell, a band of two tiers of muqarnas cells in cream at the springing, the walls and floor in cream tile; a roof over the recesses between the face and the rooms behind [inferred: the recess form, the karbandi and the colours of each part, after photographs of Safavid court facades; built with the Taj's alcove (`src/styles/mughal`), its Mughal door, jali and dark-stone panels left out].

## 5. The square's life

In the mornings pedlars, in the evenings polo; 12 gates; the square lit with oil lamps at night [stated: irannewsdaily (Isfahan.ir)]. People for later.

## 6. The town

Courtyard houses of mud brick plastered with straw-mud (kahgel), blank walls to the lanes, rooms round a court with a pool and a tree, a summer iwan on one side, flat roofs, here and there a small dome. [inferred from Iranian vernacular]

## 7. Ground and water

The level plain at about 1,570 m; the river Zayandeh 1.5 km south, outside the disc. Water in the square's canal and the royal garden's pool. (`ground.js`) [inferred]

## 8. Planting

Plane trees (chenar) along the square's canal and in the royal garden; a few elms and fruit trees in the town's courts and the gardens at the edge; dry ground elsewhere. [stated: magiran for the square; inferred elsewhere]

## 9. Sky and weather

A dry, clear sky; strong sun; a little dust. Latitude 32.7° N. [inferred]

## 10. Sound

Persian classical music: the santur and the tar over a drone, the ney leading; the naqqareh-khaneh (drums and horns) over the Qeysarieh portal for later. Ours: a bright plucked string, a drone and the ney, mode close to Homayun. [inferred]

## 11. Detail (2026-11-06)

**The domes.** Lotfollah's dome is covered in "creamy tiles", a "floral dome" [stated: Tehran Times (restoration report)]; the exterior drum carries a running inscription in white tile on a blue ground (surahs 91, 76 and 108) [stated: Wikipedia (kiwix 2023)]; a ring of grilled windows pierces the drum at the dome's base, sixteen in the diagram [stated: Studio Matrx]. Ours: on the cream dome a net of lapis bands with a white line along each, sixteen spiralling up each way so that they cross in lozenges shrinking toward the crown, a turquoise medallion at each crossing; over the drum's sixteen windows a white frame and lattice; above them the band of white letters on lapis [inferred: the net stands for the dome's arabesques, which are scrolling vines round lozenge medallions; the letters are abstract strokes]. The Shah Mosque's turquoise dome is given the same treatment, its arabesques in cream and white with lapis medallions, the drum's inscription and grilles likewise [inferred from its well-known appearance; not yet sourced].

**Ali Qapu's talar.** An open portico whose roof rests on eighteen wooden columns; a fountain on the talar itself, fed from a cistern along the building's north face; the talar dated 1643–44 [stated: Encyclopaedia Iranica]. The columns were covered with mirror work and the ceiling painted; a copper pool belongs to the columned balcony [stated: Wikipedia]. The talar is thus later than our moment (c. 1630); kept as built at L1 and noted here. Ours: the eighteen columns sixteen-sided, tapering, on stone bases, banded with mirror work (silver) at three heights, with four stepped tiers of painted niches as their capitals and a bolster under the main beams; the main beams over the column rows, joists every 1.5 m and the frames of the coffers under the painted ceiling; a balustrade of posts, rails and wooden lattice on the three open sides; the copper-lined pool between the two middle columns with its marble rim and the bronze spout of the fountain [inferred: the column form, the capitals, the balustrade, the pool's size].

## 12. Ali Qapu inside; the arcade shops (2026-11-06)

**Ali Qapu's interior.** Six floors, "each reached by a steep spiral staircase"; the music hall on the top floor, its walls set with deep niches and "cut-out plaster decorations ... depict[ing] pots and vessels", ornamental and said to carry sound clearly; the reception floors above with the largest rooms [stated: Wikipedia]. The gateway "a central vaulted passage flanked by two storeys of smaller rooms", the tālār on top; behind it a square building of three principal levels with its reception chamber on the tālār's level, flanked by storeys of smaller rooms; Della Valle (1617) climbed "a narrow stairway" to many small rooms; an enclosed stairway on the south side of the square building gave access to the tālār [stated: Encyclopaedia Iranica]. Ours: in the gate block a vaulted passage (a shallow segmental vault with two ribs) from the door on the square, solid piers either side, the hall of the chancellery over it on painted beams; in the tower behind, on the south side, an enclosed stair winding round a square newel in four steep flights a storey (riser 0.2 m, about 39°) with a door into every floor; the reception chamber at the tālār's level (13 m) opening on the tālār through three doors, with carpets and a low throne platform with bolsters; two storeys of rooms (19.5, 26 m); the music room at the top (32.5 m), its walls lined with stucco 0.22 m thick cut through in rows of vessels — long-necked bottles, vases, goblets, jars — under a row of arched niches, the cut-outs showing a darker plaster behind; wooden lattice in every window; hanging lamps. The square stair stands for the spiral ones, and the storey heights are ours [inferred].

**The square's shops.** "A continuous two-story arcade of shops" round the square, about 200 shops [stated: audiala; iransafar]. Ours: behind each lower arch a shop front on the rooms behind the arcade's passage — door leaves folded open, a lattice transom — and a stone platform with wares (cloth, rolled carpets, copper vessels, jars), in each upper arch a lattice balustrade [inferred from Iranian bazaar practice].

## 13. The Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque inside (third pass)

**What the sources say.** The sanctuary is "a single domed chamber (19 meters on a side)"; the mosque lies "at an angle (almost 45 degrees)" to the maydan; one passes through "two corridors, oriented respectively to the northeast and southeast", then "turning southwest to face the qibla wall, one enters the domed chamber to see the mihrab on the opposite wall"; "four squinches of pointed-arched panels, framed by an inscription band in white and blue"; the drum has "alternating double-grilles windows"; "the interior dome has a sunburst from which descend medallions inscribing floral motifs", which "become larger as they descend away from the center" [stated: Archnet]. The passage is "long, low, gloomy"; eight arches of turquoise cable-form tilework "rise from a low dado to the full height of the wall", four in the squinch positions and four against the walls; a "turquoise cable moulding" runs below the dome; the dome is inset with lemon-shaped compartments — ogee-mandorla units set in a plain brick lattice, in concentric rings of thirty-two (Brend) — decreasing toward the "formalised peacock" at the apex (Byron); "nearly a score of windows"; the mihrab "enamelled with tiny flowers on a deep blue meadow" (Byron) [stated: Wikipedia, after Brend and Byron].

**Ours.** The chamber is now 19 m inside (half-width 10.5 m with 2 m walls; centre moved 1 m east). The portal's iwan opens by a door into a vestibule 9.4 × 5.6 m under a plaster ceiling at 7 m; from it a passage 2.6 m wide and 4.5 m high runs north-east along the chamber's north-west wall, turns south-east along its north-east wall, and ends at a door 2 × 3.2 m in marble jambs in the middle of that wall, facing the mihrab; two hanging lamps light it. Inside: a marble dado 1.45 m with a moulded top; walls lined with lapis tile; eight pointed arches 7.9 m wide (the sides of the octagon) rising from the dado to 13.4 m, outlined in a twisted turquoise cable; inside each wall arch a band of white letters on blue edged in white, the field cream with a lozenge medallion (turquoise, lapis, white, yellow) and little white flowers; the spandrels cream; across each corner the arch on the diagonal closing over the corner as a squinch — a cream hood with five turquoise ribs and a band of letters along its arch; a ring at 14 m, then a drum 4 m high with a band of white letters on lapis, sixteen windows of daylight behind white grilles in turquoise frames, a cable at the dome's foot; the inner dome (9 m radius, 27 m at the crown) of buff brick set with rings of thirty-two lemon-shaped medallions — white rim, yellow field, lapis and turquoise heart — twelve rings, shrinking toward a sunburst of turquoise and white rays round a yellow disc with lapis petals inside a lapis ring. The mihrab in the qibla wall: a niche 2.2 × 4.2 m, 1.4 m deep, under a lapis half-dome with turquoise ribs and cream muqarnas cells, its back strewn with little white flowers with yellow hearts, a cable round its arch, a frame of white-edged letters on blue round it, flowers over the arch. [inferred: the storey heights, the inner dome's radius and profile, the drum's height, the passage's width and height, the colours of the medallions and fields, the letters as abstract strokes; the peacock left as a sunburst]

## 14. The Qeysarieh portal's Sagittarius (third pass)

The portal's iwan, the Naqqara-khana, is "crowned with the representation of Sagittarius in mosaic tile" [stated: Archnet, Maydan-i Naqsh-i Jahan]; the sign is "the tiger's body, human's head and dragon's tail"; the bazaar was finished about 1605 and the portal in 1617; paintings attributed to Reza Abbasi of Shah Abbas's hunt and his war with the Uzbeks are on its upper walls, and the naqqareh-khaneh, where drums, horns and karna sounded at sunset and sunrise, stood above until 1889 [stated: iranroute]. Ours: the door at the back of the iwan lowered to 5 m (the portal element's `doorH`), and over it a tile panel 5 × 2.5 m: a turquoise frame, a white border, a blue ground strewn with little white flowers with yellow hearts; on it the tiger's body in yellow with black stripes and white paws, the archer rising from its shoulders in a white robe with a turquoise girdle and a yellow crown, turned back and drawing his black bow, the arrow aimed at the dragon's head at the end of the turquoise tail curling up from the tiger's rump, its spine picked out in white, its jaws open [inferred: the composition, the colours, the size and place in the iwan]. The paintings and the drum gallery are left for later.

## 15. The square's people and horses (third pass)

"In the mornings pedlars, in the evenings polo" [stated: irannewsdaily, §5]. Ours (`life.js`): at each end of the polo field, a little in from the stone goalposts, six saddled horses held by grooms with riders talking by some of them; four horses of the guard held before Ali Qapu by men in red; people before the Qeysarieh portal and along the walks by the arcades — men in long robes with sashes and turbans, the red turban of the Qizilbash now and then, women veiled [inferred: the numbers and places; the dress after Safavid paintings]. Kept clear of the arrival view down the square to the Shah Mosque's portal. (The pedlars' stalls are the settlement-wide life's market.)

## 16. Phase D: the furnished house, the bazaar's trades, the street life, the Lotfollah's furnishing (fourth pass)

**The courtyard house.** The Persian house is entered through "a small enclosed transitional space called Hashti", which turns the visitor from the street into a corridor (dalan); the court is a private garden with "a central pool (howz) with surrounding gardens containing trees of figs, pomegranates, and grape vines"; houses of the hot regions have "sunken sitting rooms (talar)" and iwans, and "thick massive walls" [stated: Wikipedia "Traditional Persian residential architecture"]. Ours (`khaneh_open`, the house the lane from the square's east gap meets, `layout.js` `OPEN_HOUSE`; built with the shared courtyard-house builder `src/styles/mughal/courthouse.js`): the street door on the west in a frame of fired brick, its two studded leaves standing open, two iron knockers, a stone step; the vestibule takes the middle of the west wing, its door to the court set off to one side so that the lane sees nothing of the court; the court paved in brick round a stone-rimmed pool lined in turquoise tile, a bed of green either side; on the north the talar, 7.2 m high, open to the court by a pointed arch 4 m wide in a frame of fired brick under a band of lapis tile, latticed windows either side; inside, its floor raised 0.45 m, a medallion carpet, bolsters along the back and sides, cushions, a brass tray on its stand with cups and an ewer, four niches (taqcheh) in the back wall with an ewer or a blue jar in each, a painted ceiling on dark beams, a bronze lamp; on the east a room of three doors with a kilim, a chest with the bedding folded on it, a bolster, a lamp on a ledge; a water jar and pots in the court; the master of the house seated on the talar's carpet, a servant in the court (`life.js`) [inferred: the plan, the sizes, the furnishing — the house type after the source, the objects after Safavid paintings]. **All the town's houses** (`khaneh`) now have the brick door frame, studded leaves, knockers and step, and the foot of their walls darkened to between 0.65 and 1.1 m by the lane's dust (§5 of DESIGN) [inferred].

**The bazaar's trades.** The caravanserais of the bazaar — more than a hundred after the Safavid era — were "single or double storied buildings" with "a courtyard surrounded by several rooms", stores, shops and stables below and travellers above, housing "the land loads of camels and horses"; the roofed market streets (qaisariya) were where "shopkeepers and craftsmen with the same trade" displayed their goods [stated: Iran Chamber Society, "Bazaar of Isfahan"]. Ours: every arch of the vaulted bazaar is now a shop 2.6 m deep between partitions, its floor a platform 0.45 m high with a stone lip, a painted ceiling of planks; the trades in runs — the cloth sellers (shelves of bolts on the back wall, bolts on the platform), the carpet sellers (rolls, a carpet spread, one hung on the back wall with its medallion), the coppersmiths (cauldrons, nested pans, trays stood on edge and hung on the wall, an anvil), the spice sellers (open sacks with their heaps and a scoop, jars on a shelf), the potters; a shopkeeper sitting cross-legged on his cushion in most shops, buyers before them, porters (hammal) with sacks and bales on their backs, a donkey led through [inferred: the trades' order and goods].

**Animals and street life.** At the bazaar's north end a caravan come in: five camels with their pack saddles and bales (one unloaded), cameleers with their staffs, two donkeys with panniers; water carriers (saqqa) at the canal with the waterskin on the back [inferred: the places; the caravans' camels, horses and donkeys after Iran Chamber Society above].

**The Lotfollah's furnishing.** Carpets laid toward the qibla (a medallion carpet before the door, a blue one before the mihrab, two runners at the sides), a Qur'an open on its folding stand (rahle) by the mihrab with a prayer rug before it, two glass lamps on long chains from the dome [inferred: the furnishing of a royal oratory].

## 17. The Shah Mosque's sanctuary inside (fifth pass)

The qibla iwan now opens by a door in its back wall (5 m wide, 6.5 m high, `pass`) into the domed sanctuary, 22.5 m square inside as in §2, built with the Lotfollah's interior builder at its scale: marble dado, eight pointed arches (17 m) with inscription bands and cable mouldings, squinches, a windowed ring, the inner dome of buff brick with rings of lemon-shaped medallions round a sunburst (crown about 39 m), under the outer turquoise dome (52 m): the double shell of the real mosque, the inner dome lower than the outer [stated: §2 sources for the outer dome; the double shell is in Wikipedia's Shah Mosque article]; a larger mihrab (3.2 x 6 m) in the qibla wall, carpets, a Qur'an stand, lamps [inferred: all proportions and furnishing]. The sanctuary's entrance wall abuts the iwan's back wall.

## 18. The coffeehouse (qahveh-khaneh) (fifth pass)

Safavid coffeehouses were "large vaulted buildings with central pools"; along the whitewashed walls arched recesses with raised floors about 1 m high, carpeted, served as benches; lamps hung from the roof; Tavernier saw a vessel of running water in the middle of each room for cleaning pipes; a 1619 hall had "a central fountain with several jets"; poets sat on high chairs at the centre; coffee was the original drink, water pipes were smoked; reciters of Ferdowsi's epic, storytellers and dervishes performed; they clustered round the Maydan-e Shah, the Chehel Sotun and the Chahar Bagh [stated: Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Coffeehouse"]. Ours (`qahveh_khaneh`, `layout.js` TEAHOUSE, 18 x 16 m by the Qeysarieh's east side, door on the west lane): a brick hall with arched lattice windows, a painted ceiling and frieze, a dome on the roof, a marble pool with a fountain, carpeted platforms 0.5 m high (so that they can be climbed) along three walls with bolsters, trays, cups, ewers and pipes, a counter with the coffee pots on a brazier, a high chair for the poet beside the pool, four hanging lamps [inferred: size, siting, the furnishing]. No samovar or tea: the sources give coffee.

## 19. The Chahar Bagh avenue (fifth pass)

At most 48 m wide; water ran down the middle in stone channels, falling in miniature cascades from terrace to terrace; each side lined with plane trees, cypresses, pines and junipers; a pedestrian walk either side of the channel; a latticed pavilion for the harem; a place of recreation with animated social and commercial activity [stated: Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Chahar Bagh"]. Ours (`CHAHAR`, west of the royal garden, 46 x 220 m): brick-paved, a stone channel down the middle with a square stone basin every 44 m and low footbridges, two rows of plane trees each side (8 and 14 m from the channel) [inferred: the spacing; the cypresses, pavilion and the shops left out]. Not built: the caravanserai (the dossier's §16 sources), the movers, fauna and smoke systems.
