# Southern Song Lin'an (Hangzhou), c. 1200 — dossier

Settlement id: `song-hangzhou` · Style: `song` · Region: Jiangnan (the Qiantang River) · Code: `src/settlements/song-hangzhou/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the Liuhe Pagoda on the slope of Yuelun Hill with Kaihua Temple's halls; the hill's woods; the riverside: the stone flood wall on the Qiantang, the road along it, tea houses for watching the bore; the Longshan quarter by the river — a canal down to the river with its lock, arched bridges, a street of shops and two-storey houses, warehouses, boats; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 built (§10): the pagoda's bracket sets and wind bells, the river boats under sail, the day of the tide (the coloured stands along the flood wall, the crowd, tea tables on the road). L3 built since: the pagoda walkable inside (corridor, stair, central chamber), the riverbank market of the tide day; the chambers' seated Buddhas and the carved sumeru bases; three tea houses on the road; the shops' name boards. L3 recorded for later: the tidal bore itself, the river's timber rafts and salt boats (no Song source found yet; the Jianggan timber landing described in aisixiang 175775 is Ming–Qing), the city wall and the palace on Phoenix Hill, markets in full. West Lake with the Su Causeway and the Leifeng Pagoda, the city wall with its gates and the Imperial Way, the life and the furnished interiors are built since (§12–§21); the palace on Phoenix Hill and the tidal bore itself are not.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Liuhe (Six Harmonies) Pagoda, at the foot of Yuelun Hill facing the Qiantang River; first built 970 by Wuyue, destroyed 1121, rebuilt fully by 1165 under the Southern Song [stated: Wikipedia]; built to calm the tidal bore and as a navigational light [stated: Wikipedia; chinatoursguide].
- **Moment shown:** about 1200, the pagoda standing in its Song rebuilding, Lin'an the capital.

## 3. Orientation

The river runs east–west along the south; the pagoda faces it from the north bank. [stated]

## 4. The style

Song timber architecture: halls with gently curved hip-and-gable roofs on bracket sets, white walls, red-brown timber; the pagoda's brick core plastered, its galleries and eaves of wood with bells at the corners (104 iron bells today) [stated: trip.com; aroundus]. Houses: the Jiangnan type, white walls, dark tiles, shops open to the street. [inferred]

## 5. The town

Two-storey shophouses and tea houses on the riverside road and along the canal; warehouses by the lock; boats on the canal and moored at the river. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The river's water about 2 m below the flood wall's top; the hill rising about 60 m. (`ground.js`) [inferred]

## 7. Planting

Camphor, pines and bamboo on the hill; willows along the canal and the road. [inferred]

## 10. Detail (L3)

- **The pagoda's eaves.** Bells at the corners of the eaves, 104 iron bells today [stated: trip.com; aroundus]. Ours: an iron wind bell (hook, bell, clapper and wind plate) at each of the eight corners of every eave and the crowning roof. Under each gallery eave a row of bracket sets — at each corner one pointing out along the diagonal, two on each face — of bearing block, cross arm with small blocks and two tiers of arms stepping out to the eave purlin, on an architrave round the core; the gallery posts carried up into the eaves' corners [inferred: the Song bracket set in its usual form; red timbers, ochre blocks].
- **The tide.** On the 18th of the eighth month the people of Lin'an emptied the city to watch the bore on the Qiantang; below the Liuhe Pagoda the great houses put up coloured curtained stands for more than twenty li, carriages and crowds shoulder to shoulder [stated: hznews (Hangzhou Daily) citing *Wulin jiushi*]. Ours: the stands along the top of the flood wall west of the canal, curtained at the back and sides, roofs of red, green, ochre and blue cloth, people seated in them; the crowd along the wall's edge facing the river; tea tables before the tea houses on the road, people walking down to the river.
- **The pagoda inside.** The Song brick body is, from the outside in, an outer wall, a corridor, an inner wall and the central chamber; seven storeys inside ("seven open, six dark": the 13 outer eaves are Qing, 1900); a spiral stair joins the storeys, 226 steps in all, steep; the chamber's ceiling a caisson (zaojing) painted red and blue with cloud dragons; brick-carved figures, apsaras, flowers, birds and beasts on the waists of every Sumeru base [stated: hznews 2022-09-04]. Ours: in each of the seven storeys the outer wall (1.4 m thick at the top, thickening downward as the tiers widen to 4.7 m at the foot), a corridor 2 m wide, an inner wall 0.8 m and an octagonal chamber 4.3 m across, one plan up the whole height; in the corridor a stair of granite treads climbs each storey up the east side to a landing in the south-east corner and up the south side, arriving in the south-west corner above (about 40° above the ground storey, 49° in the tall ground storey) [inferred: the stair's course]; doorways with red frames and door pins through the outer wall onto the gallery on the three diagonals the stair leaves free, the fourth diagonal a shut door; the chamber entered from the north, with a two-tier painted caisson and an offering table with a bronze censer [inferred]; steps up the stone base on the south and a gap in the lowest gallery's railing there [inferred]. The images and the Sumeru carvings recorded for later.
- **The riverbank market.** On the tide day small peddlers came to the bank for the crowd and prices doubled; merchants took whole stretches of the bank, temporary sheds for more than ten li from Miaozitou to the Liuhe Pagoda [stated: hznews 2022-09-15, citing *Mengliang lu* 4 and *Wulin jiushi* 3]. Ours: along the foot of the flood wall west of the canal, stalls under cloth awnings facing the road (fruit and greens, pots, cloth, baskets) with sellers behind and buyers before, hawkers squatting by the two baskets of their shoulder poles, a fruit seller under a parasol [inferred: the goods].
- **River boats.** Ours: the Bian type of boat (`song_boat`) with its bipod mast up; moored by the wall with sails down, out on the river under a batten lug sail of matting [inferred].

- **The pagoda's images and sumeru bases.** Brick-carved figures, apsaras, flowers, birds and beasts on the waists of every sumeru base inside [stated: hznews 2022-09-04]. Ours: in each storey's corridor a sumeru base 0.9 m high along the foot of the inner wall wherever the stair leaves the corridor free (west, south-west, north-west, north-east, and the north either side of the chamber's door) — plinth, two mouldings, the waist, two mouldings, the cap, in grey brick — its waist set with carved panels (a rosette, flying figures over clouds, a seated lion, in that order along each face) [inferred: where the bases stand and the order of the panels]; in each chamber a seated Buddha (1.9 m with his mandorla) on a six-sided sumeru throne against the south wall, facing the door, before him an altar table (top with everted ends, apron, legs and stretchers) with a bronze censer between two candlesticks [inferred: one image to each chamber, the triad of censer and candlesticks].
- **Tea houses.** Lin'an's tea houses: "flowers of the four seasons set out, paintings by famous hands hung up, to adorn the shop"; "flower racks with rare pines and junipers on them"; teas and soups sold all year; porcelain cups on lacquer stands; storytellers at a long table [stated: hznews 2020-03-30 citing *Mengliang lu* 16]. Ours (`tea_house`, `src/styles/song/hangzhou.js`): three of the wide shop lots on the road nearest the pagoda's stair — a stone plinth and paved floor, white walls, the front open between three or four posts under a beam with a red lattice valance, a gable roof; three hanging scrolls of ink landscapes (far, middle and near ranges, mist between) over a long table with two vases of plum branches in blossom; the counter on the east wall with a brazier and its copper kettle, black tea bowls on red lacquer stands, two shelves of bowls above; by the door a stepped rack of potted pines; two tea tables with their benches and seated customers, the host by the counter, a two-character name board on the front beam [inferred: the plan and the furnishing beyond what is quoted].

## 12. The city and the lake (townscape, B1)

The disc grows to 520 m (large class). It is a **compressed composite** of Lin'an: the real city is long north–south, with the palace on Phoenix Hill at its south end and the West Lake outside its west wall, some 3 km from the Liuhe Pagoda [stated: §2 above, Nottingham]; ours puts the pagoda, the south wall, the Imperial Way, the canal and the lake within one walk [inferred: a design choice for the walkable disc].

- **The Imperial Way (御街).** The street ran from Hening Gate, the palace's north gate, through the city; its southern section was about 15.5 m wide after an 1158 widening, about 16.5 m after the renovation of 1271 (nine wagons abreast); paved with cut stone slabs (of about 35,300 stones in the 1271 renovation, some 20,000 replaced), with vaulted drains beneath carrying rainwater to the canals; "all the households on both sides were engaged in business" [stated: Nottingham repository 794882, citing Zhu Mu, Wu Zimu *Mengliang lu*, Marco Polo]; hundreds of stalls, goldsmiths, pearl shops and pawnshops in its central part [stated: same]. An excavated stretch of stone-slab street with a waterway beside it was found in 2003–04 [stated: chinaculture.org]. Ours (`layout.js`, `town.js`): the Way 16 m wide, stone-paved, running from the river road north through the south gate and the city to the lake gate, shop houses 22 m deep on both sides (a third of them courtyard houses), nearly all shops with name boards; the vaulted drains and railings are not modelled [inferred: that the street reaches our gates; the paving colour].
- **The city wall.** Rammed earth faced with brick, a walk on its top with a crenellated outer parapet (nü qiang) and a solid inner one, bastions (mamian) projecting from the outer face, gates with a passage between timber-lined piers under a tower, the canal passing through a water gate [stated: Qing wall about 9 m high and 11 m across the top; Fengshan Water Gate joined the Jiangnan Canal to the Qiantang: Wikipedia, Hangzhou City Wall; the Song wall's own dimensions: no source at hand]. Ours (`town.js`, `townscape.js` `song_parapet`): the south wall (z = −32) and north wall (z = −232) 9 m high, base 16 m battering to 11 m, the west wall between them; gates on the Way at both walls (the north one the lake gate, onto the shore promenade); at the canal a lintel of grey brick over the canal and both canal streets (the water gate); five bastions on the south wall's outer face, four on the north, three on the west [inferred: measures after the Qing wall and Kaifeng's, bastion spacing].
- **Houses of the Song, not the Ming.** The houses are `house_jiangnan` with plain gable (xuanshan) or hipped ends — never the stepped horse-head gables, which are of the Ming and later; one or two storeys; the tall ones on the Imperial Way [inferred]. The Su Causeway: about 2.8 km long, Su Shi's (1089), peach trees and willows along it [stated: theworldofchinese.com]; the ten scenes of the lake date from the Southern Song, the lotus of Quyuan, once a Southern Song wine brewery, and the orioles in the willows [stated: same]. ## 15. The moving life

- **Water ways.** The canals' rectangles and straight lanes across the lake kept 22 m from the shore (`ways.js` `lakeLanes`).

## 16. Animals (`fauna.js`)

- **Fish.** Huge five-coloured carp kept in the release pond at Yuquan "since the Song"; Gaozong, retired, built a goldfish pond at the Deshenggong, and the eunuch Lu Yunsheng's garden had a pond with dozens of goldfish kinds [stated: hznews 2020-03-17 (https://hznews.hangzhou.com.cn/wghz/content/2020-03/17/content_7696714.htm)]; the Tiantai monk Ciyun Zunshi (964–1032) asked the emperors to make West Lake a release pond; release ponds especially popular in the Song, Zhenzong's edict of 1017 [stated: Fangsheng Pond (https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Fangsheng_Pond)]; "Viewing Fish at Flower Pond" one of the Ten Scenes of the lake, the ranking Song, the present set Qing [stated: West Lake, Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lake)]. Ours: schools of dark carp, goldfish and large red carp along the lake's shore in 4–26 m of water, dark carp in the canals [inferred: the numbers and the sizes; the colours are the engine's palettes].
- **Birds.** Orioles in the willows of the lake's shore ("Listening to Orioles at Willow Waves") [stated: hznews 2020-03-17; Wikipedia]. Ours: little egrets and a few grey herons on the lake's bank and the canals' edges, swallows over lake and canals, sparrows on the paving, crows at twilight [inferred: the engine's birds; the orioles are not modelled].

## 17. Smoke and mist (`smoke.js`)

Kitchen smoke from a third of the houses at the three meals; steam from the tea, wine, noodle and bun shops (a floor of steam all night: the Imperial Way's night stalls sold pancakes, soups, buns and dumplings, and its morning stalls fried sausage, blood soup and mutton noodles [stated: hznews 2024-07-05]); incense at the temples' censers all day; mist banks over the canals and over West Lake on a 34 m grid in the water, thick at dawn and gone by midmorning [inferred: the lake's morning mist; no Song source for its hour].

## 18. The goods of each trade (`shops.js`)

- **The coloured-silk gate** (cailou huanmen): "the doors of the wine houses are all bound with coloured towers and huanmen" [stated: *Dongjing meng Hua lu*, as quoted by rujiazg 3505 (https://www.rujiazg.com/article/3505)]; the grander the shop, the grander the gate, Sun Yang's rising two or three storeys [stated: same]. The Southern Song wine houses of Lin'an kept the Bianjing custom [inferred]. Ours: two lacquered posts and a lintel with a second tier stepped in above, rows of scalloped silk swags and white drops hung from both (cloth that sways in the wind), tassels, a gilt finial; two widths, 4 m and 6 m; on wine houses with a front of 4.6 m or more (all on the Imperial Way).
- **The wine flag**: "several widths of blue and white cloth" [stated: *Rongzhai xubi*, rujiazg 3505]. Ours: a bamboo pole on a stone foot with a long flag of three strips, blue, white, blue (red and white at the food shops), swaying [inferred: the proportions].
- **Lantern signs and hanging boards**: freestanding lantern boxes lit by candles inside ("孙羊", "正店", "香醪"); hanging signs; shop names such as "钱家干果铺" [stated: rujiazg 3505, on the Qingming scroll]. Ours: a lantern box of oiled paper on two posts with a tiled hood, at the end of a front on the Imperial Way and on a third of the others; a lacquered signboard with gilt border hung from a bracket on a post [inferred].
- **Goods.** "People of the capital generally bought from famous shops" [stated: *Mengliang lu* in rujiazg 3505]; Lin'an's Guanxiang quarter sold brocades, gauze, embroidered clothing [stated: hznews 2024-11-22]; wine in lidded jars (the lists of nearly a hundred wines in the *Wulin jiushi*) [stated: hznews 2026-02-14]; tea houses with flowers of the four seasons, famous paintings and "flower racks with rare pines and junipers" [stated: hznews 2020-03-30, as §10]. Ours, by trade: silk (a rack of hung lengths, trestles with bolts), tea (cakes, black bowls on red lacquer stands, a brazier with a copper ewer), books (stitched volumes in blue and brown covers), medicine (a drawer cabinet, an iron boat-mill, jars, hung herbs), fans (round silk fans), lacquerware, wine (jars with red cloth lids, silver-white measures), food (a bun stove with stacked steamers; a noodle pot, bowls and stools), incense and candles, flowers (a stepped rack of pots and pines), daily wares, a pawnshop counter [inferred: the forms; the trades' list from the Mengliang lu's shop lists, which names them but not their furnishing].
- Trades by lot: the layout's own `trade`, else by seed (the Imperial Way: silk, tea, wine, books, medicine, lacquer, fans, incense, flowers; the lanes: food, wares, tea, incense, flowers) [inferred].

## 19. Interiors (`interiors.js`, `life.js`)

- **Tea house.** As §10: "flowers of the four seasons, paintings by famous hands, flower racks of rare pines" [stated: hznews 2020-03-30]; "large tea houses hung famous calligraphy and painting" [stated: *Dushi jisheng*, rujiazg 17425 (https://www.rujiazg.com/article/17425)]. Ours (`hz_teahouse`): three or four hanging scrolls of ink landscapes on the back wall over a long table with two vases of plum; the host's counter with a brazier, a copper ewer, black bowls on lacquer stands and two shelves of bowls; rows of tables with a long bench each side and customers seated; a stepped rack of potted pines by the door; palace lanterns hung [inferred: the plan and the furniture's forms: the Song table with square legs and stretchers, the long bench].
- **Wine house.** "The wine houses of Hangzhou had more than ten small private rooms each and vessels all of silver, vying in luxury"; an old woman with a small burner to light incense at the table for a tip, called the "incense woman" [stated: *Wulin jiushi*, rujiazg 17425]; Marco Polo's boats' banquet furniture is the same idea [stated: Fordham]. Ours (`hz_restaurant`): private rooms along the back wall, each with a lattice front and a half-rolled bamboo curtain, a table with four lantern-back chairs, silver-white wine ewers and a lantern; in the hall tables with chairs; a wine counter with jars; a three-fold screen; the incense woman walking between the tables [inferred: the plan, the sizes].
- **Shops of the Imperial Way** (`hz_imperial_shop`): a counter across the room, shelves along the back wall with the trade's goods (bolts, bowls, volumes, jars), the keeper behind, a table and a stool; trade-specific objects on the counter (silk, tea brazier, a drug mill) [inferred].
- **House** (`hz_house`): the ancestors' altar on the back wall (tablets in a shrine box, a bronze censer between candlesticks), two lantern-back chairs and a table before it, a couch (ta) with a screen back, a chest, a qin on its table, hanging scrolls, a lantern [inferred: the household's arrangement after the Song furniture generally: the lantern-hanging chair, the couch].
- Which lots: `interiorLots()` chooses up to 2 wine houses and 6 shops on or by the Imperial Way, 4 tea houses and 3 houses among the wide lots; the town must raise those lots as enterable shells (floor at street + 0.45).

## 20. Sound

By room (`music.js`): out of doors the qin's slow phrases (a plucked string stands in) with a dizi over the water; the temple hall a sheng drone, a monk's chant and a bell; tea house: pipa under a storyteller's voice; wine house: a quick pipa and the song-lyric singers' voice (the wine houses' singing girls, *Wulin jiushi* [stated in outline: hznews 2026-02-14, which lists the lake boats' vendors and wines; the singers are [inferred]]); shop: a thread of pipa; house: qin alone with the ney. The engine chooses a zone by the room one stands in, so the lake shore and the Imperial Way both use `open`; their ambience (water, hubbub, hawkers) is the engine's.

## 21. Second pass (integration)

- **Furnished lots are raised, not doubled.** `life.js` `interiorLots()` chooses up to 2 wine houses, 6 shops, 4 tea houses and 3 houses (never the three river-road tea houses, which keep the older `tea_house` element with its own furnishing); the town raises exactly these lots as `hz_shell`: granite plinth and brick floor 0.45 m over the street, white walls on a grey dado, the front open between red-brown posts under a beam with a lattice valance (tea house, wine house, shop; shop fronts with stacked shutters at the ends) or a plaster wall with the door under a tiled hood and two lattice windows (house); the wine house of two storeys, its upper front a plaster wall with lattice windows behind bamboo blinds and a gallery over the rear; a gable or hipped roof of dark tiles. The furniture, people and music zones are the life's (`interiors.js`, rooms from `interiorRooms()` in the pagoda plan's rooms) [inferred: the forms; the Song tea house's open front from *Mengliang lu* as in §10].
- **House variety.** Each lot carries its own wash — fresh white, rain-streaked (`plaster_streak`), greened by the damp (`plaster_damp`, more by the canal), or dull grey over a brick plinth where it was not renewed — and 18 % have weathered pale tiles (`tile_grey`); the upper floor juts over the street in over half the Imperial Way's two-storey shops and a third elsewhere; the end houses of each run are hipped. A separate random stream keeps the plots as they were [inferred: Suzhou's method; the Song houses' overhang after the Qingming scroll's shops, not re-checked].
- **The Imperial Way's surface** (`hz_imperial_way`): "paved with stone and brick, the middle filled with gravel, round pipes draining the rain into the canals" [stated: §15 sources]: worn slab bands 3.3 m each side, a granite kerb, the gravel bed with two dark wheel ruts, a drain cover with slots every 16 m alternating sides [inferred: the proportions].
- **Routes.** The pagoda's walk goes round the base by its east side (the censer stands on the axis before the stair); a second climbs the base's stair, goes round the gallery and into the first storey by the south-west doorway.
- **Not built:** the third storeys of the great wine houses, the palace, the drains' grates as openings, a worn-slab variation by street.

## 23. Third pass: close detail

- **City gates** (`hz_gate`, replacing `song_gate` at Lin'an's two gates): a segmental brick vault (10 m wide, the springing at 4.4 m, 3 m rise, courses of mortar rings across the soffit), jambs with a granite plinth, a cap and coursed brick, on each portal a ring of nineteen granite voussoirs with a longer keystone and impost blocks, the facade faced as the wall, a granite string course under the platform's edge; the passage paved in granite slabs laid in staggered rows with two wheel tracks worn down and darker; the two door leaves folded back against the jambs at the outer portal, boards in two tones, three iron straps, rows of iron studs and a ring [inferred: Song gates were timber-lined passages or brick vaults; Lin'an's walls were brick-faced; the segmental form and the studded doors are our reading, as the Shine/Wikipedia accounts of Song architecture give no gate drawings]. The wide Imperial passage is 10 m (the Way is 16 m, §12), the vault leaves 2 m of masonry under the platform.
- **Trees, rebuilt** (`hz_tree`): the crown of each is lobes of smooth dark foliage (one at each limb's end and one over the fork) under hundreds of pointed leaf cards laid on the shell like scales, their normals bent outwards so the crown shades as one mass (as the engine's flora cards do); the camphor's broad dark leaves, the ginkgo's fans (green), the peach's narrow leaves with five-petalled blossom, the willow's drooping withes hung with narrow leaves, reaching to 0.9 m above the ground; the trunk flared at the foot, buttressed, twisting; limbs in two segments with twigs [inferred: the species' habit; leaves at about twice life size for legibility].
- **The painted pleasure boat** (`lake_boat`): strakes on the hull, a continuous gilt wale and timber gunwale; the cabin with its tie beams, gilt fascia, stern doors (one ajar), bamboo blinds rolled and half down on the side windows, the floor, a low table with a tea set, two cushioned benches and a paper lamp; at the bow a painted shield board, a bollard, an iron anchor and its rope; two long oars on thole posts; at the stern the yuloh (sculling oar) on its post, a brazier with a kettle, a bucket, a basket, a straw hat on a peg, a rolled mat, poles laid along the sides and a stern lantern [stated: painted pleasure boats and their hire on the lake, *Mengliang lu*, see Sources; the cabin and fittings follow the covered boats of the Song West Lake paintings, inferred: Cleveland Museum, *Boats at Anchor on West Lake at the City Gate*].
- **The Liuhe Pagoda's lower storeys** (`liuhe_pagoda`, `pagodaFront`): on the two lowest storeys a brick plinth with a granite cap, a pilaster at each corner (stone foot, red timber shaft, capital), lattice windows in frames over the dark openings of the compass faces, studded lacquer doors in a frame with a granite threshold on the others, and the gallery's rail with a baluster every metre and lattice panels between the rails; checked at noon and at dusk [inferred: the present pagoda's brick body in timber dress, §13].
- **The temple gate** (`temple_gate`): carved panels with a raised border and a lotus roundel on each pier face, drum stones (baogu shi) outside the piers on the court's outer side, a step up to the threshold on both sides [inferred: Song temple gates]. The pagoda's walks now pass the open door leaf (the waypoint had been behind it).
- **The Imperial Way's wear** (`hz_imperial_way`): patches of wet dark gravel, cobbles and flints worked up through the bed, horse dung, straw and the sweepings of the stalls, and places where a slab was lifted and relaid in gravel [inferred; the stalls and the paving are [stated], §12 and §18].
- **The canal streets' paving** (`hz_slabs`): worn stone slabs in staggered rows (darker down the middle), an iron-slotted granite grating near the canal edge every 18 m and brick patches where a slab was lifted [inferred: "paved with stone or brick" is Marco Polo's, in §15].
- Boats moored at the causeway's landing moved 0.2–1.2 m so that none touches the bank.
