# Ajanta, c. 480 — dossier

Settlement id: `ajanta` · Style: `ajanta` (new: `src/styles/ajanta/`), painted figures and the Pure-Land-style panel machinery from `dunhuang/paint.js` (read-only import of the generic `Panel`), figures and beasts from `life` · Region: Deccan plateau (Maharashtra, India) · Code: `src/settlements/ajanta/` · Size class Small ●●● (radius ≤ 190 m)

Moment shown: about 480, in the last years of the Vakataka work. King Harishena reigned 460–477, work was disrupted in 468 and 472 and largely ended about 477–480 [stated: Wikipedia "Ajanta Caves", after Walter Spink's chronology]. The painted viharas of the patrons' circle (Cave 1, Caves 16–17, the chaitya hall Cave 19) stand fresh and in use; the older Satavahana chaitya halls (Caves 9, 10) are already 500 years old; Cave 26 is being cut at the north end of the cliff [stated: Wikipedia, dated "late 5th or early 6th century"]. The visitor sees the cliff as a working monastery, not the ruin of 1819.

## 2. The real place

- **The gorge.** The caves are cut in the northern wall of a U-shaped gorge of the Waghora, an escarpment about 76 m high [stated: Wikipedia; World History], the river below them, waterfalls in the gorge audible from the caves when the river is high [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: the cliff face is 30–36 m above the gorge floor (the compression every settlement makes) [inferred], a squared horseshoe: the long main face (z −60…60, facing east) with two returning wings (north and south faces) closing the pocket; the river enters round the north wing's end, curves under the main face and leaves round the south wing [inferred: the topology is simplified to axis-aligned faces so that interiors stay walkable].
- **Rock.** Deccan Trap basalt, a dark grey-brown layered rock, here the palette's grey rock materials (`sandstone_carved`, `granite_rough`, `sandstone_green`) with iron-red staining (`tuff_red`) [inferred: the basalt's colour; no palette material is basalt-like in the "rock" kind: the existing `basalt` is paving].
- **The ledge.** The caves stand in two tiers and above; the main caves are reached by rock-cut stairs from the gorge floor along the face [inferred: usual description; Wikipedia notes Cave 29 sits at the highest level and 28–29 are now inaccessible]. Ours: a rock-cut ledge 6 m wide at +9 m before Caves 1 and 19, a rock-cut stair of 53 steps from the path, shallow shrine-niches at the ledge, at the gorge floor on the wings and, unreachable, at +20 m.
- **Climate** [inferred: the Deccan]: hot, dry for most of the year, the south-west monsoon June–September filling the river and the falls; a hazy bright sun, dry teak forest on the hills.
- **Planting** [inferred: Deccan dry forest]: teak, neem, banyan and tamarind-like fig, bamboo in the gorge, date palms near the village, dry grass.

## 3. The painted vihara (Cave 1)

- **Size and plan** [stated: Wikipedia: 35.7 × 27.6 m overall; Smarthistory: square hall, aisles on four sides, fourteen small chambers, twenty pillars]. Ours: a pillared porch 3.8 m deep and 24 m wide, six pillars across the front; a square hall 19.5 m on a side (the figure of Cave 16's hall: Wikipedia) with a ring of 20 pillars (six to a side) 12 m square, ceiling 5.2 m, flat with painted beams; five cells in each side wall and four in the rear wall (14), each 2.2 m wide, 2.8 m deep, a stone bed in each [stated: cells and bed form: World History for Cave 12]; at the rear the shrine, a sanctum on the axis with a seated Buddha [stated: Wikipedia] in the dharmachakra (teaching) pose on a throne.
- **Murals** [stated: Wikipedia, Smarthistory]: Bodhisattvas Padmapani and Vajrapani, over life-size, flank the shrine entrance; Jataka scenes along the walls; the ceiling painted with flowers, birds and animals [inferred: the usual description; the flat ceiling is painted as a patterned carpet]. Ours, in abstract form but true in colour and composition: dark red-brown ground, brown-skinned figures with high crowns and a swayed stance, jewel-hung, a lotus in the hand; a Jataka band of tiers of small figures and architecture; a frieze of lotus and geese; a ceiling of black and red panels with white-ochre medallions and animals.
- **Pigments** [stated: Smarthistory six colours; the rest inferred]: red and yellow ochre, green (malachite/terre verte), lapis lazuli blue, lamp black, lime white; the palette's `clay_robe`, `paint_ochre`, `clay_green`, `fresco_green`, `egyptian_blue`, `lacquer_black`, `paint_white`.
- **Light.** The caves are nearly dark inside [stated: Smarthistory]; the painters worked by daylight reflected in, and by oil lamps [inferred]. Ours: a few oil lamps; the porch's light falls through the three doors.
- **Painters** [inferred: the work of the 5th c. is by workshops under a patron's gift; stated by Wikipedia: "dry fresco"]: on scaffolds of poles and lashed planks against the walls, with bowls of pigment, brushes of animal hair, a boy grinding colour on a stone, a lamp at hand.

## 4. The chaitya hall (Cave 19)

- **Plan** [stated: Wikipedia: 16.05 × 7.09 m, 15 pillars between nave and aisles, 5th c.; World History: 17 pillars]. We follow Wikipedia: 15 octagonal pillars (six to a side and three round the apse), a stupa in the apse with a standing Buddha carved in front [stated: both], a barrel vault ribbed in rock to copy the timber ribs of the older halls [inferred: the vault of Caves 9, 10, 19, 26 is rock cut with ribs: usual description].
- **Facade** [stated: World History: the facade carved with figures, two life-size yakshas flanking the chaitya arch; Wikipedia: two fluted round pillars supporting a porch]. Ours: a porch with two fluted pillars over the door, above it the great horseshoe window, flanked by two standing yakshas in niches, bands of horseshoe-arch ornament and a frieze of small Buddhas; the whole facade is the rock's own, left the grey-brown with some plaster and paint [inferred: the facade was painted].
- **Stupa** [inferred: the form of Cave 26's stupa as given by Wikipedia: panelled drum, dome, a harmika of tiers]: a drum on a plinth, a dome, a square harmika and three umbrellas rising to the vault; plaster.

## 5. A monk's cell, the ledge and the stairs

- **Cell** [stated: World History: double stone beds with raised pillows in Cave 12; viharas as cells round a hall]: ours is a small vihara hall 5.8 m square with a bench round, two cells with stone beds and pillows, a lamp niche, plain lime plaster with a painted band.
- **Shrine niches** [inferred]: shallow niches with a painted seated Buddha in a mandorla and a carved doorframe, as the unfinished or small caves show.
- **Stairs.** Rock-cut, 15 m long for the 9 m rise; no rail [inferred].

## 6. The gorge below and the village

- **The river** [stated: Waghora; U-shaped gorge; falls audible]: ours is 8 m wide, shallow and clear in the dry season with a gravel bed, a fall from the plateau down the north wing's face into a pool [stated: "waterfalls in the gorge": Wikipedia; the exact place is ours].
- **The path and the village** [inferred: a monastery of perhaps 200 monks and a workforce of masons, painters and labourers had to be fed and housed; the Vakataka patrons' towns lay a day away]: a worn footpath along the gorge floor, a ford of stepping stones, and on the east bank a small cluster of mud-walled huts with thatch or tile roofs for labourers and lay servants, a store, a well, goats and zebu, a shrine tree.
- **Monks' dwelling** [inferred]: the cells in the viharas; monks cooked with the village.

## 7. People and work

- **Monks** in ochre-red robes over one shoulder, shaven, bare feet, alms bowls; lay worshippers in white wrapped cloth and shawls, women in saris of red and indigo cotton with a pot or basket; masons and painters in a dhoti with bare torso; labourers carrying baskets of rubble, water carriers; goatherds [inferred: the dress of the 5th-c. paintings themselves: dhotis, saris and turbans, bare feet].
- **Work in progress** [stated: Cave 26 and others unfinished, Wikipedia]: a stone mason with chisel and hammer at an unfinished niche on a scaffold, rubble baskets, a heap of chips at the cliff's foot [inferred].
