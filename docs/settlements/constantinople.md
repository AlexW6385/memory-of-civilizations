# Justinian's Constantinople (the first hill), about 562 — dossier

Settlement id: `constantinople` · Style: `byzantine` (Hagia Sophia) and `roman` (the city round it) · Region: the Bosphorus · Code: `src/settlements/constantinople/`
Replaces the legacy district of the previous design. Hagia Sophia itself is the full building from that design (`src/buildings/hagia-sophia`, dossier `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md`), with its atrium.
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (Hagia Sophia; the Augustaion with its porticoes and Justinian's column; Hagia Eirene; the Baths of Zeuxippus; the Hippodrome; the Great Palace's courts and halls on terraces; the Mese with its porticoes and the Forum of Constantine with its porphyry column; houses; ground, planting, weather, music). Built since: the Hippodrome's carceres, spina, Obelisk of Theodosius, Serpent Column, statues of Porphyrius, turning posts, kathisma and its sphendone curve at the south; Hagia Eirene as a domed basilica; the Milion; the columns of Constantine and Justinian with their statues; the Mese re-routed round the Hippodrome. Second pass (§4.4–§4.6, §10): the Senate house and the Chalke on an opened Augustaion, the Mese's porticoes with shops, the Forum of Constantine's two-storeyed ring and arches, street life. L3 pass (§4.7, §10): Hagia Sophia's ambo of 562 rebuilt after Paul the Silentiary, a procession from the Chalke to the church with the cross, candles, the Gospel and the emperor. L3 recorded for later: the harbours of Julian and Sophia, Hagia Eirene's interior, the sea walls and the Bosphorus itself (the sea is not yet shown: the ground falls toward it), the Theodosian land walls (5 km west, outside this settlement), the Basilica Cistern, the Walled Obelisk (its stone core is 10th century), the mosaics of Hagia Sophia's neighbours beyond the Chalke.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Hagia Sophia, built 532–537, its dome rebuilt in 562 [stated: `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md` and its sources].
- **Its neighbours on the first hill:**
  - the Augustaion square to the south, with the Column of Justinian [stated: Wikipedia "Augustaion"];
  - Hagia Eirene to the north, rebuilt by Justinian [stated: Wikipedia "Hagia Irene"];
  - the Baths of Zeuxippus between the Augustaion and the Hippodrome [stated: Wikipedia "Baths of Zeuxippus"];
  - the Hippodrome, about 450 m long, to the south-west [stated: Wikipedia "Hippodrome of Constantinople"];
  - the Great Palace stepping down on terraces toward the Sea of Marmara to the south-east [stated: Wikipedia "Great Palace of Constantinople"];
  - the Mese, the main street, leaving west to the oval Forum of Constantine with its porphyry column [stated: Wikipedia "Mese (Constantinople)"; "Forum of Constantine"].
- **Moment shown:** 562, the dome just rebuilt.

## 4. The style

Brick and stone with marble; domed halls; colonnaded porticoes along the Mese and round the squares; tiers of seats in the Hippodrome; porphyry columns. [stated: materials; inferred: details]

### 4.1 The Hippodrome (as built)

- U-shaped track; the carceres (starting gates) at the north end with four gilded copper horses on top (now the Horses of Saint Mark); the sphendone, the curved tribune, at the south; the kathisma, the emperor's box, on the east side, reached from the Great Palace by a private passage; stands for about 100,000 [stated: cavacopedia mirror of Wikipedia "Hippodrome of Constantinople"]. As built: the stadium element with the curve south and twelve arched stalls with engaged Ionic columns, a terrace with the four horses on a pedestal, a tall block behind with windows, pilasters and a cornice [inferred: twelve stalls after the Circus Maximus; the block's form]. The outer façade as the Stadium of Domitian's (rome.md §4.1) [inferred: a Roman circus of the 4th century].
- **Obelisk of Theodosius** (Thutmose III, Karnak; set up in 390): the surviving granite shaft about 18.5–19.6 m; one central column of hieroglyphs on each face; the lower marble pedestal about 3.77 × 3.87 × 1.40 m with red granite corner blocks, reliefs on two faces and inscriptions on the other two; a setback about 0.55 m; the upper block about 2.50 × 2.25 × 2.36 m with reliefs on all four sides (the emperor and court in the kathisma, races, the raising of the obelisk); four bronze cubes about 0.49 m between shaft and pedestal; a two-stepped limestone foundation about 7.05 × 7.10 m [stated: ancient-history-sites.com]. Built to these; the reliefs as registers of standing figures in framed panels [inferred: the composition].
- **Serpent Column**: bronze, three intertwined serpents whose heads carried the gold tripod of Plataea; about 8 m; brought from Delphi by Constantine in 324 [stated: Wikipedia mirror "Serpent Column"]; 29 coils [inferred: the count usually given, not in the source read]. Built 6.6 m of coils on a 1.3 m pedestal, the three heads spreading, no bowl [inferred: the bowl gone by the 6th century].
- **Statues of Porphyrius**: seven statues of the charioteer stood in the Hippodrome; two bases survive [stated]; three built on the spina, bronze on inscribed bases [inferred: placement].
- **Spina and metae**: a marble barrier with basins; three gilded cones on a half-round base at each end [inferred: the Roman circus type].
- **Kathisma**: a loggia of six porphyry columns over the track under a lead roof, with a parapet, a purple hanging and the throne [inferred: form].

### 4.2 Hagia Eirene (as built, `domed_basilica`)

- Rebuilt by Justinian after 532; probably a domed basilica: a dome on pendentives over an elongated nave, narthex west, apse east, aisles with columns, vaulted galleries on three sides; about 57 × 32 m without the atrium, dome about 16 m; thin red bricks in mortar, probably marble revetment inside; the atrium burned in 564; after the earthquake of 740 a second dome was added over the west bay [stated: thebyzantinelegacy.com "Hagia Eirene"]. Built as of 562: one dome on a drum of twenty windows over the east bay, the west bay under a barrel vault with a lunette window, two rows of arched windows in the outer walls, lead roofs, the apse with three windows and a half-dome, three west doors, the atrium's porticoes [inferred: the drum, the vault and the windows, from Hagia Sophia and the later building]. Exterior only; the interior is closed.

### 4.3 The Augustaion, the Milion and the honorific columns

- **Milion**: in the west of the Augustaion; a dome on four arches, perhaps like the Arch of Janus in Rome; statues of Constantine and Helena with a cross on top; Justinian added a sundial [stated: livius.org "Augusteôn Square"]. Built 11 m square, arches 5.2 m, a dome on a drum, the two gilded statues and the cross, the sundial on a pillar [inferred: dimensions].
- **Column of Justinian** (543): on the west side of the Augustaion; a marble base of seven steps; a column of masonry covered with brass plates and garlands; Justinian on a colossal bronze horse facing east, in the dress of Achilles with a plumed helmet, the orb with the cross in his left hand, his right raised to the east; about 70 m high by Buondelmonti's estimate [stated: wikiwand "Column of Justinian"; livius.org]. Built about 49 m: seven steps, a pedestal, a bronze shaft of 34 m with gilded garland bands, the gilded rider [inferred: height reduced; the figure from the shared figure builder].
- **Column of Constantine** (330): porphyry drums (seven to eleven) with bronze wreaths over the joints, a white marble pedestal; the bronze emperor with a spear and a seven-rayed crown, probably holding an orb; the column 37–40 m, the whole nearly 50 m [stated: Wikipedia mirror "Column of Constantine"]. Built: eight porphyry drums, 30 m, bronze wreaths, a marble capital, a 6 m statue with the radiate crown, spear and orb [inferred: the number of drums].

### 4.4 The Augustaion: the Senate house and the Chalke (as built)

- **The square.** Originally the Tetrastoon, "the place of the four porticos"; Hagia Sophia on its north-east, the Senate on its south-east, the Chalke at its south corner, the Baths of Zeuxippus on its south-west, the Milion on its west [stated: livius.org "Augusteôn Square"]. As built: porticoes on the west and south sides (Corinthian marble columns 7 m at 3.9 m, back walls, lean-to tiled roofs), open at the corners, toward Hagia Sophia and before the Senate and the Chalke, so the square is walkable from the Mese [inferred: the old enclosed porticus was a closed box; the openings].
- **The Senate house** stood on the eastern side of the square; six white columns, "the largest columns in the whole world" by Procopius' estimate, two of them framing the wall of the Senate house facing west and four standing a little beyond; they form a portico "covered by a circular dome-shaped roof", the upper parts finished in marble and "wonderfully ornamented with a number of statues standing on the roof" [stated: Procopius, *Buildings* I.10, Gutenberg text]. As built: a brick hall 24 × 26 m, 18 m to the eaves, bands of marble, a bronze door and two rows of windows, a tiled gable roof; the portico 12 m deep: four Corinthian columns of 13 m in front and two against the wall, an entablature round three sides, a domical (sail) vault over the rectangle in marble with a lead roof and marble tympana, six marble statues on the cornice [inferred: the hall's size and roof, the column height, the sail vault as the reading of "dome-shaped" over a rectangle, the statues' number].
- **The Chalke** lay on the south-eastern corner of the Augustaion; Justinian's was a rectangular building with four engaged piers carrying a central dome on pendentives over four barrel arches; the piers to the north and south lower; two smaller chambers north and south, each vaulted; the name perhaps from the gilded bronze tiles of its roof or from its bronze doors; the walls faced with coloured marble, the ceilings in mosaic: Justinian and Theodora flanked by the Senate, Belisarius' victories in the Vandal and Gothic wars and his return [stated: Wikipedia "Chalke Gate"]. Procopius: four lofty walls, eight arches, four carrying the spherical roof and the others the vaults at the sides; mosaics of the wars and the cities taken in Italy and Libya, Belisarius returning with the army and the spoils, and in the middle the emperor and Theodora rejoicing over the captive Vandal and Gothic kings, the Senate round them; the interior, walls and floor, encrusted with marble [stated: Procopius, *Buildings* I.10]. The statues in niches over the door and the icon of Christ are of about 600 or later [stated: same Wikipedia article] and are left out. As built: the domed bay 12 m square, walls 1.6 m, chambers 5.8 m deep, the arches springing at 7.6 m, pendentives and a hemispherical dome of 6 m radius in gold mosaic, barrel vaults over the chambers, a roof and dome of gilded bronze in 24 ribbed bands with a cross; opus sectile floor, revetment up to the lunettes; in the east lunette the emperor and empress (nimbed, purple) with the two kings kneeling, senators in white either side; in the west lunette Belisarius among his army; the west bronze doors open, the east door into the palace shut [inferred: dimensions; the arrangement of the mosaics in the lunettes; the figures as flattened figures].

### 4.5 The Mese's porticoes and shops (as built)

- The Mese was 25 m wide and "lined with colonnaded porticoes which housed shops" [stated: Wikipedia "Mese (Constantinople)"]. As built (the street compressed to 12 m): porticoes 5 m deep on Corinthian columns of 6 m at 3.8 m with Proconnesian and some pavonazzetto shafts, an architrave of two fasciae, frieze, dentilled cornice, a beamed and painted timber ceiling, a lean-to tiled roof; behind, brick shops 5 m deep with a wide door under a timber lintel and a window to the room upstairs; on the first leg a shallow portico against the Baths of Zeuxippus, on the second a portico backed by a wall along the Hippodrome [inferred: the section, the shop form; the porticoes' storeys are not stated].

### 4.6 The Forum of Constantine (as built)

- Circular, with two monumental gates to the east and west; the Column of Constantine in the centre; the first Senate house on its north side [stated: Wikipedia "Forum of Constantine"]. Zosimus: circular, "enclosed by a two-story colonnade", "two facing arches of Prokonnesian marble"; Konstantinos of Rhodes: its columns were white; the diameter is not known (perhaps 140 m); on the western arch two bronze female statues; a Gorgon keystone in the Istanbul museum possibly from one of the arches; marble pavement of Proconnesian slabs found in 1929–30 [stated: brewminate.com, after Kaldellis]. As built (compressed): a ring of white columns at 36 m from the centre in two storeys (Corinthian below, 7 m; smaller basket-capital columns above, 5 m), entablatures, a gallery floor, a tiled roof sloping in from a brick back wall with doors every third bay; the arches where the Mese enters and leaves, 11 m openings between pilastered piers, archivolts with a Gorgon keystone, cornice and attic with an inscription panel; two bronze women on the western arch [inferred: dimensions, the upper order, the arches' detail]. The Senate house of the forum is not built.


### 4.7 Hagia Sophia's ambo of 562 (L3 pass)

- Paul the Silentiary's *Description of the Ambo*: "as an island rises amidst the swelling billows ... so in the middle space of the boundless temple rises upright the tower-like ambo of stone, with its marble pastures like meadows"; it stands in a slightly raised enclosure bounded by eight large columns ("flowers of stone") carrying an ornate entablature, a wall about as high as a person between the columns; the platform raised on eight columns with a domed space beneath for a chorus; ornamented in gold, silver, bronze, ivory and exotic marbles [stated: Paul via New Liturgical Movement / Marcantonio]. "A circular or oval platform on eight posts, encircled by eight columns separated by parapet slabs and topped by an architrave"; the solea projected from the sanctuary to the ambo; the chancel screen had twelve columns, its architrave with images of Christ, its parapet panels with the monograms of Justinian and Theodora [stated: fhw.gr, Justinian's Constantinople]. **Built** (replacing the plain oval of the shared Byzantine style, in the church's own style): the oval platform (6 × 4.2 m, its floor 2.4 m up) with a moulded edge and, under it, a shallow vault of gold mosaic over the singers' space; its eight posts of verde antico with marble bases and silver capitals; the parapet of pavonazzetto with gilt bands, a silver coping and a silver cross on each panel, open at the stair heads; the two flights from east and west walked up between stepped parapets of green marble with silver copings; round it the enclosure of eight verde antico columns on an oval, the passages east and west between their pairs, Proconnesian parapet slabs 1.75 m high between the others with silver frames and a silver ring on each face, the oval architrave of marble faced with silver on the capitals [inferred: sizes, which marbles where, the panels' ornament].

### 4.8 The forecourt, the arrival and the deepening pass (as built)

- **The forecourt.** Between Hagia Sophia's south front (the south door at x = −10) and the Augustaion proper lies a paved court 96 × 66 m (`FORECOURT` in layout.js), where the visitor now arrives, 50 m from the front [inferred: the Augustaion's north side lay against the church; the layout's old plan left it bare]. On its west and east sides a row of shops under a Corinthian portico like the Mese's (nine and ten bays, enterable and furnished through the same shopfit builder) [inferred: the Mese's section applied to the court; the Augustaion's porticoes are stated in §4.4]. West of the western shops a garden court of plane trees and cypresses against the atrium's south wall [inferred].
- **The people of a great church's door.** Sellers of candles, bread, fruit, cloth and pottery at stalls and on mats; sitting beggars along the wall either side of the door; pilgrims waiting; water-carriers at a cistern head with a windlass; porters with donkeys; clergy in black; two guards of the church at the door; groups in conversation, a mother with her children [inferred: the usual life of a pilgrimage church in a city; no source read for the 6th-century court]. The processional line from the Chalke to the door (§10) crosses the court and is kept clear, as is the way to the door.
- **Statues and phialai.** Honorific statues on marble bases in the Augustaion and the forecourt (Procopius and the Patria speak of an Augustaion full of statues of emperors and empresses; the figures are the shared bronze figure on a base) and two canopied basins [inferred: number and places].
- **Hagia Sophia from outside.** The lead roof of the dome now shows its forty raised ribs on the line of the piers between the windows, a gilded cross over the crown, and radiating seams on the semidomes and exedrae [inferred: the ribbed form of the roof as seen in the surviving building and from the interior; the seam lines are ours].
- **Hagia Eirene inside.** The central west door stands open; verde antico arcades in two orders with a gallery floor and parapet over each aisle, four brick piers under the dome bay, a plastered barrel vault over the west bay, a marble chancel screen with a gold cross, the altar under a silver ciborium, three tiers of the synthronon with the bishop's seat in the apse, hanging lamps, a priest and worshippers [inferred: the furnishings of a Justinianic church after Hagia Sophia (§4.7) and the sources of §4.2; the galleries' stair is not built, so they cannot be climbed].
- **The Hippodrome's crowd.** The four circus factions in their colours (the Blues and the Greens the great ones, the Reds and the Whites the lesser) standing on the tiers, Blues and Greens facing each other across the track and Reds and Whites toward the carceres; at the starting gates the teams with grooms and charioteers in faction tunics [stated: the four factions and their colours; inferred: their places in the stands, the numbers].
- **The lanes at eye level.** Before the ground floors of the houses of the grid, within 210 m of the middle: amphorae and water pots, stone benches with someone sitting on them, firewood, barrels, sacks and baskets, cats, a woman at her door, men talking (lanelife.js) [inferred].

### 4.9 Hagia Sophia's doors and floor (L3 pass 2)

- **The portals.** The narthex doors of the church are covered in bronze (brass) with 6th-century Byzantine renderings of classical forms such as acanthus; gilt incised ornament imitating metal straps; the marble frames have a lintel carrying a Greek inscription (John 10:7–9) over the Hetoimasia; the central door of the second narthex, the Imperial (Silver, Beautiful) Gate, had a core said to be the wood of Noah's Ark, sheathed in silver-gilt; the mosaic of Leo the Wise over it is of 906–907 and is not built [stated: pallasweb.com "Collection of doors in Hagia Sophia", https://pallasweb.com/deesis/collection-of-doors-in-hagia-sophia.html; the page is not consistent on which parts are 6th-century]. The vaults of Justinian's church were plain gold mosaic with crosses and no figures [stated: `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md` §4, after pallasweb]. **Built** (`portal`, src/buildings/hagia-sophia/style.js): round every door of the nave's west wall (the Imperial Door and the eight side doors, on both faces) and of the inner and outer narthexes' west walls (five each, both faces) a stone frame: two marble jambs, an architrave, a projecting cornice slab, and over it a lunette: a marble arch band round a half-disc of gold mosaic with a gold cross [inferred: proportions (jambs about a sixth of the opening's width, cornice 0.28 m), the cross in the lunette; the 6th-century mosaic over each door is not known]. The doors themselves (bronze leaves open inward against the reveals) are the building's earlier object.
- **The floor.** "A mass of Proconnesian marble, a pale stone that reads as a frozen sea", its only specific pattern four green stripes of Thessalian marble, read as the four rivers of paradise (Pishon, Gihon, Tigris, Euphrates) [stated: byzantineambassador.com "Beauty beneath the feet", https://www.byzantineambassador.com/post/beauty-beneath-the-feet-cosmogonic-paving; the page does not say where the stripes run or when they were laid]. **Built** (`pavement`): the nave's floor, the inner narthex's and the atrium's laid with joints between slabs 2.0–2.4 m apart (grey marble lines a few millimetres proud), and four green verde antico bands 0.8 m wide with a pale edge running east-west across the nave from the west wall to the chancel at z = −12, −6, +6, +12 [inferred: the slab size, the joints, and the bands' number (the four rivers), width, direction and places].

### 4.10 The houses of the outer lanes (L3 pass 2)

- Houses of the Byzantine city: wood for beds, chairs, tables and bookshelves [stated: Wikipedia "Byzantine architecture"]; a ground floor for storage and workshops (ergasterion) with the living rooms and the reception room (triclinium) upstairs; courtyard houses limiting direct contact with the street, the entrance leading into a controlled inner space; rooms with several uses [stated: ancient-history-sites.com "Byzantine Housing and Living Conditions", https://www.ancient-history-sites.com/?p=132578, mostly of later centuries and other cities (Mystras, Pergamon)]. Workshops beside living space; shops and the Mese's tabernae [stated: dossier §4.5, §11].
- **Built, enterable houses** (`cn_home`, src/styles/constantinople/homes.js, a family grown from the single house of `styles/byzantine/interiors.js`): sixteen more working and family houses beyond 120 m of the arrival point (layout.js SPECIAL w01–w16, plus the earlier six): the door and dining room, store and stair as before, the west half of the ground floor furnished by trade: **potter** (a beehive kiln with its stoke-hole and flame, a kick-wheel on its stool, shelves of drying and fired ware, sacks of clay, a water jar), **smith** (a brick forge with hood and chimney and a glowing bed, bellows, an anvil on its stump, a quenching trough, tools hung on the wall, charcoal in sacks, stacked iron bars), cook-shop, bakery, weaver as before, and the family's home; with household members inside (interiors.js). [inferred: the trades' furniture after the general picture of the Late Roman craft house; no 6th-century Constantinopolitan excavated workshop was read this pass.]
- **Fronts by rule** (not by add-ons): every house is drawn from its seed through the same rules: the wall's skin (brick, plaster or stucco), the door's place along the front and its frame (arch of voussoirs on a stone surround; lintel on jambs under a cornice slab; pilasters with a cornice), the kind of **maenianum** (open timber gallery on posts under a lean-to; closed timber box with shuttered windows and its ends walled to the roof; corbelled balcony with an iron rail), courses of travertine in brick fronts, a plinth, sills and lintels at the windows, the roof's pitch [inferred: after the maenianum of Zeno's law and the general picture, dossier §11]. For the tenements (`cn_house`, src/styles/constantinople/fronts.js) the **ground floor of the long face** is by rule one of three: a row of **tabernae** (in the busy streets, within about 200 m of the middle, seven in ten), a **dwelling front** (one shut door in a stone frame, small barred windows high in a plain wall, a stone bench against it, in the outer lanes), or on the north face of a row an **arcade**: stone piers on bases with a marble capital under a timber architrave and joists, a limestone terrace with an iron rail above, the tabernae behind it, the maenianum left out [inferred: the arcaded fronts after the Mese's porticoes (§4.5) and the Roman city's porticoed streets; dwellings after the courtyard house's limited contact with the street, above].

### 4.11 Carving and figured surfaces, seen from two metres (round 4)

- **Date.** The brief for this round named the apse Virgin and Child, the archangels, the Imperial Door's lunette of Christ enthroned and the gallery panels; all are of the 9th century or later (the apse's of 867), and the dossier's stated choice is that Justinian's vaults were plain gold with crosses and no figures (§4.9). **Not built**, following the dossier. The mosaics of 562 that the sources do give are the Chalke's.
- **Capitals** (`src/buildings/hagia-sophia/carve.js`): the basket capitals of the nave, galleries, exedrae and atrium are rebuilt as a gilt core with acanthus standing off it: eight leaves in the lower tier (the great columns also four leaves at the faces' middles), each a broad central lobe with serrated side leaflets that are separate pieces with air between them (the "drilled lace" of the church's capitals: pallasweb, liturgicalartsjournal), curled tips, and four corner volutes under the abacus; three levels of richness by column size [stated: pierced, drilled acanthus capitals bearing the monograms; inferred: leaf count, curl, volutes]. The impost blocks carry the cruciform monograms of Justinian (I, Y, N, T) and Theodora (Θ, E, Δ, A) as bevelled gilt relief 2.8 cm proud of a green plaque, alternating by face; the lesser columns carry the cross with serifs only [inferred: the form, the letters' arrangement].
- **Spandrels and cornices:** the inlaid discs of the arcades' spandrels are now opus sectile: a porphyry disc, a ring of twelve tiles of pale and green marble, a gilt fillet and a band of sixteen porphyry and green tiles, each tile a separate bevelled piece with its mortar line [stated: opus sectile in the spandrels; inferred: the pattern]; the nave's cornices carry an egg-and-dart cut on the cyma [inferred].
- **Bronze doors** (`doors.js`): leaves with a grid of panels in raised moulded frames, gilt bosses and straps across the joints, the Imperial Door's upper panels with the monograms; the south door from the Augustaion, which had no leaves, now has them open into the aisle [stated: bronze doors with gilt strap ornament, pallasweb; inferred: the grid and monograms].
- **Chancel screen and ambo:** the roundels on the screen's architrave hold busts in relief (Christ with the cruciform nimbus over the middle door; the Virgin, apostles and prophets either side), after Paul the Silentiary and fhw.gr (architrave with images; §4.7) [stated: images of Christ on the architrave; inferred: which figures where]; the ambo's parapet panels carry the monograms in gilt relief [stated: parapet panels with the monograms: fhw.gr].
- **The Column of Justinian's rider** (`src/settlements/constantinople/rider.js`): the bridled horse of the Parthenon kit with the emperor in a short tunic, plumed helmet, right arm raised east, the orb with the cross in his left, in gilt bronze; within 110 m of the column, the old form beyond [stated: §4.3; inferred: pose].
- **The Chalke's east lunette** (`chalke.js`): the emperor and Theodora in purple with gold nimbi, the two kings bowed, six senators in white, as modelled figures in low relief with flesh-toned heads laid on the gold [stated: Procopius, Buildings I.10; inferred: poses]. Still not done: the tesserae themselves (the figures are smooth colour fields), the west lunette, the Milion's statues.

## 10. Life (as built)

- Dress: ankle-length clothes for women and men of rank, working men in shorter tunics; hose worn; the chlamys fastened on the right shoulder; purple reserved to the imperial family; women's hair covered by head-cloths and veils in public; clergy bearded, in black [stated: Wikipedia "Byzantine dress" — not specific to the 6th century]. As built: goods before about half the Mese's shops (amphorae, baskets, sacks, crates, cloth and produce on stalls) with their keepers; people along the Mese, donkeys with drivers, an ox-cart with sacks, a dog; officials in white before the Senate house, clergy on the Augustaion, two guards in red at the Chalke; people in the Forum of Constantine [inferred].
- Not shown on purpose: the eighty bronze statues of the Baths of Zeuxippus described by Christodorus — the baths burned in the Nika riot of 532 and the statues with them [inferred: from the baths' history; to be checked].


- **A procession (L3 pass):** the Diegesis (a later, legendary account) tells of Justinian coming "in procession from the Palace to the gates of the Augusteion", mounted on a chariot with four horses, then entering on foot "with the cross", the Patriarch Eutychius with him; the church was dedicated again in 562 after the dome's repair [stated: *Tomb and Temple* (Cambridge), the chapter "I have defeated you, Solomon"]. **Built:** a procession on its way from the Chalke to the south door of Hagia Sophia, its head 40 m from the door: the processional cross of gold (a disc of carnelian at the crossing) on its gilt staff between two tall silver candlesticks burning; four deacons in white with red stoles; four priests in white with ochre capes; the patriarch carrying the Gospel book bound in gold and set with stones, a candle on either side of him; the emperor in purple with a gold-bordered cloak, two attendants; eight guards in white tunics, hose and red cloaks with their spears beside and behind; people stopped to watch along the way, some praying. The square's other figures keep clear of its line. The Chalke's guards now hold spears (they held a porter's pole by mistake) [inferred: the order, dress and numbers; the cross and candles after Byzantine processional crosses and candlesticks].

## 11. Houses and street fittings (as built, batch pass)

- Zeno's law on private buildings (c. 475–491): a building that blocks the view of the sea may not be put up within 100 feet of it; the fine is ten pounds of gold [stated: Scott's *Corpus Juris Civilis*, Cod. Just. 8.10.12, droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Anglica/N63_Scott.htm]. Houses in Constantinople stood close, sometimes sharing walls, and neighbours disputed boundaries and light [stated: summary of "The Byzantine House 400–912" on Scribd, only the abstract could be read]. Balconies (maeniana) as a common feature of the city's streets [inferred: from the law's concern with them, not re-read in the sources I could open this pass].
- Byzantine walls: bricks and stone in courses, "cloisonné" masonry of a regular mix of stone and brick, often with more brick; brick and plaster used with stone to decorate important buildings [stated: Wikipedia "Byzantine architecture"].
- **Built** (`byz_house`, src/styles/byzantine/houses.js, replacing the Roman `insula` of the first pass): tenements of two or three storeys (7–10 m), the wall brick or limewashed, courses of travertine every metre or so on most; a limestone plinth; on the long face a row of tabernae (doors 2.8 m under timber lintels with limestone jambs, some shut with boards, some with a counter in the door and a striped awning), the stair door in the middle under an arch; a little mezzanine window over each door; upstairs windows with semicircular heads in stone surrounds, a third of them paired on a colonette; limestone string course at each floor, brick and limestone cornice; the **maenianum** on the long face of about two houses in three, in one of three kinds: an open timber gallery on limestone brackets with posts, a balustrade and a lean-to of tiles; a closed timber box with shuttered windows and a pent roof; or a corbelled balcony with an iron rail; tiled hip roofs on rafter ends, or (about one in four) a flat roof with a parapet and a **heliakon** (sun-room) of four posts and a lean-to; one house in five has a walled yard behind with a gate, a well-head and a pot [inferred: all proportions, the choice of kinds, the sun-room's form; the 3 kinds and the heliakon after the general picture of Byzantine private houses, not checked against an excavation].
- **Fountains** (`byz_fountain`): a niche fountain on a brick wall with a bronze lion-head spout over a marble basin on two steps; a phiale, a round basin under four columns and a low lead dome with a gilt finial [inferred: forms after the phiale of Hagia Sophia's atrium and the usual Roman street fountain; not placed in the town yet].

## 12. Interiors (as built, batch pass)

- Houses used couches and a round table (the sigma table of the dining room), chests, braziers and lamps on stands, icons, curtains [inferred: general Late Roman and Byzantine furnishing; Wikipedia "Byzantine architecture" says only that wood was used for furniture such as beds, chairs, tables and bookshelves, stated]. **Built**: an enterable house (`byz_home`, on the lot nearest (−140, 70)): the door in the south wall, a dining room with a sigma table, three couches, a brazier, a lamp, an icon and a hanging; a store with pithoi, a shelf of amphorae and a brick stove; a timber stair rising to the upper floor with a bed, chests, a loom and a table; the maenianum gallery over the street, walkable; beamed ceilings, hip roof.
- **Chapel** (`byz_chapel`, lot nearest (−135, 20)): a single-nave church with aisles on verde antico columns, 19 × 17 m with its apse east, narthex west with three doors, floor of opus sectile, revetted walls to 3 m, painted timber ceiling, a templon of six columns with silver capitals, parapets, a central door, curtains and icons, the altar under a ciborium, a bishop's seat, an ambo, a polykandelon, a half-dome of gold mosaic with a cross [inferred: form after the early Byzantine basilica and the dossier's Hagia Sophia furnishings; the templon of the 6th century was low with columns and architrave, stated in general by Wikipedia "Templon", which could not be fetched this pass].
- **Bath** (`byz_bath`, lot nearest (−150, −30)): a small public bath: dressing hall with benches, niches and a fountain, a warm room with marble benches, a hot room with a plunge pool, a labrum and the furnace's glow, a dome over the hot room, steam from the furnace flue [inferred: after the usual sequence of the Roman bath].
- **Shops** (`byz_shopfit`): about three of four shops behind the Mese's porticoes furnished by trade (grocer, cloth, potter, silversmith, cook-shop, icon painter): shelves with goods, a counter with steelyard and lamp, jars, a stool, a keeper [inferred: trades after the Mese's character as a street of shops and the Book of the Eparch's guilds, not read this pass].

## 14. Deepening pass: lanes, gardens, working houses

- **The grid.** North-south lanes every 41 m and east-west lanes every 35 m (5 m wide), the Mese's own corridor taking one slot at x = -180 and the lanes at z = 40 and z = 110. Between two east-west lanes stand two rows of houses back to back, 15 m deep, the houses of a row sharing their side walls so that each lane is a continuous front on both sides [inferred: the dense, party-walled tenement street, after "Houses in Constantinople stood close, sometimes sharing walls" (§11, stated)]. About 800 tenements replace the 400 spaced ones; the outer ring is thinned with walled gardens and market gardens, which the city's inner walls enclosed [inferred; the Theodosian walls enclosed fields and gardens, general knowledge, not checked this pass].
- **Walled gardens** (`byz_walled_yard`, src/styles/byzantine/lanes.js): a whole block behind a brick wall with a gate: a well-head, a vine pergola with a bench, beds, a store; planted with figs, olives, apples and cypresses; some with a niche fountain on the lane wall [inferred].
- **Lane kit** (lanes.js): house-bridges (a passage on a timber lintel across the lane between two tenements, with a window), cloth awnings strung from eave to eave with a hung lamp, wall shrines (a pier with an icon in a niche and a lamp). Zeno's law on balconies and the closeness of houses (§11) are the grounds for the narrow, built-over lanes [inferred].
- **Fronts.** `byz_house` now takes `front` (the face to the lane, so the shared street dressing puts doors' goods, benches and washing on the right side), `abut` (party walls drawn plain, no overhang toward the neighbour) and `storeys`; the tabernae have goods on their counters, oil lamps on brackets and painted boards; weathering at eye level: rising damp on plastered walls, rain streaks under sills, patches where the plaster has fallen away and the brick shows.
- **Working houses you can enter** (`byz_home` variants): a cook-shop (popina) with tables, benches, a counter with jars, wine pithoi and a hearth; a bakery with a dome oven, a kneading trough, bread racks and sacks; a weaver's with two warp-weighted looms, bolts of cloth, dye jars and a vat; three more homes; all with the stair and the upper floor and the gallery of the house [inferred: trades after the Book of the Eparch's guilds, not read this pass; the loom type is general Mediterranean knowledge].
- **Life.** Walkers (56 by day) also use the lanes; pigeons and sparrows perch at the lane ends; the Great Palace's gaps between courts are grass with cypresses, planes and olives.
- Not done: the sea and the harbours (need the site registered with water, a rim and a sea level; the coordinator's files), Hagia Eirene's, the Senate house's and the Chalke's interiors, the Basilica Cistern.

## 15. Upstairs in the houses you can enter (round 6)

- **The plan above** (`src/styles/constantinople/upper.js`, from `cn_home`): a ground floor of storage and workshop (ergasterion) with the family's rooms and the reception room (triclinium) upstairs, the pattern of the Late Roman and Byzantine house [stated: ancient-history-sites.com "Byzantine Housing and Living Conditions", §4.10]. As built: a straight timber stair of one flight rises along the east party wall from the store to a landing in the hall of the floor above (its hole railed on the open side); round the hall, cut off by light plastered partitions with timber-framed doors and a curtain each, the reception room on the street with two door-windows onto the maenianum (walkable; the kind of maenianum is the house's own, §4.10) and its two windows with reveals and folded shutters, a second room beside it, and two chambers behind [inferred: the plan, the partitions, the stair's place; rooms of several uses, wood for beds, chairs, tables and bookshelves are stated, "Byzantine architecture" (Wikipedia)].
- **By kind** (17 of the 22 houses; the five on the sloping east and south, whose fronts lie in the ground, keep their single room upstairs): the **merchant's house** (home, w03, w07, w13, w15) has the triclinium (a sigma table with three couches, brazier, lamp, icon), a counting room (writing table with scrolls and a balance, a shelf of amphorae, chests), the strongroom (chests, pithos, sacks, shelf of jars), the bedroom, and the hall with the household's loom and table; the **tenement** (home2) is let in rooms, a family in each of the street rooms with a bed, a table and a hearth or brazier, lodgers in the chambers, a washing place in the hall; the **inn** (tavern, w05) has guest rooms of two beds each, a common room with two long tables and benches, a kitchen with a brick stove, and benches in the hall for waiting guests; the **shop with dwelling** (bakery, w04, w12) has flour in sacks and bread racks over the baker's store and workroom; the **craftsman's** house (weaver, w06, w14; potter, w01; smith, w02) has the family's dayroom and bedroom and the workroom of the trade (looms by the light of the windows, drying shelves of ware, iron and charcoal stored) [inferred: the use of each room after the general picture of the Late Roman house and the working houses of §4.10; no excavated Constantinopolitan house was read].
- **The roof** of the merchant's house and the tenement is flat, as one tenement in four is (§11): a second timber stair from the hall through a hole in the deck, a parapet a metre high with a coping and scuppers, a sun-room (heliakon) of four posts and a lean-to of tiles with a couch and a table, a washing line with cloth, jars [inferred: after §11, the heliakon of the Byzantine house]. The other kinds keep their tiled hip roofs.
- **Walked**: walk-check routes in the settlement plan (`houseWalks`, interiors.js) take the walker from the lane through the door, the store, up the stair, across the hall to the bedroom and the reception room and out on the maenianum, and, in the flat-roofed houses, up the second stair on to the roof.

## 16. Reference-standard pass: walk, night, sound, animals (round 7)

Marks: [stated] with its source, [inferred] where the place and date suggest it. Only what Constantinople about 562 had.

### 16.1 The visitor's walk
- [inferred] The walk runs from the arrival on the forecourt to the south door of Hagia Sophia, west under the forecourt's shop porticoes to the Milion, along the Mese's porticoes round the Hippodrome's end, and south across the Augustaion to the Chalke (`walk.js`, WALK). Walk-check routes enter ten shops (`walk.js`, OPEN) through the portico and the door (2.2 m) into the front room, keeping to the side of the door away from the counter.
- [stated] Shops in the porticoes of the Mese and the colonnaded squares, with workshops of the silversmiths, cloth sellers and bakers, are in §4.5 and Procopius' Buildings; the Mese as a colonnaded, shop-lined street is in the sources below.
- [inferred] Goods stand beside the doors, not in front: pots, baskets, sacks and a small stall at 1.5 to 2 m off a door's middle, the keeper by them. Sacks, barrels and amphorae stacked on the forecourt's porticoes stand on the piers between doors.
- Not built: more enterable houses along the walk, street shrines, and keepers in every walked shop. Seventeen further shop routes were written and fail in the walker's raster (the counter of the style's shop fitting leaves a lane under a hand's breadth beside the door); they are not kept.

### 16.2 Night
- [stated] Oil lamps of clay and bronze, hung from chains in churches and porticoes, and the polykandelon, a bronze ring of glass lamps hanging in the church (Paul the Silentiary, Descriptio S. Sophiae, on the silver lamps of the dome; lamps and candles in the dossier §3 and §4).
- [inferred] A clay lamp on an iron chain in every second intercolumniation of the Mese's porticoes; a hearth or oven glow in bakeries and cook-shops; lit upper windows in houses (the ground floor stays dark behind its shutters); the ring of 40 windows at the dome's foot lit from within. All as `halo` (additive, night only); the day view is unchanged. Code: `nightlight.js`.
- [inferred] Street lighting by the city (Constantinople had none by the state at this date): none is made. Torches and lanterns carried by walkers are not built.

### 16.3 Sound
- [stated] Chant (Kyrie, psalmody, acclamations of the emperor) and the church organ's absence: the organ was a court instrument in the Hippodrome and palace ceremony, not in the church (Byzantine liturgy was unaccompanied). The voice `cnOrgan` is therefore for the palace and is placed only there; marked [inferred] for its tone.
- [stated] Chariot racing in the Hippodrome, with the factions' acclamations, is in §4.1 and in Procopius' Wars (the Nika riot, 532). The racing hours and the pace are [inferred].
- [inferred] Hawkers' cries (candles, bread, water, fruit, cloth) by the shops and the forecourt; mules and carts on the Mese; windlass and well at the cistern head; fountain and bath; hammering and chasing in the silversmiths; bellows in smithies; doves; a household's supper. Wordless, synthesised (`voices-constantinople.js`, `soundmap.js`). Not heard by the author: measured only (about 4.5e-3 RMS per voice).
- Not built: the semantron (the wooden board struck to call to prayer, [stated] in monastic use) and bells at the church (bells were in use in Constantinople by this date only in a few places; left out).

### 16.4 Animals
- [inferred] Dogs and cats lying by the piers of the shop porticoes, a caged songbird at one door, doves on the paving of the forecourt and the Augustaion, fowl before the bakery and the weaver's, a dog at two house doors, crows, swallows and sparrows already in `fauna.js`. Code: the `critters` list of `fauna.js`.
- Not built: the swallows' nests under the porticoes, horses at the Hippodrome, fish in the Golden Horn's boats (the sea is not built).

### 16.5 Wear and writing; the landmark
- Not done: worn thresholds and steps, and Greek inscriptions (the shop signs, the lintel texts, the dedications of Hagia Sophia). No letters are written: none could be checked in this pass, so the boards stay plain.
- Not done, from the landmark's own gaps: the Pharos, the sea walls and the harbours.
