# Justinian's Constantinople (the first hill), about 562 — dossier

Settlement id: `constantinople` · Style: `byzantine` (Hagia Sophia) and `roman` (the city round it) · Region: the Bosphorus · Code: `src/settlements/constantinople/`
Replaces the legacy district of the previous design. Hagia Sophia itself is the full building from that design (`src/buildings/hagia-sophia`, dossier `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md`), with its atrium.
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (Hagia Sophia; the Augustaion with its porticoes and Justinian's column; Hagia Eirene; the Baths of Zeuxippus; the Hippodrome; the Great Palace's courts and halls on terraces; the Mese with its porticoes and the Forum of Constantine with its porphyry column; houses; ground, planting, weather, music). Built since: the Hippodrome's carceres, spina, Obelisk of Theodosius, Serpent Column, statues of Porphyrius, turning posts, kathisma and its sphendone curve at the south; Hagia Eirene as a domed basilica; the Milion; the columns of Constantine and Justinian with their statues; the Mese re-routed round the Hippodrome. Second pass (§4.4–§4.6, §10): the Senate house and the Chalke on an opened Augustaion, the Mese's porticoes with shops, the Forum of Constantine's two-storeyed ring and arches, street life. L3 pass (§4.7, §10): Hagia Sophia's ambo of 562 rebuilt after Paul the Silentiary, a procession from the Chalke to the church with the cross, candles, the Gospel and the emperor. L3 recorded for later: the harbours of Julian and Sophia, Hagia Eirene's interior, the sea walls and the Bosphorus itself (the sea is not yet shown: the ground falls toward it), the Theodosian land walls (5 km west, outside this settlement), the Basilica Cistern, the Walled Obelisk (its stone core is 10th century), the mosaics of Hagia Sophia's neighbours beyond the Chalke.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Hagia Sophia, built 532–537, its dome rebuilt in 562 [stated: `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md` and its sources].
- **Its neighbours on the first hill:**
  - the Augustaion square to the south, with the Column of Justinian [stated: Wikipedia "Augustaion"];
  - Hagia Eirene to the north, rebuilt by Justinian [stated: Wikipedia "Hagia Irene"];
  - the Baths of Zeuxippus between the Augustaion and the Hippodrome [stated: Wikipedia "Baths of Zeuxippus"];
  - the Hippodrome, about 450 m long, to the south-west [stated: Wikipedia "Hippodrome of Constantinople"];
  - the Great Palace stepping down on terraces toward the Sea of Marmara to the south-east [stated: Wikipedia "Great Palace of Constantinople"];
  - the Mese, the main street, leaving west to the oval Forum of Constantine with its porphyry column [stated: Wikipedia "Mese (Constantinople)"; "Forum of Constantine"].
- **Moment shown:** 562, the dome just rebuilt.

## 3. Orientation

Hagia Sophia faces west (the apse east). [stated]

## 4. The style

Brick and stone with marble; domed halls; colonnaded porticoes along the Mese and round the squares; tiers of seats in the Hippodrome; porphyry columns. [stated: materials; inferred: details]

### 4.1 The Hippodrome (as built)

- U-shaped track; the carceres (starting gates) at the north end with four gilded copper horses on top (now the Horses of Saint Mark); the sphendone, the curved tribune, at the south; the kathisma, the emperor's box, on the east side, reached from the Great Palace by a private passage; stands for about 100,000 [stated: cavacopedia mirror of Wikipedia "Hippodrome of Constantinople"]. As built: the stadium element with the curve south and twelve arched stalls with engaged Ionic columns, a terrace with the four horses on a pedestal, a tall block behind with windows, pilasters and a cornice [inferred: twelve stalls after the Circus Maximus; the block's form]. The outer façade as the Stadium of Domitian's (rome.md §4.1) [inferred: a Roman circus of the 4th century].
- **Obelisk of Theodosius** (Thutmose III, Karnak; set up in 390): the surviving granite shaft about 18.5–19.6 m; one central column of hieroglyphs on each face; the lower marble pedestal about 3.77 × 3.87 × 1.40 m with red granite corner blocks, reliefs on two faces and inscriptions on the other two; a setback about 0.55 m; the upper block about 2.50 × 2.25 × 2.36 m with reliefs on all four sides (the emperor and court in the kathisma, races, the raising of the obelisk); four bronze cubes about 0.49 m between shaft and pedestal; a two-stepped limestone foundation about 7.05 × 7.10 m [stated: ancient-history-sites.com]. Built to these; the reliefs as registers of standing figures in framed panels [inferred: the composition].
- **Serpent Column**: bronze, three intertwined serpents whose heads carried the gold tripod of Plataea; about 8 m; brought from Delphi by Constantine in 324 [stated: Wikipedia mirror "Serpent Column"]; 29 coils [inferred: the count usually given, not in the source read]. Built 6.6 m of coils on a 1.3 m pedestal, the three heads spreading, no bowl [inferred: the bowl gone by the 6th century].
- **Statues of Porphyrius**: seven statues of the charioteer stood in the Hippodrome; two bases survive [stated]; three built on the spina, bronze on inscribed bases [inferred: placement].
- **Spina and metae**: a marble barrier with basins; three gilded cones on a half-round base at each end [inferred: the Roman circus type].
- **Kathisma**: a loggia of six porphyry columns over the track under a lead roof, with a parapet, a purple hanging and the throne [inferred: form].

### 4.2 Hagia Eirene (as built, `domed_basilica`)

- Rebuilt by Justinian after 532; probably a domed basilica: a dome on pendentives over an elongated nave, narthex west, apse east, aisles with columns, vaulted galleries on three sides; about 57 × 32 m without the atrium, dome about 16 m; thin red bricks in mortar, probably marble revetment inside; the atrium burned in 564; after the earthquake of 740 a second dome was added over the west bay [stated: thebyzantinelegacy.com "Hagia Eirene"]. Built as of 562: one dome on a drum of twenty windows over the east bay, the west bay under a barrel vault with a lunette window, two rows of arched windows in the outer walls, lead roofs, the apse with three windows and a half-dome, three west doors, the atrium's porticoes [inferred: the drum, the vault and the windows, from Hagia Sophia and the later building]. Exterior only; the interior is closed.

### 4.3 The Augustaion, the Milion and the honorific columns

- **Milion**: in the west of the Augustaion; a dome on four arches, perhaps like the Arch of Janus in Rome; statues of Constantine and Helena with a cross on top; Justinian added a sundial [stated: livius.org "Augusteôn Square"]. Built 11 m square, arches 5.2 m, a dome on a drum, the two gilded statues and the cross, the sundial on a pillar [inferred: dimensions].
- **Column of Justinian** (543): on the west side of the Augustaion; a marble base of seven steps; a column of masonry covered with brass plates and garlands; Justinian on a colossal bronze horse facing east, in the dress of Achilles with a plumed helmet, the orb with the cross in his left hand, his right raised to the east; about 70 m high by Buondelmonti's estimate [stated: wikiwand "Column of Justinian"; livius.org]. Built about 49 m: seven steps, a pedestal, a bronze shaft of 34 m with gilded garland bands, the gilded rider [inferred: height reduced; the figure from the shared figure builder].
- **Column of Constantine** (330): porphyry drums (seven to eleven) with bronze wreaths over the joints, a white marble pedestal; the bronze emperor with a spear and a seven-rayed crown, probably holding an orb; the column 37–40 m, the whole nearly 50 m [stated: Wikipedia mirror "Column of Constantine"]. Built: eight porphyry drums, 30 m, bronze wreaths, a marble capital, a 6 m statue with the radiate crown, spear and orb [inferred: the number of drums].

### 4.4 The Augustaion: the Senate house and the Chalke (as built)

- **The square.** Originally the Tetrastoon, "the place of the four porticos"; Hagia Sophia on its north-east, the Senate on its south-east, the Chalke at its south corner, the Baths of Zeuxippus on its south-west, the Milion on its west [stated: livius.org "Augusteôn Square"]. As built: porticoes on the west and south sides (Corinthian marble columns 7 m at 3.9 m, back walls, lean-to tiled roofs), open at the corners, toward Hagia Sophia and before the Senate and the Chalke, so the square is walkable from the Mese [inferred: the old enclosed porticus was a closed box; the openings].
- **The Senate house** stood on the eastern side of the square; six white columns, "the largest columns in the whole world" by Procopius' estimate, two of them framing the wall of the Senate house facing west and four standing a little beyond; they form a portico "covered by a circular dome-shaped roof", the upper parts finished in marble and "wonderfully ornamented with a number of statues standing on the roof" [stated: Procopius, *Buildings* I.10, Gutenberg text]. As built: a brick hall 24 × 26 m, 18 m to the eaves, bands of marble, a bronze door and two rows of windows, a tiled gable roof; the portico 12 m deep: four Corinthian columns of 13 m in front and two against the wall, an entablature round three sides, a domical (sail) vault over the rectangle in marble with a lead roof and marble tympana, six marble statues on the cornice [inferred: the hall's size and roof, the column height, the sail vault as the reading of "dome-shaped" over a rectangle, the statues' number].
- **The Chalke** lay on the south-eastern corner of the Augustaion; Justinian's was a rectangular building with four engaged piers carrying a central dome on pendentives over four barrel arches; the piers to the north and south lower; two smaller chambers north and south, each vaulted; the name perhaps from the gilded bronze tiles of its roof or from its bronze doors; the walls faced with coloured marble, the ceilings in mosaic: Justinian and Theodora flanked by the Senate, Belisarius' victories in the Vandal and Gothic wars and his return [stated: Wikipedia "Chalke Gate"]. Procopius: four lofty walls, eight arches, four carrying the spherical roof and the others the vaults at the sides; mosaics of the wars and the cities taken in Italy and Libya, Belisarius returning with the army and the spoils, and in the middle the emperor and Theodora rejoicing over the captive Vandal and Gothic kings, the Senate round them; the interior, walls and floor, encrusted with marble [stated: Procopius, *Buildings* I.10]. The statues in niches over the door and the icon of Christ are of about 600 or later [stated: same Wikipedia article] and are left out. As built: the domed bay 12 m square, walls 1.6 m, chambers 5.8 m deep, the arches springing at 7.6 m, pendentives and a hemispherical dome of 6 m radius in gold mosaic, barrel vaults over the chambers, a roof and dome of gilded bronze in 24 ribbed bands with a cross; opus sectile floor, revetment up to the lunettes; in the east lunette the emperor and empress (nimbed, purple) with the two kings kneeling, senators in white either side; in the west lunette Belisarius among his army; the west bronze doors open, the east door into the palace shut [inferred: dimensions; the arrangement of the mosaics in the lunettes; the figures as flattened figures].

### 4.5 The Mese's porticoes and shops (as built)

- The Mese was 25 m wide and "lined with colonnaded porticoes which housed shops" [stated: Wikipedia "Mese (Constantinople)"]. As built (the street compressed to 12 m): porticoes 5 m deep on Corinthian columns of 6 m at 3.8 m with Proconnesian and some pavonazzetto shafts, an architrave of two fasciae, frieze, dentilled cornice, a beamed and painted timber ceiling, a lean-to tiled roof; behind, brick shops 5 m deep with a wide door under a timber lintel and a window to the room upstairs; on the first leg a shallow portico against the Baths of Zeuxippus, on the second a portico backed by a wall along the Hippodrome [inferred: the section, the shop form; the porticoes' storeys are not stated].

### 4.6 The Forum of Constantine (as built)

- Circular, with two monumental gates to the east and west; the Column of Constantine in the centre; the first Senate house on its north side [stated: Wikipedia "Forum of Constantine"]. Zosimus: circular, "enclosed by a two-story colonnade", "two facing arches of Prokonnesian marble"; Konstantinos of Rhodes: its columns were white; the diameter is not known (perhaps 140 m); on the western arch two bronze female statues; a Gorgon keystone in the Istanbul museum possibly from one of the arches; marble pavement of Proconnesian slabs found in 1929–30 [stated: brewminate.com, after Kaldellis]. As built (compressed): a ring of white columns at 36 m from the centre in two storeys (Corinthian below, 7 m; smaller basket-capital columns above, 5 m), entablatures, a gallery floor, a tiled roof sloping in from a brick back wall with doors every third bay; the arches where the Mese enters and leaves, 11 m openings between pilastered piers, archivolts with a Gorgon keystone, cornice and attic with an inscription panel; two bronze women on the western arch [inferred: dimensions, the upper order, the arches' detail]. The Senate house of the forum is not built.


### 4.7 Hagia Sophia's ambo of 562 (L3 pass)

- Paul the Silentiary's *Description of the Ambo*: "as an island rises amidst the swelling billows ... so in the middle space of the boundless temple rises upright the tower-like ambo of stone, with its marble pastures like meadows"; it stands in a slightly raised enclosure bounded by eight large columns ("flowers of stone") carrying an ornate entablature, a wall about as high as a person between the columns; the platform raised on eight columns with a domed space beneath for a chorus; ornamented in gold, silver, bronze, ivory and exotic marbles [stated: Paul via New Liturgical Movement / Marcantonio]. "A circular or oval platform on eight posts, encircled by eight columns separated by parapet slabs and topped by an architrave"; the solea projected from the sanctuary to the ambo; the chancel screen had twelve columns, its architrave with images of Christ, its parapet panels with the monograms of Justinian and Theodora [stated: fhw.gr, Justinian's Constantinople]. **Built** (replacing the plain oval of the shared Byzantine style, in the church's own style): the oval platform (6 × 4.2 m, its floor 2.4 m up) with a moulded edge and, under it, a shallow vault of gold mosaic over the singers' space; its eight posts of verde antico with marble bases and silver capitals; the parapet of pavonazzetto with gilt bands, a silver coping and a silver cross on each panel, open at the stair heads; the two flights from east and west walked up between stepped parapets of green marble with silver copings; round it the enclosure of eight verde antico columns on an oval, the passages east and west between their pairs, Proconnesian parapet slabs 1.75 m high between the others with silver frames and a silver ring on each face, the oval architrave of marble faced with silver on the capitals [inferred: sizes, which marbles where, the panels' ornament].

## 10. Life (as built)

- Dress: ankle-length clothes for women and men of rank, working men in shorter tunics; hose worn; the chlamys fastened on the right shoulder; purple reserved to the imperial family; women's hair covered by head-cloths and veils in public; clergy bearded, in black [stated: Wikipedia "Byzantine dress" — not specific to the 6th century]. As built: goods before about half the Mese's shops (amphorae, baskets, sacks, crates, cloth and produce on stalls) with their keepers; people along the Mese, donkeys with drivers, an ox-cart with sacks, a dog; officials in white before the Senate house, clergy on the Augustaion, two guards in red at the Chalke; people in the Forum of Constantine [inferred].
- Not shown on purpose: the eighty bronze statues of the Baths of Zeuxippus described by Christodorus — the baths burned in the Nika riot of 532 and the statues with them [inferred: from the baths' history; to be checked].


- **A procession (L3 pass):** the Diegesis (a later, legendary account) tells of Justinian coming "in procession from the Palace to the gates of the Augusteion", mounted on a chariot with four horses, then entering on foot "with the cross", the Patriarch Eutychius with him; the church was dedicated again in 562 after the dome's repair [stated: *Tomb and Temple* (Cambridge), the chapter "I have defeated you, Solomon"]. **Built:** a procession on its way from the Chalke to the south door of Hagia Sophia, its head 40 m from the door: the processional cross of gold (a disc of carnelian at the crossing) on its gilt staff between two tall silver candlesticks burning; four deacons in white with red stoles; four priests in white with ochre capes; the patriarch carrying the Gospel book bound in gold and set with stones, a candle on either side of him; the emperor in purple with a gold-bordered cloak, two attendants; eight guards in white tunics, hose and red cloaks with their spears beside and behind; people stopped to watch along the way, some praying. The square's other figures keep clear of its line. The Chalke's guards now hold spears (they held a porter's pole by mistake) [inferred: the order, dress and numbers; the cross and candles after Byzantine processional crosses and candlesticks].

## 5. The town

About 400 houses and apartment blocks, built as Roman-type insulae (rome.md §4.2), balconies on most (Zeno's building law for the city regulated balconies) [inferred], windows a little wider spaced. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The level hilltop falling toward the sea; cypresses and plane trees in gardens. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Bright and windy, with cloud and rain off the sea. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

Along the Mese from the Forum of Constantine to the Augustaion, and into Hagia Sophia through its atrium. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

As for Hagia Sophia (`docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md` §9).
