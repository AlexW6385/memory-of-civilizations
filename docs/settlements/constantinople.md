# Justinian's Constantinople (the first hill), about 562 — dossier

Settlement id: `constantinople` · Style: `byzantine` (Hagia Sophia) and `roman` (the city round it) · Region: the Bosphorus · Code: `src/settlements/constantinople/`
Replaces the legacy district of the previous design. Hagia Sophia itself is the full building from that design (`src/buildings/hagia-sophia`, dossier `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md`), with its atrium.
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (Hagia Sophia; the Augustaion with its porticoes and Justinian's column; Hagia Eirene; the Baths of Zeuxippus; the Hippodrome; the Great Palace's courts and halls on terraces; the Mese with its porticoes and the Forum of Constantine with its porphyry column; houses; ground, planting, weather, music). Built since: the Hippodrome's carceres, spina, Obelisk of Theodosius, Serpent Column, statues of Porphyrius, turning posts, kathisma and its sphendone curve at the south; Hagia Eirene as a domed basilica; the Milion; the columns of Constantine and Justinian with their statues; the Mese re-routed round the Hippodrome. L3 recorded for later: the sea walls and the Bosphorus itself (the sea is not yet shown: the ground falls toward it), the Theodosian land walls (5 km west, outside this settlement), the Chalke gate, the Basilica Cistern, the Walled Obelisk (its stone core is 10th century), mosaics, people.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Hagia Sophia, built 532–537, its dome rebuilt in 562 [stated: `docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md` and its sources].
- **Its neighbours on the first hill:**
  - the Augustaion square to the south, with the Column of Justinian [stated: Wikipedia "Augustaion"];
  - Hagia Eirene to the north, rebuilt by Justinian [stated: Wikipedia "Hagia Irene"];
  - the Baths of Zeuxippus between the Augustaion and the Hippodrome [stated: Wikipedia "Baths of Zeuxippus"];
  - the Hippodrome, about 450 m long, to the south-west [stated: Wikipedia "Hippodrome of Constantinople"];
  - the Great Palace stepping down on terraces toward the Sea of Marmara to the south-east [stated: Wikipedia "Great Palace of Constantinople"];
  - the Mese, the main street, leaving west to the oval Forum of Constantine with its porphyry column [stated: Wikipedia "Mese (Constantinople)"; "Forum of Constantine"].
- **Moment shown:** 562, the dome just rebuilt.

## 3. Orientation

Hagia Sophia faces west (the apse east). [stated]

## 4. The style

Brick and stone with marble; domed halls; colonnaded porticoes along the Mese and round the squares; tiers of seats in the Hippodrome; porphyry columns. [stated: materials; inferred: details]

### 4.1 The Hippodrome (as built)

- U-shaped track; the carceres (starting gates) at the north end with four gilded copper horses on top (now the Horses of Saint Mark); the sphendone, the curved tribune, at the south; the kathisma, the emperor's box, on the east side, reached from the Great Palace by a private passage; stands for about 100,000 [stated: cavacopedia mirror of Wikipedia "Hippodrome of Constantinople"]. As built: the stadium element with the curve south and twelve arched stalls with engaged Ionic columns, a terrace with the four horses on a pedestal, a tall block behind with windows, pilasters and a cornice [inferred: twelve stalls after the Circus Maximus; the block's form]. The outer façade as the Stadium of Domitian's (rome.md §4.1) [inferred: a Roman circus of the 4th century].
- **Obelisk of Theodosius** (Thutmose III, Karnak; set up in 390): the surviving granite shaft about 18.5–19.6 m; one central column of hieroglyphs on each face; the lower marble pedestal about 3.77 × 3.87 × 1.40 m with red granite corner blocks, reliefs on two faces and inscriptions on the other two; a setback about 0.55 m; the upper block about 2.50 × 2.25 × 2.36 m with reliefs on all four sides (the emperor and court in the kathisma, races, the raising of the obelisk); four bronze cubes about 0.49 m between shaft and pedestal; a two-stepped limestone foundation about 7.05 × 7.10 m [stated: ancient-history-sites.com]. Built to these; the reliefs as registers of standing figures in framed panels [inferred: the composition].
- **Serpent Column**: bronze, three intertwined serpents whose heads carried the gold tripod of Plataea; about 8 m; brought from Delphi by Constantine in 324 [stated: Wikipedia mirror "Serpent Column"]; 29 coils [inferred: the count usually given, not in the source read]. Built 6.6 m of coils on a 1.3 m pedestal, the three heads spreading, no bowl [inferred: the bowl gone by the 6th century].
- **Statues of Porphyrius**: seven statues of the charioteer stood in the Hippodrome; two bases survive [stated]; three built on the spina, bronze on inscribed bases [inferred: placement].
- **Spina and metae**: a marble barrier with basins; three gilded cones on a half-round base at each end [inferred: the Roman circus type].
- **Kathisma**: a loggia of six porphyry columns over the track under a lead roof, with a parapet, a purple hanging and the throne [inferred: form].

### 4.2 Hagia Eirene (as built, `domed_basilica`)

- Rebuilt by Justinian after 532; probably a domed basilica: a dome on pendentives over an elongated nave, narthex west, apse east, aisles with columns, vaulted galleries on three sides; about 57 × 32 m without the atrium, dome about 16 m; thin red bricks in mortar, probably marble revetment inside; the atrium burned in 564; after the earthquake of 740 a second dome was added over the west bay [stated: thebyzantinelegacy.com "Hagia Eirene"]. Built as of 562: one dome on a drum of twenty windows over the east bay, the west bay under a barrel vault with a lunette window, two rows of arched windows in the outer walls, lead roofs, the apse with three windows and a half-dome, three west doors, the atrium's porticoes [inferred: the drum, the vault and the windows, from Hagia Sophia and the later building]. Exterior only; the interior is closed.

### 4.3 The Augustaion, the Milion and the honorific columns

- **Milion**: in the west of the Augustaion; a dome on four arches, perhaps like the Arch of Janus in Rome; statues of Constantine and Helena with a cross on top; Justinian added a sundial [stated: livius.org "Augusteôn Square"]. Built 11 m square, arches 5.2 m, a dome on a drum, the two gilded statues and the cross, the sundial on a pillar [inferred: dimensions].
- **Column of Justinian** (543): on the west side of the Augustaion; a marble base of seven steps; a column of masonry covered with brass plates and garlands; Justinian on a colossal bronze horse facing east, in the dress of Achilles with a plumed helmet, the orb with the cross in his left hand, his right raised to the east; about 70 m high by Buondelmonti's estimate [stated: wikiwand "Column of Justinian"; livius.org]. Built about 49 m: seven steps, a pedestal, a bronze shaft of 34 m with gilded garland bands, the gilded rider [inferred: height reduced; the figure from the shared figure builder].
- **Column of Constantine** (330): porphyry drums (seven to eleven) with bronze wreaths over the joints, a white marble pedestal; the bronze emperor with a spear and a seven-rayed crown, probably holding an orb; the column 37–40 m, the whole nearly 50 m [stated: Wikipedia mirror "Column of Constantine"]. Built: eight porphyry drums, 30 m, bronze wreaths, a marble capital, a 6 m statue with the radiate crown, spear and orb [inferred: the number of drums].

## 5. The town

About 400 houses and apartment blocks, built as Roman-type insulae (rome.md §4.2), balconies on most (Zeno's building law for the city regulated balconies) [inferred], windows a little wider spaced. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The level hilltop falling toward the sea; cypresses and plane trees in gardens. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Bright and windy, with cloud and rain off the sea. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

Along the Mese from the Forum of Constantine to the Augustaion, and into Hagia Sophia through its atrium. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

As for Hagia Sophia (`docs/buildings/hagia-sophia.md` §9).
