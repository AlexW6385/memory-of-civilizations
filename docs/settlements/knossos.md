## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the palace of Knossos, the largest Minoan palace [stated: allovergreece]. It was a multi-storey building of about 20,000 m² [stated: Knossos brochure 2022], built of ashlar with frescoed walls [stated: allovergreece].
- **The Central Court:** about 50 × 25 m [stated: Oxford Classics; pointurier], 27 × 49 m [stated: Brown/Joukowsky] or 54 × 27 m [stated: brewminate], oriented NNE–SSW [stated: Oxford Classics].
- **Around the court:** the Throne Room suite opens off the court at its north-west, beside the Stepped Portico [stated: odysseyadventures], with the Tripartite Shrine to its south [stated: Oxford Classics]. The residential quarter is in the south-east [stated: Oxford Classics].
- **Approaches:** the West Court is the official approach. A paved processional way, the Royal Road, leads from the town to the theatral area at the north-west corner [stated: heraklion.gr]. There is a main entrance on each of the four sides [stated: Wikipedia text via sites.google].
- **Columns:** of cypress, narrower at the bottom than the top, painted red, on stone bases with round, pillow-like capitals [stated: Wikipedia text via sites.google].
- **Reconstructions:** Evans rebuilt a bastion above the north ramp and the west façade [stated: Brown/Joukowsky].
- **Moment shown:** about 1600 BC, the Second (Neopalatial) Palace.

## 4. The style

A gypsum ashlar base; upper storeys of rubble and timber framing, plastered ochre, with timber bands; red columns tapering downward with black cushion capitals; red-framed windows; flat roofs edged with horns of consecration. [stated: columns; inferred: wall colours and horns along the roofs, after Evans and frescoes]

## 4a. Details added 2026-11-06

- **Columns:** of cypress, narrower at the bottom than the top, painted red, on stone bases with round, pillow-like capitals [stated: Wikipedia text via sites.google]. **Built:** a gypsum base disc, the red shaft swelling upward, a black collar ring, the round cushion and the square abacus in black [inferred: profile after the restored columns].
- **Horns of consecration** along the roof edges: a stepped base and two horns curving outward, plastered white [inferred: shape after the horns restored at Knossos].
- **Throne Room:** an alabaster (gypsum) seat against the north wall, "the oldest stone throne of the Aegean region"; gypsum benches on three sides; two griffins either side of the throne; a sunken "lustral basin", partially partitioned off at one side; entered from the anteroom by two double doors; Evans thought room and anteroom could hold thirty [stated: Wikipedia "Throne Room, Knossos" via cavac mirror]. A wooden throne and alabaster benches in the antechamber, a porphyry basin in the centre, the lustral basin in the south part forming the inner sanctum and light-well [stated: explorecrete]. **Built:** the room (6 × 7 m [inferred]) cut into the West Wing behind the antechamber with its two doorways; the throne with its tall rounded back, benches along the north and west walls; couchant griffins among papyrus on a red ground above a wavy blue band, facing the throne from either side [inferred: after the restored fresco]; the porphyry basin; the lustral basin sunk 1 m in the south-west corner, lined with gypsum, reached by steps, behind a parapet with a column, open above to a light well with a parapet on the roof [inferred: dimensions].
- **North Entrance:** an open, paved passage climbs from the North Entrance to the Central Court, raised colonnades ("bastions") on either side, Evans reconstructing the western one; there he placed a copy of the restored relief fresco of a bull, perhaps part of a hunting scene [stated: HMdb marker]; Evans read it as the capture of a wild bull in an olive grove [stated: searchculture.gr]. **Built:** the West Bastion as a podium carrying a portico of two red columns, the charging bull in painted relief (16 cm) on its back wall with an olive tree, under the flat roof and its horns [inferred: the column count and colours].
- **Light wells and the Grand Staircase:** a light well open to the sky in the East Wing with colonnades rising storey above storey on its north side, the columns standing on the balustrades of the landings [inferred: after Evans's restoration of the Grand Staircase]; a passage in from the court's east portico.
- **Queen's Megaron and its dolphins:** a room opening off the light well with the dolphin fresco (dolphins and small fish on a pale ground) on its south wall [inferred: the fresco's subject is standard; its wall here chosen to face the light well].
- **Bull-leaping fresco:** on the back wall of the east portico, toward its north end: a red-skinned youth vaulting over the back of a galloping bull, a pale-skinned girl at the horns and another behind, inside a border of painted stones [inferred: the fresco came from the East Wing (Court of the Stone Spout); its place here is ours].

## 5. The town

Two- and three-storey flat-roofed houses along lanes, thinning toward the fields. [inferred, after the "Town Mosaic" plaques]

## 6. Ground and water

The low hill; olive groves and cypress; plane trees and oleander along the Kairatos. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Bright and clear, with the summer north wind (meltemi). [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the West Court north to the theatral area, along the Royal Road's end, in by the North Entrance Passage to the Central Court. [inferred]

## 9. Sound
