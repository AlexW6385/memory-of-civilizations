# Knossos (the palace of Minos), about 1600 BC — dossier

Settlement id: `knossos` · Style: `minoan` · Region: the Aegean (Crete, the Kairatos valley) · Code: `src/settlements/knossos/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the palace's wings round the Central Court, porticoes of red columns tapering downward with black capitals, the Throne Room antechamber and the Tripartite Shrine on the court, the North Entrance with its bastion portico, the south propylon, the West Court, the theatral area and the Royal Road; the town of two- and three-storey houses; the slope to the Kairatos; ground, planting, weather, music). Since 2026-11-06 also: the Throne Room with its griffins and lustral basin, the West Bastion with the charging bull, the light well of the Grand Staircase, the Queen's Megaron with the dolphins, the bull-leaping fresco, horns of consecration and columns redrawn (§4a). Second pass (§4b): the West Magazines with their pithoi, the theatral area, the Royal Road, the town houses after the Town Mosaic. L3 pass (§4c, §10): the Procession Fresco in the south propylaeum, the antechamber's throne and benches, the Queen's bathroom and the dancing lady, weathering of the houses, the potter's yard and people. Still for later: the rest of the interior (the Grand Staircase's flights, the Hall of the Double Axes, the lustral basins), the frescoes, the drainage, the Little Palace and the Caravanserai, the cemeteries, people.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the palace of Knossos, the largest Minoan palace [stated: allovergreece]. It was a multi-storey building of about 20,000 m² [stated: Knossos brochure 2022], built of ashlar with frescoed walls [stated: allovergreece].
- **The Central Court:** about 50 × 25 m [stated: Oxford Classics; pointurier], 27 × 49 m [stated: Brown/Joukowsky] or 54 × 27 m [stated: brewminate], oriented NNE–SSW [stated: Oxford Classics].
- **Around the court:** the Throne Room suite opens off the court at its north-west, beside the Stepped Portico [stated: odysseyadventures], with the Tripartite Shrine to its south [stated: Oxford Classics]. The residential quarter is in the south-east [stated: Oxford Classics].
- **Approaches:** the West Court is the official approach. A paved processional way, the Royal Road, leads from the town to the theatral area at the north-west corner [stated: heraklion.gr]. There is a main entrance on each of the four sides [stated: Wikipedia text via sites.google].
- **Columns:** of cypress, narrower at the bottom than the top, painted red, on stone bases with round, pillow-like capitals [stated: Wikipedia text via sites.google].
- **Reconstructions:** Evans rebuilt a bastion above the north ramp and the west façade [stated: Brown/Joukowsky].
- **Moment shown:** about 1600 BC, the Second (Neopalatial) Palace.

## 4. The style

A gypsum ashlar base; upper storeys of rubble and timber framing, plastered ochre, with timber bands; red columns tapering downward with black cushion capitals; red-framed windows; flat roofs edged with horns of consecration. [stated: columns; inferred: wall colours and horns along the roofs, after Evans and frescoes]

## 4a. Details added 2026-11-06

- **Columns:** of cypress, narrower at the bottom than the top, painted red, on stone bases with round, pillow-like capitals [stated: Wikipedia text via sites.google]. **Built:** a gypsum base disc, the red shaft swelling upward, a black collar ring, the round cushion and the square abacus in black [inferred: profile after the restored columns].
- **Horns of consecration** along the roof edges: a stepped base and two horns curving outward, plastered white [inferred: shape after the horns restored at Knossos].
- **Throne Room:** an alabaster (gypsum) seat against the north wall, "the oldest stone throne of the Aegean region"; gypsum benches on three sides; two griffins either side of the throne; a sunken "lustral basin", partially partitioned off at one side; entered from the anteroom by two double doors; Evans thought room and anteroom could hold thirty [stated: Wikipedia "Throne Room, Knossos" via cavac mirror]. A wooden throne and alabaster benches in the antechamber, a porphyry basin in the centre, the lustral basin in the south part forming the inner sanctum and light-well [stated: explorecrete]. **Built:** the room (6 × 7 m [inferred]) cut into the West Wing behind the antechamber with its two doorways; the throne with its tall rounded back, benches along the north and west walls; couchant griffins among papyrus on a red ground above a wavy blue band, facing the throne from either side [inferred: after the restored fresco]; the porphyry basin; the lustral basin sunk 1 m in the south-west corner, lined with gypsum, reached by steps, behind a parapet with a column, open above to a light well with a parapet on the roof [inferred: dimensions].
- **North Entrance:** an open, paved passage climbs from the North Entrance to the Central Court, raised colonnades ("bastions") on either side, Evans reconstructing the western one; there he placed a copy of the restored relief fresco of a bull, perhaps part of a hunting scene [stated: HMdb marker]; Evans read it as the capture of a wild bull in an olive grove [stated: searchculture.gr]. **Built:** the West Bastion as a podium carrying a portico of two red columns, the charging bull in painted relief (16 cm) on its back wall with an olive tree, under the flat roof and its horns [inferred: the column count and colours].
- **Light wells and the Grand Staircase:** a light well open to the sky in the East Wing with colonnades rising storey above storey on its north side, the columns standing on the balustrades of the landings [inferred: after Evans's restoration of the Grand Staircase]; a passage in from the court's east portico.
- **Queen's Megaron and its dolphins:** a room opening off the light well with the dolphin fresco (dolphins and small fish on a pale ground) on its south wall [inferred: the fresco's subject is standard; its wall here chosen to face the light well].
- **Bull-leaping fresco:** on the back wall of the east portico, toward its north end: a red-skinned youth vaulting over the back of a galloping bull, a pale-skinned girl at the horns and another behind, inside a border of painted stones [inferred: the fresco came from the East Wing (Court of the Stone Spout); its place here is ours].
- **Pigments (2026-11-06):** "Colours employed were black (carbonaceous shale), red (haematite), white (hydrate of lime), yellow (ochre), blue (silicate of copper), and green (blue and yellow mixed)" [stated: Cartwright, World History Encyclopedia]. Ours: every painted blue — the bull-leaping fresco's ground and its border stones, the griffins' wavy band, the dolphins, the bands under the eaves — in the palette's Egyptian blue (the copper silicate); the olive tree's foliage behind the charging bull in the fresco green, as blue and yellow mixed (it was a cloth green) [inferred: which parts take which colour].

## 4b. Details added 2026-11-06 (second pass): the stores, the theatral area, the Royal Road, the town houses

- **The West Magazines:** the Long Corridor runs north–south through the ground floor of the West Wing with the long store-rooms opening off its west side, filled with pithoi and with stone-lined cists sunk in their floors [inferred: from the published plans of the palace as generally known; no source checked in this pass gives the count or sizes — to verify]. **Built:** the corridor 3.5 × 70 m with a way in from the north, eighteen magazines 16 m long and about 2 m wide between walls of 1.6 m, each with a row of pithoi along its north wall and gypsum lids over cists down the middle, lamps in the corridor [inferred: sizes].
- **Pithoi:** great storage jars about 1.5 m high with raised bands round the body and rows of small handles [inferred: the form after the giant pithoi left in place at Knossos].
- **The theatral area:** the Royal Road ends at it; "the eastern flight consists of 18 low steps a little over 10 metres broad", the southern flight has six steps, originally about 15.5 m long; the court before them about 13 × 10 m; the southern steps are bounded on the east by the bastion, where "some sort of pavilion may have stood ... where the ruler and his family sat and watched" [stated: Evans via odysseyadventures]. **Built:** the paved court where the road arrives, the eastern flight of 18 steps of 0.12 m, the southern flight of six of 0.25 m, the bastion at their corner carrying a pavilion of two red columns under a flat roof [inferred: rises and the pavilion's form]. (Before: two flights on the wrong sides.)
- **The Royal Road:** on a bed of rough stones about half a metre deep, its surface concrete packed over stone slabs, about 3.8 m across, with a central strip about 1.4 m wide of oblong slabs, fit for porters and pack animals rather than wheels [stated: Evans via odysseyadventures]. **Built:** from the west edge of the town to the theatral area's court, following the ground: a bed of packed lime between stone kerbs, the raised strip of slabs of uneven lengths down the middle.
- **Town houses:** houses of two storeys (some three), the ground floor lit only by narrow slits, most windows on the upper storey; timber beams giving structure to walls and ceilings, the rest mud brick or stone; roofs used for sleeping in the heat; a model house from Arkhanes has a room with a balcony on its upper floor [stated: antiquatedantiquarian]. The faience plaques of the Town Mosaic each show a house front [stated: fhw.gr]. **Built** (replacing plain blocks): a base course of ashlar or rubble, plastered walls, timber bands at each floor with the round ends of the floor beams showing in a row on the front, a door under a timber lintel with a stone step, slits on the ground floor, upper windows in red frames divided into four panes, a low roof edge and on half the houses a small room on the roof; each house turned with its door toward the palace [inferred: the window panes, beam ends and roof rooms after the Town Mosaic plaques].

## 4c. Details added in the L3 pass (Phase D): the Procession Fresco, the antechamber, the Queen's Megaron, weathering

- **The Procession Fresco:** the walls of the Corridor of the Procession were painted with the "Fresco of the Procession", and a partial copy is shown at the South Propylaeum [stated: explorecrete]; Evans placed copies of it in the reconstructed western part of the South Propylaeum, showing "men carrying precious vases as offerings to the deity"; its columns were wooden, "gently tapering downwards", on stone bases [stated: searchculture.gr, South Propylaeum]. The Cup-Bearer, a fragment 82 × 58 cm found in 1900 in the South West Quarter, is a "male figure carrying a silver rhyton" and was "part of the Procession Fresco" [stated: Ashmolean Minoan Room catalogue]. **Built:** both walls of the south passage (the propylaeum) carry the procession over the gypsum dado, 28 m long and 2.1 m high, the figures nearly life-size walking in toward the court: red-skinned men with long black hair, the belt pinching the waist, the kilt with its patterned hem, carrying conical silver rhyta banded in gold, jars held before the chest, pitchers; every fifth a white-skinned woman in a flounced skirt; a white ground under a blue band with a waving lower edge, red and black stripes framing it [inferred: the composition, colours of the kilts and grounds, the share of women, after the restored panels; the walls chosen as Evans's copies stand in the South Propylaeum].
- **The Throne Room's antechamber:** a wooden throne and alabaster benches in the antechamber [stated: explorecrete]. **Built:** its north and south walls closing the portico, the wooden throne against the north wall (the copy of the gypsum throne's form), the gypsum benches either side of it and along the south wall, the walls above painted red as in the Throne Room [inferred: the red walls and the benches' runs].
- **The Queen's Megaron:** a smaller hall "comparably arranged and richly decorated", with fragments of frescoes of dolphins and dancing ladies; at the end of the room "a low partition wall with one column" enclosed a small space thought to be the Queen's Bathroom, because pieces of a clay "bath" were found there [stated: HMdb marker, the Queen's Megaron]. **Built:** at the east end the bathroom behind its low gypsum partition with the column at its open end carrying the beam; the clay bath with its rolled rim and side handles, a red band under the rim; a stone lamp on its tall foot burning beside it; the gypsum bench on the west wall and over it the dancing lady, her hair flying, one arm raised, in the open bodice and flounced skirt [inferred: the bath's size and paint, the lamp, the bench, the dancer's pose after the restored panel].
- **Weathering of the town houses:** the plaster's foot darkened by damp and the splash of the lane (highest on the street front), rain streaks down the front's corners from the roof edge [inferred: §5 of DESIGN, the materials of the walls].

## 4d. Interiors added in the Suzhou-standard batch: the residential hall and the lapidary's workshop

- **Residential quarter:** the East Wing was the domestic quarter, with the Hall of the Double Axes (the king's megaron) opening by pier-and-door partitions onto a light well, its walls carrying the figure-of-eight shield fresco and the double-axe sign [stated: Oxford Classics crete course, the Palace; brewminate]. Pier-and-door partitions (rows of square piers with doorways between) are the characteristic Minoan way of lighting and ventilating rooms from a well [stated: standard account of Minoan palatial architecture]. **Built:** a hall 18 × 10 m in the East Wing (the room cut into the four-storey wing; ceiling 3.4 m), entered from the court's east portico by a short passage; the east side a row of five piers (gypsum footing, plastered shaft, timber beam) opening onto a light well with pots of plants; gypsum dado, figure-of-eight shields in ochre hide on a white ground along the north wall, a frieze of blue and red double axes along the south; a high-backed wooden chair with footstool and a low offering table (bronze bowl, serpentine rhyton), two woven rugs, a gypsum bench with cushions, a bronze double axe on its stand, a warp-weighted loom with clay loom weights against the south wall, a painted chest, a pithos and amphorae, two lamps [inferred: all sizes and the furnishing, after finds and frescoes; the room's position in the wing is ours].
- **Loom weights:** the warp-weighted loom with clay weights is the loom of Bronze Age Crete (weights found in quantity in palatial and house contexts) [inferred: general Aegean archaeology; no source checked in this pass].
- **Lapidary's workshop:** Evans named a "lapidary's workshop" in the East Wing, where stone vases in all stages of manufacture were found [inferred from memory of Evans's account; to verify]. Stone vases of serpentine, limestone, porphyry (lapis lacedaemonius) and others were cut with the bow drill and tubular drill with sand or emery abrasives [stated: general account of Minoan stone-vase making, e.g. Warren, Minoan Stone Vases; to verify page]. **Built:** a workshop 18 × 10 m behind the hall (a passage from it), benches along the north wall with vases at every stage (the rough block, the outside turned, the inside drilled, the finished ewer, bowl, lamp and rhyton), a middle bench, a south bench, the bow drill over a vase held on a stool (bronze bit, bow, thong), a water trough with sand and emery, a grindstone on its trestle, raw blocks of serpentine, limestone and porphyry stacked below a shelf of finished vases, baskets and a sack, the second pier-and-door row onto a light well with a worktable [inferred: layout and sizes].
- **People:** a weaver at the loom, a woman at the bench, the lapidary at the drill, two workmen at the benches [inferred].

## 10. Life (added in the L3 pass)

- **Potters:** the potter's wheel was available from MM IB, introduced from the Levant; pottery was made "probably in small artisanal workshops, often clustered in settlements near good sources of clay", with "two classes of workshop, one catering to the palaces"; conical cups were made in steatite and imitated in clay; liquids and grain were stored in pithoi [stated: Wikipedia, Minoan pottery]. A Late Minoan IA kiln at Kommos is a "cross-draft kiln ... of a type popular during the Neopalatial period" [stated: Hesperia Supplement 30 summary, andromedabooks]. **Built:** a potter's yard by the Royal Road: a rubble wall round three sides, open to the road; the kiln of stone and clay under its low vault, the stoke-hole glowing at the yard end, the flue at the far end, ash before the mouth; the wheel (a clay wheel-head on a pivot stone) with a pot on it, the potter on a low stool, a heap of clay, a water jar; two drying boards on trestles with rows of conical cups and jugs; finished jars by the wall; the boy squatting by the kiln with the firewood [inferred: the layout, the sizes, the wheel's form after the clay wheel-heads].
- **People:** the dress after the kits (men in kilt or loincloth, bare above the belt, long-haired; women in long flounced robes; §4 of the life kit). Porters bringing jars on their heads up the Royal Road (fit for porters and pack animals, §4b), a laden donkey with its driver; people in the West Court and the Central Court; a priestess praying before the Tripartite Shrine with water jars set down [inferred: the donkey as the pack animal of Bronze Age Crete; the numbers kept few, §5 of DESIGN].

## 4e. Deepening pass (Suzhou standard): the town in rows, the West Court furnished, rooms of the palace

- **Size and density of the town:** the Neopalatial city covered "between 105 and 125 ha." (later revised to ca. 115–130 ha.), with "dense, continuous occupation across the city", the density "possibly decreasing with distance from the palace", an estimate of 200–225 persons/ha and a population of ca. 24–28,000; elite houses near the palace "increasingly elaborate and differentiated", smaller houses further away; "dense occupation did not extend east of the Kairatos river" [stated: Whitelaw, 12th ICCS paper, UCL]. **Built:** the houses continuous along the lanes with no gaps in the core (r < 100 m), thinning only beyond ~100 m and sharply from ~160 m; no houses east of the Kairatos (the town stops at x = 170 m, the stream is at x = 205 m); the near houses are of several designs (plain, balcony, stepped with a roof terrace, open front).
- **Lanes and rows [inferred]:** no source checked gives the street plan of the Neopalatial town; the rows of party-wall houses 11 m deep along lanes 3.4 m wide (the Royal Road, 3.8 m, is the first of them), 26 m apart, with 3.2 m cross streets every 78 m and 2.6 m alleys between some houses, are our reading of "dense, continuous occupation" and of the Town Mosaic's house fronts. Lanes are beaten earth (pebbles) with a drain stone down the middle, kerbs against the house walls. Some houses (30 %) are shorter and have a walled yard behind them with a bread oven, jars, a stack of firewood and a vine arbour on poles [inferred: ordinary Aegean practice].
- **Houses at eye level [inferred from the Town Mosaic plaques and the Arkhanes house model (§4b)]:** a timber lintel and door leaf (an open leaf where the house can be entered), a stone bench beside the door on half the houses, a cloth shade on poles over the door on a third, a timber balcony on the upper floor of a quarter (the Arkhanes model has a balcony), red-framed windows with open shutters, the beam ends in a row, strings of onions and a hide hung to dry, a painted band under the roof's edge on two in five, plaster fallen from the rubble in patches, a rain spout with its streak, reed shades and jars on the roofs, horns of consecration on a few. A stepped house (upper floors set back, leaving a terrace on the first floor's roof with a parapet).
- **Open houses [inferred]:** 56 houses nearest the arrival can be entered — the ground floor lit through the door and a lamp, an earth floor, plastered walls, joists and planks of the ceiling. Kinds: home (hearth with its stones and pot, bed of mats, jars, shelf of cups, low table), weaver (the warp-weighted loom with its row of clay weights — loom weights are found in quantity in Bronze Age Crete (§4d)), shop (the counter, a balance, jars on shelves), store (pithoi, sacks). No source checked gives the plan of a Knossian town house's ground floor; the furniture is the ordinary kit of Aegean houses.
- **The West Court:** it "was crossed by a number of raised walkways or causeways"; one 1.40 m wide (the same as the Royal Road) ran from the north along the palace façade to the West Porch, and 20 m past the treasury it split, one branch heading south-west [stated: odysseyadventures, The West Court]. Evans named three stone-lined pits "circular ... about 5–6 metres in diameter and 3 metres deep" koulourai (kouloures), filled later with First Palace rubbish; granaries or "blind wells" [stated: same]. The western façade had storage blocks with niches facing the court, and "in front of two of them, Evans found bases for altars"; a steatite vase fragment shows an altar topped with sacral horns [stated: same]. The court was paved with large irregular limestone slabs [stated: same]. **Built:** three raised causeways 1.4 m wide (one from the west entrance's portico out to the Royal Road, one north–south, one along the north), the three pits as stone kerbs 5.4 m across with dark earth within (they cannot be sunk into the paving here) [inferred: layout of the causeways after the description; their route is ours], altar bases with horns in front of two façade blocks, gypsum benches between the setbacks, torch stands either side of the west entrance, plant tubs and jar stands along the causeways. The court is a place of gathering and exchange: stalls of the town's goods stand in it [inferred].
- **Rooms of the palace added:** the archive (shelves of baskets and boxes of clay tablets along three walls, a table with a tablet and stylus, the scribe's stool) in the North Wing, east of the entrance passage; an oil store in its west part; the kitchen (a long hearth with pots on stones, a beehive oven, the saddle quern on its bench, pithoi) in the South Wing with a doorway on the court [inferred: Linear B tablets were kept in clay boxes in rooms of the palace archives at Knossos; Evans' "archive" rooms; the kitchen and store are ours].
- **The Kairatos:** a stream about 6 m across (11 m with its banks) with a bed 1.5 m below the banks, wandering a little, with plane trees, oleander, tamarisk and reeds along it; women kneeling at the bank washing cloth, small fish in the pools, egrets [inferred: the Kairatos is a seasonal Cretan stream; the washing is the common custom].
- **Planting:** figs and olives in the yards, olives and cypress on the hills, maquis on the slopes, wheat in plots round the edge of the town [inferred].
