## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Palace of Versailles, enlarged by Louis XIV. The garden front of Le Vau and Hardouin-Mansart is about 670 m long across the central block and the north and south wings, and the Hall of Mirrors (1678–1684) lies behind the central garden front [stated: Wikipedia "Palace of Versailles"; "Hall of Mirrors"].
- **The gardens:** Le Nôtre's design runs west down the axis: the water parterre, the Latona fountain, the Tapis Vert, the Apollo fountain and the cross-shaped Grand Canal, about 1.6 km long, with bosquets either side [stated: Wikipedia "Gardens of Versailles"].
- **The town:** three avenues converge on the Place d'Armes before the palace [stated: Wikipedia "Versailles, Yvelines"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1690.

## 1a. Detail (built: `src/styles/classical_fr/front.js`, `fountain` in `index.js`, `town.js`)

- **The garden front:** three storeys — a slightly rusticated ground floor with arched windows whose keystones are carved with figures; the main floor with tall arched windows between unfluted Ionic pilasters; an attic of small rectangular windows between pilasters; a flat roof behind a balustrade; the central front 25 bays wide [stated: Sullivan, Bluffton "Garden facade"]. Several slightly projecting avant-corps with detached columns; the balustrade set with trophies and vases hiding the roof; the 17 windows of the Hall of Mirrors on the gardens [stated: Larousse "château de Versailles"]. Built on every stone front of the central block and the wings: rustication in 0.55 m courses, glazing of small panes in white frames set back in the reveals, iron rails to the long windows, the band courses and entablature breaking forward over the avant-corps (the central five bays and three at each end), detached columns on pedestals with a statue above each before the attic, trophies of arms on the avant-corps' dies and fire-vases (pots-à-feu) elsewhere [inferred: bay grouping, sizes].
- **The Latona fountain:** the marble group of Latona and her children by the Marsy brothers; the peasants of Lycia turned into frogs and lizards; remodelled 1687–89 by Hardouin-Mansart, who replaced the rock with a marble pyramid and turned Latona to face the Grand Canal [stated: chateauversailles.fr "Fountains"]; the tiers carry frogs, the lowest turtles [stated: KidzSearch "Latona Fountain"]. Built: the four tiers with gilded frogs and turtles on their rims and frogs in the basin, each spouting an arc of water toward the goddess; Latona with a hand raised to Jupiter and the two children at her knees, facing west. The basin now stands on a level landing at the foot of the slope (it had been set below the ground there).
- **The Grand Canal:** 1.67 km by 62 m, finished 1679; its flotilla of Louis XIV's time — a three-masted ship, a galley, rowing boats, galiots, brigantines, gondolas given by Venice, two English yachts (1675); six gondoliers in 1684 [stated: Wikiwand "Grand Canal of Versailles"]; two gondolas sent by Venice in 1674 with gondoliers who settled at "Little Venice" [stated: parisdigest "Grand Canal"]. Built: two gondolas with their gondoliers and two rowing boats (the canal is compressed and only its eastern end lies inside the settlement).
- **The court:** courtiers in coats and plumed hats and ladies in gowns walking and talking on the terrace, down the walk between the water basins and at the Latona basin [inferred: the gardens as the court's promenade].

## 1b. The court side (v. lv; `src/styles/classical_fr/court.js`)

- **Sources:** Louis XIII's château of 1624 (Philibert Le Roy), of stone and red brick, round an entrance court with the corps de logis at its west end and wings north and south; Le Vau's envelope (1669–72) wrapped it on the north, west and south [stated: "History of the Palace of Versailles" (Wikipedia mirror)]. Inside the U of Louis XIII's red brick and cut stone lies the court of black-and-white marble; the central avant-corps has three storeys, with eight red marble columns carrying a gilded wrought-iron balcony, and above it a triangular group of lead statues framing a large clock; the rest of the façade has painted and gilded wrought-iron balconies and stone tables on consoles holding marble busts of Roman emperors; Hardouin-Mansart gave the slate mansard elaborate dormers and gilt lead roof dressings in 1679–81 [stated: HiSoUR "Palace of Versailles"]. The clock is framed by Mars and Hercules, by the Marsys and Girardon [stated: D. P. Henry, "The façade of the king's bedroom"]. Almost a hundred busts on pedestals, of Roman emperors and other figures, some ancient, some of the 17th century, among Louis XIV's first sculpture commissions of the 1660s, decorate the façades of the Marble and Royal Courts [stated: Château de Versailles, "Busts of the Marble Courtyard"]. The court is paved geometrically in black and white marble and reached from the Royal Court by a short flight of steps [stated: AroundUs "Cour de marbre"]. The Royal Gate, designed about 1680 by Hardouin-Mansart, gilded, separated the forecourt from the Royal Court; groups of Peace (Tuby) and Abundance (Coysevox) stood on its gatehouses [stated: Wikipedia "The Royal Gate of the Palace of Versailles"]; an entrance gate with the royal arms in gilded wrought iron by Hardouin-Mansart [stated: Sullivan, Bluffton "East front"].
- **Ours:** the solid block that stood for the old château is replaced by its plan: the corps de logis (12 m deep) and two wings round the Marble Court (22 by 24 m), raised 0.85 m and paved in white marble with black lozenges and a black border, five marble steps across its open east side; the Royal Court before it (paved) between two ranges, the ministers' wings beside the forecourt [inferred: all dimensions, compressed with the rest]. Every court front in brick with stone quoins, plinth, band and modillioned cornice; each window bay in a stone chain toothed into the brick, the windows glazed in small panes in white frames within moulded surrounds, sills and carved agrafes; a stone balcony on consoles with a painted iron rail, gilt top rail and gilt rings at each first-floor window; a marble bust on a stone console between the first-floor windows; the slate mansard — steep brisis and low terrasson, a gilt lead roll at the break, a lead ridge with a gilt cresting of small vases and tall épis at its ends, brick chimneys with stone dressings — and over each bay a dormer of stone and lead with alternately triangular and segmental pediments, gilt scrolls at its feet and a gilt vase on top [inferred: the dormers' and cresting's forms]. The corps de logis: eight Doric columns of red marble (breccia standing in for Rance marble) in four pairs before the three middle bays carrying the entablature and the gilded balcony across them; above, an attic storey of three windows and a pediment with the clock — a dial in a gilt frame within gilt rays — and the lead figures of Mars (spear, shield) and Hercules (club) reclining on the rakes, lead trophies at the attic's corners [inferred: the pediment's shape, the figures' attributes]. A gilded railing on a stone plinth with stone piers and fire-vases across the Royal Court's mouth, its gate piers carrying Peace and Abundance, the leaves standing open; a second railing across the forecourt from the Place d'Armes [inferred: the railings' bars, spacing and heights; the Royal Gate drawn straight, not in its quarter circles].

## 3. Orientation

The garden front faces west; the town and its avenues lie east. [stated]

## 4. The style

Pale stone in three storeys with balustrades and trophies on the garden front; brick and stone under steep slate roofs on the court side; clipped box parterres; round basins. [stated]

## 5. The town

About 450 houses. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): an auberge and two homes, with stone-flagged floors; a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

## 6. Ground and water

Gravel walks; lawns; dense bosquets (elm and oak stand in for hornbeam and lime); the canal. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Fair to cloudy; rain sometimes. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the Place d'Armes through the courts, round to the parterre and down the axis. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Lully's strings, the grands motets, the harpsichord. [inferred]
