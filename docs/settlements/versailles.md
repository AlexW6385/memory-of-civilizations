## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Palace of Versailles, enlarged by Louis XIV. The garden front of Le Vau and Hardouin-Mansart is about 670 m long across the central block and the north and south wings, and the Hall of Mirrors (1678–1684) lies behind the central garden front [stated: Wikipedia "Palace of Versailles"; "Hall of Mirrors"].
- **The gardens:** Le Nôtre's design runs west down the axis: the water parterre, the Latona fountain, the Tapis Vert, the Apollo fountain and the cross-shaped Grand Canal, about 1.6 km long, with bosquets either side [stated: Wikipedia "Gardens of Versailles"].
- **The town:** three avenues converge on the Place d'Armes before the palace [stated: Wikipedia "Versailles, Yvelines"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1690.

## 1a. Detail (built: `src/styles/classical_fr/front.js`, `fountain` in `index.js`, `town.js`)

- **The garden front:** three storeys — a slightly rusticated ground floor with arched windows whose keystones are carved with figures; the main floor with tall arched windows between unfluted Ionic pilasters; an attic of small rectangular windows between pilasters; a flat roof behind a balustrade; the central front 25 bays wide [stated: Sullivan, Bluffton "Garden facade"]. Several slightly projecting avant-corps with detached columns; the balustrade set with trophies and vases hiding the roof; the 17 windows of the Hall of Mirrors on the gardens [stated: Larousse "château de Versailles"]. Built on every stone front of the central block and the wings: rustication in 0.55 m courses, glazing of small panes in white frames set back in the reveals, iron rails to the long windows, the band courses and entablature breaking forward over the avant-corps (the central five bays and three at each end), detached columns on pedestals with a statue above each before the attic, trophies of arms on the avant-corps' dies and fire-vases (pots-à-feu) elsewhere [inferred: bay grouping, sizes].
- **The Latona fountain:** the marble group of Latona and her children by the Marsy brothers; the peasants of Lycia turned into frogs and lizards; remodelled 1687–89 by Hardouin-Mansart, who replaced the rock with a marble pyramid and turned Latona to face the Grand Canal [stated: chateauversailles.fr "Fountains"]; the tiers carry frogs, the lowest turtles [stated: KidzSearch "Latona Fountain"]. Built: the four tiers with gilded frogs and turtles on their rims and frogs in the basin, each spouting an arc of water toward the goddess; Latona with a hand raised to Jupiter and the two children at her knees, facing west. The basin now stands on a level landing at the foot of the slope (it had been set below the ground there).
- **The Grand Canal:** 1.67 km by 62 m, finished 1679; its flotilla of Louis XIV's time — a three-masted ship, a galley, rowing boats, galiots, brigantines, gondolas given by Venice, two English yachts (1675); six gondoliers in 1684 [stated: Wikiwand "Grand Canal of Versailles"]; two gondolas sent by Venice in 1674 with gondoliers who settled at "Little Venice" [stated: parisdigest "Grand Canal"]. Built: two gondolas with their gondoliers and two rowing boats (the canal is compressed and only its eastern end lies inside the settlement). The reach inside the settlement now ends where the settlement does, at a landing: stone side walls and head wall with a coping, a flight of steps down into the water across its width between two piers with vases, a paved quay with mooring posts and rings, a rowing boat and a gondola moored at the steps and a boatman on the quay [inferred: the canal continues beyond the edge; Petite Venise, the flotilla's yard, stood at the canal's eastern end — stated: Wikipedia "Petite Venise" — and the landing stands for the flotilla's moorings].
- **The court:** courtiers in coats and plumed hats and ladies in gowns walking and talking on the terrace, down the walk between the water basins and at the Latona basin [inferred: the gardens as the court's promenade].

## 1b. The court side (v. lv; `src/styles/classical_fr/court.js`)

- **Sources:** Louis XIII's château of 1624 (Philibert Le Roy), of stone and red brick, round an entrance court with the corps de logis at its west end and wings north and south; Le Vau's envelope (1669–72) wrapped it on the north, west and south [stated: "History of the Palace of Versailles" (Wikipedia mirror)]. Inside the U of Louis XIII's red brick and cut stone lies the court of black-and-white marble; the central avant-corps has three storeys, with eight red marble columns carrying a gilded wrought-iron balcony, and above it a triangular group of lead statues framing a large clock; the rest of the façade has painted and gilded wrought-iron balconies and stone tables on consoles holding marble busts of Roman emperors; Hardouin-Mansart gave the slate mansard elaborate dormers and gilt lead roof dressings in 1679–81 [stated: HiSoUR "Palace of Versailles"]. The clock is framed by Mars and Hercules, by the Marsys and Girardon [stated: D. P. Henry, "The façade of the king's bedroom"]. Almost a hundred busts on pedestals, of Roman emperors and other figures, some ancient, some of the 17th century, among Louis XIV's first sculpture commissions of the 1660s, decorate the façades of the Marble and Royal Courts [stated: Château de Versailles, "Busts of the Marble Courtyard"]. The court is paved geometrically in black and white marble and reached from the Royal Court by a short flight of steps [stated: AroundUs "Cour de marbre"]. The Royal Gate, designed about 1680 by Hardouin-Mansart, gilded, separated the forecourt from the Royal Court; groups of Peace (Tuby) and Abundance (Coysevox) stood on its gatehouses [stated: Wikipedia "The Royal Gate of the Palace of Versailles"]; an entrance gate with the royal arms in gilded wrought iron by Hardouin-Mansart [stated: Sullivan, Bluffton "East front"].
- **Ours:** the solid block that stood for the old château is replaced by its plan: the corps de logis (12 m deep) and two wings round the Marble Court (22 by 24 m), raised 0.85 m and paved in white marble with black lozenges and a black border, five marble steps across its open east side; the Royal Court before it (paved) between two ranges, the ministers' wings beside the forecourt [inferred: all dimensions, compressed with the rest]. Every court front in brick with stone quoins, plinth, band and modillioned cornice; each window bay in a stone chain toothed into the brick, the windows glazed in small panes in white frames within moulded surrounds, sills and carved agrafes; a stone balcony on consoles with a painted iron rail, gilt top rail and gilt rings at each first-floor window; a marble bust on a stone console between the first-floor windows; the slate mansard — steep brisis and low terrasson, a gilt lead roll at the break, a lead ridge with a gilt cresting of small vases and tall épis at its ends, brick chimneys with stone dressings — and over each bay a dormer of stone and lead with alternately triangular and segmental pediments, gilt scrolls at its feet and a gilt vase on top [inferred: the dormers' and cresting's forms]. The corps de logis: eight Doric columns of red marble (breccia standing in for Rance marble) in four pairs before the three middle bays carrying the entablature and the gilded balcony across them; above, an attic storey of three windows and a pediment with the clock — a dial in a gilt frame within gilt rays — and the lead figures of Mars (spear, shield) and Hercules (club) reclining on the rakes, lead trophies at the attic's corners [inferred: the pediment's shape, the figures' attributes]. A gilded railing on a stone plinth with stone piers and fire-vases across the Royal Court's mouth, its gate piers carrying Peace and Abundance, the leaves standing open; a second railing across the forecourt from the Place d'Armes [inferred: the railings' bars, spacing and heights; the Royal Gate drawn straight, not in its quarter circles].

## 1c. The state rooms of the first floor (`src/styles/classical_fr/grand.js`, `rooms.js`, `stair.js`)

**Sources.**
- *Hall of Mirrors* (Hardouin-Mansart and Le Brun, 1678–84): 73 m long, 10.50 m deep, 12.30 m high; seventeen windows on the park and seventeen mirrors of the same shape and size opposite; begun after Le Vau's open terrace was enclosed [stated: Wikipedia "Hall of Mirrors"; Sullivan, Bluffton "Hall of Mirrors"]. Seventeen arcades of 21 mirrors each, 357 in all; walls of white marble, pilasters veined with white, gilt capitals; trophies of gilt bronze on green marble; thirty compositions on the vault (about 1,000 m²), half on canvas marouflé, half painted on the vault; originally three rows of chandeliers, 32 girandoles, about 3,000 lights; the Salon of War to the north leads to the King's apartments, the Salon of Peace to the south to the Queen's [stated: Vikidia "Galerie des Glaces"]. Pilasters of Rance marble with gilt bronze capitals of the "French order" designed by Le Brun — a fleur-de-lis under the royal sun between two Gallic cockerels; 30 compositions from 1661 to the peace of Nijmegen [stated: chateauversailles.fr "Hall of Mirrors"]. Trophies of gilt bronze chased by Pierre Ladoyreau on piers of green Campan marble; the mirrors held by bronze mouldings and cabochons; each arcade crowned in turn by a head of Apollo and the skin of the Nemean lion [stated: techno-science.net "Galerie des Glaces"]. A barrel vault with Le Brun's paintings framed in stucco [stated: Sullivan]. In the 1680s orange trees in great silver vases stood along the walls; the silver furniture was melted in 1689 [stated: chateauversailles.fr "Revealing the Hall of Mirrors"]. About 200 pieces of solid silver furniture by 1682, many designed by Le Brun and made at the Gobelins under Claude Ballin — tables, candelabra lining the hall, benches, vases, a silver throne — sent to the mint in 1689 [stated: "This is Versailles, Madame", "Silver furniture of Louis XIV"]. The parquet de Versailles — square oak panels laid on the diagonal — designed in 1684 for the Hall of Mirrors, the War Room and the King's guard room, finished 1693 [stated: Marc Maison "Versailles parquet floor"].
- *Salons of War and Peace*: furnished and decorated as the hall, opening on it by wide openings; their outer walls Le Vau's [stated: Wikipedia]. War: marble panels with six gilt bronze trophies; Coysevox's oval stucco relief of the King on horseback riding over his enemies, two Fames above, two chained captives below; Clio in the false chimney; the cupola with France armed on a cloud; Germany, Spain, Holland and Bellona in the arches. Peace: symmetrical, the benefits of peace in its cupola [stated: chateauversailles.fr "Hall of Mirrors"; Sullivan, Bluffton "Salon of War"].
- *The King's Grand Apartment*: in the 1680s Diana the billiard room and the west landing of the Ambassadors' Staircase, Mars the ballroom, Mercury the gaming room with a state bed, Apollo the throne room with the silver throne on its dais against the south wall until 1689, Venus the buffet room [stated: Wikipedia "Grand appartement du roi"]. Venus with marble pilasters and columns and Warin's King; Diana's two tiers of seats for those watching the billiards, Iphigenia over the chimney and Diana and Endymion opposite; Mars's cornice with helmets and two tribunes for the musicians until 1750; Mercury's brocades and the alcove's silver balustrade; Apollo's silver throne, 2.6 m high, on a platform under a canopy [stated: chateauversailles.fr "King's State Apartment"]; six candelabra flanking the throne [stated: "Silver furniture of Louis XIV"].
- *Ambassadors' Staircase* (1672–74, demolished 1752): a vestibule with a barrel vault and three arches, a flight to a platform with a fountain — a red marble basin held by two bronze dolphins — under Warin's bust of the King, steps left and right to the doors of Diana and Venus; marbles of France, red, green, white and grey; Le Brun's Four Parts of the World and the King's victories; a glass roof [stated: Party like 1660 "L'escalier des Ambassadeurs"].

**Ours.** The central block is no longer solid: its first floor (14.3 m, 8.3 m over the garden) holds the hall behind bays 4–20 of the garden front's 25 — 74.8 by 10.5 m, 12.3 m to the crown of a segmental barrel (radius 6.45 m) springing over the cornice at 9.6 m [inferred: the section from the stated height]. The window wall and the mirror wall are one elevation: white marble facing, Rance pilasters (breccia) on dark plinths at every bay with the French capital in gilt bronze (bell, fleur-de-lis, sun, two cockerels, corner volutes, abacus), green panels with a gilt trophy (shield, spears, standards, cuirass, helmet, laurel) each side of each pilaster [inferred: the trophies' place on the piers], archivolts (marble round the windows, gilt round the mirrors), keystones breaking through the architrave with Apollo's head and the lion's skin in turn, architrave with gilt fillet, frieze with gilt rosettes over the pilasters, modillion cornice, and over each pilaster two gilt children with Jupiter's thunderbolt [stated: "trumeaux dorés avec angelots et insignes de Jupiter", Vikidia; inferred: their form]. Each arcade of mirrors: six rows of three glasses and three in a fan in the head (21), gilt bars and cabochons; the windows glazed in small panes, lit from inside by the sky. The vault: gilt bands over every pilaster and along the vault, the crown alternating large framed paintings and ovals (one per bay, the middle one larger: *The King governs by himself*), framed pictures and gold camaïeu ovals on the haunches, painted lunettes on the end walls — drawn as framed colour panels of sky, cloud, robes and flesh [inferred: their number and arrangement follow the bays, not the thirty compositions one by one]. The ends: great arches 6 m wide into the salons, gilt archivolts and lined soffits. Three rows of crystal chandeliers (17 in the middle row, 9 in each side row), unlit by day. Silver: orange trees in silver tubs before the window piers, silver tables with cassolettes and guéridons with girandoles in turn before the mirror piers, silver benches with red cushions under the mirrors [inferred: the arrangement]. Parquet de Versailles throughout the hall and salons.
The salons (12.4 by 12.4 m, bays 1–3 and 21–23; the corner windows on the north and south are left out [inferred: compression — the north wing abuts the block]): three windows, two arcades of mirrors and a door opposite; War's chimneypiece of green marble under Coysevox's relief (the King on his rearing horse with baton and streaming cloak, Glory with a crown, the fallen enemies; the gilt oval, the gilt Fames with trumpets, the chained captives), four trophies on its wall and two by the arch; Peace with a glass over its chimney and a shut door toward the Queen's side; coved ceilings with the central oval painting and a painted cove on each side; a chandelier; guéridons.
The Grand Apartment runs east from the Salon of War on one axis of doors (the enfilade), its rooms reduced to 9 by 10 m and 7.8 by 10 m and without windows [inferred: compression — in the real palace they face north over the Parterre du Nord; here the north wing abuts the block]: Apollo (crimson velvet; the silver throne on a three-step dais with a carpet, the canopy's tester, dorsal and fringes, six silver candlestands, a chimneypiece with a painting); Mercury (gold brocade; the state bed with posts, tester and plumes behind the silver balustrade with its gate; a silver table; a silver chandelier); Mars (the chimneypiece between two musicians' tribunes with gilt balusters, gilt helmets along the cornice); Diana (green; the billiard table, two tiers of seats, the painting opposite the chimney; a marble floor); Venus (blue; Warin's King in a niche between red marble columns, the silver buffet; a marble floor). Each: marble dado and skirting, hangings in gilt frames, frieze and gilt cornice, white and gilt door cases with overdoor paintings, a coved ceiling with its central oval and gold camaïeu coves, lit chandeliers.
The Ambassadors' Staircase stands in the court north of the Royal Court, east of the block's extension (26.8 by 13.8 m inside): the door from the court, the vestibule under a white barrel vault with gilt bands, the arcade of three arches; the flight of 24 steps between cheeks faced in marble to the platform; the fountain against the landing's wall — the red basin on two bronze dolphins — with the bust of the King in a gilt niche above; two flights of 22 steps up the sides to the landing and the doors of Diana and Venus; marble balustrades with red rails; Rance pilasters and gilt capitals, the windows of the garden-front order on the north, four painted Parts of the World on the south wall, a victory over the doors and another over the arcade; a coved ceiling whose middle is the glass roof (panes and iron bars, a glazed lantern on the roof) [inferred: the plan's dimensions and the flights' arrangement, after the stated sequence].

## 1d. Orange trees, chairs and gardeners (L3, built: `src/styles/classical_fr/garden.js`, `src/settlements/versailles/garden.js`)

- **Sources:** the Orangerie built by Jules Hardouin-Mansart in 1684–86, replacing Le Vau's of 1663; over a thousand trees, mostly citrus, kept in boxes in winter and set out from May to October; Louis XIV had some orange trees in solid silver tubs for the state rooms [stated: Wikipedia "Versailles Orangerie"]. ## 1e. The Royal Chapel (c. 1700–1710; `src/styles/classical_fr/chapel.js`)

- **Sources:** the fifth chapel, begun by Hardouin-Mansart in 1689, stopped in the war of the League of Augsburg, resumed 1699 and finished by Robert de Cotte after Mansart's death in 1708; consecrated 1710 to Saint Louis; two storeys on the palatine model, the tribune level with a Corinthian colonnade; floor of polychrome marble with the crowned double L at the altar steps; vault paintings by Coypel (God the Father in His Glory, nave), La Fosse (the Resurrection, apse) and Jouvenet (the Pentecost, over the royal tribune); organ by Clicquot and Tribuot 1709–10 [stated: Wikipedia "Chapels of Versailles"]. Base 24 m wide and 42 m long, roof ridge 40 m, Corinthian pilasters on the façade, at least 30 statues by 16 sculptors crowning the balustrades and the pediment, lead roofwork originally gilt, an uninterrupted vault with frescoes, large windows, interior colonnade of free-standing columns, the organ case with King David above the altar [stated: chateauversailles.fr "Royal Chapel"]. Arcaded ground level for the court, the colonnaded first storey level with the royal apartments, royal pews at one end [stated: Sullivan, Bluffton "Royal Chapel"]. Great Stables and Small Stables: see §1f.

Inside, walkable: the ground hall with white-and-black marble floor, the altar on three steps with gilt frontal, communion rail, benches, the arcade of piers carrying the tribune; a flight of stairs up the east aisle to the tribune (floor at the first floor's level, 14.3 m), the colonnade of 14 Corinthian columns with balustrade, the royal tribune across the south end with a crimson cushion, the organ with five flats of pipes above the altar, one segmental vault (rise 7 m) with Coypel's painting between gilt ribs and oval camaïeux, the half-dome with La Fosse's Resurrection, three chandeliers [inferred: their number and the stair's place].

## 5. The town

About 450 houses. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): an auberge and two homes, with stone-flagged floors; a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

**The town's fabric (deepening pass; `src/styles/classical_fr/townhouse.js`, `src/settlements/versailles/layout.js`).** Louis XIV had the town laid out in 1671 on the three avenues (Paris, Saint-Cloud, Sceaux) converging on the Place d'Armes, with lots sold or given to courtiers and builders who raised their houses to a set façade [stated: Wikipedia "Versailles, Yvelines"; chateauversailles.fr]. ## 5d. The service yards of the gardens (L3, built: `src/settlements/versailles/service.js`, `layout.js` YARDS, `src/styles/classical_fr/interiors.js`)

**Why.** The arrival stands on the water parterre; no house of the town lies within 180 m of it (the town begins east of the Place d'Armes). What can be entered near the arrival is therefore what stood in the gardens: the lodgings and workshops of the people who kept them. The gardens were a permanent works site — Le Nôtre's gardeners and the fontainiers who ran the waterworks, the sculptors who made and remade the groups (Latona was remodelled 1687–89 [stated: Wikipedia "Gardens of Versailles"; this dossier §1a]), the Orangerie that wintered the orange trees in their boxes (Hardouin-Mansart, 1684–86 [stated: Wikipedia "Orangerie of the Palace of Versailles"]), and the Cent-Suisses, the King's Swiss guard (founded 1496, guarding the King's person [stated: Wikipedia "Cent-Suisses"]).

**Ours.** Two levelled terraces (3.55 m, the garden's ground at the gate) behind the hornbeam walls of the Latona walk, 34 by 44 m, each entered by the gate already in the wall at x = -104; the Le Nôtre manner of terracing the slope [inferred]. Four lodgings on each, the fronts different in height, bays, wall (brick, plaster, limestone, grey brick, cream stucco, Sienna stucco), openings (a house door, a workshop's wide opening, the carriage gate of the chair house) and roof: the head gardener's lodge (two storeys, brick; tools on the wall, onions hung from the beams); the tool house (barrows, stacked pots, ladders); the sculptors' atelier (marble blocks, a figure half carved, plaster casts, the chisels and mallets, the clay); the chair-bearers' room (two blue chairs, their poles, blue coats on pegs); the Cent-Suisses' guardroom (halberds in the rack, the drum, red-blue-white coats and helmets on pegs, a bunk, dice on the table); the fontainiers' room (the plan of the waterworks on the table, lead pipes, bronze jets and cocks, the lead pot, coils of hose); the store of the orange tubs (the green boxes with their trees, the stove, watering pots, mats); the joiner's shop (bench and vice, planks, trellis and tubs in the making, a foot lathe) [all furnishings inferred from the trades; the dossier has no inventory]. Figures stand in most of them.
