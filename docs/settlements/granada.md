## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Alhambra, the palace-fortress of the Nasrid emirs on the Sabika hill above Granada. The walls run about 740 m long and 205 m wide at the broadest, with towers [stated: Wikipedia "Alhambra"]. The Court of the Myrtles, with its pool and the Comares Tower (about 45 m), dates from Yusuf I [stated]. The Court of the Lions, with its 124 slender columns and the lion fountain, dates from Muhammad V (1362–1391) [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Lions"]. The Alcazaba with the Torre de la Vela stands at the west end [stated].
- **The setting:** the Darro valley to the north, and the Albaicín quarter climbing the hill beyond it [stated: Wikipedia "Albaicín"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1390, under Muhammad V.

## 3. Orientation

The Court of the Myrtles runs north–south toward the Comares Tower; the hill runs east–west. [stated]

## 4. The style

Red rammed earth and brick walls and towers, crenellated; plain-walled palaces round courts; slender marble columns; tiled roofs; whitewashed houses. [stated: materials; inferred: details]

### 4.1 The Court of the Lions (L3, built)

- **Galleries.** A rectangular court with galleries all round, on 124 white marble columns with slender, ringed shafts, cubic capitals and large abaci [stated: alhambradegranada.org]; the columns stand singly, in pairs and in threes, in a subtle rhythm [stated: Bluffton]. Ours: corners in threes (an L of a corner column and one along each side), pairs and singles alternating along the sides; stilted round arches; over the arches, panels of sebka (the lattice of rhombs) in relief on the stucco [stated: the plaster arches and rhomb-shaped carving, alhambradegranada.org; the panel layout inferred]; the wood-carved frieze over the arcade [stated]; the galleries roofed in tile, mitred at the corners, with a timber ceiling below [inferred]. Columns 3.2 m to the springing, the galleries 2.6 m deep [inferred].
- **The halls.** A larger arch at the middle of each long side, before the Hall of the Abencerrajes and the Hall of the Two Sisters [stated: alhambradegranada.org; Bluffton]; ours: a wider arch in the arcade and a framed door with a band of script in the hall's wall [inferred].
- **Pavilions.** One at the centre of each short side, built partly out over the court, square in plan, with a semispherical dome of wood inside [stated: alhambradegranada.org]; the arches over the pavilions are of muqarnas [stated] — ours lobed (scalloped) arches, a carved timber band, the wooden dome on corner fans, wide eaves and a tiled pyramid [inferred: roof form]. Columns in threes at the corners [inferred].
- **Water.** White marble channels start inside the pavilions and the halls and meet at the fountain in a cross, with jets at their ends [stated]. The fountain: a twelve-sided white marble basin on the backs of twelve lions spouting water, Ibn Zamrak's poem carved round its border [stated: alhambradegranada.org; Bluffton]. The second, upper basin is of the early 17th century [stated] and is left out. Ours: the basin 2.7 m across on a central drum, the lions standing outward on a low step in a ring of water, stylised (blocky bodies, a grooved mane, square muzzles) [inferred: sizes and carving], the poem as a band of strokes in relief; small round basins with jets at the channels' ends.
- **Garden.** A low garden once occupied the centre, removed later for the damp it caused [stated: alhambradegranada.org]; ours: four beds between the channels, edged with clipped myrtle, low shrubs in them [inferred: planting].

### 4.2 The Comares palace (L3, built)

- **The court.** 23–23.5 m wide and 36.6 m long, its long axis north–south [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Myrtles"]; ours 23 × 36 m. A reflecting pool about 34 m long and 7.1 m wide with myrtle hedges along both sides; two round floor fountains at its ends, each feeding a short channel that lets the water enter the pool without ripples [stated: ibid.]. Ours: the pool 7.1 m wide but 28.6 m long, to keep the settlement's arrival view (`src/planet/arrivals.js`, at z = 0 in the court) on the paving [inferred; the arrival should move to about z = 3 if the pool is lengthened to the true 34 m]; the water brimming 4 cm under the marble kerb; the fountains flush with the paving, a jet in each, a short channel to the pool; the hedges clipped, 1 m wide, in lengths of 6.6 m [inferred: lengths].
- **The porticoes.** At the north and south ends, each a wide central arch flanked by three smaller arches each side, seven in all, carved with arabesque, sebka and epigraphic stucco [stated: ibid.]. Ours: single slender columns (the Nasrid column of §4.1) with piers at the ends, the middle span 4.2 m and the others 2.4 m, stilted round arches springing at 3 m, the sebka panels over them, a carved timber frieze, a ceiling of cedar on beams, tiled roofs; behind the north portico the muqarnas-arched door to the Barca [stated: "entered through a muqarnas-decorated archway"; ours lobed]; over the south portico a gallery of seven smaller arches, as at the Alhambra today [inferred: its form]; the entrance in the south-west corner [inferred]. The muqarnas niches at the galleries' ends are not built.
- **The side ranges.** The east and west sides plain, with closed doors in stucco frames and small windows high up, the tile dado and the stucco above [inferred: the rooms behind are not built]. A passage through the east range leads to the Court of the Lions [inferred: the real way runs through the Sala de los Mocárabes].
- **The Sala de la Barca.** A wide rectangular hall north of the court, a rounded wooden vault covered in geometric motifs, alcoves at its ends behind round arches with muqarnas spandrels [stated: ibid.; the ceiling reconstructed after the fire of 1890]. Ours: 23 × 4.3 m, the vault of cedar planks (rise 1.55 m) ribbed in a grid of timber, the alcoves 2.3 m deep behind lobed arches [inferred: sizes].
- **The Comares Tower.** About 45 m high and 16 m wide, walls 2–3 m thick; the Hall of the Ambassadors inside it [stated: ibid.]. Ours: 16 × 16 m, walls 2.35 m, 45 m to the crenellations, on a plinth down the slope; solid above the hall; the small upper windows of the hall showing in the brick, and two rows of paired windows high up [inferred].
- **The Hall of the Ambassadors.** Square, 11.3 m a side, 18.2 m high [stated: Wikipedia; about 11 × 18 m: Bluffton]. Entered through two archways from the Barca [stated] — ours one deep arch through the tower's wall with a small niche (taqa) in each jamb [stated: the niches; the oratory and the stair between the archways are not built]. Three alcoves in each of three walls, with windows; the middle alcove's window double, divided by a column, the side ones single; the middle alcove of the back wall the sultan's seat [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: alcoves 2.4 and 2.0 m wide, 1.8 m deep, under round arches in stucco frames (alfiz); a dais with cushions in the sultan's alcove [inferred: the furnishing]. Zellij on the lower walls [stated], stucco above in arabesque, geometric and epigraphic motifs [stated] — ours a dado of green-glazed tiles with lapis borders and a row of yellow lozenges, bands of script, a lattice of rhombs (sebka) over the upper walls. A row of small grilled windows just below the ceiling, probably once glazed in colours [stated]; an inscription band with Surah 67 under the ceiling [stated]. The ceiling: a wooden dome of 8,017 pieces in a mainly twelve-pointed star pattern, read as the seven heavens [stated: Wikipedia]; a cedar ceiling, "beautiful Moslem carpentry" [stated: Bluffton]. Ours: four coved faces of cedar rising in three slopes to a small lantern with a muqarnas cupola; seven rows of twelve-pointed stars inlaid in light wood, gilt bosses at their centres, the hips ribbed [inferred: the form of the armature and the count of stars]. The floor of lustre tiles [stated: Wikipedia; marble once, Bluffton] — ours a cream field with lapis lozenges.

### 4.3 The halls round the Court of the Lions (L3, built)

- **The court's walls.** The court about 28.7 × 15.6 m, long axis east–west; 17 bays on the long sides and 11 on the short; the central bays of the long sides wider, before the halls [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Lions"]. The walls behind the galleries now carry real doorways (horseshoe arches in deep reveals with stucco frames, marble sills) and the tile dado; the channels run from the fountain through the doors into the halls to their own fountains [stated: channels from the halls].
- **The Hall of the Two Sisters (north).** A large square under a muqarnas dome 8 m across in an eight-sided cupola with two windows on each side; at least 5,000 prismatic pieces rising from sixteen small domes at window level to the apex; stucco above zellij; a small fountain at the centre; two small side chambers [stated: ibid.]. Ours: 8 × 8 m; the square turned to the octagon by stepped squinches with small niches; the octagonal drum with two pierced windows a side; the dome in seven tiers of niches (16, 16, 32, 32, 16, 16, 8 cells), each tier stepping in over a soffit, pendants at the cells' meeting points, a gilt boss at the crown [inferred: the tier counts]; the side chambers behind lobed arches, vaulted in cedar; the hall rising as a tower under a tiled pyramid [inferred: the roof]. The upper floor round the hall is not built.
- **The Sala de los Ajimeces and the Mirador de Lindaraja.** A wide rectangular chamber under a long vault of consecutive muqarnas domes; the Mirador projecting north with paired arched windows on three sides, blind muqarnas arches framing them, and a lantern vault of wooden lattice filled with coloured glass, the only one in the Alhambra [stated: ibid.]. Ours: three bays under small muqarnas cupolas between lobed arches; the Mirador 3.2 × 2.8 m, paired windows divided by marble colonnettes, the lantern a square of stained glass over a timber lattice set in a cedar ceiling [inferred: sizes; the lattice pattern].
- **The Hall of the Abencerrajes (south).** A central square, almost a small court, flanked by two niche-like chambers behind double arches; a three-dimensional muqarnas dome on a sixteen-sided cupola in the shape of an eight-pointed star, a window in each of the sixteen sides; a small round fountain at the centre with a channel toward the court [stated: ibid.]. Ours: 6.8 m square; the star of two squares (half 2.4 m) on a ceiling across the square's corners, its sixteen faces each with a window, the dome in seven tiers springing from the star and closing to a circle; the double arches on a Nasrid column; the side chambers vaulted in cedar [inferred: sizes].
- **The Hall of the Kings (east).** A wide rectangular space in seven units divided by muqarnas arches; three larger square units under cupolas pierced with windows, opening to the court through triple muqarnas archways; four smaller units between and at the ends; behind each unit a niche-like chamber, the three largest with rounded wooden plank vaults covered in painted leather — the middle one ten Muslim men in noble dress in discussion, the side ones court life, jousting and hunting among gardens and palaces [stated: ibid.]. Ours: the hall 4.8 × 20.4 m, the squares 4.8 m under muqarnas cupolas on squinches and drums, the small units as thick lobed arches, triple lobed arches on paired Nasrid columns to the court; the three alcoves 3.2 m deep, their vaults painted: ten seated figures in white and robes on cushions in the middle one, riders, trees and a castle in the side ones, borders in gilt and red [inferred: the figures abstracted to patches of colour]. The alcoves behind the small units are not built.

### 4.4 Construction notes [inferred]

- Interior walls are dressed with a tile dado (green field, lapis borders, a row of yellow lozenges), a band of script at the dado's top and another under the ceiling, the stucco lining between; doors and windows are real holes, their arches built across the wall's thickness.
- Muqarnas domes are tiers of niches, each tier a ring of concave cells stepping inward over a soffit, from the drum to the crown; the dome's base is filled across from the drum's polygon.
- The unbuilt rooms round the halls are closed blocks under tiled roofs, so that nothing open or floating shows from outside.
