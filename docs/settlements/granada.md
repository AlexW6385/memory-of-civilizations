## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Alhambra, the palace-fortress of the Nasrid emirs on the Sabika hill above Granada. The walls run about 740 m long and 205 m wide at the broadest, with towers [stated: Wikipedia "Alhambra"]. The Court of the Myrtles, with its pool and the Comares Tower (about 45 m), dates from Yusuf I [stated]. The Court of the Lions, with its 124 slender columns and the lion fountain, dates from Muhammad V (1362–1391) [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Lions"]. The Alcazaba with the Torre de la Vela stands at the west end [stated].
- **The setting:** the Darro valley to the north, and the Albaicín quarter climbing the hill beyond it [stated: Wikipedia "Albaicín"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1390, under Muhammad V.

## 4. The style

Red rammed earth and brick walls and towers, crenellated; plain-walled palaces round courts; slender marble columns; tiled roofs; whitewashed houses. [stated: materials; inferred: details]

### 4.1 The Court of the Lions (L3, built)

- **Galleries.** A rectangular court with galleries all round, on 124 white marble columns with slender, ringed shafts, cubic capitals and large abaci [stated: alhambradegranada.org]; the columns stand singly, in pairs and in threes, in a subtle rhythm [stated: Bluffton]. Ours: corners in threes (an L of a corner column and one along each side), pairs and singles alternating along the sides; stilted round arches; over the arches, panels of sebka (the lattice of rhombs) in relief on the stucco [stated: the plaster arches and rhomb-shaped carving, alhambradegranada.org; the panel layout inferred]; the wood-carved frieze over the arcade [stated]; the galleries roofed in tile, mitred at the corners, with a timber ceiling below [inferred]. Columns 3.2 m to the springing, the galleries 2.6 m deep [inferred].
- **The halls.** A larger arch at the middle of each long side, before the Hall of the Abencerrajes and the Hall of the Two Sisters [stated: alhambradegranada.org; Bluffton]; ours: a wider arch in the arcade and a framed door with a band of script in the hall's wall [inferred].
- **Pavilions.** One at the centre of each short side, built partly out over the court, square in plan, with a semispherical dome of wood inside [stated: alhambradegranada.org]; the arches over the pavilions are of muqarnas [stated] — ours lobed (scalloped) arches, a carved timber band, the wooden dome on corner fans, wide eaves and a tiled pyramid [inferred: roof form]. Columns in threes at the corners [inferred].
- **Water.** White marble channels start inside the pavilions and the halls and meet at the fountain in a cross, with jets at their ends [stated]. The fountain: a twelve-sided white marble basin on the backs of twelve lions spouting water, Ibn Zamrak's poem carved round its border [stated: alhambradegranada.org; Bluffton]. The second, upper basin is of the early 17th century [stated] and is left out. Ours: the basin 2.7 m across on a central drum, the lions standing outward on a low step in a ring of water, stylised (blocky bodies, a grooved mane, square muzzles) [inferred: sizes and carving], the poem as a band of strokes in relief; small round basins with jets at the channels' ends.
- **Garden.** A low garden once occupied the centre, removed later for the damp it caused [stated: alhambradegranada.org]; ours: four beds between the channels, edged with clipped myrtle, low shrubs in them [inferred: planting].

### 4.2 The Comares palace (L3, built)

- **The court.** 23–23.5 m wide and 36.6 m long, its long axis north–south [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Myrtles"]; ours 23 × 36 m. A reflecting pool about 34 m long and 7.1 m wide with myrtle hedges along both sides; two round floor fountains at its ends, each feeding a short channel that lets the water enter the pool without ripples [stated: ibid.]. Ours: the pool 7.1 m wide and 31.3 m long (it was 28.6 m), from just inside the north fountain to 2 m short of the court's south edge, leaving the strip z = 2…4 at the south end clear for the settlement's arrival view (`src/planet/arrivals.js`, moved to about z = 3 in the court) [inferred; the court being 36 m to the true 36.6, the true 34 m would leave no room for the arrival and the south fountain]; the fountains' basins 0.92 m across, 0.55 m from the kerb; their jets now of broken white water (`water_jet`), like the jets of the Lions' fountain and of the channels' basins; the water brimming 4 cm under the marble kerb; the fountains flush with the paving, a jet in each, a short channel to the pool; the hedges clipped, 1 m wide, in lengths of 6.6 m [inferred: lengths].
- **The porticoes.** At the north and south ends, each a wide central arch flanked by three smaller arches each side, seven in all, carved with arabesque, sebka and epigraphic stucco [stated: ibid.]. Ours: single slender columns (the Nasrid column of §4.1) with piers at the ends, the middle span 4.2 m and the others 2.4 m, stilted round arches springing at 3 m, the sebka panels over them, a carved timber frieze, a ceiling of cedar on beams, tiled roofs; behind the north portico the muqarnas-arched door to the Barca [stated: "entered through a muqarnas-decorated archway"; ours lobed]; over the south portico a gallery of seven smaller arches, as at the Alhambra today [inferred: its form]; the entrance in the south-west corner [inferred]. The muqarnas niches at the galleries' ends are not built.
- **The side ranges.** The east and west sides plain, with closed doors in stucco frames and small windows high up, the tile dado and the stucco above [inferred: the rooms behind are not built]. A passage through the east range leads to the Court of the Lions [inferred: the real way runs through the Sala de los Mocárabes].
- **The Sala de la Barca.** A wide rectangular hall north of the court, a rounded wooden vault covered in geometric motifs, alcoves at its ends behind round arches with muqarnas spandrels [stated: ibid.; the ceiling reconstructed after the fire of 1890]. Ours: 23 × 4.3 m, the vault of cedar planks (rise 1.55 m) ribbed in a grid of timber, the alcoves 2.3 m deep behind lobed arches [inferred: sizes].
- **The Comares Tower.** About 45 m high and 16 m wide, walls 2–3 m thick; the Hall of the Ambassadors inside it [stated: ibid.]. Ours: 16 × 16 m, walls 2.35 m, 45 m to the crenellations, on a plinth down the slope; solid above the hall; the small upper windows of the hall showing in the brick, and two rows of paired windows high up [inferred].
- **The Hall of the Ambassadors.** Square, 11.3 m a side, 18.2 m high [stated: Wikipedia; about 11 × 18 m: Bluffton]. Entered through two archways from the Barca [stated] — ours one deep arch through the tower's wall with a small niche (taqa) in each jamb [stated: the niches; the oratory and the stair between the archways are not built]. Three alcoves in each of three walls, with windows; the middle alcove's window double, divided by a column, the side ones single; the middle alcove of the back wall the sultan's seat [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: alcoves 2.4 and 2.0 m wide, 1.8 m deep, under round arches in stucco frames (alfiz); a dais with cushions in the sultan's alcove [inferred: the furnishing]. Zellij on the lower walls [stated], stucco above in arabesque, geometric and epigraphic motifs [stated] — ours a dado of green-glazed tiles with lapis borders and a row of yellow lozenges, bands of script, a lattice of rhombs (sebka) over the upper walls. A row of small grilled windows just below the ceiling, probably once glazed in colours [stated]; an inscription band with Surah 67 under the ceiling [stated]. The ceiling: a wooden dome of 8,017 pieces in a mainly twelve-pointed star pattern, read as the seven heavens [stated: Wikipedia]; a cedar ceiling, "beautiful Moslem carpentry" [stated: Bluffton]. Ours: four coved faces of cedar rising in three slopes to a small lantern with a muqarnas cupola; seven rows of twelve-pointed stars inlaid in light wood, gilt bosses at their centres, the hips ribbed [inferred: the form of the armature and the count of stars]. The floor of lustre tiles [stated: Wikipedia; marble once, Bluffton] — ours a cream field with lapis lozenges.

### 4.3 The halls round the Court of the Lions (L3, built)

- **The court's walls.** The court about 28.7 × 15.6 m, long axis east–west; 17 bays on the long sides and 11 on the short; the central bays of the long sides wider, before the halls [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Lions"]. The walls behind the galleries now carry real doorways (horseshoe arches in deep reveals with stucco frames, marble sills) and the tile dado; the channels run from the fountain through the doors into the halls to their own fountains [stated: channels from the halls].
- **The Hall of the Two Sisters (north).** A large square under a muqarnas dome 8 m across in an eight-sided cupola with two windows on each side; at least 5,000 prismatic pieces rising from sixteen small domes at window level to the apex; stucco above zellij; a small fountain at the centre; two small side chambers [stated: ibid.]. Ours: 8 × 8 m; the square turned to the octagon by stepped squinches with small niches; the octagonal drum with two pierced windows a side; the dome in seven tiers of niches (16, 16, 32, 32, 16, 16, 8 cells), each tier stepping in over a soffit, pendants at the cells' meeting points, a gilt boss at the crown [inferred: the tier counts]; the side chambers behind lobed arches, vaulted in cedar; the hall rising as a tower under a tiled pyramid [inferred: the roof]. The upper floor round the hall is not built.
- **The Sala de los Ajimeces and the Mirador de Lindaraja.** A wide rectangular chamber under a long vault of consecutive muqarnas domes; the Mirador projecting north with paired arched windows on three sides, blind muqarnas arches framing them, and a lantern vault of wooden lattice filled with coloured glass, the only one in the Alhambra [stated: ibid.]. Ours: three bays under small muqarnas cupolas between lobed arches; the Mirador 3.2 × 2.8 m, paired windows divided by marble colonnettes, the lantern a square of stained glass over a timber lattice set in a cedar ceiling [inferred: sizes; the lattice pattern].
- **The Hall of the Abencerrajes (south).** A central square, almost a small court, flanked by two niche-like chambers behind double arches; a three-dimensional muqarnas dome on a sixteen-sided cupola in the shape of an eight-pointed star, a window in each of the sixteen sides; a small round fountain at the centre with a channel toward the court [stated: ibid.]. Ours: 6.8 m square; the star of two squares (half 2.4 m) on a ceiling across the square's corners, its sixteen faces each with a window, the dome in seven tiers springing from the star and closing to a circle; the double arches on a Nasrid column; the side chambers vaulted in cedar [inferred: sizes].
- **The Hall of the Kings (east).** A wide rectangular space in seven units divided by muqarnas arches; three larger square units under cupolas pierced with windows, opening to the court through triple muqarnas archways; four smaller units between and at the ends; behind each unit a niche-like chamber, the three largest with rounded wooden plank vaults covered in painted leather — the middle one ten Muslim men in noble dress in discussion, the side ones court life, jousting and hunting among gardens and palaces [stated: ibid.]. Ours: the hall 4.8 × 20.4 m, the squares 4.8 m under muqarnas cupolas on squinches and drums, the small units as thick lobed arches, triple lobed arches on paired Nasrid columns to the court; the three alcoves 3.2 m deep, their vaults painted: ten seated figures in white and robes on cushions in the middle one, riders, trees and a castle in the side ones, borders in gilt and red [inferred: the figures abstracted to patches of colour]. The alcoves behind the small units are not built.

### 4.4 Construction notes [inferred]

- Interior walls are dressed with a tile dado (green field, lapis borders, a row of yellow lozenges), a band of script at the dado's top and another under the ceiling, the stucco lining between; doors and windows are real holes, their arches built across the wall's thickness.
- Muqarnas domes are tiers of niches, each tier a ring of concave cells stepping inward over a soffit, from the drum to the crown; the dome's base is filled across from the drum's polygon.
- The unbuilt rooms round the halls are closed blocks under tiled roofs, so that nothing open or floating shows from outside.


### 4.5 The Partal (L3, built)

- **The real one.** Built in the early 14th century by Muhammad III (1302–1309), the oldest surviving palace of the Alhambra; the portico on the palace's north side, originally on brick pillars (the slender marble columns are of the 20th century), its arched façade carved in stucco; the large reflecting pool before it; behind the portico a mirador chamber projecting outward and northward from the walls with windows on three sides; the Torre de las Damas, whose larger top chamber had a wooden cupola ceiling (now in Berlin) [stated: Wikipedia "Partal Palace"]. "The portico, with its five arches, overlooks a large pool in the centre of the garden"; the portico built into the premises wall, as with Comares; the main room inside the tower [stated: Patronato de la Alhambra]. The small oratory east of the tower (4.16 × 3 m, Isma'il I and Yusuf I) and the four houses of the Partal without courtyards west of it [stated: ibid.] are not built.
- **Ours.** The tower 10 m square standing in the line of the north wall (the run is cut round it and the mirador), red outside, 11.6 m to a tiled hipped roof [inferred: the roof form, the size]; the room 7.2 m square inside, dressed with the tile dado, stucco and bands of script, a floor of lustre tiles, paired windows on a marble colonnette east and west, a wooden star cupola (the Hall of the Ambassadors' kind, `starCeiling`) [inferred: its pattern]; the mirador 5.2 × 3.4 m beyond the wall on its own foot down the slope, paired windows on its three sides, a plank vault of cedar, a little tiled roof. The portico 18 m wide and 3.4 deep before the tower's south face: five stilted round arches on brick pillars, the middle one wider, sebka over the arches, a cedar ceiling on beams, a lean-to roof of tiles; the pool 14 × 11 m in a marble kerb, brimming, three small jets where the water enters at its south end, in a paving of brick [inferred: the pool's size, the jets, the paving].

### 4.6 The Rauda (L3, built)

- **The real one.** The royal cemetery by the Great Mosque, separated by the Calle Real Baja from the Partal palaces; founded by Isma'il I and used by the later sultans (Muhammad IV, Yusuf I, Yusuf III certainly, Muhammad V possibly). The building rectangular; a square space at its centre, probably a qubba open at the sides and covered with a dome; access through a slightly pointed, scalloped horseshoe arch to a small vestibule; three tombs below ground, two in the central room and one in each side room; an enclosed garden to the south-east with about seventy trapezoid tombs [stated: Patronato, "The Rauda at the Alhambra"]. A royal headstone of the Rawda: a block of marble slightly over 170 cm long, carved and polished, with epigraphy [stated: Patronato, "Burial stone from the Rawda"].
- **Ours.** South of the Palace of the Lions, 15 × 10.2 m, long east–west [inferred: the real one runs south-east to north-west]: the door north under a horseshoe arch, the vestibule under a plank vault, the qubba 6 m square through a lobed arch, its walls rising above the rest to small windows and a star cupola of cedar under a tiled pyramid; the side rooms through double arches on marble columns, under plank vaults; the dado, stucco and script throughout; prismatic marble tombstones (maqabriyya) 1.7 m long over the graves, their sloping faces carved with script; the garden behind a low coped wall with a gate on its west side, twelve graves under smaller stones, beds of myrtle [inferred: the stones' prismatic form (the usual one in al-Andalus), the garden's planting; seventy graves thinned to twelve].

### 4.7 The Alcazaba (L2, built)

- The Torre de la Vela with four levels of chambers inside, each larger than the one below; the bell only since the Christian conquest (not built). The Torre del Homenaje, the keep, 26 m tall on a base of 12.12 × 10.46 m, at the north-east. The military quarter: a long east–west street, ten to seventeen houses of different sizes round patios on its north side, storehouses or quarters on a more regular plan on its south; the walls of rammed earth [stated: Wikipedia "Alcazaba of Granada"]. Ours: the keep at the Alcazaba's north-east corner at its true size, the Vela at the west, both with paired windows in deep frames high up; three houses either side of the quarter's street (the vernacular Moorish house) [inferred: the houses' form; the hammam, cistern and silos are not built].

### 4.8 Tilework, shields and the Myrtles' niches (L3, built)

- **The dados as tile mosaic.** In zellij "glazed squares, typically 10 cm per side, were cut by hand with a small adze-like hammer into a variety of pre-established shapes" and fitted together; geometric motifs predominated, "six-pointed and twelve-pointed star compositions" with eight-pointed stars between them; Nasrid and Marinid work used "more colours", among them yellow, blues and a dark brown manganese, commonly on a white ground; the dadoes of the Mirador de Lindaraja and the Torre de la Cautiva among the finest surviving [stated: Wikipedia "Zellij"]. Ours: every dado of the palaces (the `dress` of a wall face) now a white glazed ground between its lapis borders, set with rows of eight-pointed stars in cobalt, green, honey yellow and manganese black, a small four-pointed cross of green or blue at each meeting of four stars, kept clear of every door and window [inferred: one pattern for all the rooms, the size of the stars, about 0.37 m].
- **The Nasrid shield.** "A rectangular device with its lower side ending in an angle, surrounded by a simple frame", crossed by "a transverse band, which usually contains a text in Arabic calligraphy", the motto of the sultanate, wa-la ghalib illa-llah ("there is no victor but God"); included very often in the buildings the sovereign paid for, among them the Court of the Myrtles (one example "on a blue ground") and painted in the stucco of Yusuf I [stated: UCM, Base de datos de iconografía medieval, "Escudo nazarí de la banda"]. Ours: the shield in relief in the stucco above the dado of the palaces' walls, one every 3.6 m or so where the wall is clear: the field blue, the band across it from the upper left gilt with the motto in dark strokes, a plain frame of warm stucco [inferred: the colours of the field and band, the spacing].
- **The niches at the ends of the Myrtles' galleries.** The porticoes' galleries are "flanked at their east and west ends by decorative niches covered with muqarnas vaulting" [stated: Wikipedia "Court of the Myrtles"]. Ours: in each end wall of both galleries a niche 3 m wide and 1.5 m deep, one step up, its floor of marble, the dado and stucco carried round inside, under a half dome of muqarnas in five tiers (12, 12, 8, 8, 4 cells) with pendants, the spandrel over it flat in the wall's face, an alfiz of mouldings round the mouth [inferred: the sizes and the tiers].

### 5.1 The Albaicín's houses and cisterns (L3, built)

- **Houses round a patio.** The Casa de Zafra and Dar al-Horra "include large rectangular courtyards oriented in a north–south direction"; "the main rooms of the houses were located behind porticos on the north and south sides of the courtyard"; "traditionally, the northern rooms were larger, allowing them to take advantage of the northern winds and the midday sun" [stated: Wikipedia "Albaicín"]. The Casa de Zafra, 14th century: a central courtyard round which the rooms are arranged, a small pool in it, two floors and a viewing tower, a horseshoe arch at the entrance, plaster decoration [stated: Love Granada, "House of Zafra"]. A Nasrid patio in the Casa del Chapiz: "a small central pond and a hallway or zaguán entrance", "wooden galleries on all four sides providing access to all the rooms on the upper floor" [stated: EEA-CSIC, Casa del Chapiz leaflet; the house is Morisco, on Nasrid remains]. Ours (`src/styles/nasrid/albaicin.js`, `patio_house`): about two in three of the larger lots across the Darro (258 houses) built as houses round a patio in place of the plain town house: ranges of rooms on four sides, two storeys, whitewashed, the roofs of tile sloping in from a parapet to eaves over the patio; on the patio's north side a portico of two marble columns under a timber beam and ceiling, a timber gallery with a balustrade above it under the eaves; the hall's door and windows behind the portico; a small pool in a marble kerb in the brick paving; the way in from the lane through a passage in the front range under a horseshoe arch in an alfiz, the door leaf folded back inside; a few small windows high in the lane fronts; on some houses a mirador on the north range with windows on four sides under a tiled pyramid [inferred: the sizes, the portico on the north side only, the straight passage (the real zaguán often bent), the share of miradors].
- **The cisterns.** "A network of numerous cisterns, dating from as far back as the 11th century", at least twenty still extant; the Aljibe de Trillo, 50 m³, "a good example of a cistern from the Nasrid period"; the Aljibe de San Miguel Bajo part of a former mosque [stated: Wikipedia "Albaicín"]. Ours (`aljibe`): fourteen cisterns in the open places the lots leave in the lanes, one in about every 90 m: a little house of brick 3 × 2.6 m over the vault, a tiled gable roof, on the front a horseshoe arch in an alfiz opening on the dark water, a stone curb across its mouth; women filling jars at each, at some a water-carrier with his donkey [inferred: the head's form, the places, the people].

## 10. The Generalife (refinements)

- **Planting of the Acequia's beds.** The beds were sunk below the walks; shallow, suggesting low plants such as myrtle, with deeper pits for larger plants like orange trees [stated: Wikipedia "Generalife"]; today myrtle, orange trees, cypresses and roses, the planting having changed with taste [stated: alhambradegranada.org]. Ours: the bed edges as continuous clipped myrtle hedges (0.6 m wide, 0.62 m high, rounded tops) broken at mid-length, rounded rose bushes with red and white flowers within, orange trees down the middle; the Sultana's beds edged all round with hedges, low shrubs inside [inferred: layout and sizes]. No cypresses (their date there is unknown) [inferred].
- **Water jets.** The lines of jets along the channel "are not original and were installed in the 19th century" [stated: Wikipedia "Generalife"], so ours has none along the channel: only low jets in the fountain at the middle crossing, the two end basins, the Sultana's pool and the landings of the water stairway [inferred]. Each is now a short column of water breaking into a crown of threads falling back into its basin, from a small marble spout [inferred: form].
- **Stucco of the porticoes.** The north portico's arches carry carved stucco with sebka and bands of cursive Arabic inscriptions [stated: Wikipedia "Generalife"]. Ours: a frieze of script in relief between moulded fillets over the sebka of the north and south porticoes, on both faces, kept under the gallery ceilings [inferred: its height and extent on the south portico].
- **People (§10.7).** A private retreat and summer palace of the Nasrid rulers and their family, away from official business; much of the grounds orchards, horse pasture and kitchen gardens [stated: Wikipedia "Generalife"]. Turbans of any colour were back in favour; red and brown predominated in clothing [stated: balagan.info, a secondary hobby source]. Ours (`life.js`): a gardener squatting in an Acequia bed, two men talking by the channel, two men seated in the north gallery with a servant carrying a jar, a woman at a window of the west mirador, two veiled women in the Sultana's garden, a man at prayer in the oratory, two horses with grooms in the Dismount court, a man hoeing and another weeding in the huerta, a woman with a basket [inferred: numbers, places and dress].

## 11. The Darro and the Carrera del Darro (built)

- **The real one.** The Darro runs through Granada between the Alhambra's hill (south) and the Albaicín (north); the hammam El Bañuelo stands "in the Albaicín quarter, on the banks of the Darro" [stated: Wikipedia "El Bañuelo"]. The Alcaicería's district was tied to the Darro by the "New Bridge" leading to the Funduq al-Jadida, built under Yusuf I (1333–1354) [stated: Wikipedia "Alcaicería of Granada"].
- **Ours** (`layout.js` DARRO, `medina.js` `darro`, `darro_bridge`): the river straight along z = −110, 7 m of water about 1 m deep between two quay walls of rubble with a coping and a 0.85 m parapet on the street side; a street (the Carrera del Darro) level at −2.0 on both banks, the ground rising from it to the Albaicín and to the Alhambra's wooded slope; the water walkable-blocking (`ground.water`) with the stain line (`climate.waterline`) at its level; two humpbacked single-arch stone bridges (a segmental arch of 7 m, a ring of voussoirs, flagged deck rising 1.1 m, parapets) at x −44 (the Puente del Cadí's place) and x 118 [inferred: straight course and sizes; the real river meanders and was later vaulted over; the number and places of the bridges]. Poplars and planting kept off the quays.

## 12. The medina's buildings (built)

- **El Bañuelo (`hammam`).** 11th–12th century bathhouse; rooms in sequence: entrance room on a square basin, changing room, cold room (bayt al-barid), warm room (bayt al-wastani, the largest), hot room (bayt al-sakhun), the service area lower with furnace and boiler, heat and smoke running under the hot and warm floors and out by chimneys; cold and hot rooms rectangular under barrel vaults with a column between two horseshoe arches at each end; the warm room a central vault flanked by three smaller; star-shaped and octagonal skylights in the vaults for light and to let steam out; marble columns with capitals reused from Roman and caliphal buildings [stated: Wikipedia "El Bañuelo"]. Ours, 19 × 13 m on the Darro's north bank, the door south on the street: vestibule with stone benches and a basin; cold room with a marble-kerbed pool and two arcades of four columns under horseshoe arches; warm and hot rooms with marble platforms; each room under a plastered barrel vault with star skylights (`daylight`) and a low tiled hip with a skylight drum; tile dado inside; the furnace of brick at the north-east corner with a 7 m stack and a woodstack [inferred: sizes; the warm room reduced to a barrel vault like the others, the changing room and latrines left out].
- **The mosques (`mosque`).** The Nasrid minarets of the Albaicín are of brick with panels of sebka and a lantern under a tiled spire [inferred from the surviving minaret now in San José; not fetched]. Ours: the qibla to the south; a sahn of paving round a marble pool with orange trees, a riwaq on east and west under lean-to roofs, the gate in the north wall under a horseshoe arch in an alfiz; the hall of aisles running toward the qibla wall (5 aisles in the Albaicín's mosque, 7 in the Great Mosque at the town) with arcades of horseshoe arches on piers, each aisle under its own gabled tile roof over a plank ceiling, matting on the floor; the mihrab a niche behind the qibla wall under a half dome, tiled dado, its own little roof; the minaret at the north-west corner, 14 m (18 m for the Great Mosque): brick shaft with arched slits, two sebka panels on each face, cornice, lantern with horseshoe windows, a green-glazed spire and three gilt balls (yamur) [inferred: everything but the existence of the types]. The Albaicín's mosque at (−12…9, −198…−168) on a levelled terrace; the Great Mosque at (−38…−4, 114…152).
- **The Alcaicería (`alcaiceria`).** Named from al-qaysariyya; the former main bazaar, Nasrid (13th–15th c.), in the 11th century area of the Great Mosque, which it lay south-west of; up to 152 shops on a 1787 plan, in several perpendicular streets, some 4,600 m²; silk and other valuable goods taxed at a higher rate; owned by the Nasrid state, locked and guarded at night [stated: Wikipedia "Alcaicería of Granada"]. Ours: a walled block 56 × 30 m at (−98…−42, 122…152) west of the Great Mosque, four lanes 2.2 m wide along x and two edge and two cross lanes along z, flagged; booths 2.5 m wide and 2.4 deep in rows (144 of them) in a row on each end wall and three double blocks between; four gates of horseshoe arches with door leaves folded back; each booth with a counter, a raised shutter as awning, a valance of cloth and goods by trade (silk and rugs, pottery, brass, spices, leather) on shelves; pitched roofs of tile over each block, lanterns at the crossings [inferred: the plan, the trades, the booth size (the survivals are Neo-Moorish)]. The Corral del Carbón and the other funduqs are not built.

## 13. Life: movers, birds, smoke (built)

- Walkers (`movers.js`): merchants in turbans, cloaked men, townsmen in tunics and caps, veiled women in long robes, water-carriers with jars, porters with baskets; the lanes of the Albaicín and the south town along the grid's gaps, the three lanes and cross lanes of the medina, the Carrera del Darro with its bridges, the souk's lanes (busy by day) and the approaches to its gates [inferred: dress from the general Nasrid-period costume; no source fetched].
- Birds (`fauna.js`): swifts and martins round the towers and low over the river, jackdaws over the Albaicín and the town, sparrows in the courts, lanes and souk.
- Smoke (`smoke.js`): kitchens at the three meals from about three in ten houses, the hammam's stack and its steam by day.
