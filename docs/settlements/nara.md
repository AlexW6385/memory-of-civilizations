## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Tōdai-ji's Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden). The first hall, finished for the eye-opening of the Great Buddha in 752, was about 86 m wide; the present rebuilding of 1709 is 57 m wide and about 49 m high [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"].
- **Pagodas:** two seven-storey pagodas, about 100 m tall (estimates vary), stood east and west in their own corridored courts [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"].
- **Gates:** the Great South Gate (Nandaimon) and the Middle Gate (Chūmon) stand on the axis, with corridors (kairō) round the hall's court [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"].
- **The setting:** Wakakusa hill, its grassy slope, rises to the east. Tōdai-ji stood at the eastern edge of the capital, Heijō-kyō [stated: Wikipedia "Mount Wakakusa"; "Heijō-kyō"].
- **Moment shown:** 752, the eye-opening of the Great Buddha.

## 4. The style

Tang-style timber halls with vermilion columns, white plaster and grey tiles on stone platforms (a palette on the shared vocabulary); pagodas; timber houses with bark or thatch roofs. [stated: colours; inferred: details]

**The Great Buddha (L3, built).** Rushana (Vairocana) in bronze: 14.98 m seated by the temple's figure, the original about 16 m; the face 5.33 m, the eyes 1.02 m, the nose 0.5 m, the ears 2.54 m; 960 curls on the head; cast in eight pours over three years, the head and neck together; the eye-opening in 752 by Bodhisena before Emperor Shōmu; the throne's lotus petals carry 8th-century incised images; the present head (Edo) and hands (Momoyama) are later [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"]. Ours: 15.8 m from the seat to the top of the head, bronze (the gilding came after the eye-opening [inferred]), on a lotus throne 2.6 m high and 18 m across with two rows of 24 upturned ribbed petals; legs crossed with the soles up, the robe over both shoulders open in a V, folds over the lap and the belly; the right hand raised palm out, the left on the knee palm up [inferred: the mudras of the statue as it stands]; the curls in rows over the head and the protuberance, the urna, brows, lowered lids, the nose and mouth, the long ears, three folds on the neck [inferred: proportions from the stated sizes]. The hall's lower walls were raised from 15 to 21 m so that the figure stands under its ceiling; the hall reaches about 49 m to the ridge, as the 8th-century hall's stated ~50 m [stated: University of Idaho "Todai-ji"].

**The octagonal lantern (L3, built).** Gilt bronze, 4.62 m, presumed cast in 752, before the hall; from the bottom a single base, a double lotus pedestal, the octagonal shaft, the middle stand, the eight-sided fire box, the octagonal roof and the flaming jewel; each side a panel about 125 × 50 cm; lions on the four faces toward the compass, musician bodhisattvas on the diagonals — cymbals (NE), mouth organ (SE), transverse flute (SW), end-blown flute (NW) [stated: tabi-mag "Tōdai-ji octagonal lantern"; World History Encyclopedia]. Ours: as stated, the panels of openwork lattice with the figures in relief on clouds, a lamp dish and flame inside, the roof's corners turned up with curling ribs; on the axis 19 m before the hall's stairs [inferred: position].

**The pagodas (L3, built).** Two seven-storey pagodas, east and west, in walled courts with four gates, about 100 m tall by one estimate (70 m by another) [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"; Ancient Pages]; the excavated (Kamakura) east pagoda: three bays a side, the middle bay about 6 m and the side bays about 5.4 m, on a foundation about 27 m square, stairs on the sides [stated: Ancient Pages]. Ours (replacing the octagonal Song pagodas of before, which were wrong for Japan): square, 16.8 m at the first storey narrowing 1.15 m a storey; vermilion posts and head beams, white plaster between, a studded door in the middle bay of every face, barred windows in the side bays of the first storey; bracket sets of three steps on every post and diagonally at the corners; deep hipped eaves of grey tile; the spire of a dew basin, inverted bowl, lotus, nine rings, water flame, dragon wheel and jewel, 16 m; on a stone platform 24 m square with steps on four sides; about 75 m in all [inferred: proportions].

## 5. The town

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): a raised board floor with the sunken hearth and its kettle on a hook, straw mats, a low table, a lacquered chest, a hanging shelf of pots; a step up to the door, the leaf open inside, a ceiling of boards on beams [inferred: the period's village interiors].

**House detail (vernacular `house_japan`).** As at Uji: the visible frame of posts, sill and head beams, lattice windows and gable vent, deep eaves with boards beneath, thatch ridges with crossed poles, some board roofs held by poles and stones [stated: JAANUS, "machiya": shingles weighted with stones or thatch; inferred: the details].
