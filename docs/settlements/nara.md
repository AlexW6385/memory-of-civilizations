# Nara (Tōdai-ji), 752 — dossier

Settlement id: `nara` · Style: `nara` (the qing/ming vocabulary with a Nara palette) and `vernacular` (houses) · Region: Japan (Yamato, the Nara basin) · Code: `src/settlements/nara/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (Tōdai-ji: the Great Buddha Hall in its corridored court, the Middle Gate, the Great South Gate, the twin seven-storey pagodas in their courts, the lecture hall and the monks' quarters; the grassy slope of Wakakusa and the wooded hills; the capital's houses to the west; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 built: the Great Buddha, the octagonal lantern, the square pagodas (§4). L3 built (this pass): the attendant bodhisattvas, the guardian kings of the Great South Gate, deer, the steps up to the gates (§4, §6, §8). L3 built (third pass): the eye-opening ceremony in the court, the offerings on the altar, damp weathering (§10). L3 recorded for later: the hall's true bracketing (the halls are still the shared qing vocabulary with a Nara palette), the eye-opening cord and the scaffold at the Buddha's face, Kōfuku-ji and Kasuga shrine nearby, the Heijō palace and Suzaku avenue.

Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning, written next to it.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Tōdai-ji's Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden). The first hall, finished for the eye-opening of the Great Buddha in 752, was about 86 m wide; the present rebuilding of 1709 is 57 m wide and about 49 m high [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"].
- **Pagodas:** two seven-storey pagodas, about 100 m tall (estimates vary), stood east and west in their own corridored courts [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"].
- **Gates:** the Great South Gate (Nandaimon) and the Middle Gate (Chūmon) stand on the axis, with corridors (kairō) round the hall's court [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"].
- **The setting:** Wakakusa hill, its grassy slope, rises to the east. Tōdai-ji stood at the eastern edge of the capital, Heijō-kyō [stated: Wikipedia "Mount Wakakusa"; "Heijō-kyō"].
- **Moment shown:** 752, the eye-opening of the Great Buddha.

## 3. Orientation

The temple faces south. [stated]

## 4. The style

Tang-style timber halls with vermilion columns, white plaster and grey tiles on stone platforms (a palette on the shared vocabulary); pagodas; timber houses with bark or thatch roofs. [stated: colours; inferred: details]

**The Great Buddha (L3, built).** Rushana (Vairocana) in bronze: 14.98 m seated by the temple's figure, the original about 16 m; the face 5.33 m, the eyes 1.02 m, the nose 0.5 m, the ears 2.54 m; 960 curls on the head; cast in eight pours over three years, the head and neck together; the eye-opening in 752 by Bodhisena before Emperor Shōmu; the throne's lotus petals carry 8th-century incised images; the present head (Edo) and hands (Momoyama) are later [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"]. Ours: 15.8 m from the seat to the top of the head, bronze (the gilding came after the eye-opening [inferred]), on a lotus throne 2.6 m high and 18 m across with two rows of 24 upturned ribbed petals; legs crossed with the soles up, the robe over both shoulders open in a V, folds over the lap and the belly; the right hand raised palm out, the left on the knee palm up [inferred: the mudras of the statue as it stands]; the curls in rows over the head and the protuberance, the urna, brows, lowered lids, the nose and mouth, the long ears, three folds on the neck [inferred: proportions from the stated sizes]. The hall's lower walls were raised from 15 to 21 m so that the figure stands under its ceiling; the hall reaches about 49 m to the ridge, as the 8th-century hall's stated ~50 m [stated: University of Idaho "Todai-ji"].

**The octagonal lantern (L3, built).** Gilt bronze, 4.62 m, presumed cast in 752, before the hall; from the bottom a single base, a double lotus pedestal, the octagonal shaft, the middle stand, the eight-sided fire box, the octagonal roof and the flaming jewel; each side a panel about 125 × 50 cm; lions on the four faces toward the compass, musician bodhisattvas on the diagonals — cymbals (NE), mouth organ (SE), transverse flute (SW), end-blown flute (NW) [stated: tabi-mag "Tōdai-ji octagonal lantern"; World History Encyclopedia]. Ours: as stated, the panels of openwork lattice with the figures in relief on clouds, a lamp dish and flame inside, the roof's corners turned up with curling ribs; on the axis 19 m before the hall's stairs [inferred: position].

**The pagodas (L3, built).** Two seven-storey pagodas, east and west, in walled courts with four gates, about 100 m tall by one estimate (70 m by another) [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"; Ancient Pages]; the excavated (Kamakura) east pagoda: three bays a side, the middle bay about 6 m and the side bays about 5.4 m, on a foundation about 27 m square, stairs on the sides [stated: Ancient Pages]. Ours (replacing the octagonal Song pagodas of before, which were wrong for Japan): square, 16.8 m at the first storey narrowing 1.15 m a storey; vermilion posts and head beams, white plaster between, a studded door in the middle bay of every face, barred windows in the side bays of the first storey; bracket sets of three steps on every post and diagonally at the corners; deep hipped eaves of grey tile; the spire of a dew basin, inverted bowl, lotus, nine rings, water flame, dragon wheel and jewel, 16 m; on a stone platform 24 m square with steps on four sides; about 75 m in all [inferred: proportions].

**The attendant bodhisattvas (L3, built).** Two large gilded bodhisattvas flank the Buddha [stated: World History Encyclopedia "Tōdai-ji"]: Kokūzō (Ākāśagarbha), "the bodhisattva of wisdom and memory", and Kannon [stated: Muza-chan]; "Kokūzō on the left and Nyoirin Kannon on the right" as one faces them [stated: FUN! JAPAN]; the Kokūzō now in the hall dates to 1709 (the present pair are Edo works) [stated: World History Encyclopedia]. The 752 attendants are lost, so ours follow the present pair in kind and arrangement [inferred]: each seated in the lotus posture, 5.2 m from the seat to the top of the knot, on a lotus throne 2.2 m high (an octagonal plinth of two steps in red lacquer edged with gilt, a drum, a ring of sixteen downturned petals, a lotus of three rows of sixteen upturned petals with gilt ribs, the seat), before a boat-shaped halo 6.6 m high with a raised rim, flame tongues round its upper edge, the round head halo of rings and rays and the body halo in two rings; gilt, the hair dark; a crown of five openwork plaques with jewels on a band, rosettes at the temples and ribbons to the shoulders; the hair in a high knot bound in three rings, strands over the shoulders; long ears, the urna, lowered lids; two strands of beaded necklace with pendants and a jewel; armlets and bracelets; a sash across the bare chest, the skirt's waistband and its folds over the crossed legs, the soles turned up; the scarf over the shoulders and arms falling in loops onto the throne [inferred: the standard dress of bodhisattva images]. Kokūzō (west) raises a sword upright in the right hand and holds the wish-fulfilling jewel on the left palm; Nyoirin Kannon (east) holds the jewel before the chest in the right hand and the wheel of the law upright on the left knee [inferred: the common attributes — the sword and jewel of Kokūzō, the jewel (nyoi) and wheel (rin) that name Nyoirin].

**The guardian kings of the Great South Gate (L3, built).** The Great South Gate dates from the Nara period; the first fell in a great wind in the Heian period and the present gate was rebuilt by Chōgen from 1199 [stated: tabi-mag "Nandaimon"]; its Kongō Rikishi (Niō) by Unkei and Kaikei stand with the "un" (mouth closed) on the right and the "a" (mouth open) on the left, the reverse of the usual order, facing each other as they had "since the Tenpyō founding" [stated: tabi-mag]; the present pair are about 8 m, added in 1203 [stated: World History Encyclopedia]. Ours (the 752 gate): a pair in the outer bays facing each other across the passage, the "a" on the west (the visitor's left), the "un" on the east [stated: arrangement]; 5.7 m on rocks 0.5 m high, smaller than the Kamakura pair to stand under our gate's lower eaves [inferred]; bare to the waist, chest, belly and arm muscles swelling, a skirt knotted at the waist swinging out, a scarf in a loop over the shoulders, the hair knotted, brows knotted, staring eyes; the "a" raising a vajra overhead and thrusting the other hand down, fingers spread; the "un" holding the vajra down at his side and lifting the other hand open before the chest; of plain wood with a gilt vajra [inferred: forms after the type; the Tenpyō figures are lost].

**Steps to the gates (built).** The Middle Gate's platform (1.0 m) and the Great South Gate's (0.8 m) have flights of stone steps on the axis, front and back, so the way from the south up the axis into the court can be walked [inferred].

## 5. The town

About 300 houses on the capital's grid. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): a raised board floor with the sunken hearth and its kettle on a hook, straw mats, a low table, a lacquered chest, a hanging shelf of pots; a step up to the door, the leaf open inside, a ceiling of boards on beams [inferred: the period's village interiors].

**House detail (vernacular `house_japan`).** As at Uji: the visible frame of posts, sill and head beams, lattice windows and gable vent, deep eaves with boards beneath, thatch ridges with crossed poles, some board roofs held by poles and stones [stated: JAANUS, "machiya": shingles weighted with stones or thatch; inferred: the details].

## 6. Ground and water

White gravel courts; lawns; Wakakusa's open grass; cedar and maple woods. [inferred]

**Deer (built).** Sika deer (Cervus nippon); the deer round Kasuga split from their ancestral group probably in the Asuka period (592–710), so they were there in 752; the Kasuga shrine was founded in 768 and the legend of Takemikazuchi riding a white deer made them messengers of the gods; they feed on grass, leaves and acorns, moving from their resting places to the feeding grounds at sunrise; the males carry antlers [stated: Nara Visitors Bureau "Deer"]. In 752 they were wild deer of the hills, not yet the shrine's [inferred]. Ours: nine small herds (34 deer) on the lawns outside the temple's courts and on Wakakusa's slope; stags with antlers of up to four points a side, hinds without; standing, grazing or lying with the legs folded; the summer coat chestnut with rows of white spots, the white rump patch edged in black, the white tail with a dark line [inferred: the species' appearance].

## 10. Detail (L3, third pass)

- **The eye-opening ceremony (752).** "The eye-opening ceremony was held with an attendance of 10,000 monks and 4,000 dancers"; "the Indian priest Bodhisena performed the eye-opening for Emperor Shōmu" [stated: Wikipedia "Tōdai-ji"]; on 26 May 752 (by the Western calendar; 9th day of the 4th month) Japan's political, military and monastic forces gathered for it, with religious specialists from home and abroad and "music and dance troupes specializing in performing arts from Japan and mainland Asia", their costumes and movements preserved [stated: University of Bristol, "The World on Stage"]; the Shōsō-in keeps the brush Bodhisena used and the ceremonial dress of Shōmu and Kōmyō; dignitaries and monks from China, India and the Korean kingdoms attended [stated: Sainsbury Institute report]. Ours, at a scale the court can hold: 96 monks seated cross-legged on rush mats in four rows either side of the axis, facing the hall, some with palms joined, in ochre, brown and grey robes with ochre or maroon kesa, the way up the axis left open; a dance stage on the axis between the lantern and the hall's stairs — 5.6 m square and 0.8 m high, lacquered red with a black skirt, the floor spread with green silk, a railing with gilt finials, steps north and south [inferred: after the later bugaku stage]; two dancers on it in red and in green (the colours of the music of the left, Tang, and of the right, Korean) [inferred: the later convention], six musicians on mats each side with flutes and mouth organs, lutes, zithers and drums; ten officials in their ranks' colours standing either side of the hall's stairs [inferred]. The figures do not block the arrival's view of the hall.
- **The altar's offerings.** On the altar before the Buddha (the red-lacquered table of before) a bronze censer on three feet with a domed lid, two bronze vases with a gilt band each holding a lotus flower, a bud and two leaves, and two oil lamps on slender stands, lit [inferred: the arrangement of incense, flowers and lamps; none of the 752 altar furniture survives in our sources].
- **Weathering.** The climate's dampness set to 0.65 [inferred: the humid basin].

## 11. The capital and its halls (Suzhou-standard pass)

- **The grid.** Heijō-kyō was laid out as nine blocks north to south by eight east to west, each "about 532 meters on a side" (4.8 km by 4.3 km); the city was modelled on Chang'an but "lacked walls"; Suzaku Avenue ran 3.7 km and was about 74 m wide between the centres of its side gutters; it peaked at 50,000–100,000 people [stated: JLIT "Heijo Capital"; Wikipedia "Heijō-kyō"]. Late Nara house plots were one-sixteenth or one-thirty-second of a block (a chō, about 16,900 m²); lower ranks had smaller parcels; plots held a main hall, a well and a storehouse; minor streets had side ditches; walls dividing blocks were in places replaced by fences [stated: Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, Monograph 46]. Ours, scaled to the 340 m radius [inferred]: avenues every 80 m (the axis avenue south of the Great South Gate 18 m wide, the others 10 m), blocks of about 70 × 70 m, each ringed by an earthen wall (tsuiji) 2.4 m high on a stone footing under a tiled coping with a roofed gate (munamon) in the middle of each side, inside four rows of houses facing two lanes and a garden with a well between the pairs; houses as before (§5).
- **Tōdai-ji's Great Buddha Hall in Tang timberwork.** The hall is of eleven bays in the 8th century (§1). Ours: a ring of 1.5 m vermilion columns 21 m high on a 2.2 m stone platform; on every column and between the columns of the front a stepped bracket set — the Tang set (four tiers, cantilevers, bearing blocks) of Foguang Hall as built at Wutai [stated: dossier tang-wutai §10–§11], scaled 1.8 times for this hall [inferred]; plaster walls, the five central bays of the front with tall plank doors (13 m) under a lattice transom, windows in the others; rafters fanning round the corners of a hip roof of grey tile; a smaller upper body set in one bay with its own columns, lattice windows, brackets and roof; gilt-bronze owl-tail finials (shibi) 6 m high at the ridge's ends, the ridge about 50 m above the ground [inferred: proportions; the finials after the hall's present shibi, which are replicas of Nara-period ones — not sourced in detail].
- **The gates.** The Middle Gate and the Great South Gate use the same hall element (five bays by two, bracketed, a passage through the middle, solid end walls) [inferred].
- **Kōfuku-ji.** Moved in 710 to the east side of the new capital, the Fujiwara clan's temple; the five-storey pagoda ordered by Empress Kōmyō in 730 (the present one, 1426, is 50.1 m high with deep eaves) [stated: Wikipedia "Kōfuku-ji"; MLIT "Five-story Pagoda"]. Ours: a walled precinct west of Tōdai-ji at the grid's edge, its south gate, the Central Golden Hall (two storeys, seven bays by four) and the five-storey pagoda (10.8 m at the base, about 50 m with the spire) south-east of it, white gravel [inferred: arrangement; Kōfuku-ji lay about a kilometre south-west of Tōdai-ji, drawn nearer].
- **The East Market.** The two markets lay on either side of Suzaku Avenue, the capital's trade and redistribution centres [stated: JLIT; Monograph 46 for the West Market]. Ours: an open square of 66 × 70 m on the axis avenue's east side with two rows of awninged stalls facing a central aisle (baskets of vegetables, cloth, water vats), rows of mats with goods and sellers sitting behind them, handcarts and barrows, shop houses on the north edge [inferred: the goods and the stalls' form].

## 12. Interiors

The Great Buddha Hall (the Buddha, attendants, ceiling, banners, §4) is entered through its open doors; the three houses nearest the arrival are open (§5). Kōfuku-ji's golden hall and the gates are shells.

## 13. The living town

- **People.** Monks (shaven, robes of ochre, brown and grey with the kesa), officials in the robes of their ranks with the black gauze cap, townsmen and farmers in short robes or jackets with topknots and woven hats, women in long robes with the hair in a bun, porters with shoulder poles; 40 near the visitor by day, 4 at night; they walk the avenues, lanes, the market aisle and the temple court [inferred: dress after the figures of the period; Nara dress is only roughly rendered].
- **Animals.** Swallows over the courts and avenues, crows over the cedar hills, sparrows on the paving and in the market; deer as figures (§6).
- **Smoke.** Incense at the altar before the Buddha and in Kōfuku-ji; kitchen smoke from a third of the houses at the three meals; steam from the market's cookshops [inferred].

## 14. Deepening pass (towards the Suzhou standard)

- **The Shōsōin.** A storehouse with a raised floor at Tōdai-ji, nine bays by three, about 33 m long and 9 m deep; north and south sections of triangular-cut log (azekura-zukuri), the centre closed with thick planks; supported by 40 heavy posts standing about 2.4 m from the base stones to the floor joists; a tiled hipped roof; the treasures were given by Empress Kōmyō in 756 [stated: JAANUS "Shōsōin"]. Ours: one log block 33 × 9.4 m on forty posts over base stones, courses of logs standing out from the wall, a ladder to a plank door, a gabled tile roof (a hip in the original [inferred: simplification]), west of the court; assumed standing by 756 [inferred].
- **Storehouses of the plots (kura).** The same type at house scale: raised 1.1 m on posts, log walls, a bark roof, a jar at the door [inferred: plots held "a main hall, a well and a storehouse", Monograph 46, §11].
- **Fences and kitchen gardens.** The capital's walls were in places replaced by fences [stated: Monograph 46, §11]; ours: board fences close the narrow gaps between neighbours so the lane fronts run on, vacant plots get a wattle fence with a gate and a garden; each block's middle has two kitchen gardens either side of the well (raised beds of greens, a bamboo drying rack hung with cloth, a woodpile under a lean-to, jars, a kura) behind a wattle fence [inferred].
- **Hokke-dō and the bell tower.** The Hokke-dō (Sangatsu-dō) is among Tōdai-ji's oldest halls (its origin in the 730s–740s [inferred from memory; not re-sourced]); a bell tower (shōrō) stands on its hillside: ours, a stone platform, four vermilion posts, a tile roof, a bronze bell 2.8 m with studs and a striking log, north-east of the court [inferred: position and size].
- **The court.** A flagged way with kerbs from the Middle Gate to the stage; three pairs of banner poles (9.5 m, silk pennants) along it for the ceremonies; sweeping monks, praying pilgrims, gate guards and travellers on the avenue [inferred]. The avenue south of the Great South Gate is flagged 6 m wide with kerbs (Suzaku Avenue's side gutters were about 74 m apart, scaled [stated: Wikipedia "Suzaku Avenue"]).
- **Willows** line the streets' edges [inferred: the capital's tree-lined streets].
- **Interiors.** 30 houses near the arrival are open (§12), up from 3.
