# Dunhuang and the Mogao caves, c. 850 — dossier

Settlement id: `dunhuang` · Style: `dunhuang` (new: `src/styles/dunhuang/`), figures and animals from `life`, clay statues and the lattice ceiling from `chinese` (read-only) · Region: Silk Road (Gansu, China) · Code: `src/settlements/dunhuang/` · Size class Small ●●● (radius ≤ 190 m)

Moment shown: about 850, two years after Zhang Yichao drove the Tibetan rulers out of Shazhou (848) and began the Guiyi Army period [stated: Wikipedia "Mogao Caves"]. Dunhuang had been under Tibetan rule from 787 to 848 and the Mogao site escaped the Buddhist persecution of Emperor Wuzong (845) because of it [stated: same]. About sixty caves were cut in the late Tang (848–906) [stated: same]. The cliff at this date is whole, busy and freshly painted; it is not the ruin seen today.

## 2. The real place

- **The cliff.** The caves are cut into a cliff close to 2 km long, facing east over the dry bed of the Daquan (Dachuan) river, with the Singing Sand dunes behind and above [stated: Wikipedia "Mogao Caves"; "Dunhuang"; the cliff's east face and the river are inferred from the site's usual description]. Our cliff shows 270 m of it, centred on Cave 96, with the face at x = −72 and the rock behind it (west) [inferred: compression, as for every settlement].
- **Height and tiers.** The cliff is 25–35 m high at its middle with caves in up to four tiers [inferred: the usual description of the site]. Ours: top 29–34 m, caves at about 0.45 m (the forecourt), 9.6 m, 18 m and 25 m, reached by galleries and stairs of timber [inferred].
- **Rock.** A pale tan-grey conglomerate, rough and pitted, cut with picks; walls were plastered with clay and straw and a thin lime coat before painting [inferred: general knowledge of the site].

## 3. The caves (L2)

- **Types** [stated: Our China Story]: central-pillar caves (a square pillar in the middle, modelled on the Indian chaitya hall, for walking round; most of the Northern Dynasties caves, Cave 428 the largest at over 145 m²); truncated-pyramid (fudou, "inverted bowl") caves, the commonest form, the ceiling a truncated pyramid painted like a tent; hall caves of the same ceiling with an altar and statues at the back, sometimes a backdrop wall up to the ceiling (Caves 16, 85, 98, 61); nirvana caves; monumental-statue caves (Cave 96); meditation and monks' caves, small, with side chambers; image caves for a high monk (Cave 17); burial caves, mostly north.
- **Ours, enterable:** a hall cave of the Tang type (fudou ceiling, altar with niche and a group of seven clay figures, west wall tableau, the walls in tableaux and thousand Buddhas, donors along the foot of the wall), a central-pillar cave of the older type still in use in 850 (pillar with niche, gabled "human-shaped" front ceiling and flat rear ceiling, apsaras in the upper wall) [inferred: from the types above; the exact layouts are composites, not copies of Cave 220 or 427], the Great Buddha's hall (Cave 96, below), a monk's cell with kang and stove [inferred: the northern caves were lived in; stove and kang are the Hexi winter habit]; Cave 17 (the memorial shrine of the monk Hongbian: Wikipedia) is not built: its date of cutting is not given.
- **Murals** [stated: Wikipedia; Our China Story]: flying apsaras, Jataka tales, avadanas, donor portraits; from the Sui and Tang the great narrative "sutra transformation" tableaux, the Pure Land paradise with Buddha, bodhisattvas, pavilions, musicians and a lotus pond (the Western Paradise of Cave 220 as the model). Ours: abstract in form but true in composition and colour: a central Buddha in a halo and mandorla on a lotus throne, bodhisattva attendants, a pavilion and a pond with lotus below, flying apsaras above with long scarves, a frieze of lozenges and palmettes, rows of small seated Buddhas ("thousand Buddhas"), donors in a procession at the wall's foot; the fudou ceiling with its well (a lotus medallion with apsaras) and slopes of Buddhas and clouds, a fringed valance round its lower edge [inferred: after the usual descriptions].
- **Pigments** [inferred, general knowledge]: malachite green, azurite blue, cinnabar and red lead, ochre, lead white (oxidised to a dark brown in many places today, fresh in 850), soot black, gold leaf. We use the palette's nearest materials (clay_green, paint_blue, lacquer_red, paint_ochre, paint_white, clay_gilt).
- **Statues** [stated: over 2,400 coloured statues: Our China Story]: painted clay on wooden armatures, here with the chinese style's clay figures (bodhisattva, disciple, heavenly king, kneeling figure) and its seated Buddha.

## 4. The Nine-Storey building and the Great Buddha (Cave 96)

- The Maitreya of Cave 96 is about 35.5 m tall, made in 695, the largest statue at Dunhuang; it is housed in a wooden structure that was originally four storeys and has been repaired many times to its present nine [stated: Wikipedia; Our China Story gives the pavilion's height as about 45 m]. The task of this settlement asks for the nine-storey building; for c. 850 we follow the brief and draw it as the nine-storey timber facade: the wooden structure covers the front of the cliff-cut niche, each storey a tiled eave with a row of bays and a gallery [inferred: the present building's nine storeys; the 9th-century storey count is uncertain: Wikipedia says four at first. Recorded here: a later pass may reduce it to the four- or five-eaved Tang form].
- Ours: the structure rises 43 m against the cliff, the roofs stepping back as they rise, the lowest storey open as a porch through which one walks into the statue's hall; inside, the Buddha seated in the rock, his face reached by the upper galleries.

## 5. The cliff-foot and the forecourt

- Many caves had wooden porches or fore-temples [stated: Wikipedia]; only five survive, two of them Song. Ours: timber porches of columns, brackets and tiled eaves on the larger caves; the plain ones with a dark door and a painted lintel; the upper tiers with galleries (zhandao) hung from the rock on beams, with stairs [inferred].
- The forecourt: a stone-and-earth apron along the cliff foot, then the dry bed of the river with a shallow stream, poplars and willows, a timber bridge, the monks' cells and a few small monastery halls on the east bank [inferred: Tang Mogao had monasteries and many pilgrims; the layout is ours].

## 6. The oasis town (Shazhou) and the road

- Shazhou was the oasis county seat in the Tang, with walls and beacon towers beyond [stated: Wikipedia "Dunhuang"]; where the Silk Road's three routes met [stated]. At about 850 population and plan are not given [stated: not in the source]. Ours is a compressed street quarter, not the whole city: a main street from a rammed-earth gate to the river, houses of rammed earth (hangtu) with flat roofs of beams, reed and earth, and a few with tiled gables for the rich (Sogdian and Chinese merchants), courtyards, poplars and willows watered by channels, small fields of wheat and millet and melon patches [inferred: Hexi corridor practice; the melons from Guazhou's name: Wikipedia].
- **The caravanserai** [inferred: the Silk Road inn; Sogdian merchants lived in the oasis towns]: a courtyard with a gate high enough for loaded camels, open stalls for animals on two sides, rooms with kang platforms and rugs, a well, bales and saddles in the yard. Camels: Bactrian, two-humped, of the Silk Road [inferred; the `animals.js` camel is one-humped and stands in].
- **Crescent Spring** [stated: Crescent Lake is in the oasis, with the Singing Sand Dunes: Wikipedia]: a crescent pool among the dunes at the south end of our ground, with reeds, tamarisk and poplars, no building [inferred: the later temples are Qing].
- **Climate** [stated: Wikipedia]: cool arid, one of the sunniest places in China. Sky dry and clear, dust haze at times; wind from the west and north [inferred]; our climate gives a high, bright sun, clear sky, little fog.

## 7. People and work

- Monks in the Tang dress (grey, ochre and brown robes, shaven heads), painters at work on scaffolds of poles and boards with bowls of pigment and brushes, a sculptor and an assistant at a clay figure, donors, pilgrims, merchants in Sogdian dress (cloth caps, belted coats, boots), camel drivers, women with water jars, farmers [inferred from the site's use and the Silk Road trade; Our China Story: the caves were carved over about 1,500 years through ten dynasties].
- Ours: still figures by the caves (a painter on a scaffold with pigment bowls, a monk at a lamp, an elder reading a sutra), walkers in the street (movers.js), camels and loads at the caravanserai.

## 9. Sound

Wind, a dry stream, the small bells of camels and wind bells (fengling) on the eaves of the cave porches and the Nine-Storey building [inferred: eave bells hang on every Chinese Buddhist eave], a distant temple bell, chanting in the caves; music in the pentatonic zhi mode with a flute (qiang di) and a plucked lute (pipa, which came by the Silk Road) in the town, a low drone and a human voice in the caves [inferred].

## 12. Deepening pass: the houses, the street, the caves, the caravanserai
