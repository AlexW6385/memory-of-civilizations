## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Great Temple of Ramesses II, cut into the sandstone cliff facing east over the Nile; façade about 35 m wide and 30 m high with four seated colossi of the king about 20 m high [stated: Wikipedia, "Abu Simbel temples"; UNESCO]. Beside it, 112 m to the north in our frame, the Small Temple of Hathor and Nefertari [inferred distance from the plan in the Nubian Salvage record; the two temples stand some 100 m apart].
- **Moment shown:** about 1255 BC, soon after the completion of the temples (work c. 1264–1244 BC) [stated: Wikipedia]. Our date sits inside the work; the temples are shown finished.
- **Location:** 22.34 N, 31.63 E, Lower Nubia, in the Egyptian province of Kush [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Today:** the temples were cut out and raised 65 m up and 200 m back in 1964–68, for Lake Nasser [stated: UNESCO]. We show the original place, the cliff on the bank.

## 3. Orientation

- The temples face east to the river and the rising sun [stated: Wikipedia]. The sun enters the Great Temple's sanctuary on about 22 February and 22 October, lighting the seated gods save Ptah [stated: Wikipedia; commonly dated to the king's birthday and coronation, an interpretation]. The sanctuary lies about 63 m deep [stated: Wikipedia]. We model it about 62 m deep; the interior depth is stretched to this, which is longer than the plan we first assumed. We do not light it specially on those days. [inferred: simplification]

## 4. The style

### 4.1 The Great Temple's façade
- A façade shaped like a pylon, leaning in; four seated colossi of Ramesses II, those at the south and centre-left in place, one head fallen [stated: Wikipedia: the second colossus from the left lost its head and torso in an earthquake in antiquity]. We show the temple whole and unbroken. [inferred]
- Small statues of the king's mother Tuya, his queen Nefertari and his children beside the legs [stated: Wikipedia].
- A frieze of baboons greeting the sun along the top of the façade [stated: Wikipedia]; 22 baboons and a cobra frieze are built here. [inferred: count]
- Over the door a niche with the falcon-headed Ra-Horakhty, flanked by relief of the king offering to him [stated: Wikipedia]; the figure holds the user-ma-re symbols of the king's name. [stated]
- Batter: the façade leans 2.4 m over 31 m. [inferred: simplification of the true inclination]

### 4.2 The interior
- A first hall with eight Osiride pillars of the king, the walls with relief; the battle of Kadesh on the north wall of the hall [stated: Wikipedia]. Our Kadesh is composed from its known parts (the camp, the chariots, the Orontes, the fortress of Kadesh) and is not a copy. [inferred]
- Four chambers off the first hall (the Salvage plan shows eight side chambers; we build six). [inferred: simplification]
- Second hall with four pillars, a vestibule, and the sanctuary, where four seated gods, Ra-Horakhty, the deified Ramesses, Amun-Re and Ptah, sit against the back wall [stated: Wikipedia].
- Ceilings of the first hall painted with vultures among stars, as at other Egyptian temples [stated: Wikipedia; the Egyptian astronomical ceiling helper is reused]. Colours: egyptian blue, ochre, red, green. [stated: the temple's pigments]
- The Thoth chapel and the other minor details are not built. [inferred: omitted]

### 4.3 The Small Temple
- A façade with six standing colossi about 10 m high, the king and the queen Nefertari in niches between buttresses, with children at their feet [stated: Wikipedia: four of the king, two of the queen]. We build three recesses a side in the order king, queen, king. [inferred: the order is as on the standing record, simplified]
- Hall with six pillars crowned with the head of Hathor, a vestibule, a sanctuary with Hathor as a cow [stated: Wikipedia].

### 4.4 Terrace, altar, gate and quay
- The terrace before the Great Temple was paved and walled in mudbrick; the remains of a mudbrick enclosure, a sun altar and quays on the river are known [stated: Wikipedia and excavation reports; the exact plan is [inferred]].
- The quay and a barge with sandstone blocks, with reed skiffs. [inferred: by analogy with the contemporary boats of Egypt; sandstone blocks stated by the quarrying record]

## 5. The town

- Priests' houses: flat-roofed mudbrick, doors to the south. [inferred]
- The workmen's camp: the builders of the temples lived near the site; no settlement has been found there, so the camp, in rows of small mudbrick houses, with bakeries and round granaries, is [inferred] by analogy with Giza's workers' town and the village of Deir el-Medina. [inferred]
- Desert weathering: sand against the walls and the terrace's edge, the sandstone paler where it is cut and darker where it is old. [inferred]

## 10. Simplifications and omissions (summary)

- Façade batter, six side chambers rather than eight, the interior stretched to about 62 m, the composition of Kadesh, the order king–queen–king on the Small Temple's façade, the workmen's camp: all [inferred]. The Thoth chapel is not built. The solar days are not specially lit.

## 11. The camp as a town of lanes (deepening pass) [inferred, after Deir el-Medina]

- Deir el-Medina, the workmen's village of the Theban necropolis, is the model: "around sixty-eight houses" on 5,600 m², a narrow road the length of the village which "may have been covered to shelter residents from the sun", houses of about 70 m² of mudbrick on stone foundations, mud plaster with the exterior painted white, high windows, niches for statues and small altars, a kitchen with a cellar and a stair to the roof, the wooden door perhaps bearing the occupant's name [stated: Wikipedia, "Deir el-Medina"]. Its people were free, salaried state workers paid in rations, a mixed population of Egyptians, Nubians and Asiatics, with water carried in from a well half an hour away [stated: same].
- Our camp follows it: rows of houses stand back to back with 0.6 m between their rear walls and their doors on lanes 3.4–6.4 m wide, cross lanes 3–4 m; some fronts whitewashed (the priests' all of them), the doors framed in painted ochre, blue or red [inferred: the colours of the contemporary painting]; the market street under reed shades laid across beams (the covered road) [inferred]. The people's skin runs from dark to medium for the Egyptian-Nubian mix [inferred].
- Houses are the Old Kingdom mudbrick type (courtyard, oven, granary, sitting bench, sleeping niche) of Giza's workers' town, turned to either lane; the camp is "workers' housing by analogy" and nothing of it is excavated at Abu Simbel [inferred].

## 12. The market street and its shops [inferred]

- Egyptian exchange was by barter, grain in standard units (the deben) serving as a measure of value; the tomb scenes show fish sold and cleaned and a necklace bartered for pots of perfume; markets dealt in grain, vegetables, fruit, pottery, jewellery, textiles, handicrafts, livestock and salt [stated: general survey of market scenes, studenthandouts.com "Ancient Egyptian Market Scenes"; the tomb it draws on is not named there].
- Shops built (each a room you can enter, with its counter on the lane): potter (wheel, kiln, shelves of jars), weaver (ground loom, bolts of linen), scribe (low table, papyrus rolls, palette), sandal-maker (soles of plaited papyrus on a rail), jeweller (trays of lapis, carnelian, gold beads, a balance), carpenter (bench, logs and planks, adzes), butcher (joints, geese, strung fish), brewer (beer jars, vats), and the bakeries of Giza's workers' town (bread pots over ash). The trades are those of the Theban villages and markets [inferred]; the layout is ours.

## 13. Eye level and weathering [inferred]

- Terrace: benches of dressed stone along the enclosure walls, offering tables with loaves, a jar and lotus before each colossus, tripod braziers, two ablution basins, two pilgrims' shades of reed on poles, two stelae, processional ways of slabs; sand banked against the mudbrick, drifted in patches, flakes of fallen stone. Tables of offerings stood before the temples' images, and temples kept lamps and braziers burning [stated in general for Egyptian temples; the placement is ours].
- Halls: tables of offerings and braziers off the axis in the first hall, benches for the priests between the Osiride pillars, the god's barque in a shrine of cedar and gold on a stand in the vestibule (the pillars of the second hall show the king and Nefertari before the sacred barques [stated: Wikipedia]; a barque shrine stood in the temples' inner rooms by analogy [inferred]), lotus bouquets before the four gods.
- Cliff: strata of paler and redder sandstone, dark fissures, ledges with fallen stone, a talus of sand and blocks at the foot [inferred].
- Quay: blocks of sandstone with their sledges and ropes (the blocks quarried near the site, carried by river [inferred]), sacks, beer and oil jars, reed bundles, nets on poles, fish drying, a mud store; women washing and a man coiling rope at the water.
- Masons' yard south of the camp: blocks in the rough and dressed, sledges, rollers of palm log, a mud ramp and a lashed scaffold, a tool rack with copper chisels and mallets under a reed shade, a baboon of the frieze half-carved on its block. The temples are shown finished; the yard is the upkeep and the last of the work [inferred].
- Bank: the shaduf, the counterpoise well, came to Egypt in the New Kingdom [stated: general histories of Egyptian irrigation; first attested in the 18th dynasty], so shadufs stand at the fields' lower edge, between contour ridges of mud holding the water; emmer and barley in the basins [inferred].
- Washing on lines across the lanes; stacks of mudbricks drying; the lanes' sparrows, the river's egrets and a heron; tilapia and perch in the shallows.

## 14. Still short of the standard (honest list)

- The boats on the river stand moored or poled by still fishermen; none sail or scull (the movers' boat set is the canal boat of Suzhou, written for canals).
- No sound is made by zone beyond the music's two zones (open, hall); no market murmur, hammering or river sound of its own.
