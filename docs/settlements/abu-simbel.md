## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Great Temple of Ramesses II, cut into the sandstone cliff facing east over the Nile; façade about 35 m wide and 30 m high with four seated colossi of the king about 20 m high [stated: Wikipedia, "Abu Simbel temples"; UNESCO]. Beside it, 112 m to the north in our frame, the Small Temple of Hathor and Nefertari [inferred distance from the plan in the Nubian Salvage record; the two temples stand some 100 m apart].
- **Moment shown:** about 1255 BC, soon after the completion of the temples (work c. 1264–1244 BC) [stated: Wikipedia]. Our date sits inside the work; the temples are shown finished.
- **Location:** 22.34 N, 31.63 E, Lower Nubia, in the Egyptian province of Kush [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Today:** the temples were cut out and raised 65 m up and 200 m back in 1964–68, for Lake Nasser [stated: UNESCO]. We show the original place, the cliff on the bank.

## 3. Orientation

- The temples face east to the river and the rising sun [stated: Wikipedia]. The sun enters the Great Temple's sanctuary on about 22 February and 22 October, lighting the seated gods save Ptah [stated: Wikipedia; commonly dated to the king's birthday and coronation, an interpretation]. The sanctuary lies about 63 m deep [stated: Wikipedia]. We model it about 62 m deep; the interior depth is stretched to this, which is longer than the plan we first assumed. We do not light it specially on those days. [inferred: simplification]

## 4. The style

### 4.1 The Great Temple's façade
- A façade shaped like a pylon, leaning in; four seated colossi of Ramesses II, those at the south and centre-left in place, one head fallen [stated: Wikipedia: the second colossus from the left lost its head and torso in an earthquake in antiquity]. We show the temple whole and unbroken. [inferred]
- Small statues of the king's mother Tuya, his queen Nefertari and his children beside the legs [stated: Wikipedia].
- A frieze of baboons greeting the sun along the top of the façade [stated: Wikipedia]; 22 baboons and a cobra frieze are built here. [inferred: count]
- Over the door a niche with the falcon-headed Ra-Horakhty, flanked by relief of the king offering to him [stated: Wikipedia]; the figure holds the user-ma-re symbols of the king's name. [stated]
- Batter: the façade leans 2.4 m over 31 m. [inferred: simplification of the true inclination]

### 4.2 The interior
- A first hall with eight Osiride pillars of the king, the walls with relief; the battle of Kadesh on the north wall of the hall [stated: Wikipedia]. Our Kadesh is composed from its known parts (the camp, the chariots, the Orontes, the fortress of Kadesh) and is not a copy. [inferred]
- Four chambers off the first hall (the Salvage plan shows eight side chambers; we build six). [inferred: simplification]
- Second hall with four pillars, a vestibule, and the sanctuary, where four seated gods, Ra-Horakhty, the deified Ramesses, Amun-Re and Ptah, sit against the back wall [stated: Wikipedia].
- Ceilings of the first hall painted with vultures among stars, as at other Egyptian temples [stated: Wikipedia; the Egyptian astronomical ceiling helper is reused]. Colours: egyptian blue, ochre, red, green. [stated: the temple's pigments]
- The Thoth chapel and the other minor details are not built. [inferred: omitted]

### 4.3 The Small Temple
- A façade with six standing colossi about 10 m high, the king and the queen Nefertari in niches between buttresses, with children at their feet [stated: Wikipedia: four of the king, two of the queen]. We build three recesses a side in the order king, queen, king. [inferred: the order is as on the standing record, simplified]
- Hall with six pillars crowned with the head of Hathor, a vestibule, a sanctuary with Hathor as a cow [stated: Wikipedia].

### 4.4 Terrace, altar, gate and quay
- The terrace before the Great Temple was paved and walled in mudbrick; the remains of a mudbrick enclosure, a sun altar and quays on the river are known [stated: Wikipedia and excavation reports; the exact plan is [inferred]].
- The quay and a barge with sandstone blocks, with reed skiffs. [inferred: by analogy with the contemporary boats of Egypt; sandstone blocks stated by the quarrying record]

## 5. The town

- Priests' houses: flat-roofed mudbrick, doors to the south. [inferred]
- The workmen's camp: the builders of the temples lived near the site; no settlement has been found there, so the camp, in rows of small mudbrick houses, with bakeries and round granaries, is [inferred] by analogy with Giza's workers' town and the village of Deir el-Medina. [inferred]
- Desert weathering: sand against the walls and the terrace's edge, the sandstone paler where it is cut and darker where it is old. [inferred]

## 9. People and sound

## 10. Simplifications and omissions (summary)
