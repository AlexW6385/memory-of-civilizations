# Venice (San Marco), about 1500 — dossier

Settlement id: `venice` · Style: `venetian` (with `vernacular` houses) · Region: Italy (the Venetian lagoon) · Code: `src/settlements/venice/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the Piazza San Marco with the basilica's five domes and its west front of arches and gold mosaic, the campanile, the Procuratie on three sides; the Piazzetta with its two columns opening to the basin; the Doge's Palace with its two storeys of arcades and the diamond-patterned wall; the Grand Canal and smaller canals; houses on every island; the lagoon round the disc; ground, planting, weather, music). Built since toward L3: San Marco's interior, the timber Rialto bridge and its market, the quay walls with their tide line, a traghetto (§4, §6). L3 recorded for later: palaces on the Grand Canal, the Arsenale, galleys, Carnival, a merchant's house (casa-fondaco) to enter, the Doge's Palace's Porta della Carta and canal front.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** St Mark's Basilica, a Greek-cross church under five domes, with tall lead-covered outer shells and a west front of five arched portals with mosaics [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Basilica"].
- **The Campanile:** about 98.6 m high, of brick, with its belfry and a pyramidal spire topped by an angel [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Campanile"].
- **The Doge's Palace:** two storeys of arcades carrying a wall patterned in pink and white stone, facing the basin and the Piazzetta [stated: Wikipedia "Doge's Palace"].
- **The squares:** the Procuratie Vecchie line the north side of the piazza. The two columns of San Marco and San Todaro stand at the Piazzetta's waterfront [stated: Wikipedia "Piazza San Marco"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1500.

## 4. The style

Brick and Istrian stone; marble and gold mosaic on the basilica; lead domes; arcades; houses plastered under tiled roofs. [stated]

**The Doge's Palace (L2–L3, built).** A portico of columns with carved fourteenth-century capitals supporting the arcades, and above it a light Gothic loggia with twice as many arches, with four-petalled circular openings in the tracery above them [stated: travelmate]; on the Molo front a lower gallery of 17 pointed arches, about 5.76 m high, the Piazzetta side following it; the quatrefoils [stated: jc-r]. The upper wall in a two-tone diamond pattern of white Istrian stone and pink Verona marble [stated: travelmate; jc-r], pierced besides small oculi only by seven major windows [stated: jc-r]; large balconies with sculpture on the two fronts [stated: travelmate] — ours on the Molo (the balcony of 1404) with Justice above [inferred: the figure's attributes]. The top is not battlemented but crowned with spires and pinnacles [stated: travelmate]; ours a cresting of marble leaves [inferred]. Corner groups: Adam and Eve, the Drunkenness of Noah [stated: travelmate]; ours simple figures round a tree on the two Molo corners [inferred]. The Piazzetta wing was rebuilt from 1424 [stated: jc-r], so in 1500 both fronts stand. The Porta della Carta (1438–42) and the canal front are not detailed [inferred: recorded for later].

**The horses of San Marco (L2, built).** Four almost life-size gilt-bronze horses, brought after the Fourth Crusade and set on the basilica's front in 1254 [stated: tuscanynowandmore], on the loggia over the central portal [inferred: from views]; ours modelled whole, each pair turned toward the other, two lifting a foreleg [inferred from photographs], on a terrace in front of the central arch [inferred].

**The Molo (L2, built).** Gondolas moored stern to the quay before the Piazzetta and the palace between mooring posts [inferred]. Gondolas of the 1490s as Carpaccio painted them (the "Miracle of the Relic of the Cross at the Rialto", 1494): long, rising at both ends, some with a cabin (felze) amidships, rowed standing with one oar [inferred: from the painting, Gallerie dell'Accademia (Wikipedia "Miracle of the Relic of the Cross at the Ponte di Rialto"), not fetched here]; the hull black [inferred: the colour was made compulsory only by the sumptuary law of 1562; many were already dark].

**The Clock Tower (L2–L3, built).** Built 1496–97 at the entrance to the Merceria on the north side of the piazza, adjoining the east end of the Procuratie Vecchie; the clock commissioned 1493, inaugurated 1 February 1499; the side wings added by 1506 — Barbari's view of 1500 shows the tower alone, rising above the lower old Procuratie either side; generally given to Mauro Codussi [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Clocktower"]. From the bottom: the archway two storeys high through which the Merceria leaves the piazza; the great blue and gold dial in a fixed marble circle of the 24 hours in Roman numerals, with a sun pointer, the gold zodiac turning inside it, the earth and a moon at the centre among fixed stars; a semicircular gallery with the seated Virgin and Child in gilt beaten copper, round which the Magi process; the winged lion with the open book on blue with gold stars, Doge Agostino Barbarigo kneeling before it until 1797; the terrace with the bell (Simeone Campanato, Arsenal, 1497) and the two bronze figures that strike it, cast 1494 and originally gilt [stated: Wikipedia]. The hour and minute panels beside the gallery are 19th-century and not shown [inferred: from the article's description of the present state]. Ours: 8.5 m wide and 10 m deep, the terrace at 24 m [inferred: proportions from photographs]; the passage barrel-vaulted; paired colonnettes on the piers; the dial 5 m across with the hour marks, twelve gold bosses for the zodiac, the earth in blue with a silver moon and the sun on its pointer; the gallery with its balusters, the gilt Virgin with the Child in a blue niche, a door either side for the Magi; the lion in gilt relief against the starred blue; the doge not shown (a kneeling figure we cannot yet pose) [inferred]; the bell on an iron frame, the giants striding with their hammers, gilt [inferred: their dress simplified].

**The old Procuratie (L2, built).** Before the fire of 1512 the north range was the older building, which Gentile Bellini's Procession of 1496 shows as two storeys, apartments above shops on the ground floor; the 13th-century procurators' houses and Orseolo's pilgrim hospice stood on the south side [stated: cavac.at "Procuratie", after Bellini]. Ours on the north, south and west sides of the piazza alike: an arcade of stilted round arches on columns with leaf capitals before a portico of shops (a door, a window and a propped shutter in each bay, brick paving under the portico's ceiling), a string course, two round-headed windows in white stone to each bay above, a cornice and square merlons with white copings under a hipped tile roof; walls whitewashed, 11 m high so that the Clock Tower rises over them [inferred: from Bellini's painting, not fetched here; the west side, where San Geminiano stood, is not yet drawn as the church].

**The columns of the Piazzetta (L2, built).** The two columns of San Marco and San Todaro at the Piazzetta's waterfront [stated: Wikipedia "Piazza San Marco"]; ours on stepped bases of three steps with an octagonal plinth, the shafts of granite (grey for St Mark, red for St Theodore), a Veneto-Byzantine basket capital of stiff leaves under a broad abacus; on the east column the winged lion of St Mark in bronze, standing with a forepaw on the open book and facing the basin; on the west St Theodore in marble with helmet, shield and spear, the dragon at his feet [inferred: from photographs; the shafts' colours and the reliefs of the trades on the steps not checked].

**San Marco inside (L3, built: `src/styles/venetian/interior.js`).** Inside 76.5 m long and 62.6 m wide, the inner domes 28.15 m high; the central and western domes the larger [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Basilica"]. The walls sheathed in marble and precious stones; gold-ground mosaics cover the domes, vaults and upper walls, reaching the lower walls in the 13th century after the galleries were cut back to narrow walkways (kept in fuller form only at the ends of the north, south and west arms); many windows walled up for the mosaics, so that the space under the galleries is dim; arcade columns, gallery balusters and parapets [stated: Wikipedia]. The pavement mostly opus sectile with some opus tessellatum, late 11th or early 12th century, geometric and animal designs in coloured stones [stated: Wikipedia]. The chancel raised over the crypt in the 11th century [stated: Wikipedia]; the Gothic altar screen dated 1394, signed by Pierpaolo and Jacobello dalle Masegne, a columned transenna with a rhombus motif and fourteen white marble statues — the twelve apostles, the Virgin and St Mark — with the crucifix of bronze and silver at the top [stated: basilicasanmarco.it "The iconostases"; Wikipedia]; smaller Gothic screens before the chapels of St Peter and St Clement [stated: basilicasanmarco.it]. Before the screen the ambo on the left for the readings and on the right the platform where the new doge was shown to the people [stated: Wikipedia]. The high altar's ciborium on four columns carved with the lives of Christ and the Virgin; the Pala d'Oro, Byzantine enamel on gilded silver, installed on the high altar in 1105 [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours, in our smaller church (arms 68 by 22 m and 54 by 22 m, after the exterior): five square bays of 13 m, each dome raised on spherical pendentives (the sphere through the bay's corners, so that its edges are the great arches) and a drum of 3.1 m with sixteen small arched windows, the domes' tops 28.6 m over the pavement; barrel vaults of 6.5 m radius between the bays, springing at 13 m; aisles 3 m wide behind colonnades of Proconnesian and verde antico columns with basket capitals carrying narrow galleries at 6.5 m with parapets of slabs with green lozenges; the crossing's four great piers and piers at every bay's corners; the apse a half-cylinder under its half-dome, standing out beyond the east wall [inferred: proportions from plans and photographs]. Marble slabs (veined, framed by white fillets, on a verde antico plinth, under a cornice) to the galleries, mosaic above; mosaic figures as decals on the curved surfaces — in the central dome the Ascension (Christ in the crown's blue medallion, the Virgin and the twelve apostles with trees round the foot), in the west dome Pentecost (the dove, rays to the twelve apostles), in the east and the transept domes prophets and saints round a figure in the crown, the four evangelists seated in the central pendentives, standing saints in the others, a figure in each barrel's crown, Christ between the Virgin and St Mark over the west door, Christ Pantocrator enthroned in the apse with two saints below [inferred: the programme generalised; the subjects of the five domes as commonly described]. The pavement in opus sectile; the presbytery 1.2 m up, reached by seven steps through the screen's door; the screen of eight red columns (our marble_pink) with gilt capitals, a parapet of slabs with a green lozenge and a porphyry roundel, the architrave with the fourteen statues (figures in white marble) and the crucifix on its post [inferred: the number of columns and the arrangement]; the north ambo double, octagonal on columns with a smaller upper ambo under a gilt cupola, the south platform square [inferred: forms]; the altar under a ciborium of verde antico on four white columns banded as if carved, the Pala d'Oro 3.3 by 2.1 m — gold, three rows of enamel panels, Christ enthroned in a blue roundel, small roundels across the top [inferred: size as commonly given, layout simplified]; candlesticks with their flames, walnut choir stalls, eleven hanging oil lamps under bronze crowns on chains, the south transept's rose window [inferred: the rose is 15th century]. The narthex's central bay is a vestibule entered through the central portal (bronze leaves open inward, a gold barrel vault with apostles, St Mark over the door into the church); the rest of the narthex stays solid [inferred]. The light bake is now stale for the interior (re-bake Venice).

**The Rialto (L3, built: `src/styles/venetian/rive.js`, `src/settlements/venice/rialto.js`).** The wooden bridge of 1255, rebuilt after its collapse of 1444, had two ramps meeting at a movable central section that could be raised for tall ships; two rows of shops were built along its sides in the first half of the 15th century; it stood until the stone bridge of 1588–91, which is similar to it [stated: Wikipedia "Rialto Bridge"]. The market moved to the Rialto in 1097; before the fire of 1514 it was retail and wholesale, with warehouses, shops of luxury goods and banks round it; the Erberia (greens) and the fish market [stated: Wikipedia "Rialto"]. Ours: the bridge across the Grand Canal where it runs north–south, 39 m between the quays, the ramps rising 3.4 m to a level middle span of 6.8 m between two tall timber frames with ropes to its leaves (as Carpaccio's "Miracle of the Relic of the Cross", 1494, shows them) [inferred: the frames' form from the painting, not fetched here]; trestles of three piles under cross beams; on each ramp a row of booths each side of a 3.2 m walk, stepped to the slope, open to the walk with counters, propped shutters and pent roofs of tile, cloth, jars and scales on the counters [inferred]. The campo at its west foot cleared for the market: fishmongers' stalls along the water, greengrocers' and bakers' facing them, sellers and buyers, barges moored with crates, barrels and sacks set down, a porter [inferred]. A traghetto (the gondola ferry across the Grand Canal, where for centuries the Rialto was the only bridge [stated: historywalksvenice "Tragheto"]) crossing south of the bridge, the boatman standing at the stern, a passenger standing and one seated; three gondolas waiting at each bank's station with a boatman on the quay [inferred].

## 4b. Palazzi, bridges, wells, squero, life (batch B2, built)

**The palazzi of the Grand Canal (`src/styles/venetian/palazzo.js`, `layout.js` `palazzi()`).** In the Gothic palace the front is a tripartite composition: a ground floor with the water portal and a loggia, and two piani nobili, the portego opening on the front in a four-arched loggia; the front always faces the water, the back is plainer; Gothic arches are pointed ogival with quatrefoil roundels, "gotico fiorito" after the upper gallery of the Doge's Palace; Gothic palaces were still being built late in the 15th century, and Codussi's Renaissance fronts were still Gothic in composition [stated: imagesofvenice.com "Venetian Palace Architectural Styles"]. The Ca' d'Oro has a multi-arched pointed water entrance, wide in the middle, a gondola landing leading into a courtyard with a wellhead, a first-floor loggia of columns under quatrefoil windows and a lighter one above, Istrian stone with Verona red marble details, gilded finials [stated: Wikipedia "Ca' d'Oro"]. Ours: 16 to 26 m wide, 19 to 23 m deep, floors at 4.9, 10.1 and 14.9 m, a loggia of four arches in the middle 7.6 m between pairs of windows with a roundel above; three fashions — gothic (ogee arches), veneto (stilted round arches, green roundels) and lombardo (round pairs under a cornice, pilasters, no loggia); the water gate a pointed portal or a three-arch arcade, a stair of stone steps into the canal, two striped mooring poles, a gondola tied beside; plaster of six colours over a stone plinth, the corners and string courses in white stone, a deep cornice, a low hipped roof of tile, two or three flared chimneys, sometimes an altana [inferred: proportions and arrangement from the type; palazzi placed along both banks of the Grand Canal's straight stretches, clear of the piazza and the Rialto].

**Chimneys and altane.** The inverted-cone chimney pot was common in Venice to stop sparks; Bellini and Carpaccio show elongated flues; the altana is a wooden roof terrace on pillars with wood or iron balustrade [stated: imagesofvenice.com "Chimneys, Roof Terraces and Aerials"]. Ours: a brick shaft with a terracotta flared cone; the altana of timber under a small tile roof.

**Bridges (`ponte`).** Bridges were built without railings until the 18th century; the Ponte del Chiodo still has none [stated: seevenice.it]. Early bridges were flat and of wood, arched ones of stone from about the 16th century to let boats pass; the first stone bridge is dated 1337 near San Barnaba [stated: veneziaautentica.com; the source is inconsistent]. Ours: a single arch of brick under a ring of Istrian stone, 4.4 m wide, the crown 1.9 m above the quays, steps 17 cm high and 40 cm deep, no parapet, at the streets' ends over the small canals and about every 46 m along them [inferred: the arch's rise and the stone for 1500].

**Wells.** Venice had no usable groundwater: wells were rain cisterns, a clay-lined pit 5 to 6 m deep filled with graded river sand and gravel, the rain led in by gutters from a paved surface that sloped to it; the wellhead (vera) mostly of Istrian stone, Renaissance ones polygonal; state wells under decrees of 1322 and 1424 bore the Lion of St Mark [stated: imagesofvenice.com "Venetian Wells"]. Ours: a stepped base with a moulded round curb, an iron crossbar with its pulley and four drain slabs let into the paving, in the streets and on the piazza [inferred: forms and the piazza's places].

**The streets.** The grid of houses leaves a 12 m street every 44 m, paved in brick with a stone gutter down the middle and kerbs of Istrian stone [inferred].

**The squero.** A squero has a slipway into the canal and the tezon, one or two joined wooden sheds where boats are repaired, the upper part once the squeraroli's home; the larger yards are by the Arsenal [stated: venicewelcome.com "The Squeraroli"]. Ours: on the eastern canal's south bank an open timber shed on posts with boarded walls and a tile roof, a gondola on trestles, the keel and ribs of another, planks seasoning, barrels of pitch, two timber ways stepping into the water [inferred].

**Movers, fauna, smoke (`movers.js`, `fauna.js`, `smoke.js`).** Walkers by trade after the pictures of the 1490s: patricians in the black gown and cap, citizens, artisans in doublet and parti-coloured hose, women with veils, friars, porters, Levantine merchants in turbans. Gondolas rowed standing at the stern with the felze, and cargo boats with sacks, barrels and crates, on the basin, the Grand Canal by the Rialto and the straight canals. Mullet in the canals, swifts over the water, hooded crows round the campanile, sparrows, a heron and an egret on the quays; kitchen smoke at the meals and mist at dawn [inferred].

**Weathering.** `waterline` set at the canal's level and `damp` 0.9: algae and a pale salt band on the quays and walls at the tide line [inferred: the engine's generic weathering].

## 5. The town

About 1,000 houses on the islands. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): a bacaro-like inn and two homes, furnished as in Florence; a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

**House detail (vernacular `house_med`).** Casement frames in the windows; ground-floor windows behind iron gratings over stone sills; balconies as a slab of Istrian stone on stepped brackets with an iron railing of bars; cornices as deep as the eaves [inferred].

## 6. Ground and water

Paving of trachyte and brick; a few gardens; the canals and the lagoon. [inferred]

**The quay walls (L3, built: `rive` in `src/styles/venetian/rive.js`).** Every canal, the basin and the island's edge walled: brick on a footing; below the high-water mark the green band of weed and slime; at the tide line a course of white Istrian stone; above it the brick bleached and crusted by salt for half a metre; at the walk's edge a coping of Istrian stone, the walk behind paved in grey trachyte [inferred: the familiar look of Venetian rive; the Istrian stone at the waterline because it resists the salt]. The faces stand 0.6 m inside the canal so that their tops cover the bank's slope [inferred: a fit to the ground].
