## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** St Mark's Basilica, a Greek-cross church under five domes, with tall lead-covered outer shells and a west front of five arched portals with mosaics [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Basilica"].
- **The Campanile:** about 98.6 m high, of brick, with its belfry and a pyramidal spire topped by an angel [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Campanile"].
- **The Doge's Palace:** two storeys of arcades carrying a wall patterned in pink and white stone, facing the basin and the Piazzetta [stated: Wikipedia "Doge's Palace"].
- **The squares:** the Procuratie Vecchie line the north side of the piazza. The two columns of San Marco and San Todaro stand at the Piazzetta's waterfront [stated: Wikipedia "Piazza San Marco"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1500.

## 4. The style

Brick and Istrian stone; marble and gold mosaic on the basilica; lead domes; arcades; houses plastered under tiled roofs. [stated]

**The Doge's Palace (L2–L3, built).** A portico of columns with carved fourteenth-century capitals supporting the arcades, and above it a light Gothic loggia with twice as many arches, with four-petalled circular openings in the tracery above them [stated: travelmate]; on the Molo front a lower gallery of 17 pointed arches, about 5.76 m high, the Piazzetta side following it; the quatrefoils [stated: jc-r]. The upper wall in a two-tone diamond pattern of white Istrian stone and pink Verona marble [stated: travelmate; jc-r], pierced besides small oculi only by seven major windows [stated: jc-r]; large balconies with sculpture on the two fronts [stated: travelmate] — ours on the Molo (the balcony of 1404) with Justice above [inferred: the figure's attributes]. The top is not battlemented but crowned with spires and pinnacles [stated: travelmate]; ours a cresting of marble leaves [inferred]. Corner groups: Adam and Eve, the Drunkenness of Noah [stated: travelmate]; ours simple figures round a tree on the two Molo corners [inferred]. The Piazzetta wing was rebuilt from 1424 [stated: jc-r], so in 1500 both fronts stand. The Porta della Carta (1438–42) and the canal front are not detailed [inferred: recorded for later].

**The horses of San Marco (L2, built).** Four almost life-size gilt-bronze horses, brought after the Fourth Crusade and set on the basilica's front in 1254 [stated: tuscanynowandmore], on the loggia over the central portal [inferred: from views]; ours modelled whole, each pair turned toward the other, two lifting a foreleg [inferred from photographs], on a terrace in front of the central arch [inferred].

**The Molo (L2, built).** Gondolas moored stern to the quay before the Piazzetta and the palace between mooring posts [inferred]. Gondolas of the 1490s as Carpaccio painted them (the "Miracle of the Relic of the Cross at the Rialto", 1494): long, rising at both ends, some with a cabin (felze) amidships, rowed standing with one oar [inferred: from the painting, Gallerie dell'Accademia (Wikipedia "Miracle of the Relic of the Cross at the Ponte di Rialto"), not fetched here]; the hull black [inferred: the colour was made compulsory only by the sumptuary law of 1562; many were already dark].

**The Clock Tower (L2–L3, built).** Built 1496–97 at the entrance to the Merceria on the north side of the piazza, adjoining the east end of the Procuratie Vecchie; the clock commissioned 1493, inaugurated 1 February 1499; the side wings added by 1506 — Barbari's view of 1500 shows the tower alone, rising above the lower old Procuratie either side; generally given to Mauro Codussi [stated: Wikipedia "St Mark's Clocktower"]. From the bottom: the archway two storeys high through which the Merceria leaves the piazza; the great blue and gold dial in a fixed marble circle of the 24 hours in Roman numerals, with a sun pointer, the gold zodiac turning inside it, the earth and a moon at the centre among fixed stars; a semicircular gallery with the seated Virgin and Child in gilt beaten copper, round which the Magi process; the winged lion with the open book on blue with gold stars, Doge Agostino Barbarigo kneeling before it until 1797; the terrace with the bell (Simeone Campanato, Arsenal, 1497) and the two bronze figures that strike it, cast 1494 and originally gilt [stated: Wikipedia]. The hour and minute panels beside the gallery are 19th-century and not shown [inferred: from the article's description of the present state]. Ours: 8.5 m wide and 10 m deep, the terrace at 24 m [inferred: proportions from photographs]; the passage barrel-vaulted; paired colonnettes on the piers; the dial 5 m across with the hour marks, twelve gold bosses for the zodiac, the earth in blue with a silver moon and the sun on its pointer; the gallery with its balusters, the gilt Virgin with the Child in a blue niche, a door either side for the Magi; the lion in gilt relief against the starred blue; the doge not shown (a kneeling figure we cannot yet pose) [inferred]; the bell on an iron frame, the giants striding with their hammers, gilt [inferred: their dress simplified].

**The old Procuratie (L2, built).** Before the fire of 1512 the north range was the older building, which Gentile Bellini's Procession of 1496 shows as two storeys, apartments above shops on the ground floor; the 13th-century procurators' houses and Orseolo's pilgrim hospice stood on the south side [stated: cavac.at "Procuratie", after Bellini]. Ours on the north, south and west sides of the piazza alike: an arcade of stilted round arches on columns with leaf capitals before a portico of shops (a door, a window and a propped shutter in each bay, brick paving under the portico's ceiling), a string course, two round-headed windows in white stone to each bay above, a cornice and square merlons with white copings under a hipped tile roof; walls whitewashed, 11 m high so that the Clock Tower rises over them [inferred: from Bellini's painting, not fetched here; the west side, where San Geminiano stood, is not yet drawn as the church].

**The columns of the Piazzetta (L2, built).** The two columns of San Marco and San Todaro at the Piazzetta's waterfront [stated: Wikipedia "Piazza San Marco"]; ours on stepped bases of three steps with an octagonal plinth, the shafts of granite (grey for St Mark, red for St Theodore), a Veneto-Byzantine basket capital of stiff leaves under a broad abacus; on the east column the winged lion of St Mark in bronze, standing with a forepaw on the open book and facing the basin; on the west St Theodore in marble with helmet, shield and spear, the dragon at his feet [inferred: from photographs; the shafts' colours and the reliefs of the trades on the steps not checked].

## 5. The town

About 1,000 houses on the islands. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): a bacaro-like inn and two homes, furnished as in Florence; a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

**House detail (vernacular `house_med`).** Casement frames in the windows; ground-floor windows behind iron gratings over stone sills; balconies as a slab of Istrian stone on stepped brackets with an iron railing of bars; cornices as deep as the eaves [inferred].

## 6. Ground and water

Paving of trachyte and brick; a few gardens; the canals and the lagoon. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Hazy, watery light; mist on the lagoon. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the west end of the piazza toward the basilica, and down the Piazzetta to the water. [inferred]

## 9. Sound
