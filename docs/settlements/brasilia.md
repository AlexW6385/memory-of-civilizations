## 1. The real place

- **The city.** President Juscelino Kubitschek began the capital after taking office in January 1956; an international jury chose Lúcio Costa's Pilot Plan in 1957; "construction took 41 months, and Brasília was inaugurated on 21 April 1960" [stated: Wikipedia "Brasília"]. The Federal District's 1960 census counted 141,742 people; the Pilot Plan was designed for about 500,000 [stated: same]. UNESCO inscribed it in 1987 [stated: Wikipedia "Praça dos Três Poderes" and the Studio Matrx essay].
- **The cross.** Costa's plan is a cross of two axes: the straight Monumental Axis (Eixo Monumental), east–west, for government, and the bowed Residential Axis (Eixo Rodoviário), north–south, for housing [stated: Wikipedia "Monumental Axis"; Studio Matrx "Brasília master plan"]. They meet at the bus-station platform (Rodoviária), a multi-level platform rather than a plaza [stated: Studio Matrx].
- **The Monumental Axis.** "The Esplanada dos Ministérios is a rectangular lawn bordered by two eight-lane avenues" [stated: Wikipedia "Brasília"]; "identical white slabs in two files, leading east to the Three Powers Plaza" [stated: Studio Matrx]; the axis was set with "twelve lanes in total" (six each way) and is recorded for the "widest median strip" [stated: Wikipedia "Monumental Axis"]. Costa compared the Esplanade to a "mall" [stated: same]. It ends at the Praça dos Três Poderes [stated: same].
- **Climate.** Tropical savanna: rainy season October–April, dry May–September; November the wettest month (253 mm), July the driest (1.5 mm); relative humidity under 30 % in the dry season; mean 21.4 °C [stated: Wikipedia "Brasília"]. The plateau stands about 1,100 m above the sea [inferred: well known; it is the reason of the clear, hard light]. The soil is red laterite [inferred: the cerrado's soil; every photograph of the early construction shows red earth].
- **Moment shown (1970).** The Congress and the Esplanade are ten years old, the Cathedral has just opened without its later stained glass (see §1b), the TV tower (1967) and the Teatro Nacional (begun 1960s, unfinished) are outside the area shown [stated: Wikipedia "Cathedral of Brasília" for the Cathedral's dates; the rest inferred].

## 1a. The National Congress (Niemeyer, 1957–1960)

- "Two towers, each with 28 floors", and two domes; the building stands "on elevated ground in the Praça dos Três Poderes, at the end of the Eixo Monumental" [stated: Câmara dos Deputados "Palácio do Congresso Nacional"]. Oscar Niemeyer designed it; Lúcio Costa planned the city [stated: same].
- "The Senate is the semi-sphere on the left and the Chamber of Deputies is the semi-sphere on the right" (seen from the Esplanade) [stated: Wikipedia "National Congress of Brazil"]. The Senate's is the convex dome; the Chamber's is the inverted bowl, open to the sky [stated: common knowledge of the building; the two silhouettes are the city's emblem]. **[inferred]** Seen from the Esplanade (looking east) the left is north: the dome on the north, the bowl on the south.
- "A two-story plinth carries the assembly chambers. Costa's avenues meet it"; "the plinth roof is marble-clad. Niemeyer intended it as a public plaza" [stated: ArchEyes "National Congress"]. The ramp to the roof rises from the lawn in front [inferred: the building's well-known approach ramp].
- The towers stand between the dome and the bowl, side by side, joined near their tops [inferred: from photographs; ArchEyes describes their plan as five-sided with angled façades; here slabs of 28 m × 11 m, 100 m high (28 floors of 3.5 m), 30 m apart, the bridge at floors 25–26].
- Size of the domes: the sources found give no diameters. **[inferred]** from the photographs' proportions: the Senate dome about 48 m across and 14 m high, the Chamber's bowl about 68 m across and 11 m deep, both on the roof deck.
- Interior: the Senate and the Chamber are each an amphitheatre of tiered seats round a presiding table under the dome or bowl, lit from above [inferred: the type of a parliamentary chamber; the Chamber's plenary seats 513, the Senate's 81 — the numbers of members, stated by the Brazilian constitution]. Art in the building includes works by Di Cavalcanti, Athos Bulcão, Alfredo Ceschiatti and Niemeyer [stated: Câmara]. Workers left handwritten messages on the slabs in 1959 [stated: Câmara].
- Built: the Congress stands on a platform 80 m deep and 240 m long with two reflecting pools beside the ramp [inferred: scaled from the plan; the pools are a documented feature of the front but their size is ours].

## 1b. The Metropolitan Cathedral (Niemeyer, with Joaquim Cardozo)

- Cornerstone 12–15 September 1958; "structure finished 21 April 1960, with only the roof visible above ground"; consecrated 12 October 1968 "still without a roof"; opened 31 May 1970 [stated: Wikipedia "Cathedral of Brasília"].
- A hyperboloid of revolution with "16 identical concrete columns, assembled on site, each weighing 90 tonnes", which "represent two hands moving upwards to heaven"; the roof of "16 fiberglass pieces, each 10 m wide at the base and 30 m long, set between the concrete pillars"; "70-meter diameter" [stated: same].
- "Mostly below ground. Only the roof, the baptistery's ovoid roof, and the bell tower are visible"; "visitors enter through a tunnel and come out into a bright space with a glass roof"; the reflecting pool is "12 m wide and 40 cm deep, surrounding the roof … visitors pass under it" [stated: same].
- The stained glass (about 2,000 m², blue, green, white and brown) is Marianne Peretti's of 1990 [stated: same]. **[inferred]** For the moment shown (1970) the roof is coloured fibreglass in the same four hues: the model glazes it in blue, green, white and brown panels between the columns.
- Four bronze Evangelists of 2.5 m by Alfredo Ceschiatti and Dante Croce (1968) stand in the entrance square; the bell tower, 20 m tall, stands to the right of the entrance with four bells; the oval baptistery to the left (its tiles by Athos Bulcão are of 1977 — not yet in 1970) [stated: same].
- Inside: three angels hang from steel cables over the nave (2.22 m, 3.4 m and 4.25 m, by Ceschiatti and Croce, 1970); the altar was a gift of Paul VI; a small chapel under the altar; capacity 4,000 [stated: same].
- **[inferred]** Sizes of our model: column feet on a ring of radius 35 m at ground level, waist radius 12 m at 18 m height, the crown ring radius 17 m at 30 m; the nave floor 4.5 m below the plaza, reached by an open ramp and a covered tunnel under the pool.

## 1c. The Three Powers Square and the Esplanade

- The square is named for the three powers around it: the Palácio do Planalto (executive), the Congress, the Supreme Federal Court (judiciary) [stated: Wikipedia "Praça dos Três Poderes"]. It holds Ceschiatti's "A Justiça" and Bruno Giorgi's "Os Guerreiros" (the Candangos) [stated: same] and the national flag, "about 90 kg … not taken down since the capital was inaugurated on 21 April 1960, except for monthly replacements" [stated: same].
- The Planalto and the Supremo palaces are Niemeyer's, both with the slender curved concrete columns of the Alvorada ("Alvorada columns") in white, round a glass box [inferred: the style of Niemeyer's 1957–1960 palaces: the columns' section is a swelling, pointed profile, in white concrete]. Sizes ours: the Planalto 100 m × 36 m, three floors; the Supremo 70 m × 28 m.
- The Ministries: "identical white slabs in two files" [stated: Studio Matrx]. **[inferred]** Slabs of ten floors (about 38 m), curtain-walled in green-tinted glass in a white concrete frame, standing on the lawn's flanks with their short ends to the avenues; the Foreign Ministry (Itamaraty) apart, a glass block under a colonnade of arches in a pool, and the Justice Palace with its cascading water curtains, both at the Congress end, facing each other across the lawn.
- **The compressed axis.** The real Esplanade is about 2 km long and the superquadras are several kilometres from the Congress; the settlement's disc is 520 m. **[inferred]** The axis is shown 450 m long (nine ministries on each side where seventeen stand in all), and one superquadra stands south of it at a distance of 200 m, so that all three scales of the plan can be walked.

## 1d. The superquadra (Costa)

- "Roughly 280 m square"; "apartment slabs are six storeys tall" standing "on pilotis, so the ground plane stays open and green beneath them"; "each superquadra is ringed by a belt of trees and an access road. Its buildings turn inward toward shared lawns"; "groups of four superquadras share schools, a church, and a strip of local commerce"; "the car is kept to the ring road" [stated: Studio Matrx essay]. The Residential Axis has 96 superblocks limited to six storeys and 12 limited to three storeys [stated: Wikipedia "Brasília"]; "a belt of tall trees and lower vegetation", and Burle Marx's landscape gardens [stated: same].
- **[inferred]** A block: 80 m × 12 m, six storeys over open pilotis of 3.4 m, a flat roof with the water tank and stair head, the end walls blank and painted or tiled (Athos Bulcão's azulejo panels), the long fronts with balconies, cobogó screens or sunshades in concrete; two blocks in four stand on the plot's edge, the rest facing inward; our superquadra is 240 m square (compressed from 280 m) with eight blocks, a belt of trees and the ring road.

## 4. The style (`brasilia`)

Niemeyer's modernism: white reinforced concrete in plain, sculptural shapes (the dome, the bowl, the hyperboloid, the Alvorada columns), glass curtain walls, reflecting pools, marble paving; Costa's pilotis, the six-storey slab and the green belt. ## 7. Sky and climate

## 8. Paths
