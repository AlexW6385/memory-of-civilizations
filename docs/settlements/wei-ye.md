## 1. The moment and the real place

- **Moment shown:** about AD 215, the Jian'an era. Cao Cao, Chancellor of Han and soon King of Wei, rules from Ye; the Emperor is far away at Xu. After taking Ye in 204 he rebuilt it on the model of an imperial capital, digging canals for irrigation and drainage, and raised the Wenchang Hall as the centre of its palace [stated: Wikipedia "Ye (Hebei)"]. The Bronze Bird (Sparrow) Terrace was built in 210 in the north-west corner of Ye; the two lower terraces followed in 213, the Ice Well perhaps finished in 214 [stated: Wikipedia "Bronze Bird Terrace"].
- **What a visitor would have felt:** a new, severe capital laid out on a plan, not grown; the three terraces rising over the north-west of the city like hills of rammed earth; Cao Cao's sons and the Jian'an poets ascending the terrace to write (Cao Zhi's "Rhapsody on Ascending the Terrace", 212, the only one of the three rhapsodies to survive whole) [stated: Wikipedia "Bronze Bird Terrace"].
- **Why it matters:** the first Chinese capital laid out on one central axis, with the palace in the north and the markets and wards south of a main avenue; the model for the Northern Wei, Sui and Tang capitals [stated: DESIGN §7; inferred: the later lineage, standard account].

## 3. Ground, climate, sky

Flat alluvial plain of the Zhang River, about 36° N: dry-winter, wet-summer, dust-haze sky; millet and wheat in the fields, willows and elms at the river [inferred]. Yellow-grey beaten earth in streets and wards; paved crowns of the terraces. Sky and fog as Han Chang'an, a little clearer and cooler (lat 36.3).

## 4. The landmark — the Bronze Sparrow Terrace (the Bronze Bird Terrace, 铜雀台)

- **Size [stated: Wikipedia "Bronze Bird Terrace"]:** "Originally 10 zhang (about 23 m). The other two terraces were 8 zhang (about 18 m)." The bases measured today: 122 m north–south, 70 m east–west, for the Bronze Bird and Metal Tiger. On top stood pavilions of more than 100 rooms each: Bronze Bird 101, Metal Tiger 109, Ice Well 145 (the Wen Xuan). The roof of the pavilion was topped with a bronze bird. Beneath the terraces were storerooms for grain, salt, water, fuel, graphite and, under the Ice Well, ice. The terraces were linked to each other and to the palace by raised walkways and had street-level access.
- **Built here (compressed):** Sparrow: base 58 × 60 m, 15 m high in four stepped, battered lifts of rammed earth (the lifts show as bands), a broad stair of 9 m up its east face to the crown, arched storeroom doors at its foot; on the crown a three-storey tower of red timber, plaster and lattice under three tiers of hipped grey-tile roofs, topped by a gilded bird on a mast, its ground storey enterable (furnished: mats, low tables, lamps, a screen), and two side halls of five bays. Golden Tiger and Ice Well: 38 m deep bases, 12 m high, each carrying a pavilion rank. Between them, covered skyways of timber (gé dào) at crown height with a roof, a lattice wall and a stair at each end. [inferred: all proportions and the tower's three storeys; the source's "five storeys" round pavilion is a later account not shown as such — the tower stands at 15 + 12 = 27 m, about the "10 zhang and its pavilion" proportion]
- **Moment inside:** the terrace is the place of banquets and poems: Cao Cao brought his sons to the terrace and Cao Zhi wrote at once [stated: Wikipedia]; the ground storey shows mats, tables, lamps, a screen; officials and scholars sit about.

### 4.1 The Wenchang Hall (文昌殿)

The "Hall of Civil Splendour", the centrepiece of the palace complex [stated: Wikipedia "Ye (Hebei)"]. Built here after the Han hall pattern (nine bays on a low terrace, hipped roof, round eave tiles) read-only via the `han` hall, furnished with throne, mats and tables; the Wei dress is the late-Han dress [inferred]. Only the plan's centre and its being the palace's main hall are sourced; its size is invented to fit.

## 5. Style vocabulary (style `wei`)

Late Eastern Han timber architecture: round red-lacquered columns on stone plinths, straight bracket arms (no cascading dougong yet), grey-tile hipped and gabled roofs with round eave tiles, plaster walls and lattice windows, rammed-earth platforms and walls [inferred: as Han, no great change to c. 215]. The style `wei` reuses the `han` elements for walls, gates, houses, offices, shops, hall, corridor and pond (read-only), and adds: the battered terrace with its storerooms and stair (`wei_terrace`), the tiered tower (`wei_tower`), the skyway (`wei_skyway`), the river (`wei_river`), the Wenchang Hall's forecourt furniture and the city gates' guard lines.

## 6. The town

Continuous street fronts of walled wards (lǐ) with courtyard houses, gatehouses, and the offices (shu) of the Wei court along the avenue; the market with its tower; a military capital, so more guards, more soldiers on the streets and on the wall [inferred].

## 7. People

Officials in black or red robes crossed right over left, the cap; scholars and poets (Jian'an: Cao Pi, Cao Zhi, the Seven Masters) in white and blue; soldiers in grey coats with the halberd; merchants; women in the curved-hem robe, hair in a bun; porters and carriers; boys. [inferred: Han dress as in the Han Chang'an dossier]

## 8. Sound

The qin, the yu and sheng, bells and drums as at Han Chang'an; in the Sparrow's hall a low chant; wind on the terrace; swallows and crows. Cicadas and frogs by the river in summer [inferred].

## 9. Weathering

Dry north China: weathered beaten earth, plaster chipped at the foot, tile dark; dust. No damp, waterline at the pond and the river.

## 11. Deepening pass — what was added and on what ground

- **The tower's second storey (enterable).** The Bronze Sparrow's tower now has a stair of twenty steps along the north wall of the ground storey rising through the ceiling to a second-storey room (a poets' loft: mats, low tables, a zither, shelves of bamboo-slip bundles, lamp trees) behind lattice walls under the great hipped roof; a pent roof (ping-zuo eave) rings the second storey over the ground storey's ceiling. The third storey stays solid under the gilded bird. [inferred: the Bronze Bird Terrace's pavilion was of several storeys and the Wen Xuan was written by the circle that met on it; the loft is a reading of "pavilions of more than 100 rooms" (Wikipedia "Bronze Bird Terrace") as rooms to be furnished]
- **Bronze, banners, drums and bells.** Cauldron-tripods (ding), braziers, lamp trees, banner poles (red and black pennants), the audience drum and bell frames stand on the terraces, the dais, the Wenchang Hall's terrace and in the palace forecourt; que (gate towers) flank the palace's south gate. [inferred: Han court ritual; the que as a gate form is attested in Han tomb-reliefs and the Han Chang'an dossier]
- **The fleet's pond.** A tower-ship (louchuan), two fighting ships with hide-shield screens and a skiff float on the Xuanwu pond. [stated: the pond was dug in 203 to train Cao Cao's navy (Wikipedia "Ye (Hebei)" as dossier §2)]; the vessel types are [inferred: standard account].
- **Houses.** Own builders in `src/styles/wei/houses.js`: court house (hip-roofed hall for the richer third), row house (a third to a half under thatch), watch-tower house (lou with a beacon drum), side wings as workrooms; six furnished rooms by trade (dwelling, kitchen with mud-brick stove, loom room with a frame loom and spinning wheel, smithy with forge and bellows, scribe's room with bamboo-slip shelves, store with grain jars); half the lots enterable. Gates carry a bench or mounting block and a lamp bracket; fronts are weathered (plaster fallen to the earth at the foot and corners, damp, rain streaks under the eave); yards have a well-sweep, millet stack, pole pen, clothes line, jars. [inferred: Han tomb models and reliefs of courtyard houses, granaries on posts, well-sweeps, pens, stoves and looms are the standard evidence; the trades chosen for a military capital's wards]
- **Shops** of the market have a door through the counter, a back shelf of stock, a stool, a hanging board or a streamer, twelve trades. [inferred]
- **Streets.** The avenue gets dressed stone kerbs and a paved royal way down its middle (the middle of the great avenue reserved for the ruler [inferred: standard account of Ye's axial plan, DESIGN §7]); booths, timber, jars, hitching rails along the wards' north wall; boards and benches at every ward gate (the ward drum marked the closing of the gates [inferred: standard account of the Han ward]); five more offices flank the palace gate. The market's cross lanes carry stalls under awnings; a Hu merchants' camel train stands in the west lane.
- **The wall's top** carries banners, braziers, straw watch huts and halberd racks, drums and bell frames beside each gate. [inferred]
- **The granary and barracks compound** (84…226 × −52…−17): a walled yard of twenty-eight granaries on posts and three barracks halls, between the Qili ward and the offices. [inferred: Ye was a military capital with large stores; Wikipedia "Bronze Bird Terrace" says the terraces themselves held grain, salt and fuel stores]
- **The river** gets three piers with a ferry and a grain barge each, nine skiffs and barges on the water, nets drying on poles; **farmsteads** stand outside the gates with millet stacks, wells and field-watchers' huts; roads run out of the gates. [inferred]
- **Planting.** Tongque Garden clumps of Chinese pine, arborvitae, elm, pagoda tree and persimmon; arborvitae along the palace's side walls; reeds and willows on the river's banks.
- **Life.** Walkers by trade (poets and clerks added), more of them (84 by day); carp in the river, swallows along the ward lanes and the river, crows over the compound and the south, sparrows on the wall's coping, egrets and herons on the river bank; incense smoke at the stairs' braziers and the palace's forecourt, cookhouse smoke at the compound.
