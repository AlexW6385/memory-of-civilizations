## 1. The moment

- **Moment shown:** c. 637–642, the years the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang spent here; the abbot is Śīlabhadra, the teacher he studied Yogācāra under. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"; Britannica "Nalanda"]
- Xuanzang recorded about 1,510 teachers: about 1,000 could explain twenty collections of sutras and treatises, 500 thirty, and ten fifty. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Teaching: Mahayana and the works of the eighteen schools, the Vedas, logic (hetuvidyā), grammar, medicine, the Atharvaveda and Samkhya. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Ours: no figure counts (the traditional 10,000 students are not drawn); a few hundred walkers by day at most, monks in ochre and maroon, lay students and teachers, porters, villagers. [inferred]

## 2. What the sources say of the place

- "Towers, pavilions and temples seemed to soar above the mists"; "an azure pool with blue lotuses winds around the monasteries". [stated: Xuanzang as quoted in Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- "A brick wall enclosed the whole establishment; one gate opened into the great college, which had eight other halls." [stated: Xuanzang, Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- A seventh-century inscription: "rows of monasteries with their series of turrets licking the clouds"; the monasteries stand "in tidy lines". [stated: Lion's Roar "The Dharma was built on these bricks"]
- Yijing (late 7th c.) describes eight viharas and great pools in and around the campus where monks bathed each morning. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Excavation found eleven monasteries and six great brick temples in a row; the whole structure is red brick, many bricks still blackened by an ancient fire. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"; Lion's Roar]
- About 108 monasteries are said once to have stood; eleven are excavated. [stated: shunya.net site pages]

## 4. The monastery (Monastery 1, the landmark)

- Oldest and most important of the group; up to nine building levels, at least two storeys high, a colossal seated Buddha. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Monasteries generally have two or more floors, 30–40 rooms a floor, one or two monks a room; a courtyard with a pillared pavilion or covered balcony; an octagonal well; a podium that held a Buddha image, served as a lectern, or both; across from it a shrine (chaitya) with a Buddha image; neighbouring monasteries joined by narrow catwalk corridors; Monastery 1's entrance faces the sunrise. [stated: shunya.net site pages]
- Niches in the room walls; original plaster survives; lime-concrete floors. [stated: shunya.net; UNESCO dossier]
- **Ours:** 52 × 46 m on a plinth of dressed courses; walls of brick (courses 9 cm, bricks about 37 cm); a ring of cells (3.4 m between partitions) on two storeys round a court, each with a doorway on a verandah of brick piers; a cell has a brick bed, a lamp niche, a pot; the upper floor on a gallery reached by a brick stair in the north verandah; a flat lime-concrete roof with a low parapet over the cells and a pent roof of tiles over the upper gallery; the west gate a vaulted-looking passage with a porch; in the court an octagonal well, the lecture platform (a podium with a seat and steps) and across from the gate a brick shrine with a seated Buddha. [inferred from the above; sizes inferred]
- The cell sizes (about 3 × 2.5 m inside) and wall thickness (about 1 m, thicker at the gate) are our estimates. [inferred]

## 5. Temple 3 (the Great Monument)

- A huge solid structure in the middle of a court, round it small votive stūpas; its rows of niches hold stucco Buddhas and bodhisattvas; flights of stairs up the north side, from several phases; four corner towers; a shrine at the top once held a Buddha. [stated: shunya.net; Wikipedia]
- **Ours:** four tiers, each wall about 5 m, setback 5.2 m; a continuous stair up the east face (riser 0.22 m); on every tier a row of stucco niches with a seated Buddha; stūpa-towers at the corners of every terrace; on the top platform four corner towers and the central shrine with its stepped roof and finial, a doorway east and the Buddha inside. The brick is plastered white-lime above the plinth in the style of the stucco panels [inferred].

## 6. The library

- Three multi-storeyed buildings of the Dharmaganja ("Piety Mart"); Ratnodadhi nine storeys. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- **Ours:** three brick towers in a walled yard, the tallest nine storeys of 3.8 m stepping in at the cornices, slit windows in rows; the lowest has an enterable ground hall with shelves of palm-leaf manuscripts in bundles and a copyists' desk. Palm leaves bundled between boards are the form of the books [inferred: period practice].

## 7. Materials and weather

Red kiln-fired brick, lime plaster and stucco, timber lintels and beams, tile or thatch pent roofs, lime-concrete floors; local stone seldom. The climate is the hot monsoon plain: hazy dry season, heavy rain July–September, so brick feet darkened and greened, plaster streaked. [inferred]

## 8. Planting

Mango groves (Xuanzang's monastery was set in them: "the mango groves" are the usual detail of the accounts), pipal (the sacred fig), neem, palms (tala), lotus in the tanks, dry grass, trampled earth. [inferred]

## 9. People

Monks in ochre and maroon robes, shaven, barefoot; novices carrying books and water; lay students in white; teachers in debate in the court (the pair of debaters, one standing and clapping); villagers with pots and baskets; porters with poles; a cow and a dog. [inferred: Xuanzang records lectures and debate daily; shunya.net]

## 10. Sound

Chanting from the shrines, the tap of a wooden gong, the debate's claps, doves and crows, cicadas and frogs from the tanks, cattle from the village. Music: a drone with a plucked string in a pentatonic mode (shared with Sanchi) and a recited chant near the shrines. [inferred]
