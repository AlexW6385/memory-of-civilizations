# Nalanda mahavihara, c. 640 (as Xuanzang saw it) — dossier

Settlement id: `nalanda` · Style: `nalanda` (own: brick monasteries, the great temple, the library, tanks), `vernacular` (`house_india`, villagers' houses) · Region: India (Bihar, the Gangetic plain south-east of Patna) · Code: `src/settlements/nalanda/`, `src/styles/nalanda/` · Size class S (radius 190).

## 1. The moment

- **Moment shown:** c. 637–642, the years the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang spent here; the abbot is Śīlabhadra, the teacher he studied Yogācāra under. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"; Britannica "Nalanda"]
- Xuanzang recorded about 1,510 teachers: about 1,000 could explain twenty collections of sutras and treatises, 500 thirty, and ten fifty. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Teaching: Mahayana and the works of the eighteen schools, the Vedas, logic (hetuvidyā), grammar, medicine, the Atharvaveda and Samkhya. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Ours: no figure counts (the traditional 10,000 students are not drawn); a few hundred walkers by day at most, monks in ochre and maroon, lay students and teachers, porters, villagers. [inferred]

## 2. What the sources say of the place

- "Towers, pavilions and temples seemed to soar above the mists"; "an azure pool with blue lotuses winds around the monasteries". [stated: Xuanzang as quoted in Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- "A brick wall enclosed the whole establishment; one gate opened into the great college, which had eight other halls." [stated: Xuanzang, Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- A seventh-century inscription: "rows of monasteries with their series of turrets licking the clouds"; the monasteries stand "in tidy lines". [stated: Lion's Roar "The Dharma was built on these bricks"]
- Yijing (late 7th c.) describes eight viharas and great pools in and around the campus where monks bathed each morning. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Excavation found eleven monasteries and six great brick temples in a row; the whole structure is red brick, many bricks still blackened by an ancient fire. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"; Lion's Roar]
- About 108 monasteries are said once to have stood; eleven are excavated. [stated: shunya.net site pages]

## 4. The monastery (Monastery 1, the landmark)

- Oldest and most important of the group; up to nine building levels, at least two storeys high, a colossal seated Buddha. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- Monasteries generally have two or more floors, 30–40 rooms a floor, one or two monks a room; a courtyard with a pillared pavilion or covered balcony; an octagonal well; a podium that held a Buddha image, served as a lectern, or both; across from it a shrine (chaitya) with a Buddha image; neighbouring monasteries joined by narrow catwalk corridors; Monastery 1's entrance faces the sunrise. [stated: shunya.net site pages]
- Niches in the room walls; original plaster survives; lime-concrete floors. [stated: shunya.net; UNESCO dossier]
- **Ours:** 52 × 46 m on a plinth of dressed courses; walls of brick (courses 9 cm, bricks about 37 cm); a ring of cells (3.4 m between partitions) on two storeys round a court, each with a doorway on a verandah of brick piers; a cell has a brick bed, a lamp niche, a pot; the upper floor on a gallery reached by a brick stair in the north verandah; a flat lime-concrete roof with a low parapet over the cells and a pent roof of tiles over the upper gallery; the west gate a vaulted-looking passage with a porch; in the court an octagonal well, the lecture platform (a podium with a seat and steps) and across from the gate a brick shrine with a seated Buddha. [inferred from the above; sizes inferred]
- The cell sizes (about 3 × 2.5 m inside) and wall thickness (about 1 m, thicker at the gate) are our estimates. [inferred]

## 5. Temple 3 (the Great Monument)

- A huge solid structure in the middle of a court, round it small votive stūpas; its rows of niches hold stucco Buddhas and bodhisattvas; flights of stairs up the north side, from several phases; four corner towers; a shrine at the top once held a Buddha. [stated: shunya.net; Wikipedia]
- **Ours:** four tiers, each wall about 5 m, setback 5.2 m; a continuous stair up the east face (riser 0.22 m); on every tier a row of stucco niches with a seated Buddha; stūpa-towers at the corners of every terrace; on the top platform four corner towers and the central shrine with its stepped roof and finial, a doorway east and the Buddha inside. The brick is plastered white-lime above the plinth in the style of the stucco panels [inferred].

## 6. The library

- Three multi-storeyed buildings of the Dharmaganja ("Piety Mart"); Ratnodadhi nine storeys. [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]
- **Ours:** three brick towers in a walled yard, the tallest nine storeys of 3.8 m stepping in at the cornices, slit windows in rows; the lowest has an enterable ground hall with shelves of palm-leaf manuscripts in bundles and a copyists' desk. Palm leaves bundled between boards are the form of the books [inferred: period practice].

## 7. Materials and weather

Red kiln-fired brick, lime plaster and stucco, timber lintels and beams, tile or thatch pent roofs, lime-concrete floors; local stone seldom. The climate is the hot monsoon plain: hazy dry season, heavy rain July–September, so brick feet darkened and greened, plaster streaked. [inferred]

## 8. Planting

Mango groves (Xuanzang's monastery was set in them: "the mango groves" are the usual detail of the accounts), pipal (the sacred fig), neem, palms (tala), lotus in the tanks, dry grass, trampled earth. [inferred]

## 9. People

Monks in ochre and maroon robes, shaven, barefoot; novices carrying books and water; lay students in white; teachers in debate in the court (the pair of debaters, one standing and clapping); villagers with pots and baskets; porters with poles; a cow and a dog. [inferred: Xuanzang records lectures and debate daily; shunya.net]

## 10. Sound

Chanting from the shrines, the tap of a wooden gong, the debate's claps, doves and crows, cicadas and frogs from the tanks, cattle from the village. Music: a drone with a plucked string in a pentatonic mode (shared with Sanchi) and a recited chant near the shrines. [inferred]

## 12. The way's furniture (deepening pass)

- Lamp pillars of brick with a dish of oil along both edges of the way between the gates; pilgrims' booths (flowers, clay lamps and incense, pots, grain, cloth, vegetables) on the margins; thatched water-sheds with rows of pots; brick benches (otla) against the monasteries' fronts where monks sit. Lamp-lighting and offering of flowers and incense are the ordinary acts at Buddhist shrines; booths and water-sheds are [inferred] from the pilgrim traffic Xuanzang and Yijing describe and from Indian practice of the period (the water-hut, *prapā*); sizes [inferred].
- The way ends north in a great votive stūpa; the enclosure's gate stands at the south end: two brick towers, a lintel and a room over the passage, walls running off. Xuanzang: "one gate opened into the great college". [stated: Wikipedia "Nalanda"]; towers [inferred].

## 13. The monasteries, all of them, and the annexes

- All five monasteries are enterable (the excavated monasteries share one plan: cells round a court). Courts differ: Monastery 1 teaches (podium and mats), two have a great votive stūpa with small ones at its corners and devotees' mats, two a pillared pavilion with a seated Buddha. Cells carry a robe on the partition, a lamp on a stand and a bowl; robes dry on poles between the verandah's piers; a wooden gong hangs at a corner (the wooden gong is how the monastic day was called). [inferred; shunya.net describes the plan, podium, shrine and well]
- Annexes on the lanes east of each monastery: refectory, two kitchens with three hearths each, granary with clay bins, infirmary (medicine, *cikitsāvidyā*, was taught here). [inferred from the taught subjects and the monastic day; Yijing describes the dining and the hospital-like care of the sick monks in India: inferred]
- Temples 2 and 12 have a small top shrine with its Buddha, as Temple 3.
- The library: all three towers have a ground hall of shelves (the tallest at its door a hall of the most sacred manuscripts); Ratnarañjaka has a stair to an upper hall with shelves and reading stands.

## 14. The tanks and the village

- Tanks: brick coping cut at the ghats; lotus leaves and flowers on the water ("an azure pool with blue lotuses winds around the monasteries", Xuanzang, as quoted in Wikipedia "Nalanda"); roofed ghat pavilions on the east tank.
- Village: houses in continuous rows on lanes, yards between (pens with zebu and goats, the potter's with kiln and stacked pots); behind the houses dung-cake stacks, clay grain bins, charpoys, hay; a bazaar of booths on the road to it and along its main street. [inferred: village practice of Bihar]
- Mango/neem groves thickened round the compound; the lanes kept open.

## 15. Detail pass: Temple 2, the faces of the great temple, the monasteries' dressing

- Temple 2 (a stone temple): "a dado of sculptural panels above a moulded plinth", 211 panels (20 each side of the entrance, 57 on each of the other three walls), pilasters between them with pot-and-foliage decoration, some arches pointed; subjects human figures, kinnaras, Siva and Parvati, Kartikeya, Agni, Kubera, Gajalakshmi, the child Gautama, jataka scenes. [stated: ASI report quoted at https://hindubooks.org/temples/bihar/nalanda/page10.htm] Ours: a dado of 0.7 m arched panels with a small figure each and pilasters with a pot, on the stone-faced first tier of Temple 2 (fewer panels than the 211; sizes [inferred]).
- Temple 3: seven building phases, the fifth (6th c.) with stucco figures in niches on the walls and four solid corner towers; stucco "most elaborate at site no. 03"; the only panchayatan (quincunx) chaitya of the property. [stated: https://photodharma.net/India/Nalanda/index.htm; UNESCO nomination https://whc.unesco.org/document/152813] Ours: each tier has a three-course plinth, pilasters between arched stucco niches (chaitya-window frames, [inferred] from the period's brick and stucco vocabulary), a string course and a band of dentils under the cornice; the corner towers are shrine-like with a niche and Buddha on each face and stepped upper stages; the top shrine has a chaitya-arched tympanum over its door, lamps and flower offerings. Forms and sizes [inferred]. (The sources say the temple faces north; we keep the east stair of the earlier plan.)
- Monasteries: plinth of baked brick, pilasters at every cell partition, dentil band under the cornice, a chaitya-window medallion on each cell's plaster band, plaster frames round the slit windows, an arched tympanum with a Buddha over the gate's porch; in the court timber door jambs and plaster pilasters; the verandah's piers with bases and neck rings; the shrine with a doorway tympanum, an aureole frame, lamps, flower heaps and a mat. A shrine in the court was a Pala-period addition at several viharas (Sites 1, 4, 5, 8, 10) [stated: UNESCO nomination]; we keep it at 640 as an [inferred] simplification of the earlier plan.
- Library: the towers step in every second storey, the ledge carrying a small votive stupa at each corner ("turrets licking the clouds", Lion's Roar); windows with arched frames; a crown of parapet, corner stupas and a chamber with niches. [inferred]

## 16. The village houses

Four kinds by lot size and seed: mud-walled under hipped thatch, mud-walled under gabled thatch, plastered under half-round tiles with a verandah, and brick under a flat lime-concrete roof with parapet, chhajja and bench. The front varies by bays, door bay, windows, verandah or platform, front yard, whitewash border round the door. Houses within 175 m of the arrival stand open, their halls furnished for a use: home, weaver (frame loom), grain merchant (clay bins, weighing beam), oil-presser (mortar and lever pestle), garland-maker, a scribe's lodging (mat, desk, palm-leaf bundles, lamp), pilgrims' rest; a partition room holds the clay hearth with a lit lamp, grinding stone and pots. Three brick wells on the lanes. All [inferred] from village practice of Bihar and the pilgrim and student trade of the centre.

Level: L2, with parts of L3 (the landmark's decoration, furnished village houses); missing for L3: weathering and wear, the interiors' light, figures inside houses.

## 17. Landmark detail pass (round 2): relief on the great temple, the shrines, the gates and the votive stupas

Sources: the fifth phase of Temple 3 "was decorated with rows of niches containing well-modeled stucco figures of the Buddha and Bodhisattvas" ("fine specimens of Gupta art"), "four corner towers, of which three have been exposed", stairs of the fifth, sixth and seventh periods, a shrine on top that once held a Buddha, small votive stupas round it [stated: https://shunya.net/Pictures/NorthIndia/Nalanda/Nalanda.htm]; each monastery had a well, often octagonal, gateways at Monasteries 4 and 8, doorways with plaster, bricks blackened by fire [stated: same page]. The forms below follow the vocabulary of Gupta-period brick temples of the plain (moulded plinths, projecting piers between recessed bays, arched niches in deep frames, chaitya-window crowns, receding stages under a pinnacle); that vocabulary is [inferred], as are all sizes.

- **Faces of the temple tiers** (Temples 3, 2, 12, 13): between the moulded plinth and the dentil course each wall is no longer flat. Piers of brick (a base of baked brick, a shaft in courses with a plaster strip on its face, a capital) stand 0.3 m out at every station and at the corners; between them each niche is a deep arched ring of plaster (0.5 m proud of the wall, a reveal 0.4 m deep), a dark recess behind, a stepped panel of the base and a throne; the figures are stucco in high relief (a seated Buddha in dhyāna with a red painted halo; every third niche a standing bodhisattva with a crown), replacing the earlier lathe-turned cones. The red halo is [inferred] (Gupta stucco was painted); a row of miniature stepped shrines runs along every cornice.
- **The stair**: the flights keep their steps but are now flanked by cheek walls that step with them, with a coping of baked brick, and a newel at each foot with a small votive stupa.
- **The shrine on the summit** (and the court shrine of each monastery): a moulded plinth of three courses, piers at the corners and pilasters, a deep niche with a seated Buddha on each of the three blind sides flanked by niches with bodhisattvas, two guardians' niches beside the doorway, string course, dentils, a two-course cornice, a deep chaitya-arched tympanum over the door; above, a roof of receding stages (five on Temple 3, three in the court), each with chaitya windows and miniature shrines at its corners, then neck, amalaka, parasols and finial. The total height of the temple (about 32 m) is kept. The seated Buddha inside is rebuilt with throne, legs, body with arms and lap hands, head with ushnisha and a ten-sided halo.
- **Monasteries' gate and roof**: the four turrets have quoins, string course, arched windows on their two outward faces, a cornice with miniature shrines; the gate tower has pilasters and arched windows on its four faces and shrines on its cornice; the porch has piers with base and capital on its stubs, a lintel course and shrines along its slab; the tympanum with the Buddha over the porch is a deep ring. The cell medallions are plain slabs.
- **Votive stupas**: a plinth of three courses (a baked-brick moulding between two squared ones), a plaster dome, a harmika of two steps, a stack of parasols with a finial.
- Not contradicted: the dossier's four tiers, the east stair, the corner towers on the summit, the shrine door facing east on Temple 3 and west on the monastery shrine. Departures: none; the dentil course is spaced wider (0.56–0.62 m) and the cell pots have fewer facets, to keep the arrival frame within its triangle budget.
