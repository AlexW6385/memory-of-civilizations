## 1. The moment and the real place

- **Moment shown:** about 1200, the high noon of the Pagan kingdom, under Narapatisithu (Sithu II, r. 1174–1211; Sulamani was built in his reign, 1183). Temples are still being raised and gilded; the city is full. [stated: Wikipedia "Bagan": Sulamani 1183 "sponsored by Sithu II"; "more than 10,000 monuments were built"; the capital "from the 9th to 13th centuries"]
- **Place:** the Bagan plain, a bend of the Irrawaddy, "the core is on the riverbank, and a main strip of monuments extends about 9 km along the river's east bank"; the archaeological zone 13 × 8 km, 104 km² of plain, "about 2,229 temples and pagodas remain". [stated: Wikipedia "Bagan"] The Dry Zone: "little rain because the Rakhine Yoma range shelters it", about 740 mm a year, April highs of 37–39 °C. [stated: same]
- **Population:** "50,000 to 200,000" at its height. [stated: same]
- **Why this moment:** the Ananda (1105), Shwezigon (1102), Thatbyinnyu (1150) already old; Sulamani new; the city is walled and the plain fills with merit-making foundations: "about 1,000 stupas, 10,000 small temples, and 3,000 monasteries" were built. [stated: Wikipedia "Bagan"]

## 3. Ground, climate, sky

Flat, sandy, dry plain: pale tan sand and thin soil, dry grass, thorn scrub; palmyra (toddy) palms, neem, acacia, tamarind, a few teak and banyan; the river's bank is sand and silt. The dry season: hot, hazy, dust; the rains (June–Oct, peak August) bring dark stains and moss on the brick. [stated: Wikipedia "Bagan" for the climate; the plants inferred from the Dry Zone's flora and the later travellers' accounts] `damp` is modest (0.5): brick darkened by the monsoon but dry most of the year. The sky: pale, hazy, high sun; fog light and warm.
Canals and tanks: "streams from the Tuyin Range fed tanks and channels, including the Mya Kan reservoir" [stated: dailylifehistory.pages.dev]; we show one village tank. [inferred]

## 4. The Ananda Temple (the landmark)

- **Facts:** [stated: Wikipedia "Ananda Temple"] cruciform with four entrances; two vaulted circumambulatory passages round the central cube; cross passages link the porches to the Buddhas; four standing Buddhas on the cube's faces, each about 9.5 m tall above a 2.4 m throne (Kakusandha north, Konagamana east, Kassapa south, Gautama west), teak and gilded; outer walls 12 m with fortified parapets; a main plinth carrying two receding curvilinear roofs and four receding terraces; the top a small pagoda with a hti; a ringed pagoda at each corner of the outer wall; a stupa finial over each porch; 554 glazed Jataka plaques; 80 stone reliefs in the corridors; walls whitewashed; the spires gilded (1990).
- **Ours:** the plinth 1.2 m, steps at each porch; the outer wall 53 m square, 12 m, with a crenellated parapet and corner stupas; four porches 16 m wide projecting 17 m, each a vaulted hall under a stepped gable, a stupa finial on top; behind the porch the doorway through the outer wall (3.5 m thick) into the outer corridor (vaulted, 3.2 m wide), the opening through the middle wall into the inner corridor, then the cross passage's end at the cube's face where the Buddha stands in his niche, lit from above (a lamp stands in for the clerestory); the corridors lined with bands of carved stone and niches for the images. Above the outer wall the terraces: a walk of 6 m with a parapet and corner stupas, four receding stepped roofs (the curvilinear roofs approximated by steps), the sikhara of bands with niches on each face, the gilded upper spire, the bud and the hti. [inferred: the proportions; the interior plan's dimensions are ours]
- **Colour:** white stucco on the walls and spire; red brick showing at the plinth and worn places; bands of green glaze (the Jataka plaques) along the plinth and terrace edges; gilded finials and the upper spire. [stated for white and gilded; green after "the last ten Jatakas, which are green" — Wikipedia "Ananda Temple"]
- **The Buddhas:** each a standing figure (a column of cloth in folds with the shoulders, head and the ushnisha), gilded, on a lacquered throne, in a niche with a pointed-arch frame; the south one (Kassapa) with the sad-close, mirthful-far face is not modelled in detail. [stated for the trick; the modelling inferred]
- **Kneeling images:** lacquer statues of Kyansittha and the monk Shin Arahan kneel at the west Buddha's feet. [stated: Wikipedia "Ananda Temple"] Ours: two kneeling figures.

## 5. Style vocabulary (style `burmese`)

- **Brick:** large flat bricks, the temples plastered white (stucco) and whitewashed repeatedly; vaulting (true arches and corbelled vaults); pointed arches for doors and passages. [stated: "stucco on brickwork pediments"; "developed vaulting"; "walls are whitewashed and repainted regularly" — Wikipedia "Bagan", "Ananda Temple"]
- **The sikhara:** the curvilinear tower of the Pagan temples rising from the terraces; the stupa of the bell form ("a prototype of Burmese stupas" — Wikipedia "Shwezigon Pagoda"): square terraces, an octagonal platform, a bell, bands, a banana-bud finial, a gilded hti (umbrella). [stated for Shwezigon; the details inferred]
- **Corner stupas / miniature replicas** at the corners of every terrace. [stated: Shwezigon, Ananda]
- **Temple plan types:** the cella-and-porch temple (a square cell with a porch and a sikhara) and the hollow multi-storey temple (Thatbyinnyu, Sulamani); the solid stupa. [stated: Wikipedia "Thatbyinnyu", "Bagan"]
- **Timber:** palace and monastery halls of teak on posts with tiered roofs (pyatthat) and carved fascias; known from later Burma, here a conjecture for the 12th c. since "most homes were likely built from timber, bamboo, thatch, and other perishable materials". [stated: dailylifehistory.pages.dev; shape inferred]
- **Houses:** raised on posts, floors of split bamboo or plank, walls of woven bamboo mats (sasa), steep thatch roofs of palm or grass, a ladder, a veranda, the water pot at the foot. "Raised floors are plausible, but no 11th- or 12th-century house plan confirms a standard layout." [stated: same] [inferred]

## 6. The town

- **City:** the main road from the river ghat through the city to the Tharabar Gate and on to the Ananda's west gate; a cross street; lots of stilt houses with fenced yards, palm and neem shade; the palace compound in the north-west; the Thatbyinnyu's enclosure in the south; monastery compounds. [inferred]
- **Ananda's gate and road:** stalls selling flowers, candles, gold leaf, water and food at the gate, in the shade of banyan and neem. [inferred from the pilgrim custom]
- **The plain:** brick temples and stupas of every size, built by donors "who gave livestock to religious foundations" [stated: dailylifehistory.pages.dev], villages and tanks among them; paths of beaten sand.
- **Trades:** "brick-making, moulding, firing, vaulting, plastering, stucco work, timber carving, painting, and making religious objects"; "earthenware at Otein Taung: vessels, roof tiles, well linings". [stated: dailylifehistory.pages.dev] Ours: potters' stacks, brick stacks at the temple works, a kiln's smoke.

## 7. People (dress, bearing)

Little clothing survives; "murals and sculpture show draped garments, robes, and jewellery". [stated: dailylifehistory.pages.dev] Ours: men and women in the wrapped cloth (the ancestor of the longyi) and a shoulder cloth, bare feet; women with the hair in a knot and water pots or baskets on the head; farmers with the shoulder pole and the conical straw hat; monks in the maroon-saffron robe, the head shaven, one shoulder bare, some with the alms bowl, some with a parasol; pilgrims carrying offerings; boys; boatmen.
