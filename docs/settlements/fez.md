## 4. The landmarks

### 4.1 The Bou Inania Madrasa (L2–L3)

- **Size and siting.** Built 1350–55 by Abu Inan; 34.65 × 38.95 m, on the south side of the Talaa Kebira [stated]. Ours the same footprint.
- **Entrance.** A horseshoe portal and a vestibule with a carved cedar ceiling; a bent rear entrance on the Talaa Seghira [stated]. Ours the portal, steps and vestibule; the rear entrance bent as a short passage [inferred].
- **The court.** Paved in marble with a central ablution fountain and a canal along the south edge with two small bridges [stated]. Ours the basin, the channel and two bridges.
- **Galleries.** A narrow gallery on three sides with wooden screens between pillars [stated]; ours pillars clad in zellij, cedar spindle screens [inferred: the pattern].
- **Classrooms.** Two classrooms of 5 m square east and west, with cedar doors and wooden cupolas [stated]; ours furnished with rahles and rugs [inferred].
- **Prayer hall.** On the south, an arcade on marble and onyx columns, a stucco mihrab, a berchla ceiling [stated]; ours with a minbar and green-tiled gables [inferred].
- **Minaret.** Of brick at the north-west corner, tiled in darj wa ktaf zellij [stated]; ours 24 m with a gallery, a lantern and a green pyramid [inferred: height].
- **Students' quarters.** On the upper floor, by stairs at the north-west and north-east corners [stated]; ours a platform with a balustrade and closed cell doors; the stairs are not built [inferred].
- **Walls.** A dado of zellij, over it a band of sgraffito epigraphic tiles, then carved stucco, and carved cedar under a canopy on corbels [stated]; ours the same order.
- **Dar al-Magana.** Opposite, a front of 13 carved cedar consoles (once bronze bowls), 12 windows in stucco frames, two rows of corbels; the water clock is reported in 1357 [stated]; ours the front with the consoles and windows, without the mechanism.
- **Al-Attarine.** Built 1323–25 by Abu Sa'id, L-shaped bent entrance, 30 student rooms [stated]; not built.

### 4.2 The Qarawiyyin Mosque (L2)

- The original hall was about 36 × 32 m, later extended by the Almoravids [stated]; ours a great hall of transverse naves round a court, 14 naves [inferred: the count; Wikipedia gives ten aisles in the Almoravid extension].
- Minaret 26.75 m, whitewashed by the Marinids [stated]. Ours at that height.
- A court with a fountain [stated]. The Saadian pavilions (1587–1609) are later and left out [stated: dates].
- 18 gates [stated]; Almoravid bronze doors [stated]; ours doors on the west and blind bronze doors.
- The anaza screen of 1289, a chandelier of 1337 cast from the Gibraltar bell, the library of 1349, water clocks [stated]; ours the lamps and the mihrab; the rest left out.

### 4.3 The souks

- The Attarine souk is roofed along the Talaa Kebira and named for its spice sellers [stated]; the Kissaria lies on the south side [stated]. Ours covered lanes with cedar-slat roofs, shops raised on a cell with piers and a lintel, goods by trade: spice, perfume, henna, candles, cloth, embroidery, slippers, brass, leather [inferred: the arrangement].
- The Place Seffarine with coppersmiths [inferred from the name and the trade].

### 4.4 The Chouara tannery

- Baths of lime, cow urine, salt and pigeon dung for two to three days; kneading by bare feet [stated: Wikipedia; Amusing Planet].
- Dyes: poppy (red), indigo (blue), henna (orange), cedar (brown), mint (green), saffron (yellow) [stated]. Work is done by hand, waist-deep; viewed from the shop terraces; mint sprigs for the smell [stated].
- Ours a grid of 12 × 10 stone vats with wider aisles, leather shops on the north side, a terrace on arches with a stair, hides drying on the roofs [inferred: the vat count, the drying and the layout; none given].

## 5. Houses and streets

- Courtyard houses (dar) with a central court (wast ad-dar), a bent entrance, tall narrow plans, zellij dados, carved stucco, cedar doors and lintels, plain exteriors [stated: Wikipedia "Historic house architecture in Morocco"; Maghrebi Voices]. Derbs end in guarded doors and some streets are roofed [stated].
- Houses were first single-storey and raised later [stated]; ours of one to three storeys [inferred]. Rich houses have the full zellij and a salon; ordinary ones a flat mosaic dado [inferred].
- Funduq: several storeys round a court, with a portal and carved ceilings, for workshops and lodging [stated: Wikipedia "Funduq"]; ours a court with arcaded galleries, stalls, donkeys.

## 6. Materials

Tile (zellij) in green, cream, lapis and ochre; carved stucco in cream and ochre; cedar; limewashed and ochre stucco and mud plaster; green-glazed tile roofs; brick for the minarets; marble for the courts; rubble and lime for the wall and quays; wood-slat roofs for the souks [stated in part; the colours inferred]. Dry, sunlit weathering; flat terrace roofs on the houses [inferred].

## 7. People

Fassi men in djellabas and turbans, scholars and students in white, merchants, artisans (coppersmiths, tanners, slipper makers), water-carriers, porters, veiled women in haiks, donkeys with muleteers [stated in general; the dress inferred from Maghrebi dress of the period]. Students and a teacher in the Bou Inania; worshippers in the Qarawiyyin; tanners waist-deep in the vats.
