## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Angkor Wat, built for Suryavarman II in the first half of the 12th century; unusually facing west. [stated: ancientcivilizations; general]
- **Moment shown:** completed, c. 1150, as a Vishnu temple.

## 3. Orientation

West-facing: the visitor arrives from the west along the causeway. [stated]

## 4. The style

Grey sandstone over laterite cores; towers shaped as lotus buds, redented in plan, rising in receding tiers with antefixes; galleries with square pillars and corbelled vaults, half-galleries; cross-shaped gate pavilions; terraces with stepped mouldings; steep axial stairs; naga balustrades. [stated: general; Notre Dame figure captions]

## 5. The village

Houses of timber raised on posts about 2–2.5 m, plank or woven walls, steep gabled roofs of palm thatch, a ladder to the floor. [inferred from Khmer vernacular and the Bayon reliefs]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat, wet plain; moat water 1.2 m down over a bed at 3.2 m; two basins before the temple. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

Sugar palms (a fan palm stands in) in the village and the enclosure, a few banyans and teaks; rice fields beyond the village. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

The end of the dry season: hot, hazy, towering clouds, an afternoon shower now and then. Latitude 13° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

The temple ensemble (gong circles, xylophones, oboe) — a plucked string and a reed stand in — and chant in the galleries. [inferred]

## 11. The cruciform cloister and the Bakan's stairs (2026-11-06)

**Preah Poan.** The cross-shaped galleries "a Khmer architectural invention" linking the first and second levels; the Hall of a Thousand Buddhas reached from them [stated: penang-traveltips]. "Two covered galleries in a cross shape, supported by square columns", the court "divided into four equal parts with paved basins and steps"; the Hall of Echoes "the gallery on the left" of the cross-shaped galleries, named for its acoustics [stated: Phnom Penh Post]. A basin at each corner of the cruciform gallery; doorways north, east, south and west under carved pediments; praying ascetics carved on the pillar bases; rosettes on the beams [stated: helloangkor]. Ours: between the third gallery's west entrance and the second level, the axial gallery from the entrance to the foot of the stair up to the second level and the transverse gallery across it, both on square pillars on a moulded plinth, a square crossing under a taller vault with a pediment on each face; on the north (the left, coming in from the west) the Hall of Echoes walled on both sides, lit by balustered windows, entered only from the crossing gallery; on the south a gallery open on pillars to the court; in each of the four courts a sunken basin about 12.8 × 7.8 m, its sides stepped down in three tiers of 0.5 m to a paved floor, the water over the lowest tier [inferred: sizes and tiers ours].

**The Bakan's stairs.** "Twelve sets of stairs with forty steps each, ascending at a seventy-degree angle" to the upper level [stated: Phnom Penh Post]; "breakneck steep" [stated: penang-traveltips]. Ours: the four axial stairs, forty steps of 0.325 m over 6.5 m (about 63°, a little gentler than stated, to stay walkable), 3.2 m wide between stepped cheek walls rising with them in flights of four steps; the eight others left for later [inferred].

## 14. L3: gilding, the surfaces at 1150, the market, elephants, palanquins, sugar palms (2026-11-07)

- **Gilded crowns.** The temple's "gilded stucco on the towers" and "gilding on some figures on the bas-reliefs" have been lost to looting and time; a 16th-century Portuguese account (Diogo do Couto after António da Madalena) speaks of "many smaller towers of similar style, in the same stone, which are gilded" [stated: Wikipedia, Angkor Wat]. Ours: the lotus-bud crowns of the five towers of the Bakan and their two registers of petals in gilded stucco (`clay_gilt`); the tiers below left in stone, since how far down the gilding ran is not recorded [inferred].
- **The market.** Zhou Daguan (1296): "There is a market every day from around six in the morning until midday. There are no stalls, only a kind of mat laid out on the ground"; traders paid officials for their place; small purchases in rice or grain, larger in cloth, the largest in gold and silver [stated: National Library of Australia]; "the local people who know how to trade are all women" [stated: Wikipedia, The Customs of Cambodia]. Ours: west of the moat road, south of the causeway's foot (`town.js` `MARKET`), five rows of reed mats with greens, fish, baskets, pots, melons, cloth and spices, a woman sitting cross-legged or squatting behind each; buyers in the aisles, some with baskets on the head [inferred: the place and the goods]. Zhou wrote 150 years after our moment; the market's manner is taken as unchanged [inferred].
- **Elephants, palanquins, parasols.** "Ministers and princes are mounted on elephants"; the king's wives travel "in palanquins, carriages, on horseback and on elephants"; "more than one hundred parasols, flecked with gold" in the king's procession [stated: Wikipedia, The Customs of Cambodia]; the procession relief shows palanquins borne on poles (§13). Ours: on the moat road a minister's elephant in a red caparison with a seat on its back and its mahout on the neck, a parasol-bearer walking beside it; a lady in a palanquin — a cloth hammock slung from a red lacquered pole with gilt, upturned ends — on two bearers' shoulders, a parasol carried over her, her maid behind with a basket; a working elephant with only its mahout; an ox-cart; buffalo by the fields [inferred: the hammock form after the reliefs, the colours].
- **Sugar palms.** The doum palm that stood in for the sugar palm branches; the sugar palm (*Borassus flabellifer*) does not. Ours: 267 sugar palms placed by `town.js` — singly on a loose 26 m grid over the fields (the dykes), round the houses, a few in the enclosure — each a straight trunk of about 12–17 m swelling at the foot, a round head of some thirty fan leaves on long stalks, a skirt of dead leaves below; one in four with the tapper's bamboo ladder tied up the trunk. Sugar palms are grown on the rice-field dykes, "bamboo ladders are tied to the sugar palm trees" to collect the sap, the leaves used for wickerwork and roofs [stated: WOCAT, Sugar palms on rice field dykes (Cambodia)]; their presence at Angkor in 1150 [inferred].

## 16. Movers

Walkers on the moat road and in the market's aisles (30 by day, 4 at night, thinned in rain): women in the wrapped cloth with the breast bare, the hair in a knot, some with a water pot or a basket of goods on the head; men in the wrapped cloth; farmers with the shoulder pole, two baskets and the conical straw hat; a Brahmin in white; children. In the rain the palm-leaf cape and the straw hat. "Men and women alike wrap a cloth round the loins and leave the breast bare" [stated: Zhou Daguan, general; National Library of Australia]. The market's traders are women [stated: Zhou Daguan]. No monks: at c. 1150 the temple is Hindu (§12). Elephants and the ox-cart stand in town.js; none yet walk.

## 17. Animals

Carp in the moat and the ponds, swallows over the moat and the road, egrets at the ponds' edges, sparrows on the road, crows over the towers (`fauna.js`) [inferred].

## 18. Smoke

Kitchen smoke through the thatch of half the houses at the three meals, cooking fires and steam at the market by day, incense in the sanctuary's porch (`smoke.js`) [inferred].
