## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Great Stupa, begun under Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, enlarged and given its stone railing and four gateways by the 1st century CE [stated: `docs/buildings/sanchi-great-stupa.md` and its sources].
- **The site:** the monuments stand on a hilltop about 90 m above the surrounding plain, near Vidisha [stated: Wikipedia "Sanchi"].
- **Moment shown:** the 1st century CE, with the gateways complete.

## 4. The style

Sandstone stupa and gateways (see the stupa's dossier); village houses of mud, plastered and limewashed, under tiled roofs with verandahs. [inferred]

**The gateways' sculpture and Ashoka's pillar (L2–L3, built).** Each gateway has two square pillars "crowned by a set of four lions, elephants or pot-bellied dwarfs", carrying "three curviform architraves with spirally-rolled ends"; "graceful bracket-figures of salabhanjikas" project from the capitals to support the ends of the lowest architrave; the South Gateway was built first [stated: indianetzone]. "The back-to-back lions supporting the Southern Gateway (the oldest one)"; on the East Gateway elephants; "pot-bellied dwarfs support the architraves of the Western Gateway"; the yakshi of the East Gateway is one of Sanchi's best-known images [stated: Lonely Planet]; the south capitals have "four lions, seated back to back" under a square abacus [stated: puratattva]. Ashoka's pillar stood by the South Gateway [stated: puratattva]; its original height "may have just exceeded 31 feet"; the capital is "made in the typical loti form bell-shape", "the abacus is decorated with pairs of geese, alternating with a motif of honeysuckle flowers", and it is "surmounted by four lions with their backs to each other", "about four feet high"; a wheel on top is uncertain and is left off [stated: imp-art].
Ours: the capitals' beasts carved in the round — four seated lions on the south gate of the Great Stupa, four squatting dwarfs with raised arms on the west, four elephants with spread ears and curled trunks on the north and east, and on Stupa 3's single gate [inferred: its capitals not checked; elephants as the commonest type]; the yakshi as a woman in a triple bend leaning out from the post, one leg crossed, her raised hand on a branch of the tree whose trunk springs from the post to its crown under the architrave's end [inferred: pose after the East Gateway yakshi]; the architraves' ends rolled as scrolls with a spiral in relief on both faces. Ashoka's pillar: a polished shaft of 9.5 m (it was 12.8 m before; changed to the stated figure), the bell of sixteen down-turned lotus petals [inferred: the Mauryan bell], a roped torus, the round abacus with four pairs of pecking geese alternating with honeysuckle palmettes, four seated lions 1.2 m high with flame-like manes and open jaws.

## 10. Detail (L3, second pass)

- **Stupa 2.** About 300 m west of the main group on the slope of the hill; begun in the 2nd century BCE, its railing bears the oldest extensive stupa decoration — 455 medallions and half medallions, 293 of them simply a lotus, others a lotus with another motif or elephants, lions, griffins, centaurs, a camel, the triratna; the earliest about 115 BCE [stated: Wikipedia "Sanchi Stupa No. 2"]. It stands on an artificial terrace built against a rock shelf, about 320 m down the slope; its balustrade has four L-shaped (bent) entrances; each post carries one complete and two half medallions, except at the entrances, where the carving runs from top to bottom; it has no gateway; its double stairway faces east [stated: indianetzone "Sanchi Stupa 2"]. Ours: on a terrace 32 m square, 1.5 m high, of rubble with a dressed coping and a paved top, a flight up on the north; the stupa's dome, berm, railing on the berm and double stairway at 0.39 of the Great Stupa (a dome of about 14.3 m) [inferred: size], turned so that the stairway faces east; the ground railing 2.4 m high on a circle of 11.5 m: lens-section posts at about 0.95 m, three lens-shaped bars in each bay, a rounded coping; on both faces of each post a lotus medallion at mid-height and half medallions at the foot and head, their petals 8, 12 or 16 in turn [inferred: lotuses for all, the commonest kind]; the four openings each screened by an L-shaped wing of railing, all turning the same way, so that one enters along the railing and turns in; the posts at the openings carved over their full height in four framed panels [inferred: the panels' subjects not shown]; the processional path paved.
- **Temple 40.** Partly of the 3rd century BCE (Maurya); first an apsidal hall, probably of timber, on a high rectangular stone platform 26.52 × 14 × 3.35 m with stairs on the east and west; later enlarged to 41.76 × 27.74 m, and a pillared hall of fifty columns (5 × 10) built, of which stumps remain, some pillars inscribed in the 2nd century BCE [stated: Wikipedia "Sanchi"]. Ours (the enlarged state, so standing by the 1st century): the platform 41.76 × 27.74 m and 3.35 m high [inferred: its height kept], faced with dressed stone, flights with cheek walls up its east and west ends; fifty pillars at 3.6 m in five rows of ten, square below and octagonal above, with a collar and a block capital, 4.6 m high; beams along the rows and across them; a barrel roof of thatch on curved rafters with a ridge, closed at its ends by timber gables of the horseshoe (caitya) outline with a barred window, as buildings appear in Sanchi's reliefs [inferred: the pillars' form, their spacing and height, the roof].
- **Monks and pilgrims.** Monks in ochre robes, shaven and barefoot, walking round the Great Stupa on the ground walk and the terrace, in Temple 40's hall, at Stupa 2; lay pilgrims in white with turbans, women in red with offerings in baskets and pots, at the south gateway and climbing the north path [inferred: dress after the gateways' reliefs; foreign devotees in Greek dress also appear there — stated: Wikipedia "Sanchi" — not shown].

## 11. Detail added (2026-11 L3 pass: the gateways' reliefs, the railing's coping, offerings, plants)

- **The architraves' reliefs.** On the South Gateway the top architrave shows the birth of the Buddha with elephants pouring water and, on the back, "three stūpas alternating with four trees with thrones in front of them, adored by figures both human and divine"; the middle shows Aśoka at Rāmagrāma — "heavenly figures bearing garlands" above the stūpa, "a retinue of elephants, horsemen and footmen" — and on the back the Chaddanta Jātaka, the Bodhisattva born king of a herd of elephants; the lowest shows dwarfs (kīchakas) "holding garlands in their hands" and on the back the war of the relics, the siege of Kusinārā; "seated elephants on the intervening false capitals" [stated: photodharma, after Marshall]. Ours: each face of each architrave carries a row of scenes cut in shallow relief along the curve of the beam, clear of the posts' crossings — on the top beam stūpas and trees alternating with worshippers; on the middle beam's front a stūpa between garland bearers with footmen, horsemen and elephants coming to it, on its back a herd of elephants among trees; on the lowest beam's front a row of garland bearers, on its back a procession of horsemen, elephants and footmen [stated: the subjects; inferred: the figures' forms, their order and number, the same programme on all four gateways].
- **The posts' panels.** The left pillar's top panel shows "a Persepolitan column … supporting a wheel" with "celestial figures with garlands" on either side, and below groups of worshippers [stated: photodharma]. Ours: in the three panels of each face of each post, a tree of the Enlightenment on its railing, a stūpa, and the wheel on its pillar, each between two worshippers [inferred: one subject per panel].
- **The railing's coping.** The ground and terrace railings' copings (uṣṇīṣa) are now rounded on top, as the timber rail they copy, instead of flat [inferred: the vedikā's rounded coping].
- **Offerings.** Garlands swagged between each gateway's posts and looped over their outer faces; at each post's inner foot flowers heaped on a leaf and a clay lamp; at the foot of the double stairway two offerings and a row of lamps [inferred: after the garland bearers and the offerings brought to the stūpa in the reliefs; flowers white and red, no marigolds — §11 of khajuraho.md]. A woman selling flowers from a mat where the north path reaches the summit [inferred].
- **Plants and weathering.** Dry grass, a few neem, oleander and a pipal (a fig) on the summit, clear of the monuments and the ways between them (it was bare) [inferred]; the climate set damp (0.5) for the materials' streaks and moss [inferred].

## 12. Monastery, votive stupas, life (batch pass)

- **The monastery (vihara), rebuilt.** Sanchi's monasteries are rectangular courts ringed by cells with a doorway each onto a verandah; the surviving ones are of stone and brick on the hill's slopes and summit, most of them later (Gupta and after) than the moment shown [stated: general; see the Sanchi pages at [photodharma](https://photodharma.net/India/Sanchi-Ruins/index.htm), [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/lQVRPVqleWxhZg)]. Ours (a 1st-century brick-and-timber monastery, the form of the later ones) [inferred]: 30 m square; brick walls 4 m high on a dressed-stone base course with a cornice; one entrance on the west; a ring of cells 3.4 m deep, each with a doorway under a timber lintel and a threshold stone, a stone bed (0.9 × 1.9 m) against the outer wall and a water pot; a verandah of stone posts (round base, square foot, octagonal shaft, lotus-bell capital, block and bracket) under a tiled lean-to; the cells under a pitched tiled roof hipped round the ring, with a ridge cap; in the court a well with a stone curb and an open pavilion of the assembly (eight posts on a platform, a thatched pyramid roof, a stone seat for the teacher). The roofs' material (tile, thatch) is inferred; the reliefs show timber and thatch.
- **Votive stupas.** Eight small solid stupas (dome diameters about 4–6 m) scattered on the summit clear of the ways; the hill is crowded with votive stupas of all sizes [stated: Wikipedia "Sanchi"; the number and places inferred].
- **Life.** `movers.js`: monks (ochre robes, shaven, barefoot) circling the Great Stupa on the ground walk, lay pilgrims in white with turbans, women with offerings in baskets, water-bearers, carriers with poles, boys, on the north path, summit, approach, monastery and village lanes [inferred]. `fauna.js`: swifts and swallows round the dome, crows, sparrows on the paving, egrets in the plain's fields. `smoke.js`: village kitchens at the meals, incense at the south gateway and Stupa 2 [inferred].
- **Weathering.** The climate's `damp` lowered to 0.35: a dry sandstone hill that is dark only at the foot and on the north side [inferred].
