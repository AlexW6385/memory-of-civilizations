## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Great Stupa, begun under Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, enlarged and given its stone railing and four gateways by the 1st century CE [stated: `docs/buildings/sanchi-great-stupa.md` and its sources].
- **The site:** the monuments stand on a hilltop about 90 m above the surrounding plain, near Vidisha [stated: Wikipedia "Sanchi"].
- **Moment shown:** the 1st century CE, with the gateways complete.

## 4. The style

Sandstone stupa and gateways (see the stupa's dossier); village houses of mud, plastered and limewashed, under tiled roofs with verandahs. [inferred]

**The gateways' sculpture and Ashoka's pillar (L2–L3, built).** Each gateway has two square pillars "crowned by a set of four lions, elephants or pot-bellied dwarfs", carrying "three curviform architraves with spirally-rolled ends"; "graceful bracket-figures of salabhanjikas" project from the capitals to support the ends of the lowest architrave; the South Gateway was built first [stated: indianetzone]. "The back-to-back lions supporting the Southern Gateway (the oldest one)"; on the East Gateway elephants; "pot-bellied dwarfs support the architraves of the Western Gateway"; the yakshi of the East Gateway is one of Sanchi's best-known images [stated: Lonely Planet]; the south capitals have "four lions, seated back to back" under a square abacus [stated: puratattva]. Ashoka's pillar stood by the South Gateway [stated: puratattva]; its original height "may have just exceeded 31 feet"; the capital is "made in the typical loti form bell-shape", "the abacus is decorated with pairs of geese, alternating with a motif of honeysuckle flowers", and it is "surmounted by four lions with their backs to each other", "about four feet high"; a wheel on top is uncertain and is left off [stated: imp-art].
Ours: the capitals' beasts carved in the round — four seated lions on the south gate of the Great Stupa, four squatting dwarfs with raised arms on the west, four elephants with spread ears and curled trunks on the north and east, and on Stupa 3's single gate [inferred: its capitals not checked; elephants as the commonest type]; the yakshi as a woman in a triple bend leaning out from the post, one leg crossed, her raised hand on a branch of the tree whose trunk springs from the post to its crown under the architrave's end [inferred: pose after the East Gateway yakshi]; the architraves' ends rolled as scrolls with a spiral in relief on both faces. Ashoka's pillar: a polished shaft of 9.5 m (it was 12.8 m before; changed to the stated figure), the bell of sixteen down-turned lotus petals [inferred: the Mauryan bell], a roped torus, the round abacus with four pairs of pecking geese alternating with honeysuckle palmettes, four seated lions 1.2 m high with flame-like manes and open jaws.

## 5. The town

About 120 houses. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): furnished as in Khajuraho: the clay stove, water pots, string beds, a grain bin; a step up to the door, the leaf open inside, a ceiling of boards on beams [inferred: the period's village interiors].
