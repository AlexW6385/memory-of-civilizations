## 1. The real example and the moment

- **Place.** "About 500 mudbrick tower blocks", "5 to 11 stories", "some … over 30 m", inside a fortified wall; built for the safety of the inhabitants against Bedouin raids [stated: Wikipedia "Shibam"]. More than 500 tower houses in a compact area, most from the 17th century and many rebuilt in the 19th [stated: MIT DOME, "Shibam: Vernacular Clay High-Rise Architecture"]. The town was "mostly rebuilt in the 16th century after a 1532/3 flood" [stated: Ancient Origins]; a flood of 1524 reduced it to half its size and the city as it stands dates from afterwards [stated: Aramco World, 1986 "Manhattan in the Hadramaut"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1700, after the rebuilding, a century before the boom of 1880–1930 [stated: Aramco World]; the merchants who went abroad from the 17th century and built the tall houses "partly to display wealth and partly to save land inside the walls" [stated: Aramco World].
- **Not shown:** the 2008 flood, the ruined stretches of wall and the 20th-century suburb (al-Sahil) [stated: Wikipedia; Aramco World].

## 3. Orientation and ground

The town stands on a low spur above the wadi floor [stated: Aramco World: "rocky spur"]; built here as a platform 2.5 m above the wadi floor, falling in an embankment outside the wall [inferred]. The wadi is a broad dry valley floor of silt and sand, floods rare and violent [stated: Wikipedia, 2008 flood; Aramco World, floods of 1524, 1976, 1982], cliffs of the plateau far off [inferred; not built].

## 4. The style (`src/styles/hadhrami/`)

- **Construction.** "Builders dig down to firm soil", lay a base of timbers with stone packed in and lime "up to street level"; above, "sun-dried mud brick rises up to the sixth floor"; "walls thin as they go up, so interior dimensions stay constant while the outside tapers slightly" [stated: Aramco World]. Built: a plinth of rubble to 1.1 m; walls 1.0 m thick at the foot, thinner by 0.06 m a storey, the outer face battered; interior dimensions constant [inferred: the figures].
- **Bricks and coat.** "Soil, hay and water", sun-dried several days; maintenance "after the harvest" with new mud coats [stated: Ancient Origins]; "mud-brick walls are plastered with brown earth, with light lime on the upper storeys. A whiter plaster is used for decoration"; parapets "often whitewashed" [stated: MIT DOME]. Built: brown mud plaster below, the top one or two storeys and the parapet limewashed, white frames and bands round the windows, the rain-streaked mud under every sill [inferred: pattern].
- **Rooms by floor** (a five-floor husn). Ground: storerooms for grain and tools, "sometimes livestock"; second: women's quarters; third: the owner's majlis or mafraj (reception room); fourth: "the most pleasant rooms, with carved wooden wardrobe doors and a mashrabiya window screen"; "a hidden staircase leads to three upper terraces" [stated: Aramco World]. "Ground floors house livestock and store grain. The lower floors are windowless" [stated: Ancient Origins]; "small rooms, thick outer walls, and a large reception room (mafraj) at the top" [stated: MIT DOME]. Built in the enterable house: ground store and stable; two family floors; the majlis on the 5th; the upper family floor; the roof terrace; seven storeys [inferred: the count].
- **Windows and doors.** Lower windows small for privacy; carved wooden doors with wooden locks [stated: Ancient Origins; MIT DOME]; "wealthier western districts have carved wooden doors and windows" [stated: Aramco World]. Built: slit windows low, taller shuttered windows higher with white lime frames, carved door with wooden lock bar; mashrabiya bay on a few upper floors [inferred: sizes].
- **Roofs.** "Flat terraces with parapets, waterproofed with *ramad*, a plaster of lime, wood ash, and sand" [stated: Aramco World]. Built: flat roofs, parapet 0.9 m, corner piers, spouts through the parapet [inferred].
- **Stairs.** Narrow, dark, turning; each floor "intended for one family" [stated: MIT DOME]; the buildings "connected by bridges and doors … a quick means of escape" [stated: Ancient Origins]. Built: a two-flight stair with a landing in a shaft at one end of every floor, 1.1 m wide flights [inferred].
- **Scale.** Façades 20–25 m high [stated: Aramco World]; built 5 to 11 storeys, 3.0 m a storey (3.4 on the ground) so 16–34 m [stated: Wikipedia "over 30 m"; inferred: the storey].
- **Not built:** the sculpted "qadad" window plaster of Sana'a (a different city) [stated: Ancient Origins].

## 5. The townscape

About 450 towers in lots of 6–10 m by 8–10 m; the five to eleven storeys by rule: tall near the square and the mosque (the richest, western districts), lower toward the walls; the façades continuous along the streets; alleys N–S between groups of three to six. Doors on the street, a few steps up a plinth [inferred]. About 8,000 people lived in the walled city in the 1980s [stated: Aramco World]; fewer shown.

## 6. Planting and the wadi

Date palms and tamarisk on the wadi floor south, east and west of the town, fields of barley or alfalfa between them, halfa and thorn on the dry channel [inferred: Hadhramaut's oases]. None inside [stated: no gardens].

## 7. Sky and weather

Latitude 15.9 N, hot, dry, clear, a hazy dust sky; rain rare [inferred]. Climate in `ground.js`: damp low (0.15), no waterline.

## 8. People and animals

Merchants in white robes and turbans (the Hadhrami dress: the long white thawb, headcloth, the futa waist wrap), women in black overgarments with the wide straw hat of the Hadhramaut (the "midh'a") working the fields [inferred: well known; no source fetched]; donkeys and goats, camels at the gate, sheep penned at the foot of houses [stated: livestock on ground floors — Aramco World]; pigeons and sparrows; swifts at the cliff [inferred].

## 9. Sound

The call from the Friday mosque; donkeys, goats, children; wind in palms; a stone-on-stone building sound in the alleys [inferred]. Music: a lone oud and a hand drum, the Hadhrami dan, in the pentatonic-leaning mode of the region (`music.js`) [inferred].

## 11. Deepening pass (Suzhou standard): what was added and why

- **Ground floors that can be entered.** "Windowless lower floors store grain. Household areas sit above, and family and leisure rooms are higher still. Men socialize in the second-floor main room, while women's areas are usually on the third or fourth floor" [stated: incensecaravan.blogspot.com, Hadramout]. The ground storey is also where animals and stores stand [stated: the enterable house, §4]. Built: seventeen ground floors hollowed into rooms 4.6–8.4 m wide under palm-trunk beams (open front with plank shutters folded aside, a mat canopy over the street) in the lots on the square's south edge, along the main street from the gate and near the square: a spice and incense shop, cloth, grain and dates, a coffee house, honey, an indigo dyer, a silversmith, a weaver, a blacksmith, a carpenter, a jar seller, a tailor, a barber [inferred: the trades of a Hadhrami market town of the 18th c.; the wadi's jewellery crafts, honey, incense route and weaving are stated for the region: "the spice and incense route", Seiyun's market "where traditional crafts such as jewellery are still practiced" — incensecaravan.blogspot.com]. Each is furnished by its trade, lit, with its keeper at work.
- **The sultan's palace** gets its ground-floor reception hall (the Qasr's rooms stand on the lower floors) [inferred]: a hall of lime piers with timber brackets, the dais and seat, seats on low platforms along the walls, carpets, a guard room with a rack of matchlocks, brass lamps; guards at the door.
- **Square.** Closed on its south side by a row of houses facing it, stalls under palm-mat shades, goods spread on mats, water jars, camels at rest with their loads, men seated in the shade, women at the well [inferred].
- **Fronts at eye level.** Mastabas (benches of mud and lime) beside doors, water jars on rings of stone, a niche with an oil lamp, four door fashions (carved cedar, double leaf with iron studs, plank leaf, panelled leaf), patches of fallen plaster with the mudbrick showing and a band of rising damp at the foot, rain-and-lime streaks under the larger windows and under every spout (the dry valley's rare rain runs down the plaster [inferred: pattern; "some owners limewash their homes against termites and flooding" — incensecaravan.blogspot.com]).
- **Roofs.** Palm-trunk spouts through the parapet, parapet teeth on the rich houses and the palace, a stair-head, jars, a drying frame with cloth, a pigeon cote on some [inferred].
- **Over the ways.** Covered passages (sabat) of mud on palm-trunk beams joining two houses across a street, and washing lines between the storeys across streets and alleys [inferred: Hadhrami towns knit their houses with such bridges].
- **Wadi floor.** Palm plots 20–24 m wide each walled in mud with a gate gap, a ditch of flood water along the wall's foot, a hut of fronds in some, a draw well with a donkey and trough in every third; date palms at 2.3 per 100 m² inside the plots, nothing outside; "the fabulous palm groves that surround it" [stated: incensecaravan.blogspot.com, of the neighbouring Seiyun and Tarim, which "encircled by palm groves"]. The burial ground north-east of the town: rows of graves of piled stones with upright head and foot stones, a whitewashed domed tomb (qubba) at its edge [inferred: a Hadhrami qubba of a saint].
- **Mosque.** The court gets benches, a mat shade, the sabil's water jars, the hall prayer rugs, Qur'an stands, a shoe rack, hanging brass lamps and a carved band on the qibla wall [inferred].
- **Tower house.** Mangers and a hand quern on the ground floor, the oven (tannur) and pots of the kitchen floor, a Qur'an stand, a cradle, the majlis hung with plates, daggers and long guns, the desk with books, dates in baskets, lamps on the stair; the roof terrace with washing, jars, a sleeping mat, dates drying, a stove and a cote [inferred].
- **Life.** Walkers about 66 by day (farmers with hoes, elders with staffs added), figures at the doors and counters, sparrows on the mastabas, pigeons on the parapets round the square; smoke from the coffee houses, smithies and spice shops.
