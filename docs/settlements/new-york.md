## 1. The real example

- **Empire State Building:** opened on 1 May 1931 at Fifth Avenue and 34th Street. It has 102 storeys and stands 381 m to the roof (the antenna came later). It has setbacks under the 1916 zoning law and an observatory on the 86th floor, and its mast was designed for mooring airships [stated: Wikipedia "Empire State Building"].
- **Chrysler Building:** completed in 1930 at Lexington Avenue and 42nd Street, 319 m tall. It has a terraced stainless-steel crown of sunbursts with triangular windows, eagle gargoyles and radiator-cap ornaments [stated: Wikipedia "Chrysler Building"].
- **Chanin Building** (1929, 197 m) and **Lincoln Building** (1930, 205 m) on 42nd Street [stated: Wikipedia articles of those names].
- **Grand Central Terminal:** opened in 1913. Its Beaux-Arts front on 42nd Street has three great arched windows [stated: Wikipedia "Grand Central Terminal"].
- **New York Public Library Main Branch:** opened in 1911 on Fifth Avenue between 40th and 42nd Streets, with Bryant Park behind it [stated: Wikipedia "New York Public Library Main Branch"].
- **Sixth Avenue Elevated:** ran over Sixth Avenue until 1938 [stated: Wikipedia "IRT Sixth Avenue Line"].
- **The grid:** the Commissioners' Plan of 1811. It runs about 29° east of true north [stated: Wikipedia "Commissioners' Plan of 1811"].
- **Moment shown:** autumn 1931, just after the Empire State opened.

### The Chrysler Building's crown and ornament

- The crown is "composed of seven radiating terraced arches", a cruciform groin vault. Its steel is "ribbed and riveted in a radiating sunburst pattern" with "many triangular vaulted windows", in Krupp's austenitic stainless steel sold as Nirosta. The spire was 38 m (125 ft) long. "The corners of the 61st floor are graced with eagles", and the corner ornaments of the 31st floor are "replicas of the 1929 Chrysler radiator caps" [stated: Wikipedia "Chrysler Building", via an IPFS mirror]. Built: seven tiers stepping in (half-widths 13 → 4.2 m). Each tier is a square drum with a semicircular arched head on every face, roofed by the groin vault of two crossing barrels. On each face: a rim and the sunburst's ribs, 5–9 triangular windows pointing out along the arch like rays, and a band of small triangular windows under it (lit glass). The spire's foot is hidden in the last vault. Four eagle gargoyles lean diagonally out from the 61st floor's corners (a neck, head and beak on a base). Four winged radiator caps stand on stepped bases at the 31st floor's corners. The eagles used to stand at the middles of the faces; that was wrong and has been fixed [stated: positions; inferred: the shapes and the tiers' sizes].

### The street of 1931

- **Water tanks:** each stands on a braced steel frame with a timber platform. The tub is of upright staves with five steel hoops, under a conical roof with a vent, and has a ladder up the side [inferred: the type still seen on Manhattan roofs; sizes ours].
- **Fire escapes** on about half the mid-rise lofts and houses, on the street front: a balcony of iron at each floor from the second, with its railing, and a stair between each that zig-zags. A drop ladder hangs over the shop front [inferred: the type of the period; the street front from the lot's half-block].
- **Traffic:** the avenues and cross streets run two-way with the traffic keeping right (Fifth Avenue became one-way only in 1966 — inferred). Motor cars of 1930 are black, cream and blue. About four in ten are cabs in yellow: the Yellow Cab colour dates from 1907, and "Checker Cabs produced large yellow and black taxis that became the most common taxis in New York City"; during the Depression the city had as many as 30,000 cab drivers [stated: Wikipedia "Taxicabs of New York City", via an IPFS mirror]. Yellow was ordered for all medallion cabs only in 1967 [stated: the same], so not every cab is yellow: the plain yellow stands for both liveries, and the Checker's black band is not modelled [inferred]. The cars on the Sixth Avenue keep clear of the El's columns.
- **People** on the avenues' pavements: men in dark suits and brimmed hats, women in coats with cloche-like caps [inferred]. Lamp posts every 30 m along the avenues.

### 1b. The Sixth Avenue El and its stations

### 1e. The Empire State Building's lobby (2026-11-07)

- The ground floor's interior is a designated landmark (19 May 1981); "the Fifth Avenue entrance lobby" is "arranged as a long hall focusing on a modernistic aluminum silhouette of the Empire State Building on the far wall"; the corridor lobbies round the elevator banks with the inner store entrances give "a grand-concourse effect"; the walls are of ribbed marble, the ceilings zig-zag ribbed; "aluminum mezzanine bridges span the corridors"; the elevator doors carry silhouettes; the design sought "simplicity of detail, long unbroken lines, and beautiful materials" [stated: Historic Districts Council, "Empire State Building, Ground Floor"]. The mural shows "23-karat gold stars, sunbursts, and gears", in "gold and aluminum leaf"; the elevator doors are gilded [stated: ESB, "Architecture & Design"].
- Built: the base hollowed behind the Fifth Avenue entrance; the central doors stand open (their gilded leaves folded back), the glass and revolving doors either side; the hall 28.8 × 10 m and 14.5 m high (to the top of the entrance's glass), the corridor across it 6 m wide and 7.6 m high running toward 33rd and 34th Streets; walls of rose marble in book-matched slabs with ribs every 0.6 m, a dark marble skirting, an aluminium band at the top; the floor of marble with a dark border; the ceilings gilded, stepped aluminium ribs along the walls, cross ribs, a sunburst every 5.4 m down the hall — a gold boss, alternating rays of gold and aluminium, a toothed wheel round it — and gold stars; in the corridor the elevator banks: pairs of gilded doors in aluminium frames under their indicators; at both mouths of the corridor an aluminium bridge at the mezzanine with finned fascia and railings; on the far wall a panel of dark marble with the building's silhouette in aluminium stepping back to its mast, gilt fins up its shaft, rays of gold and aluminium behind; bronze sconces of glass along the walls [inferred: all dimensions and the composition of the mural and the silhouette; the hall's height set by the entrance].

### 1l. Library, steps and Bryant Park

- "The Fifth Avenue pavilion has a portico with six Corinthian columns and three archways"; the portico is reached "at the top of the Fifth Avenue stairs"; the terrace along Fifth Avenue is 90 ft deep with a granite balustrade (the present design is of 1988); two Tennessee marble lions, "Patience" (south) and "Fortitude" (north), by the Piccirilli Brothers from a design by Edward Clark Potter, flank the stair (named by La Guardia in the 1930s); Astor Hall is 70 × 44 ft, clad in Vermont marble, with a 34 ft barrel vault in rectangular moulded frames and "two grand marble staircases" from the north and south walls; the Rose Main Reading Room is on the third floor, 78 × 297 ft and 52 ft high, lit by nine arched windows on the Bryant Park side [stated: Wikipedia "New York Public Library Main Branch"].
- Built (`src/styles/artdeco/library.js`, replacing `beaux_arts` for the Library): the front set back 13 m behind a terrace 5 m deep at 3 m above the street and 18 broad marble steps (34 m wide, walkable), a marble balustrade, the two lions on plinths at the foot; six columns on plinths, three bronze doors; wings with two ranks of windows, cornice and attic; Astor Hall 17.5 × 30 m and 11 m high, marble floor and walls, coffered ceiling, four bronze lanterns, an information desk, benches, one stair (22 steps, 8 m wide) up to a gallery on its west wall; two doorways in its west wall into the Reading Room, 24 × 66 m and 14.5 m high, an oak floor, panelled walls with two galleries of books (a thousand coloured runs of spines), arched daylight panels high in both long walls, a coffered ceiling with gilt mouldings and bronze chandeliers, two rows of long oak tables with green-shaded brass lamps and chairs on both sides, the delivery desk and catalogue cases, readers, librarians. [inferred: the compression — the Reading Room is on the terrace level here, not the third floor, so that it can be walked into; the real room is 90 m long; Astor Hall has two stairs where one is built; materials and fittings of 1931 as above.]
- **Bryant Park.** Gravel walks (3 m) round the lawns and across them to a paved plaza (14 m) at the middle, slatted benches facing the walks and the plaza (40 or so), lamp standards, men in worn coats and women sitting on a third of the benches [inferred: the 1931 park before its redesign of 1934 had lawns, walks, trees and benches; Depression crowds sat in it].

### 1m. What changed in the movers

Walkers are drawn to 56 by day; pavement ways that would run through a landmark's lot (the Library, Grand Central, the Empire State and the towers stand at the kerb) are dropped so that no one walks through a wall.

## 3. Orientation

Manhattan's avenues run about 29° east of true north [stated: Wikipedia "Commissioners' Plan of 1811"; Charles Petzold, "How Far from True North are the Avenues of Manhattan?" — "The avenues of Manhattan deviate from true north by 29°"]. Built so since 2026-11-06: the whole layout is drawn in the grid's own frame (+x along the cross streets toward the East River, −z up the avenues) and turned 29° into the settlement's frame (`GRID`, `toCity` / `toGrid` in `layout.js`), so that uptown is N 29° E. Everything turns together: the avenues and streets, the lots and their buildings, Broadway, the El and its stations, the viaduct, the landmarks, Bryant Park's lawn and planting, the ground's gentle rise, the newsstands and yards, the traffic, the people and the lamps. A plan's own `yaw` turns only by quarter turns (its colliders stay on the axes), so the plans stand unturned at the origin and their elements are built inside the style's `turned` element (`src/styles/artdeco/turn.js`), which turns their geometry, lights and obstacles exactly and lays their floors and ramps as strips of axis-aligned rectangles inside the turned ones; the life of the streets (single placed things) is moved and turned in `town.js`. The arrival (`src/planet/arrivals.js`) turned with it: Fifth Avenue at 35th Street, looking down the avenue at the Empire State. [inferred: the method]

## 4. The style

Steel-framed towers clad in limestone and brick that step back as they rise; Art Deco crowns in steel; Beaux-Arts marble and limestone; loft buildings with water tanks on their roofs. [stated]

## 5. The town

About 800 buildings on the blocks: lofts of 10–20 storeys west of Fifth (the garment district), brownstones in Murray Hill, and towers clustering toward 42nd Street. [stated: districts; counts ours — inferred]

## 6. Ground and water
