# Çatalhöyük, c. 6500 BC — dossier

Settlement id: `catalhoyuk` · Style: `catal` (`src/styles/catal/`) · Region: the Konya Plain, Anatolia · Code: `src/settlements/catalhoyuk/`
Class P (point), radius 90 m.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the East Mound of Çatalhöyük, a town of mud-brick houses packed together, occupied c. 7500–5950 BC, about 21 m high, eighteen superimposed building levels [stated: studiomatrx.org architecture canon; Wikipedia "Çatalhöyük"]. UNESCO listed it in 2012 [stated: same].
- **Moment shown:** c. 6500 BC, the Middle phase (6700–6500 BC) when newer work gives 600–800 people at the East Mound; older estimates 5,000–7,000 [stated: Wikipedia]. A tell already tall, houses on the rubble of their ancestors [stated: Wikipedia, "renewed by partial demolition and rebuilding on rubble"].
- **The landmark we enter:** one house (a "history house" of the kind Hodder names: platforms with burials under them, bull horns, painted plaster) [stated: studiomatrx.org]. Its plan is composite, from Building 1 of the 1996 report and the generic plan below; dimensions inferred.

## 2. Layout of the settlement

- Houses "packed together in a honeycomb-like maze with no footpaths or streets between them"; "rooftops effectively served as the streets" [stated: Wikipedia]. Each house had its own load-bearing walls, either separated by narrow gaps or butted against its neighbours [stated: studiomatrx.org].
- Open spaces between clusters are the middens: rubbish (sewage, food waste, ash of wood, reeds and dung) went outside the buildings, the interiors kept very clean [stated: Wikipedia]. Cattle and sheep kept in penned areas [stated: Wikipedia] [inferred: pens at the edge, on the open ground beside the middens].
- The site overlooks the Konya Plain, 140 km from Mount Hasan, beside a former channel of the Çarşamba River [stated: Wikipedia]. [inferred: reed marsh and shallow pools in the flat wet plain round the mound; Mount Hasan a double-peaked cone on the north-east horizon, as it is in the one famed wall painting often read as the town and the volcano].
- Radius 90 m: the town cluster (about 60 × 50 m, some thirty houses) on the mound centre, middens and pens at its edges, the marsh and the plain round it.

## 3. Orientation

- Hearth and oven "usually on the south wall", beside the ladder; raised platforms along the north and east walls [stated: Wikipedia; studiomatrx.org]. Our house: ladder and hearth at the south end of the main room, platforms north and east.

## 4. The style (`catal`)

- **Walls:** sun-dried mudbrick in mud mortar [stated: studiomatrx.org]; wet clay placed between boards or laid as bricks [stated: catalhoyuk.com]. Brick about 45 × 20 × 10 cm [inferred: the common figure for the period; not on the pages read]. Walls 0.4–0.5 m [inferred].
- **Roof:** flat, a grid of timber beams carrying reeds and packed mud, strong enough to walk, cook and work on [stated: studiomatrx.org]; "large communal ovens were built there" in later periods [stated: Wikipedia]. Beams of juniper or oak [inferred]. Rooms about 2.2–2.5 m high [inferred].
- **Entry:** "no ground-level doorways": through a roof hatch by wooden ladder; ceiling openings were the only ventilation and let the smoke of hearths and ovens out [stated: studiomatrx.org; Wikipedia; catalhoyuk.com]. Side doors and stairs on some houses [stated: Wikipedia] — we show roof hatches only.
- **Main room:** hearth and a domed oven near the foot of the ladder on the south side [stated: studiomatrx.org]; oven oval with clay walls to about 40 cm [stated: 1996 archive report, Building 1], opening at one end. Hearth circular, near a platform corner [stated: same]. Raised plastered platforms for sitting, working and sleeping along the north and east walls, burials beneath them in flexed position [stated: studiomatrx.org; catalhoyuk.com]. Thick wooden posts in the room may have strengthened it and divided the space [stated: catalhoyuk.com].
- **Side rooms:** small storage rooms entered through low openings [stated: Wikipedia]; a storage bin with a grinding place in front of it, carbonised lentils on the floor [stated: 1996 archive report].
- **Plaster:** fine white lime plaster on walls, platforms and floors, renewed over and over, scores of thin layers; red paint washes on wall faces beside platforms; red-painted white plaster in a corner [stated: studiomatrx.org; 1996 archive report]. Floors with traces of reed matting [stated: 1996 archive report].
- **Decoration:** paintings (hunting scenes, geometric patterns), moulded leopard reliefs (a pair of facing lionesses), animal heads — cattle above all — mounted on the walls: bucrania of horn cores and horns set in plaster, also free-standing on benches and pillars [stated: Wikipedia; catalhoyuk.com; 1996 archive report]. Plastered protrusions on the mid-points of walls, bases for horns [stated: 1996 archive report, F24 and F26; inferred as bucranium bases]. Plastered, painted skulls [stated: Wikipedia] — shown as one skull on a platform [inferred].
- **Rebuilding:** at its end a house's upper walls were knocked down, the shell packed with rubble, a new house built on the same footprint [stated: studiomatrx.org].
- **Weathering:** exterior mud faces left unplastered or thinly plastered, eroded at the foot, patched [inferred: mud-brick everywhere]; roofs mud-plastered, regularly relaid [inferred].

## 5. The town

- Houses as cells on a grid-like cluster with irregular gaps; courtyards or middens in the gaps; roofs all one height at about 2.4 m, some a storey higher (a second room built over; the tell stepped in levels) [inferred; Wikipedia says up to 18 settlement levels].
- Roof furniture: the hatch with its smoke-blackened rim, the ladder (a notched trunk or two poles with rungs [inferred]), a roof oven, baskets, drying food, reed bundles [inferred].
- The town's edges: middens heaped with ash and bone, pens of reed hurdle for sheep and cattle [stated: Wikipedia, penned animals; inferred: form].

## 7. People

- Clothing survives only as burnt textile fragments [stated: Wikipedia]; [inferred: wraps and skirts of hide and woven plant fibre, leopard-skin cloaks for some, no hats, bare feet, hair loose or braided, ochre on skin].
- Work: grinding wheat and barley, baking bread, making obsidian tools, pottery, figurines (2,500+ clay figurines; 187 human) [stated: Wikipedia]; herding on the plain, fishing and fowling in the marsh [stated: Wikipedia: hunting of fish, birds, foxes, hares].

## 8. Plants and animals

- Wheat (several varieties), barley, peas, almonds, pistachios, fruit [stated: Wikipedia]. Cattle, sheep; fox, hare, fish, birds [stated: Wikipedia].
- Marsh reeds and sedges, willows on the channel [inferred]; steppe grass on the plain; cranes, herons, ducks, swallows, larks [inferred: Konya Plain wetland birds].

## 9. Sound

- Murmur of work, grinding stone, children, a dog, goats and sheep bleating from the pens, reed rustling, frogs and marsh birds at dusk; wind over the plain; at the arrival a low hum of voices drifting from the roofs [inferred].
- Music: none survives; a bone flute and drum, a drone with a voice, a pentatonic line [inferred].
