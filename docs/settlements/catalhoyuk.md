## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the East Mound of Çatalhöyük, a town of mud-brick houses packed together, occupied c. 7500–5950 BC, about 21 m high, eighteen superimposed building levels [stated: studiomatrx.org architecture canon; Wikipedia "Çatalhöyük"]. UNESCO listed it in 2012 [stated: same].
- **Moment shown:** c. 6500 BC, the Middle phase (6700–6500 BC) when newer work gives 600–800 people at the East Mound; older estimates 5,000–7,000 [stated: Wikipedia]. A tell already tall, houses on the rubble of their ancestors [stated: Wikipedia, "renewed by partial demolition and rebuilding on rubble"].
- **The landmark we enter:** one house (a "history house" of the kind Hodder names: platforms with burials under them, bull horns, painted plaster) [stated: studiomatrx.org]. Its plan is composite, from Building 1 of the 1996 report and the generic plan below; dimensions inferred.

## 2. Layout of the settlement

- Houses "packed together in a honeycomb-like maze with no footpaths or streets between them"; "rooftops effectively served as the streets" [stated: Wikipedia]. Each house had its own load-bearing walls, either separated by narrow gaps or butted against its neighbours [stated: studiomatrx.org].
- Open spaces between clusters are the middens: rubbish (sewage, food waste, ash of wood, reeds and dung) went outside the buildings, the interiors kept very clean [stated: Wikipedia]. Cattle and sheep kept in penned areas [stated: Wikipedia] [inferred: pens at the edge, on the open ground beside the middens].
- The site overlooks the Konya Plain, 140 km from Mount Hasan, beside a former channel of the Çarşamba River [stated: Wikipedia]. [inferred: reed marsh and shallow pools in the flat wet plain round the mound; Mount Hasan a double-peaked cone on the north-east horizon, as it is in the one famed wall painting often read as the town and the volcano].
- Radius 90 m: the town cluster (about 60 × 50 m, some thirty houses) on the mound centre, middens and pens at its edges, the marsh and the plain round it.

## 3. Orientation

- Hearth and oven "usually on the south wall", beside the ladder; raised platforms along the north and east walls [stated: Wikipedia; studiomatrx.org]. Our house: ladder and hearth at the south end of the main room, platforms north and east.

## 4. The style (`catal`)

- **Walls:** sun-dried mudbrick in mud mortar [stated: studiomatrx.org]; wet clay placed between boards or laid as bricks [stated: catalhoyuk.com]. Brick about 45 × 20 × 10 cm [inferred: the common figure for the period; not on the pages read]. Walls 0.4–0.5 m [inferred].
- **Roof:** flat, a grid of timber beams carrying reeds and packed mud, strong enough to walk, cook and work on [stated: studiomatrx.org]; "large communal ovens were built there" in later periods [stated: Wikipedia]. Beams of juniper or oak [inferred]. Rooms about 2.2–2.5 m high [inferred].
- **Entry:** "no ground-level doorways": through a roof hatch by wooden ladder; ceiling openings were the only ventilation and let the smoke of hearths and ovens out [stated: studiomatrx.org; Wikipedia; catalhoyuk.com]. Side doors and stairs on some houses [stated: Wikipedia] — we show roof hatches only.
- **Main room:** hearth and a domed oven near the foot of the ladder on the south side [stated: studiomatrx.org]; oven oval with clay walls to about 40 cm [stated: 1996 archive report, Building 1], opening at one end. Hearth circular, near a platform corner [stated: same]. Raised plastered platforms for sitting, working and sleeping along the north and east walls, burials beneath them in flexed position [stated: studiomatrx.org; catalhoyuk.com]. Thick wooden posts in the room may have strengthened it and divided the space [stated: catalhoyuk.com].
- **Side rooms:** small storage rooms entered through low openings [stated: Wikipedia]; a storage bin with a grinding place in front of it, carbonised lentils on the floor [stated: 1996 archive report].
- **Plaster:** fine white lime plaster on walls, platforms and floors, renewed over and over, scores of thin layers; red paint washes on wall faces beside platforms; red-painted white plaster in a corner [stated: studiomatrx.org; 1996 archive report]. Floors with traces of reed matting [stated: 1996 archive report].
- **Decoration:** paintings (hunting scenes, geometric patterns), moulded leopard reliefs (a pair of facing lionesses), animal heads — cattle above all — mounted on the walls: bucrania of horn cores and horns set in plaster, also free-standing on benches and pillars [stated: Wikipedia; catalhoyuk.com; 1996 archive report]. Plastered protrusions on the mid-points of walls, bases for horns [stated: 1996 archive report, F24 and F26; inferred as bucranium bases]. Plastered, painted skulls [stated: Wikipedia] — shown as one skull on a platform [inferred].
- **Rebuilding:** at its end a house's upper walls were knocked down, the shell packed with rubble, a new house built on the same footprint [stated: studiomatrx.org].
- **Weathering:** exterior mud faces left unplastered or thinly plastered, eroded at the foot, patched [inferred: mud-brick everywhere]; roofs mud-plastered, regularly relaid [inferred].

## 5. The town

- Houses as cells on a grid-like cluster with irregular gaps; courtyards or middens in the gaps; roofs all one height at about 2.4 m, some a storey higher (a second room built over; the tell stepped in levels) [inferred; Wikipedia says up to 18 settlement levels].
- Roof furniture: the hatch with its smoke-blackened rim, the ladder (a notched trunk or two poles with rungs [inferred]), a roof oven, baskets, drying food, reed bundles [inferred].
- The town's edges: middens heaped with ash and bone, pens of reed hurdle for sheep and cattle [stated: Wikipedia, penned animals; inferred: form].

## 7. People

- Clothing survives only as burnt textile fragments [stated: Wikipedia]; [inferred: wraps and skirts of hide and woven plant fibre, leopard-skin cloaks for some, no hats, bare feet, hair loose or braided, ochre on skin].
- Work: grinding wheat and barley, baking bread, making obsidian tools, pottery, figurines (2,500+ clay figurines; 187 human) [stated: Wikipedia]; herding on the plain, fishing and fowling in the marsh [stated: Wikipedia: hunting of fish, birds, foxes, hares].

## 8. Plants and animals

- Wheat (several varieties), barley, peas, almonds, pistachios, fruit [stated: Wikipedia]. Cattle, sheep; fox, hare, fish, birds [stated: Wikipedia].
- Marsh reeds and sedges, willows on the channel [inferred]; steppe grass on the plain; cranes, herons, ducks, swallows, larks [inferred: Konya Plain wetland birds].

## 9. Sound

- Murmur of work, grinding stone, children, a dog, goats and sheep bleating from the pens, reed rustling, frogs and marsh birds at dusk; wind over the plain; at the arrival a low hum of voices drifting from the roofs [inferred].
- Music: none survives; a bone flute and drum, a drone with a voice, a pentatonic line [inferred].

## 11. Deepening pass (the standard of Ming Suzhou)

- **Houses one can enter:** fourteen cells besides the landmark are opened (`catal_cell`, `src/styles/catal/cell.js`; list `ENTER` in layout.js), the rest stay solid. Each has its roof hatch with ladder, a plastered main room with hearth and domed oven, platforms along the north wall and one side with burials beneath, mud bins, jars, a reed mat. Five kinds by the house's standing: painted bands and bucrania; a pair of leopards in relief; the seated figurine with diamonds; a knapper's house; a baker's with a second oven. [inferred: composite after Building 1 and the generic plan above; the variants follow the finds below.]
- **Leopards:** "moulded leopard reliefs (a pair of facing lionesses)" [stated: Wikipedia, see §4]; the seated woman flanked by two felines is the best-known figurine of the site, about 20 cm high, of baked clay [stated: widely published; inferred as a house shrine object here, shown on a platform].
- **Trades in the courts:** baking, potting, knapping obsidian, working hides, matting reeds; the dossier's finds list pottery, obsidian tools, 2,500+ figurines [stated: Wikipedia, §7]; hide and reed work from herding and the marsh [inferred]. Racks of hides, fish and reeds, fire pits ringed with stones, brick stacks (sun-dried brick about 45 × 20 × 10 cm [inferred]), lean-to shades of poles and reed mats, wattle screens, clay heaps and bone heaps lie in the ring round the town [inferred: ordinary outwork of a mud-brick town].
- **Marsh landings:** the town lived by "hunting of fish, birds" [stated: Wikipedia, §7]; three reed landings at the pools nearest the town with raft-like boats of bound reed bundles, fish traps of wicker, a net on a frame, a punting pole [inferred; no boat has been found at the site, so rafts of reed, the simplest craft, are shown].
- **Plants:** fig and tamarisk by the pens and middens, willow and tamarisk behind the reed on the old channel's bank [inferred; fig is a stand-in for the almonds and pistachios of §8, which the plant set lacks].
- **Weathering:** beam ends standing out under the roof edges, rain gullies down the unplastered faces, patches of fresh plaster, soot streaks, fallen brick at the foot [inferred: mud-brick everywhere].

## 12. Detail pass 2 (roof structure, wall programme, fronts)

Sources read this pass: the research project's visitor guidebook https://catalhoyuk.ku.edu.tr/sites/default/files/Catalhoyuk-Guidebook-ENGLISH.pdf (WebFetch worked); Wikipedia could not be fetched, so what comes from general knowledge of Mellaart's and Hodder's publications is marked [inferred: published, not on the pages read].

- **Houses one or possibly two storeys, windowless, entered only by roof openings; each building had its own outer walls abutting its neighbours, so one house could be rebuilt without touching the next; mud bricks probably made by the household** [stated: guidebook]. Walls and floors replastered again and again, "some homes were plastered up to 450 times" [stated: guidebook].
- **Bucrania** are bulls' heads made by covering part of a skull with its horns in plaster, mounted on walls, benches and pillars; those in Building 77 surround a burial place [stated: guidebook]. A plaster plinth with a pair of horn cores set in it (no skull) at a platform end [inferred: after Mellaart's horn-core benches and pedestals; form and size ours].
- **Posts:** thick wooden posts in the room strengthened it and divided the space [stated: catalhoyuk.com/node/56, §4]. Built here as a plastered trunk with a red band at eye height and a cap-block under the roof beams, standing on the floor at the inner corner of the platforms; two in the larger houses [inferred: plaster coat and red band after the plastered pillars of the shrines].
- **Animals shown in the art:** wild bulls, rams, deer, vultures, bears, leopards [stated: guidebook].
- **Vulture painting:** large vultures with spread wings over small headless human figures, painted in red and black, from the "Vulture Shrine" (Mellaart, level VII) [inferred: published, not on the pages read; the guidebook lists vultures only]. Shown as two or three birds with man-like legs over small black headless figures with raised arms. Used in one kind of house and the opened house's storeroom.
- **The painted "map":** a wall painting of a double-coned, spotted volcano over a grid of rectangular cells, read as Hasan Dag over the town's houses (Mellaart, level VII; the reading is disputed) [inferred: published, not on the pages read; the dossier's §2 already places Hasan Dag on the horizon]. Shown about 3 m wide in one kind of house and on the storeroom's east wall.
- **Plaster breasts with a vulture's beak:** breasts moulded in plaster on walls and posts, some with the skull or beak of a vulture, fox or weasel coming through the nipple (Mellaart, Hodder) [inferred: published, not on the pages read]. Shown as a pair on the east wall above a platform.
- **Fronts of the closed houses, by rule** (each free face of each house: coursed bare brick, a skin of plaster with fallen patches, a stepped mud revetment at the foot, or the plain eroded face): at its end a house's upper walls were knocked down and the shell packed with rubble, so the walls stood in rubble [stated: §4 studiomatrx]; bare brick courses of about 10 cm [inferred from the 45 x 20 x 10 cm brick, §4]; walls propped by banks of mud and rubble at the foot [inferred].
- **Level reached:** L2, toward L3 (still missing: wall niches and shelves, moulded plaster figures of the Mellaart shrines, burial goods, the finer moving life in the courts, sound of the interiors).

## 13. Detail pass 3 (the landmark seen first and close: hand-shaped mud and plaster)

No new sources were read this pass; the forms follow what §4 and §12 already state. Code: `src/styles/catal/soft.js` (soft relief blobs, rolled beads, tubes), used by `kit.js`, `cell.js` and `index.js`.

- **Hand-shaped mud** [stated: walls and floors replastered up to 450 times, guidebook §12; inferred: so edges are rounded and uneven, not cut]: every free roof edge of the town and of the opened house is a rolled bead of mud (about 17 cm across, slightly uneven in radius and line) instead of a straight-edged lip; every free corner of a house has a plaster bead up the angle, thicker at the foot; each roof hatch has a collar of mud rolled round it with chamfered corners; two low patches of fresh plaster of a different tone lie on each roof.
- **Timber beam ends** [stated: flat roof of timber beams, §4; inferred: the ends show in the wall under the roof]: the opened house now shows its roof beams standing 14 to 22 cm out of the north and south walls (the town's houses already had them).
- **Bucrania** [stated: bulls' heads of horns and skull plastered, on walls, benches and pillars, §12; inferred: form]: rebuilt as a plaster boss with a rolled edge, a modelled skull (brow ridge, long face, muzzle) and two tapering horns that sweep out and then up and forward; the horns are cut in short lengths so they stay fine detail drawn near.
- **Leopard reliefs** [stated: moulded leopard reliefs, a pair of facing lionesses, §4]: modelled as soft plaster relief (body, shoulder, haunch, neck, head with ears and muzzle, four legs, a tail curling over the back, rosettes in black set on the flank), a pair facing across a red panel widened to 2.2 m so the heads do not meet.
- **Platforms and pillar** [stated: plastered platforms, thick wooden posts, §4]: the platforms of the opened house have their top edges rolled in plaster and a foot-moulding; the pillar at their corner is a round plastered post swelling at the foot and under the beams, with the red band, and a bucranium on two faces (it was a square block).
- **Departures:** the roofs are still within 0.2 m of one level (neighbours must stay within step reach for the walkers), so the "many levels" of §5 (second rooms over some houses, ladders between levels) is not yet built; the other wall paintings (hunt, vultures, town and volcano) were left as they are.

## 14. Reference pass (the visitor's walk, night, sound, animals, wear)

No site could be fetched this pass; every line is from the sections above or from general knowledge of the published excavations, marked [inferred] where it goes beyond them. Nothing is written anywhere: there is no script at Çatalhöyük (c. 6500 BC), so no signs, tablets or inscriptions are shown.

### 14.1 The visitor's walk

- The way in is the arrival on the trodden ground south of the landmark house, then the ladder against its south wall, the roof, and the hatch [stated: §4, houses entered only from the roof]. The roofs are the streets [stated: §2], so the walk goes house to house over the roofs, and each opened house is entered by its own hatch: 14 routes (`walks` in `plan.js`) start on the house's roof, cross the hatch's rim, go down the ramp by the ladder's foot and stand on the plastered floor by the hearth [inferred: the ramp stands for the ladder, as in the landmark].
- Shops, signs and wells do not exist here [inferred: no market, no coin, no script; goods were made and used by the household]. What takes their place is already built (§11, §12): the households' trades (baker, potter, knapper, hide-worker, mat-maker) at the courts, open to the walker, and the opened rooms with their hearths, ovens, bins, platforms and burials.

### 14.2 Night

- Fire is the light: the hearth of each house under its hatch, the oven's mouth with embers, the fire pits of the courts and yards [stated: hearth and oven per §4; fire pits ringed with stones per §11 (inferred there)]. The houses have no windows [stated: §12], so the only light a passer-by sees leaves by the hatch, with the smoke lit from beneath [inferred]. No lamp is shown: pottery and stone vessels that may have burned fat exist, but none is certain from the pages read [inferred].
- About six houses in ten keep a fire in at night, the rest let it die [inferred]; the court and yard fires burn on at about seven in ten [inferred]. Light: halo glow over hatches, hearth pools on the plaster floors of the opened houses, glow at oven mouths.
