## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Temple of Horus at Edfu, built 237–57 BC, its pylon facing south [stated: `docs/buildings/edfu.md` and its sources].
- **The precinct:** the temple stands inside a mudbrick enclosure wall [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of Edfu"]. The birth house (mammisi) of Ptolemy VIII stands before the pylon, to the west [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of Edfu"].
- **The town:** ancient Edfu (Behdet, Apollinopolis Magna) lay on a mound beside the temple, near the west bank of the Nile [stated: Wikipedia "Edfu"].
- **Sacred lake:** temples had sacred lakes for the priests' rites [stated: Wikipedia "Sacred lake"]. Its place here is inferred.
- **Moment shown:** about 57 BC, the temple completed.

### 4.1 The pylon, its flagpoles and its falcons

- **Reliefs.** The pylon was erected by Ptolemy IX; its outer reliefs show Ptolemy XII "smiting foes before Horus the Elder" [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk]. The two towers were planned as mirror images, in construction and in the carved scenes [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk]; the pylon is "completely covered with inscriptions and reliefs both inside and outside" [stated: sharm-club]. Ours: in each tower's field clear of the flagpole niches, the colossal king in the double crown, his mace raised, holding three kneeling captives by the hair, facing a falcon-headed Horus in the double crown with the was-sceptre and the ankh; short columns of text between their heads; above, registers of the king (in the blue crown) offering to a seated god, each scene with its columns of text, on ruled ground lines up to the torus. The figures in relief stand out from the face (the real ones are sunk) [inferred: the engine cannot cut into the face; the shadowed outline reads the same at a distance]. The poses (the smiting king grasping the captives, the god before him) follow the standard pylon scene [inferred]; the register scenes are generic [inferred].
- **The winged disc over the gate.** The winged sun disc on the gate lintel [stated: `docs/buildings/edfu.md` §6, "type"]. Ours (redrawn): the disc with a raised rim, two uraei hanging from it, the wings with a level upper edge and a lower edge sweeping up to the tip, in three tiers of separate feathers (coverts, secondaries, long primaries), carved deeper than the door frames' discs; spanning the gate block between its corner rolls (12.1 m of the gate's 13 m) and 3.5 m high, centred 2 m over the doorway's head so that its uraei clear it [inferred: size and height].
- **The gate's other reliefs.** The pylon and its gate are covered with reliefs inside and outside [stated: sharm-club]. Ours, on both faces of the gate: above the disc two registers of the king offering to seated gods, the scenes mirrored about the axis, the gods nearer the axis facing out to the king who comes in toward it; on each jamb six scenes stacked from 1.2 m to the doorway's head, the king facing the doorway before a standing god; columns of text in every scene, ruled ground lines [inferred: the subjects, the arrangement].
- **Flagpoles.** Poles stood on stone bases in square notches in the pylon's masonry, and clamps secured to the pylon steadied their upper parts; they carried coloured cloth banners [stated: Digital Karnak, of the Karnak pylons]. Ours: a stone base with a bronze collar at the foot; two cedar clamps per pole, one in the niche and one above it, each a beam out of a dark window in the face to a collar round the pole; two long pennants at the top, red and white [inferred: the windows, the materials and the colours].
- **Falcons.** Two granite statues of Horus as a falcon flank the gate [stated: ancient-egypt.co.uk]; the surviving one wears the double crown [stated: sharm-club]. Ours (rebuilt): the bird upright and deep-chested, the folded wings wrapping the flanks and crossing over the tail, the tail falling to the plinth as the third support, short feathered legs with talons, the eye with its brow, the falcon's cheek stripe, the hooked beak, the red crown with its tall back and its curl, the white crown rising out of it [inferred: the anatomy from the statue as generally photographed].

### 4.2 The birth house (mammisi)

- **Builders.** Built under Ptolemy VIII (mid-2nd century BC), decorated under Ptolemy IX [stated: Madain Project]. Near the south-west corner of the temple, before the pylon [stated: sharm-club; Wikipedia].
- **Plan.** A central birth room with an ambulatory round it, reached through a hall [stated: Madain Project]; a sanctuary surrounded by a colonnade, the forecourt enclosed by columns with stone screens between them [stated: sharm-club]. Aligned perpendicular to the temple's façade [stated: Madain Project] — ours on a north–south axis, the door to the north facing the pylon's forecourt [inferred].
- **Columns.** Floral capitals [stated: sharm-club; Madain Project]; the abaci carry grotesque figures of Bes, god of birth and joy, to keep evil from women in labour [stated: sharm-club]. Ours: floral and composite capitals paired across the axis, each carrying a tall dado with Bes on all four faces (crown of plumes, lion's mane, hands on bowed legs) [inferred: the Bes figure's form].
- **Ours, in all:** a podium 26 × 40 m, 1 m high, a stair from the north; 42 columns (13 down each side, 8 across front and back), the screen walls between them crowned with cornices and a frieze of cobras, open in the middle of the front; the sanctuary (12 × 25 m) of two rooms, its door framed and under a winged disc, an offering table in the birth room; the ambulatory roofed, the forecourt open under its architraves; a cavetto cornice round all. [inferred: sizes]

### 4.3 The naos of Nectanebo II

- "A naos of Nectanebo II, a relic from an earlier building, is preserved in the inner sanctuary" [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of Edfu"]; the shrine is "hewn from sombre black granite" and predates the temple by nearly a century; the sanctuary's doors were of bronze [stated: egypt-museum.com, "The Temple of Edfu's Inner Sanctuary"]; a black granite shrine [stated: ancient-egypt-online].
- **Ours:** 1.6 × 1.45 m, 4 m high, of dark granite on a plinth of two steps; the body bound by torus rolls up its corners and round under the cornice; a cavetto cornice and a pyramidal top; the door (0.8 × 1.8 m, 0.62 m deep) under a frame of jambs and lintel standing proud, a small winged disc on the lintel, a column of text down each jamb; on each side two registers of the king offering to a standing god with a column of text between; the bronze doors folded back in the reveal; inside, in shadow, the gilded falcon of Horus (0.75 m) on its base [inferred: the frame, the decoration, the falcon; the pyramid top and the 4 m height follow the shrine as generally photographed].

### 4.4 The hypostyle ceilings

- Ptolemaic pronaos ceilings were painted with astronomy: at Esna the colourful ceiling reliefs "show gods, mythological figures and representations of the sun, moon", the ceiling "divided into seven sections, each featuring a different theme", the sky goddess Nut swallowing the evening sun among the constellations [stated: University of Tübingen, Esna ceiling restoration]. Edfu's halls are taken to follow the same scheme [inferred].
- **Ours, in both halls:** each bay between the architraves painted as the night sky, blue sown with gold stars; down the central aisle vultures with spread wings (tiered green feathers, a white body, a blue tail, red shen rings in their feet) alternating with winged discs, their leading edges toward the sanctuary, kept clear of the light slits in the inner hall's roof; in each side aisle Nut stretched along the bay, her feet at the entrance end, the evening sun at her mouth and the reborn sun at her womb, beside her a row of the star gods standing in their boats, each with his star [inferred: the arrangement and colours].

### 6.1 The quay on the Nile

Ours: a platform of dressed sandstone 20 × 40 m on the east bank west of the town, its top 4.2 m above the water, a coping course along the river face, two flights of steps (7 m, 0.17 m risers) going down into the water, stone mooring posts along the edge; two barges with reed awnings and two skiffs alongside; amphorae, sacks and baskets being landed; porters in white [inferred: the quay, its size and furniture — temple towns on the Nile had landings for the festival barques and for goods; the Festival of the Beautiful Meeting brought Hathor's barque to Edfu by river].
