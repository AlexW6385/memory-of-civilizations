# Abbasid Baghdad, c. 800 — dossier

Settlement id: `abbasid-baghdad` · Style: `abbasid` (new, `src/styles/abbasid/`) · Region: Mesopotamia (the middle Tigris) · Code: `src/settlements/abbasid-baghdad/`
Status: L2 built for the first time (this dossier and the settlement were written together); L3 items are listed in §11.

Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning, written next to it. Nothing of the Round City stands today: it is known from texts (al-Yaʿqubi, al-Khatib al-Baghdadi) and from the plans reconstructed from them (Le Strange, Creswell, Lassner), so the whole of this is a reconstruction; the built evidence is Samarra (836–892), a generation later and 110 km up the Tigris, and the earlier palace of al-Ukhaidir (c. 775).

## 1. The real example and the moment

- **Landmark:** the Palace of the Golden Gate (Qasr Bab al-Dhahab) with its Green Dome (al-Qubba al-Khadra), and beside it the Great Mosque of al-Mansur, at the centre of the Round City, Madinat al-Salam, "City of Peace", founded 762 by al-Mansur [stated: Wikipedia, Round City of Baghdad; Palace of the Golden Gate].
- **Moment shown:** about 800, under Harun al-Rashid (r. 786–809): the Round City is thirty-five years old and the capital of an empire; the markets are in Karkh; the library of the palace, Khizanat al-Hikma, holds the books collected by his father and grandfather [stated: muslimheritage.com, House of Wisdom]; the Tigris carries three bridges of boats [stated: Wikipedia, Karkh]. The Thousand and One Nights set Harun's court here; the house of wisdom's great age (al-Maʾmun, from 813; the observatory of 829) has not come yet, so scholars, translators and copyists are few and the books many [inferred].
- **What the Green Dome was:** a central building crowned by a green dome, 48.36 m (158.7 ft) high [stated: Wikipedia, Palace of the Golden Gate], topped by a horseman with a lance, the weathervane the caliph's people read as an omen [stated: Epoch, "horseman weathervane"; the lance is from the accounts of its fall]. The audience chamber was square, 30 ft (9.1 m) a side, with a vault as high; the iwan before it was closed by an arch 45 ft (14 m) high and 30 ft (9.1 m) wide [stated: Wikipedia]. The dome fell on the night of 9 March 941; its walls stood until 1258 [stated: same]. The Sasanian plan of an iwan with a dome chamber behind it [stated: Wikipedia, Round City].

## 3. The style: `abbasid`

- **Materials:** mud brick and baked brick; stone is rare on the alluvial plain, so mud brick is faced with plaster, which allowed very large complexes cheaply [stated: Wikipedia, Abbasid architecture]; reeds strengthening brick [stated: Wikipedia, Round City]; bitumen for boats and damp courses [inferred, Mesopotamian practice]; teak and palm timber for columns and roofs (teak columns on the gate cupolas [stated: Epoch]).
- **Arches and vaults:** the two-centred pointed arch standard, its point growing more prominent; barrel vaults for formal rooms, vaulting without centring (Sasanian) [stated: Abbasid architecture]. We use the slightly pointed two-centred arch for doors and iwans.
- **Domes and iwans:** the reception halls of Samarra a domed central chamber with four iwans; Bab al-ʿAmma (c. 836) a three-iwan gate on a grand stair with an upper storey to survey the landscape [stated: same]; hence our gate with the upper domed room.
- **Stucco** (Samarra, from 836): style A vegetal (vine leaves), B abstract and stylised, C "bevelled", repeating curves ending in spirals, the first arabesque [stated: same]; used on interior dados and the palace's iwan; ours drawn as raised-and-bevelled panels in `stucco`, mostly style C (geometry costs least) [inferred].
- **Mosque plan:** rectangular hypostyle round an arcaded sahn; at Abu Dulaf (859–861) arcades on rectangular brick piers at right angles to the qibla; "massive rounded piers with smaller engaged columns" [stated]. Al-Mansur's mosque had wooden columns [stated: Round City]; no minaret shown: the spiral Malwiya (c. 850) is later [stated: Abbasid architecture]. [inferred: no minaret at c. 800]
- **Houses:** blocks usually two storeys round courtyards; the lower level often sunken and vaulted, the upper with a timber ceiling and a flat terraced roof; plain exteriors, richly decorated interiors; latrines and cold-water bathing [stated: Abbasid architecture]. Samarra's reception plan: a central space with adjoining rooms; an entrance passage between street and rooms; cisterns and basins in some [stated: ancient-history-sites, Samarra houses].
- **Wind catchers:** the archaeology of Abbasid houses shows no trace of windcatchers [stated: Abbasid architecture]; the Baghdad summer practice is by the sardab (cellar) and the roof. We still show a few wind towers (badhanj) on rich houses, as the commission asks; recorded as [inferred], a minority (one house in six), tall open-fronted shafts on the roof, brick.
- **Colours:** baked brick buff to rose, mud-brick tan, whitewashed plaster, green dome tiles/glaze, teak brown, indigo and white cloth.

## 4. The Round City's elements (built, `abbasid` style)

- **Walls:** `ring_wall`: outer wall with a glacis, the main wall with bastions and a crenellated parapet, the inner wall; the moat as a ring of water broken by the gate causeways. [inferred proportions; stated existence]
- **Gates:** `round_gate`: a vaulted passage under the main wall with double iron-bound doors, flanking bastions, a stair and, above, the audience room under a green dome on teak columns,. [stated: Epoch for the cupola; rest inferred]
- **Avenues:** arcaded, with piers of baked brick and a shallow barrel-vaulted roof over the shops of the inner ring [stated: arcaded street, amusingplanet; inferred: form].

## 5. The Palace and the mosque (built, landmark)

- Palace: a square compound with a gate on the south (the Golden Gate), a court, the great iwan 14 m high and 9.1 m wide, the domed audience chamber 9.1 m square behind it, rising as a tower to the green dome and the horseman at 48 m. [stated dimensions: Wikipedia; the chambers round the court inferred]
- Furnished audience hall: the throne dais, carpets, cushions, lamps, curtains of the Abbasid court, a few officials and guards standing. [inferred from the descriptions of audiences]
- Mosque: sahn with arcades of teak columns, a deeper prayer hall of five aisles at the qibla, a flat roof of teak beams and palm-rib laths [stated: wooden columns, flat roof]; mihrab niche with stucco hood (style C); a wooden minbar; reed mats, hanging glass lamps. [inferred details]

## 6. The House of Wisdom (built, enterable)

- A library first: Khizanat al-Hikma, built by Harun al-Rashid from the manuscripts collected by his father and grandfather; enlarged under al-Maʾmun a generation later; translators, scribes, copyists and writers met daily to translate, read, write and debate; languages Arabic, Farsi, Aramaic, Hebrew, Syriac, Greek, Latin [stated: muslimheritage.com]. The site is not known [stated: same]; we put it on the esplanade near the palace. [inferred]
- Interior: a long barrel-vaulted reading hall, shelves of cupboards with codices laid flat and rolled scrolls, low reading desks, scribes cross-legged with reed pens and paper, a Persian, a Syriac monk and an Arab scholar at one table, stacks of paper, an astrolabe and a celestial globe on a stand, lamps. [inferred; paper was made in Baghdad from about 794 — tradition, not verified here]
- Scholars named in the sources (not shown by name): al-Khwarizmi, al-Kindi, Hunayn ibn Ishaq, the Banu Musa [stated: muslimheritage.com] — c. 800 is early for most of them. [inferred]

## 7. Karkh: the markets (built)

- Markets of fruit, food, money-changers, books, sheep [stated]; each trade its own street [inferred from the Baghdad geographers]. We build: the book-and-paper market (warraqin), the spice and perfume market, the cloth market (bazzazin), the coppersmiths, the money-changers' row, the potters and glazed ware, the fruit and grain.
- A suq row: brick shop bays 3 m wide, vaulted or roofed in palm-rib and reed matting, a canopy of mats over the street, the shutter that folds up as the awning and down as the counter, goods in front on mats. Gates (the street closed at night). [inferred]
- An enterable shop (a bookseller and paper-seller), furnished.

## 8. People of the time (movers and figures)

- Men: long tunic (qamis) with a belt; the kaftan/qaba of the Abbasid court (the Persian fashion of caftan, with tiraz bands); the turban (ʿimama) and, at court, the qalansuwa, a tall cap; the shoes of leather; porters in short tunic; boatmen bare-legged. Women in long dress and a headscarf or the ʿabaʾa; slave-girls; children. Court: guards in black (the Abbasid colour; black banners and robes), with swords; officials with the long black qaba and tall hat. [inferred from the Samarra frescoes and the miniatures of the 13th century; the black of the Abbasid dynasty is attested]
- Trades by hour: water-carriers with skins (saqqa), bread-bearers, porters, scribes, merchants, money-changers, camel-drivers, the muhtasib on his rounds [stated: Karkh, muhtasib].

## 9. Water and boats

- The Tigris: bridges of boats (a line of boats side by side with a planked deck, held by cables to the banks); small ferry boats; the round basket-boat quffa, pitch-coated; the zawraq and other river boats; rafts of inflated skins (kalak) come from upstream [inferred, from Mesopotamian river practice; the bridges' existence stated: Karkh]. We have a few moored at the quays and the moving ones the engine's two kinds.
- The Isa canal: brick-walled, cargo boats, a quay for the grain market.

## 10. Ground, climate, planting, sound

- Flat alluvial plain, dust and brick paving; hot dry climate; clear sky, dust haze; date palm groves (palm trunks and fronds as roofs), tamarisk and willow by the river, reeds, pomegranate and orange gardens [inferred]. The Round City's esplanade packed earth with some paving.
- Sound: the call to prayer (no minaret: from the roof of the mosque), water-carriers' cries, the hammers of the coppersmiths, camel bells, boatmen, pigeons, swifts; music of the court: oud, ney, rabab, frame drum [inferred]. Maqam-like scale (hijaz) over a drone.
