# Abbasid Baghdad, c. 800 — dossier

Settlement id: `abbasid-baghdad` · Style: `abbasid` (new, `src/styles/abbasid/`) · Region: Mesopotamia (the middle Tigris) · Code: `src/settlements/abbasid-baghdad/`
Status: L2 built for the first time (this dossier and the settlement were written together); L3 items are listed in §11.

Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning, written next to it. Nothing of the Round City stands today: it is known from texts (al-Yaʿqubi, al-Khatib al-Baghdadi) and from the plans reconstructed from them (Le Strange, Creswell, Lassner), so the whole of this is a reconstruction; the built evidence is Samarra (836–892), a generation later and 110 km up the Tigris, and the earlier palace of al-Ukhaidir (c. 775).

## 1. The real example and the moment

- **Landmark:** the Palace of the Golden Gate (Qasr Bab al-Dhahab) with its Green Dome (al-Qubba al-Khadra), and beside it the Great Mosque of al-Mansur, at the centre of the Round City, Madinat al-Salam, "City of Peace", founded 762 by al-Mansur [stated: Wikipedia, Round City of Baghdad; Palace of the Golden Gate].
- **Moment shown:** about 800, under Harun al-Rashid (r. 786–809): the Round City is thirty-five years old and the capital of an empire; the markets are in Karkh; the library of the palace, Khizanat al-Hikma, holds the books collected by his father and grandfather [stated: muslimheritage.com, House of Wisdom]; the Tigris carries three bridges of boats [stated: Wikipedia, Karkh]. The Thousand and One Nights set Harun's court here; the house of wisdom's great age (al-Maʾmun, from 813; the observatory of 829) has not come yet, so scholars, translators and copyists are few and the books many [inferred].
- **What the Green Dome was:** a central building crowned by a green dome, 48.36 m (158.7 ft) high [stated: Wikipedia, Palace of the Golden Gate], topped by a horseman with a lance, the weathervane the caliph's people read as an omen [stated: Epoch, "horseman weathervane"; the lance is from the accounts of its fall]. The audience chamber was square, 30 ft (9.1 m) a side, with a vault as high; the iwan before it was closed by an arch 45 ft (14 m) high and 30 ft (9.1 m) wide [stated: Wikipedia]. The dome fell on the night of 9 March 941; its walls stood until 1258 [stated: same]. The Sasanian plan of an iwan with a dome chamber behind it [stated: Wikipedia, Round City].

## 3. The style: `abbasid`

- **Materials:** mud brick and baked brick; stone is rare on the alluvial plain, so mud brick is faced with plaster, which allowed very large complexes cheaply [stated: Wikipedia, Abbasid architecture]; reeds strengthening brick [stated: Wikipedia, Round City]; bitumen for boats and damp courses [inferred, Mesopotamian practice]; teak and palm timber for columns and roofs (teak columns on the gate cupolas [stated: Epoch]).
- **Arches and vaults:** the two-centred pointed arch standard, its point growing more prominent; barrel vaults for formal rooms, vaulting without centring (Sasanian) [stated: Abbasid architecture]. We use the slightly pointed two-centred arch for doors and iwans.
- **Domes and iwans:** the reception halls of Samarra a domed central chamber with four iwans; Bab al-ʿAmma (c. 836) a three-iwan gate on a grand stair with an upper storey to survey the landscape [stated: same]; hence our gate with the upper domed room.
- **Stucco** (Samarra, from 836): style A vegetal (vine leaves), B abstract and stylised, C "bevelled", repeating curves ending in spirals, the first arabesque [stated: same]; used on interior dados and the palace's iwan; ours drawn as raised-and-bevelled panels in `stucco`, mostly style C (geometry costs least) [inferred].
- **Mosque plan:** rectangular hypostyle round an arcaded sahn; at Abu Dulaf (859–861) arcades on rectangular brick piers at right angles to the qibla; "massive rounded piers with smaller engaged columns" [stated]. Al-Mansur's mosque had wooden columns [stated: Round City]; no minaret shown: the spiral Malwiya (c. 850) is later [stated: Abbasid architecture]. [inferred: no minaret at c. 800]
- **Houses:** blocks usually two storeys round courtyards; the lower level often sunken and vaulted, the upper with a timber ceiling and a flat terraced roof; plain exteriors, richly decorated interiors; latrines and cold-water bathing [stated: Abbasid architecture]. Samarra's reception plan: a central space with adjoining rooms; an entrance passage between street and rooms; cisterns and basins in some [stated: ancient-history-sites, Samarra houses].
- **Wind catchers:** the archaeology of Abbasid houses shows no trace of windcatchers [stated: Abbasid architecture]; the Baghdad summer practice is by the sardab (cellar) and the roof. We still show a few wind towers (badhanj) on rich houses, as the commission asks; recorded as [inferred], a minority (one house in six), tall open-fronted shafts on the roof, brick.
- **Colours:** baked brick buff to rose, mud-brick tan, whitewashed plaster, green dome tiles/glaze, teak brown, indigo and white cloth.

## 4. The Round City's elements (built, `abbasid` style)

- **Walls:** `ring_wall`: outer wall with a glacis, the main wall with bastions and a crenellated parapet, the inner wall; the moat as a ring of water broken by the gate causeways. [inferred proportions; stated existence]
- **Gates:** `round_gate`: a vaulted passage under the main wall with double iron-bound doors, flanking bastions, a stair and, above, the audience room under a green dome on teak columns,. [stated: Epoch for the cupola; rest inferred]
- **Avenues:** arcaded, with piers of baked brick and a shallow barrel-vaulted roof over the shops of the inner ring [stated: arcaded street, amusingplanet; inferred: form].

## 5. The Palace and the mosque (built, landmark)

- Palace: a square compound with a gate on the south (the Golden Gate), a court, the great iwan 14 m high and 9.1 m wide, the domed audience chamber 9.1 m square behind it, rising as a tower to the green dome and the horseman at 48 m. [stated dimensions: Wikipedia; the chambers round the court inferred]
- Furnished audience hall: the throne dais, carpets, cushions, lamps, curtains of the Abbasid court, a few officials and guards standing. [inferred from the descriptions of audiences]
- Mosque: sahn with arcades of teak columns, a deeper prayer hall of five aisles at the qibla, a flat roof of teak beams and palm-rib laths [stated: wooden columns, flat roof]; mihrab niche with stucco hood (style C); a wooden minbar; reed mats, hanging glass lamps. [inferred details]

## 6. The House of Wisdom (built, enterable)

- A library first: Khizanat al-Hikma, built by Harun al-Rashid from the manuscripts collected by his father and grandfather; enlarged under al-Maʾmun a generation later; translators, scribes, copyists and writers met daily to translate, read, write and debate; languages Arabic, Farsi, Aramaic, Hebrew, Syriac, Greek, Latin [stated: muslimheritage.com]. The site is not known [stated: same]; we put it on the esplanade near the palace. [inferred]
- Interior: a long barrel-vaulted reading hall, shelves of cupboards with codices laid flat and rolled scrolls, low reading desks, scribes cross-legged with reed pens and paper, a Persian, a Syriac monk and an Arab scholar at one table, stacks of paper, an astrolabe and a celestial globe on a stand, lamps. [inferred; paper was made in Baghdad from about 794 — tradition, not verified here]
- Scholars named in the sources (not shown by name): al-Khwarizmi, al-Kindi, Hunayn ibn Ishaq, the Banu Musa [stated: muslimheritage.com] — c. 800 is early for most of them. [inferred]

## 7. Karkh: the markets (built)

- Markets of fruit, food, money-changers, books, sheep [stated]; each trade its own street [inferred from the Baghdad geographers]. We build: the book-and-paper market (warraqin), the spice and perfume market, the cloth market (bazzazin), the coppersmiths, the money-changers' row, the potters and glazed ware, the fruit and grain.
- A suq row: brick shop bays 3 m wide, vaulted or roofed in palm-rib and reed matting, a canopy of mats over the street, the shutter that folds up as the awning and down as the counter, goods in front on mats. Gates (the street closed at night). [inferred]
- An enterable shop (a bookseller and paper-seller), furnished.

## 8. People of the time (movers and figures)

- Men: long tunic (qamis) with a belt; the kaftan/qaba of the Abbasid court (the Persian fashion of caftan, with tiraz bands); the turban (ʿimama) and, at court, the qalansuwa, a tall cap; the shoes of leather; porters in short tunic; boatmen bare-legged. Women in long dress and a headscarf or the ʿabaʾa; slave-girls; children. Court: guards in black (the Abbasid colour; black banners and robes), with swords; officials with the long black qaba and tall hat. [inferred from the Samarra frescoes and the miniatures of the 13th century; the black of the Abbasid dynasty is attested]
- Trades by hour: water-carriers with skins (saqqa), bread-bearers, porters, scribes, merchants, money-changers, camel-drivers, the muhtasib on his rounds [stated: Karkh, muhtasib].

## 9. Water and boats

- The Tigris: bridges of boats (a line of boats side by side with a planked deck, held by cables to the banks); small ferry boats; the round basket-boat quffa, pitch-coated; the zawraq and other river boats; rafts of inflated skins (kalak) come from upstream [inferred, from Mesopotamian river practice; the bridges' existence stated: Karkh]. We have a few moored at the quays and the moving ones the engine's two kinds.
- The Isa canal: brick-walled, cargo boats, a quay for the grain market.

## 10. Ground, climate, planting, sound

- Flat alluvial plain, dust and brick paving; hot dry climate; clear sky, dust haze; date palm groves (palm trunks and fronds as roofs), tamarisk and willow by the river, reeds, pomegranate and orange gardens [inferred]. The Round City's esplanade packed earth with some paving.
- Sound: the call to prayer (no minaret: from the roof of the mosque), water-carriers' cries, the hammers of the coppersmiths, camel bells, boatmen, pigeons, swifts; music of the court: oud, ney, rabab, frame drum [inferred]. Maqam-like scale (hijaz) over a drone.

## 11. Not yet built (L3)

Interiors of more houses; the Karkh's baths and caravanserais; the Khuld palace by the Khurasan Gate; the east-bank Rusafa; the paper mill; the hospital (bimaristan, founded in Harun's time); the moat's water life; more courtyard gardens.

## 12. Deepening pass (Suzhou standard): what was added and on what ground

Sources for this pass are general (History of Baghdad, Wikipedia; Daily Life in the Abbasid World, ancient-history-sites.com; Daily life in Baghdad during the Abbasid period): they give the trades, the public baths, the inns for merchants and the markets as part of the city, not plans; every plan below is therefore [inferred] from the Samarra houses and from the general Mesopotamian courtyard and caravanserai types, and kept modest in size.

- **Houses at eye level** [inferred]: a timber door frame with a palm-trunk lintel and an iron-studded leaf standing open against the jamb; a brick bench (mastaba) beside the door on seven houses in ten; one lattice-grilled window (high in a one-storey house, upstairs in two); the ends of palm-trunk rafters under the parapet every 0.75 m; a clay spout at a corner; a band of bitumen at the foot of the wall (damp course; bitumen is the plain Mesopotamian waterproofing [stated: Mesopotamian practice, see §3]); patches of fallen plaster showing the brick under it. By each door: a water jar, a stack of fuel, sacks, a mat of goods, hens, an oil lamp on a post, a seated man on a stool; washing on poles on some flat roofs.
- **Eight open houses** (north and both side ranges hollow): reception room (maqʿad: divans along the walls, carpet, brazier, shelf of jars), bedroom (mattresses on mats, chest, jar), store (storage jars, sacks, baskets), kitchen (clay oven tannur, hearth with cauldron, fuel, jar) [inferred from the Samarra house plan: a central space with adjoining rooms; ancient-history-sites].
- **The shop rows of the ring avenues**: the four avenues between the inner wall and the main wall are suq rows of shops, as the "arcaded street of the inner ring" of §2 [stated: arcaded street, amusingplanet; inferred: shops]. Every bay has a painted board over the lintel, an oil lamp, a threshold and wares on the pavement by its trade.
- **The palace's rooms**: the east and west ranges of the court and the north range behind the tower are rooms, not blocks: guards' room, diwan (offices with scribes' desks), treasury (banded chests, scales), kitchens, stores, barracks, reception rooms, stables; two rooms of the gate guard flank the forecourt [inferred from the descriptions of the palace as a compound of courts and offices]. Orange and pomegranate beds in the forecourt and trees along the long court, a water channel from the pool to the iwan.
- **The khan** (caravanserai, 36 × 40 m): one gate to the quay street, doorman's rooms, a court with a pool and four trees, cells of two kinds on both sides, stables at the back; camels, donkeys, bales and merchants [inferred; inns for merchants and their goods are a standing feature of the markets].
- **The hammam** (14 × 26 m): changing hall with divans, warm room, hot room with the central marble platform, basins, domes on the roof; the furnace smoke and steam [inferred; public baths were part of the city of the age].
- **Four workshops on the quay street**: baker (two clay ovens, dough table, racks of loaves), coppersmith (hearth, anvil, hanging pots), perfumer (shelves of glass flasks, scales), potter [stated: trades of Karkh, §7; inferred: fittings].
- **Planting**: palms in rows along the esplanade's four roads, gardens of palm and fig in its quarters; courts with trees in the open houses and the khan. [inferred]
- **Water's edge**: fishermen in moored quffas, ducks and geese on the banks and the canal's quay, fuel and a fire at the canal's west end for the boat yard.

Still not built: upper storeys (rooms above the ground ranges, roofs to walk on), the cellar rooms (sardab), the bimaristan, the paper mill, the Khuld palace.

## 13. Detail pass 2 (beyond the arrival, the great mosque): what was added and on what ground

Sources added: Wikipedia, *Great Mosque of al-Mansur* (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_al-Mansur): about 100 yards a side, built against the palace; first of sun-dried brick set in clay, torn down and rebuilt in kiln-fired brick by Harun al-Rashid in 807-809; roof on wooden columns with wooden capitals, mostly several glued pieces bolted with iron, a few carved from single trunks; a minaret is mentioned (date of its building not given, so none shown) [stated]. Ancient History Sites, Samarra houses (entrance passage, court ringed by roofed rooms, cisterns and basins, stucco in some rooms) [stated]. A search for Harun's hospital and quarter mosques returned only links; the page could not be fetched, so the bimaristan stays unbuilt and the small mosques are [inferred].

- **Great mosque, outer walls** [inferred form, stated material]: sun-dried brick at c. 800 (was baked brick in the earlier build); nine bays a side, a pilaster between bays, a blind pointed niche in each bay, doors in chosen bays with a voussoir ring and a stucco field, a toothed brick band and plaster cornice, corner piers, arched windows in the qibla wall. Doors on the east wall corrected (one had been mis-placed outside the wall).
- **Mihrab** [inferred]: a niche cut through the qibla wall, its back plugged 0.7 m deep; voussoir ring, marble colonettes, bevelled stucco, an inscription band; replaces a plaster block. **Minbar**: seven steps, stepped side boards, seat under a canopy (the old one floated). Qur'an stands, drinking jars, hung lamps in the riwaq.
- **Columns** [stated]: capitals of three glued blocks with an iron strap and bolt heads.
- **Fronts by rule** [inferred]: four door forms (arch; flat lintel under a lattice transom; arch under a brick hood; wide double gate), wealth from lot area, storeys and wind tower; stucco frames on rich houses; solid, merlon or toothed-frieze parapets. One-storey iwans no longer rise above the roof.
- **Open houses**: 23 (were 8) across the ring, Karkh, the canal bank, the river suburb and east bank; storeys and wind towers from the lot; one plan per 160 m quarter.
- **Quarter mosques**: four, each of two joined lots: street door, court with basin, jars and palms, hall of teak columns on the qibla side, mihrab, minbar, lamps, worshippers. No minaret.
- **Lanes**: by some doors a trough with a tethered donkey, a ladder to the roof, a brick bench with a jar.

Still missing for L3: upper-storey rooms and roofs to walk on, sardab cellars, the bimaristan, the paper mill, the Khuld palace, wear and weathering on the new elements, coloured light.

## 14. Landmark pass (the arrival and the close view): what was rebuilt and on what ground

Sources added: Di Cesare, *reconstructive hypothesis* of the Round City's palace (2018, https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e383531d-7bb8-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Di%20Cesare_reconstructive%20hypothesis_2018.pdf): al-Khatib's text gives an iwan 30 by 20 cubits before a majlis 20 cubits long, wide and high; over it a dome, and above that a second majlis under the green dome, 80 cubits in all, the horseman on top; each city gate had a domed chamber above it; the streets are called arcades (taq, vaulted galleries) [stated]. Epoch Magazine and the Sunday Observer article (already cited) for the gate chambers [stated]. A source for a bent (dog-leg) gate passage was not found in the pages reached (Wikipedia could not be fetched); the bent passage is therefore [inferred] from the later Abbasid and Islamic gate practice and recorded as our choice.

- **The audience chamber** [stated proportions]: the chamber is now 10 m square inside (was 12), with walls 2 m thick, under a ceiling of four spherical pendentives and a shallow pointed saucer at 10.4 m above the floor, white plaster; above it the floor of the upper majlis (teak, at 19 m), the octagonal drum with framed windows, stucco panels and corner colonettes, and the green dome. Blind arcades and a stucco frieze on the tower's other faces. The south door had been 0.8 m off the axis and is centred; the chamber's stucco dado no longer covers the doors; the hanging lamps hang from the saucer. The tower now stands flush against the iwan's back.
- **The Golden Gate and the compound wall** [inferred form]: the gate-hall's pishtaq (white plaster frame, two stucco fields, engaged colonettes, gilt inscription band) and the wall's two wings in a blind arcade of pilasters and pointed niches with a stucco frieze and a saw-tooth course. Corner towers with string courses, slits and corbel tables.
- **The iwan front** [inferred]: beside the great arch a framed field of carved stucco in two tiers; beyond it the blind arcade as before with a stucco band in each bay; a stucco frieze under the cornice.
- **The Green Dome's horseman**: a horse at the walk with neck, head, ears, tail and four jointed legs, a rider in a caftan and turban, a lance with a pennon, on the finial's pin [stated: the horse and rider with a lance; the detail inferred].
- **The main gates of the Round City** [inferred]: a bent passage: the outer portal left of the axis, a cross hall under a barrel vault, the inner portal right of the axis (no sight line through; the iron-bound doors stand open in each portal); stucco fields beside the portals; the audience chamber above the hall shown by three lattice-shuttered pointed windows on each face under a stucco frieze, the green cupola on its teak columns above as before. Not entered above the hall (no stair).
- **The towers of the three walls**: corbel tables turned to face outward, string courses and slits (fewer on the outer wall); merged into the walls' existing materials.
- Style C stucco only (the geometry is the bevelled lozenge field); styles A and B not drawn.

Still missing for L3: a stair to the gate's audience chamber and the upper majlis; carved wooden ceilings in the mosque; arcaded ring streets with vaulted bays (the shop rows are as in §12); wear and weathering.

## 15. Upstairs (round 6): the dwellings over the service houses of the south gate road

What was shut: the ground floor of every enterable house was furnished, the space above was a closed void (the courtyard houses' two-storey ranges and the single-room shops alike). Opened now: eight of the twelve houses and shops along the south gate road, the nearest enterable buildings to the arrival and on the way to the Golden Gate: the palace bakehouse, the clerks of the diwan, a perfumer, the cook-shop, a coppersmith, the petitioners' waiting house, a potter, the grooms' lodging (code: `abbasid_workshop` with `upper`, `khan.js`; helpers in `upstairs.js`).

- **A dwelling over the shop** [stated: the Abbasid house is usually of two storeys, the upper with a timber ceiling-floor and a flat terraced roof; plain exteriors, richer interiors, see §3; the shop with the family's rooms over it is the common suq type, inferred]. Floors of palm-trunk joists and boards; the shop's room is 3.6 m clear (4.5 m under the 'tall' fronts), the upper room 3.3 m under the flat roof.
- **The stair** [inferred]: a straight flight of baked brick (solid, 1.25 m wide, treads 0.17 high, slope 0.66) along one party wall, rising from the back of the shop toward the street, its foot entered from a lane beside it; the opening in the floor over it with a cedar rail 1 m high. The side of the stair is mirrored by the building's seed.
- **Upstairs** [inferred]: a sleeping room at the back (mattresses on reed mats, chest, water jar), a family room in front (divans, carpet, tray, brazier; a kitchen over the bakehouse and the cook-shop, an archive of desks and bookcases over the diwan clerks), windows as open reveals on the street and the back.
- **The balcony** [stated: Baghdad houses had projecting timber oriels (shanashil / rawshan) over the street, see §12 'tall' fronts; the open balcony is inferred]: a deck on three brackets, 1.1 m out, a lattice-filled rail, a door onto it from the front room; the two 'tall' fronts (diwan, waiting house) keep their lattice oriel, now a walkable bay 2.3 m high, its door beside the shop door.
- **Changes to the fronts**: the high ground-floor lattice windows and the doorstep of these eight are left out (their openings would overlap the new ones); the bakehouse's reed-mat awning is now the broad shop arch with folded shutters (the awning made the doorway a shell for the walker), the board over the door of the buildings with a balcony is dropped.
- **Walks**: eight routes in `plan.js` from the street, up the stair, to the balcony door of each house.

Still shut: the courtyard houses (their ranges are hollow on the ground floor only: the court is too narrow to put a stair and a gallery beside the bent entrance), the khan's cells, the roofs; the other four service houses (single rooms for the guard, the store, the treasury, the stable).
