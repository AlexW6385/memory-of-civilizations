## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Nyatapola ("five-storey"), a five-roofed pagoda of Siddhi Lakshmi in Taumadhi (Tamārhi) square, raised by King Bhupatindra Malla: work began late December 1701 and was finished 15 July 1702 [stated: Wikipedia "Nyatapola Temple"]. It is the tallest pagoda in Nepal, about 30 m (33 m in another reading of the same page) [stated: Wikipedia; bhaktapur.com].
- **Plinth and guardians:** it stands on a five-stage plinth reached by a steep stone stair, with a pair of guardians on each stage, each pair said to be ten times stronger than the one below: the Rajput wrestlers Jaimal and Patta, elephants, lions (singha), griffins (sardula) and the goddesses Simhini and Vyaghrini, lioness and tigress [stated: Wikipedia "Nyatapola Temple"; oocities report].
- **Fabric:** about 1.1 million bricks, sal timber, 1,528 stones, 529 wind bells and a gilt pinnacle [stated: Wikipedia]; the lowest roof is 49 ft 6 in (about 15 m) square, the roofs diminishing upward, struts set at 45 degrees, red clay tiles, load-bearing brick in mud mortar, a square plan with a central square chamber [stated: oocities report "Nyatapola"]. The bells hang from the eaves, 168 on the lowest roof to 48 on the highest [stated: same].
- **Beside it:** the Bhairabnath temple, three-roofed, first built by Vishva Malla and remodelled by Jagajjyoti Malla; the Nyatapola divides the town into upper (Thane) and lower (Kone) parts [stated: Wikipedia "Nyatapola Temple", "Bhaktapur"]. Taumadhi also holds shrines to Siddhi Lakshmi [stated: Wikipedia "Bhaktapur"].
- **Pottery Square (Kumale Tole / Bolachha Tol):** potters throw on wheels along the main square, dry the ware in the sun in an open central area, and fire it in straw-fired clay kilns behind the wheels; the local black clay is called "dyo cha" [stated: thelongestwayhome.com guide to Pottery Square]. Bhaktapur's potters are known nationwide [stated: Wikipedia "Bhaktapur"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1710, a few years after the Nyatapola's dedication, under the Malla kings (Bhupatindra Malla, one of the most popular rulers for his building [stated: Wikipedia "Bhaktapur"]; his reign 1696–1722 [inferred from general knowledge, not cited]); the day is an ordinary market morning. The 1934 earthquake (6,224 buildings destroyed [stated: Wikipedia]) lies far ahead.

## 3. Orientation

The temples of Taumadhi face the square; the town's long axis runs east–west along the old trade road to Durbar Square [inferred]. The ground is level in the town and drops in terraces toward the fields.

## 4. The style (`src/styles/newar/`)

- **Newar house:** a brick house of three or four storeys, its brick red and laid in mud, the ground floor storeroom or shop with a low door, the upper floors lived in, the top floor under a gently pitched hipped roof of red clay tile with deep eaves carried on carved timber struts (tunal) [stated: Wikipedia "Newar architecture" — red brick, tiled roofs, tundals as decorative and functional brackets; floors of close joists with terracotta tile or plank over a 10 cm layer of fine yellow clay]. The latticed ankhi jhyal windows are the distinctive decoration [stated: same]. Built: the front of each upper storey with a projecting triple window of dark carved timber (the central one larger), smaller lattice windows on lesser houses, a timber-framed door under a carved lintel, a lime-wash or ochre band at the foot (the rain splash) [inferred: proportions, the band], an attic row of small lattice windows under the eaves [inferred].
- **Pagoda temples:** tiered roofs on wooden supports carved with deities and mythical creatures [stated: Wikipedia "Newar architecture"]. Built: a brick core, a timber post round the ground storey, roofs each a stepped square pyramid of tile on a red-painted fascia, the struts at 45 degrees, bells at the eave edge, a gilded pinnacle [stated for the bells, struts and pinnacle: oocities, Wikipedia; sizes inferred to the stated 30 m].
- **Guardians:** monolithic stone figures [stated: oocities]; sizes grow with each stage [inferred].
- **Dabali, pati, dhara, chaitya:** the platform for performance, the open rest-house, the stone water spout and the small stupa are common to Newar towns [inferred from general knowledge; not cited]; built small, in stone and brick.
- **Pavement:** brick laid flat, stone edges [inferred].

## 5. Ground, water, planting

Bhaktapur stands at about 1,400 m [stated: Wikipedia "Nyatapola Temple" gives 1,401 m] in a valley of terraced fields. Planting: pipal (a fig) in the squares as a shade tree [inferred], bamboo in the courts, pine on the rim, grass. Water by stone spouts (not modelled as water).

## 6. People

Newar townspeople, about 1710 [inferred dress, from general knowledge of Newar costume, not cited]: men in the daura (a wrapped tunic) and tight trousers (suruwal) with the topi cap; women in the haku patasi (the black sari with red border) and shawl; potters with clay-grey hands; farmers with a doko basket; children. Colours: cotton white, red, black, indigo, ochre.

## 7. Sound

Temple bells and the wind bells of the Nyatapola; a drum (the Newar dhime) now and then; the potters' wheels; crows, pigeons, sparrows, dogs, a cock [inferred]. Music: a pentatonic-leaning scale, bells and drone, the sound of the Newar religious music [inferred].
