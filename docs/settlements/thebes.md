## 1. The moment and the real place

- **Moment shown:** about 1250 BC, the reign of Ramesses II (c. 1279–1213 BC): the Great Hypostyle Hall, begun under Seti I (c. 1291–1279 BC) and completed by Ramesses II, stands finished and in use; the temple of Amun-Re at Karnak is the largest precinct of Egyptian religion. [stated: Lipscomb Univ., Karnak Great Hypostyle Hall Project; Wikipedia "Great Hypostyle Hall"] The hall is the largest ancient Egyptian columned hall; "in active use for about 1,700 years". [stated: Lipscomb]
- **Why this moment:** the first pylon and its court with the Bubastite portal (Nectanebo I, 30th dynasty, and the 22nd dynasty) do not exist yet; the temple's front is the second pylon (Horemheb/Ramesses I) with the open forecourt and the avenue of ram sphinxes from the Nile quay before it. [inferred from the dates in Wikipedia "Karnak": the first pylon "added by Nectanebo I of the Thirtieth Dynasty"]. Seti II's barque shrine, Ramesses III's temple and the Taharqo kiosk are later and are left out. [inferred]
- **Place:** the east bank of the Nile at Waset; the temple axis runs east–west, its door toward the river. In our frame +x is east, the Nile lies west (x ≈ −285) and runs north–south. [stated: the temple faces the river — Wikipedia "Karnak", general; the compressed distance is ours]

## 3. Orientation, ground, climate

Flat flood plain (y 0), the lake sunk 3.5 m, the river bed 6.5 m down; the town on its mounds. Hot desert climate: clear sky almost always, strong sun, dust haze in the khamsin season; very low rain. [stated: general; Luxor is the sunniest city on earth by climate data] Sun at lat 25.7°. Palms (date and doum), sycamore fig, tamarisk, acacia; papyrus and reeds along the water. [stated: the egyptian style's plants; Amarna gardens with sycamore and palm — "Egyptian Houses, Villages and Household Equipment", ancient-history-sites.com]

## 4. The Great Hypostyle Hall

- **Facts:** 5,000 m²; "134 columns in 16 rows"; "122 columns about 14 m tall [10 m, 15 m in other sources] with closed papyrus-bud capitals; 12 columns 21 m tall (24 m in one source), diameter over 3 m, with open papyrus-flower (bell) capitals in the two middle rows"; the raised nave has "grilled windows" (a clerestory); the roof is of stone, architraves about 70 tons each; "every surface, including the columns, walls and architraves, was carved and painted"; the north half sunk/raised relief by Seti I (raised relief), the south half by Ramesses II (sunk relief); the exterior walls carry battle scenes (Seti I north, Ramesses II south); the 134 columns stand for the primeval papyrus swamp; "the outermost six rows have 9 columns, and the row second from the middle has 7". [stated: Wikipedia "Great Hypostyle Hall" and "Karnak"; Lipscomb Univ.]
- **Ours:** inside x −98…−45 (53 m) × z −52…52 (105 m), walls 3 m thick, the floor of sandstone paving at y 0. The columns in 16 rows running east–west: the two middle rows at z ±5.6, six columns each, 21.0 m high to the top of the abacus, shaft 3.57 m across, open papyrus capitals; at z ±12.0 a row of 7 and beyond it six rows of 9 (spacing 6.2 m) each side: 12 × 9 + 2 × 7 = 122 columns of 14 m, shaft 2.74 m across, closed-bud capitals; 12 + 122 = 134. Aisle roof of stone slabs at 16.0 m; the nave's architraves at 21–22.5 m and its roof at 23.4 m; between them over each nave row the clerestory wall (16.0–21.0 m) with windows between piers, each window barred with stone slats (the grille). [inferred: the heights of the roof levels, the window sizes, the nave width, the spacing; the counts, the rows, the capitals and the heights of the columns are stated]
- **Surfaces [inferred from the statement that everything was carved and painted, and from the colours of Egyptian relief, §5]:** columns on a pale plaster ground: a band of green sepals at the foot, registers of the king before the gods in red-brown figures with blue and yellow hieroglyph columns, the lashings under the capital painted in bands of blue, red and green; the capitals' petals painted green and blue with a yellow edge; the architraves with a band of blue and the cartouche frieze; the walls in registers (egyptian style `wall_reliefs`), the ceiling of the nave painted as a night sky with vultures and winged discs, the side aisles with stars (egyptian style `astro_ceiling`).
- **Light:** daylight only through the grilles of the clerestory and the gates; shafts of sun in the dust and incense; the aisles are dim. [stated: "a high clerestory roof that let shafts of sunlight into the dim interior" — Lipscomb]

## 5. Style vocabulary (style `newkingdom`)

- **Pylon:** two battered towers (batter ~3 m over 38 m), a torus roll at the corners and a cavetto cornice, flagstaff niches in the face: "vertical grooves on the exterior face held flagpoles"; "contemporary paintings show long poles flying banners"; the gate "roughly half the height of the towers"; the towers carry colossal smiting scenes and registers of offerings, the gate cedar doors. [stated: Wikipedia "Pylon (architecture)"; shape of the reliefs from the egyptian style] Flagstaffs of cedar (Lebanon) with pennants, 40 m. [inferred]
- **Papyrus columns:** bundle shafts (eight stems, bound by five lashings under the capital), the shaft narrowing at the foot, open (bell) or closed (bud) capitals, a square abacus. [stated: Egyptian column vocabulary — Arnold, per `src/styles/egyptian/columns.js`]
- **Painted reliefs:** figures in red-brown, kilts white, crowns blue, red and white, text columns blue, ground lines blue; dado frieze of coloured blocks; khekher frieze under the ceiling. [inferred from the egyptian style's `wall_reliefs`, itself after Edfu; the pigments: Egyptian blue, green frit, red and yellow ochre, white gypsum/huntite]
- **Mudbrick:** sun-dried, straw-tempered bricks; "stone used selectively for thresholds, doorframes, and fittings"; roofs of beams, palm ribs or reeds under mud. [stated: ancient-history-sites.com "Egyptian Houses, Villages and Household Equipment"] Deir el-Medina: "mudbrick on stone foundations", walls plastered with mud, exteriors painted white, interiors whitewashed to about one metre. [stated: Wikipedia "Deir el-Medina"]

## 6. The town

- **Houses.** The workmen's village at Deir el-Medina: houses of "four to five rooms: an entrance, a main room, two smaller rooms, a kitchen with a cellar, and a staircase to the roof", average floor 70 m², windows high in the walls, "a mudbrick platform with steps" in the main room, "niches for statues and small altars"; shared side walls, one street. [stated: Wikipedia "Deir el-Medina"; "Urban planning in ancient Egypt"] Amarna small houses: three or four rooms and a forecourt; "the central square room … 'the place of sitting,' with low built-in benches"; "a staircase to the roof, used for cooking and for sleeping in warm weather". Villas: a walled enclosure with granaries, stables, servants' quarters and a garden; an entrance hall, a long narrow front hall with columns, a central hall with two columns and raised platforms along two walls "used as chairs", windows high in the walls with wooden screens, bed niches, bathrooms with a stone shower slab, a staircase to the roof, walls painted with plants and marshes. [stated: encyclopedia.com "Housing and Lighting: The Amarna House and Villa"] Houses were also places of production: querns, mortars, ovens, grain stores, weaving tools; zir water jars in the open; beds, stools, chairs, boxes, baskets, jars. [stated: ancient-history-sites.com]
- **Ours:** (a) the workman's row house (5–6 m × 14–16 m, door on the street, front room with the bench and niche, a main room, two small rooms, kitchen with an oven at the back, the stair to the roof, window slits high up; whitewashed to a metre inside; walls plastered); (b) the courtyard house (a court with oven, granary, jars, a palm; the roofed rooms along one side; the roof terrace with a shade of mats and storage); (c) the villa (walled garden with a pool, sycamore and palms, a porch of two columns, the central hall with two painted columns, benches/platforms, high windows with screens, bed niche, the roof terrace); (d) workshops (§6.2), granaries (round beehive silos with a loading hole at the top), small chapels at street corners. Doors with a stone threshold, palm-log lintel, a wooden door. [inferred: the dimensions, the colours, the arrangement along streets of 3.5–5 m]
- **6.2 Workshops** (inferred; general Egyptology): potters (kilns, wheels, pots drying in rows), weavers (ground and upright looms, flax bundles, linen on the lines), carpenters (benches, adzes, stools and beds half made), masons (blocks, a statue blank, chisels), bakers and brewers (ovens, vats), leatherworkers and metalworkers (furnace, bellows). Houses were production places [stated: ancient-history-sites.com], so the workshops are houses with yards under mat awnings.
- **The market on the quay:** stalls under mat awnings on poles: pots, baskets, onions, fish, linen, loaves, beer jars. [inferred]

## 7. The Nile and its boats

Wooden hulls copied the reed design: flat bottoms, no keel, planks tied with rope, seams packed with reeds; sails were square; "temple fleets also existed". [stated: The Mariners' Museum, "Egyptian ships"] The Opet festival: "the river barque was called the Userhat-Amun … Lebanon cedar covered in gold, with ram's-head decorations at the prow and stern"; the procession from Karnak to Luxor was about 2 km; "sometimes the statue walked along the Avenue of Sphinxes, and sometimes it traveled by river". [stated: Wikipedia "Opet Festival"] Ours: river boats with papyriform raised ends, a steering oar at the stern, a cabin or awning of mats and a square sail on a short mast (moving boats poled and sailed along the river; moored ones at the quay); a gilded sacred barque with ram heads on the lake's west landing; reed skiffs at the banks. [inferred: shapes after New Kingdom tomb paintings]

## 8. People (dress, bearing)

Priests: shaven head, white linen kilt, a sash, bare chest; the sem-priest in a leopard-skin shawl ("a sem-priest in a leopard skin shawl presents offerings … libation from a hes-vase and incense from a censer"). [stated: egypt-museum.com "Leopard Skin & Priesthood"] Wab and lector priests recite hymns and spells (Opet). [stated: Wikipedia "Opet Festival"] Scribes sit cross-legged with the palette and papyrus on the lap in the kilt; workmen in the short kilt or loincloth, bare above; women in the long sheath, often with the broad collar; children naked or in a loincloth; porters with sacks and baskets, water carriers. "A large share of the community, including women, could read." [stated: Wikipedia "Deir el-Medina"] Workers' week: eight days on, two off. [stated: same]

## 9. Sound

Hammering from the workshops, the shouts of boatmen, the sistrum rattle and clapping of the festival, the harp and flute of the singers, the temple's chant in the hall (long reverb), geese on the lake, kites and doves, donkeys, the creak of oars and the slap of water at the quay. [inferred; the sistrum and noisy crowds are stated: Wikipedia "Opet Festival"]
