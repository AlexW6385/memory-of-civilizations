## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Pantheon, rebuilt under Hadrian about AD 114–128, its rotunda 43.3 m across and high, facing north through its portico onto a colonnaded forecourt [stated: `docs/buildings/pantheon.md` and its sources].
- **Its neighbours in the Campus Martius:**
  - the Baths of Agrippa, the first great public baths of Rome, behind the Pantheon to the south [stated: Wikipedia "Baths of Agrippa"];
  - the Saepta Julia, a long porticoed enclosure east of the Pantheon, about 310 × 120 m [stated: Wikipedia "Saepta Julia"];
  - the Stadium of Domitian, about 275 m long, whose arena is now the Piazza Navona, to the west [stated: Wikipedia "Stadium of Domitian"];
  - the Via Lata (the urban Via Flaminia), running north–south on the east [stated: Wikipedia "Via Lata"];
  - the Tiber bending round the Campus on the west [stated: geography].
- **Housing:** most Romans lived in insulae, apartment blocks of several storeys of brick-faced concrete over shops [stated: Wikipedia "Insula (building)"].
- **Moment shown:** about AD 128, the Pantheon just completed.

## 4. The style

Brick-faced concrete with travertine and marble; Corinthian colonnades; tiled roofs; the baths' great arched windows and domed hall; tiers of travertine seats in the stadium. [stated: materials; inferred: details]

### 4.1 The Stadium of Domitian (as built, `stadium` in `src/styles/roman/town.js`)

- Footprint about 275 × 106 m (Colini), or 265 × 106 m; one end semicircular, the other flattened [stated: Wikipedia "Stadium of Domitian"; stadiodomiziano.com]. Built here at 275 × 106 m, the curve to the north [stated: the curved end lies under the north end of Piazza Navona].
- The outer façade in two tiers of arches on piers, the lower tier with the Ionic order and the upper with the Corinthian; outer porticoes and entrance atria of travertine, the rest brick [stated: stadiodomiziano.com]. The façades resembled the Colosseum's; the perimeter arcaded beneath the seating, travertine pilasters between [stated: Wikipedia].
- The cavea in two tiers of steps; capacity about 30,000; the track of compacted earth [stated]. Built as 14 rows on a podium of about 2.2 m round the arena [inferred].
- As built: bays of about 6.4 m, arches 3.9 m wide; an engaged half-column on each pier (angular Ionic capitals with a volute at each corner, as on the Temple of Saturn, below; Corinthian above); entablature of architrave, frieze and cornice over each tier; a parapet in each upper arch; an attic band with pilasters and a cornice; an ambulatory about 5 m deep behind the arcades, floored at the upper tier, with a dark door into the cavea in every bay [inferred: proportions and the attic, from the Colosseum's type; the number of storeys and the orders are stated]. Height raised from 16 to 20 m [inferred].
- Not built: the four entrance porches on the sides [stated as existing; left for later].

### 4.2 Insulae (as built, `insula`)

- Apartment blocks over tabernae; Augustus limited height to about 70 Roman feet (20.7 m) and Trajan to about 60 (17.75 m) [stated: Wikipedia "Insula (building)"]. Built at four or five storeys, 14.2–17.4 m.
- The Hadrianic House of the Balcony with Corbels at Ostia (I,VI,2): shops on the street, large windows over the doorways, a brick balcony on travertine corbels under the windows, an outside stair to the flats above [stated: ostia-antica.org]. Luxury blocks had large glazed windows and pilasters at the stair doors [stated: Wikipedia].
- As built: shop doorways 2.7 m wide under travertine lintels on travertine thresholds, a mezzanine window over each; about a third shut with their boards, a third with a masonry counter in the door [inferred]; one stair door between brick pilasters; brick string courses at the floors; windows with travertine sills, some with wooden shutters open or shut [inferred]; a corbelled brick cornice with a travertine crown; a hipped tile roof; on about half the blocks a brick balcony on travertine corbels with a timber rail along the first floor of the long face [stated type; inferred frequency and the rail]. Built in two levels of detail in 100 m tiles (`src/settlements/lod.js`).

### 4.3 The Pantheon's bronze roof truss (L3 pass)

- The portico's "roof truss that is made entirely made from bronze", with gilded covering plates, stood intact until 1625/26, when Urban VIII had it removed and its bronze (180 tons; 152 tons in the published study) made into cannon; two of its bronze rivets survive (Berlin, Florence); Borromini, present at the demolition, left measured drawings [stated: University of Cologne, Institute of Archaeology, "The Bronze Roof Truss of the Vestibule of the Pantheon in Rome"; Heinzelmann & Heinzelmann, "The metal roof truss of the Pantheon's portico in Rome – 152 tonnes of bronze"]. **Built:** five trusses under the portico's roof between the front and the block, each a tie beam of bronze box girders on the side entablatures (carried over the inner marble beams by short bronze posts), two rafters under the roof's slopes meeting at the ridge, a king post and two struts, a row of gilt rivet heads along the tie [inferred: the form of a king-post truss after the Renaissance drawings as generally reproduced, the number of stations, the posts].

### 4.4 A house walked into: an atrium house with a thermopolium (L3 pass)

- **The thermopolium:** the Regio V thermopolium at Pompeii is a multi-sided counter with deep terracotta jars set into it for hot food; painted on it a Nereid riding a seahorse, a nymph on a horse, mallard ducks, a rooster, a dog on a leash; in it remains of duck, goat, pig, fish and snail, beans in a wine container, nine amphorae, a bronze patera, flasks and an olla [stated: sci.news, 2020]. **Built** (Pompeii as the model for the Rome of Hadrian [inferred]): in the west shop of a house on the street north of the Pantheon, the L-shaped counter of brick under a marble top, faced in offcuts of coloured marble, three dolia sunk flush in it (one lidded), the stepped shelf of jugs at the return's end, the Nereid's panel on blue painted on the return, a bronze patera on the counter; behind it the hearth bench with its pot and the soot above, amphorae racked against the back wall, the painted lararium (the genius between two lares, the serpent's green band) with its shelf; a bronze lamp hanging [inferred: the layout, the number of dolia, the colours].
- **The atrium house:** **Built** behind the two shops (a thermopolium and a wine shop with amphorae, a board counter and a pair of scales): the fauces from the street door (a painted sign over it), the atrium with its roof sloping in to the compluvium and the marble impluvium below with the rain in it, the cistern head, the marble table (cartibulum) with a bronze bowl, the strongbox (arca) of wood bound in bronze on its base, the lararium as a little stucco temple with a lamp burning; four cubicula with beds and a chest; the tablinum on a floor of coloured marble with the writing table, a chair and a case of scrolls; the triclinium with three couches round a round marble table and a silver dish; the kitchen with its masonry hearth bench, pots and soot, amphorae; walls painted with a black dado under red, ochre or white fields and a band under the ceiling, left open over every doorway; worn travertine thresholds [inferred: the plan after the atrium houses of Pompeii, sizes and colours ours].

## 10. Life (L3 pass)

## 4.5 Streets: rows of tabernae (batch pass)

- Ground-floor shops (tabernae) opened wide under travertine lintels between brick piers, shut at night with boards set in a groove in the threshold; flats of brick-faced concrete above [stated: Wikipedia "Insula (building)"; ostia-antica.org, House of the Balcony with Corbels]. **Built** (`taberna_row`): rows of 3-5 shops 4.4 m wide on both sides of the Via Lata and on the north side of the street before the Pantheon, each walked into: a masonry counter with dolia sunk in it and amphorae along the wall (wine, oil, hot food), a shop of pots and cloth, a smith's forge with anvil, or shut with its boards; joists of the mezzanine overhead; three storeys of flats above with a corbelled cornice and, on some, a balcony [inferred: the trade mix, the sizes].

## 4.6 The forum west of the Pantheon and a podium temple

- Hadrian built a temple to his deified mother-in-law Matidia (died 119, deified at once) in the Campus Martius near the Pantheon and the Saepta Julia; its form is not recorded [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of Matidia"]. **Built** (`temple`, `colonnade`, `forum_floor`, `statue`): a square of travertine flags 96 x 88 m with a colonnade of 7.6 m travertine columns on three sides (a gap on the axis), a Corinthian hexastyle temple on a 2.6 m podium at the west end (marble stair across the front, pronaos two bays deep, cella with bronze door and a gilt cult statue, entablature all round, pediment and tiled roof), and six statues on plinths [inferred: the whole form, after the temples of the Campus Martius and of the Hadrianic type].
- The Pantheon's floor: squares of pavonazzetto framed in white marble with discs of porphyry and giallo antico alternating [stated in part: `docs/buildings/pantheon.md`; inferred: the grid]; a light hung under the oculus [inferred].

## 4.8 The mill-bakery (pistrinum)

## 4.9 Deepening pass: streets and fronts at eye level

- **Fullonica (fuller's):** washing was done by treading cloth in vats set in the floor or on a raised platform, divided by low walls the fuller held for balance; rinsing bowls; a separate drying area with racks; white cloth bleached on wicker frames over burning sulphur; a corkscrew press; urine from small public jars in the streets, which Vespasian taxed; the Fullonica of Stephanus on the Via dell'Abbondanza at Pompeii was a converted house with a large bath in the atrium and a skylight over a drying terrace [stated: worldhistory.org, "The Fullers of Ancient Rome"]. **Built** (`fullonica`): a street shop with folded cloth, a washing court with six treading vats in two rows, a water channel, a cistern with a bronze spout, a two-post press, a wicker sulphur frame over a brazier, drying racks; workmen treading in the vats; steam from the vats. Streets carry the urine jars at their corners (`street_kit`).
- **Inn and bar (caupona/popina):** an L-shaped counter fitted with large earthenware dolia, a small room with tables for eating and gambling, a back room; a caupona was a restaurant that was sometimes also an inn for travellers [stated: nunc.ch, "Popina, caupona, taberna: eating out in ancient Rome"]. **Built** (`caupona`): bar-room with L counter, dolia, shelves of amphorae and cups, tables, stools, a bench, painted dado; a passage with a shrine; a stable with stalls, mangers and two donkeys; a court with a well and a timber colonnade; four guest rooms with beds [inferred: the stable and court from Pompeian inns].
- **Street paving, stepping stones, kerbs:** Roman city streets were paved with large stone blocks (basalt at Rome), with raised stepping stones across them for pedestrians, set a cart's wheel gauge apart, and raised footways along the fronts, the gutters and the ruts of carts visible at Pompeii [stated: general; the search returned only titles (ermakvagus.com "Roman House and Street, Pompeii"; "Pedestrian crossings in antiquity", nzeldes.com, not fetched); heights and spacing here are inferred]. **Built** (`paving`, `crossing`): basalt paving over the Via Lata and the two cross streets with a travertine kerb line where the footway ends and a drain stripe down the middle; rows of five stepping stones across the Via Lata at every alley [inferred: spacing].
- **Fronts:** each taberna now has: a canvas awning on poles over the door (half of them), goods by trade (amphorae in a rack with barrels, pots on a bench, carcasses on hooks and a chopping block, loaves or fish or greens on a marble slab, strings of onions, sausages and hams on a pole), a hanging painted board on an iron bracket, a bronze lamp on a bracket, painted notices (dipinti) on the piers, plaster patches fallen from the brick, damp at the foot, rain streaks under the cornice, flower boxes and washing on the upper windows, closed wooden balconies (maeniana) on some. New trades with furnished interiors: butcher, baker, fish and greens, barber/scribe. [inferred from the Pompeian and Ostian shops; the dossier §4.5 sources]
- **Insulae:** three to five storeys; a third are plastered above the shops (white, warm, ochre), the rest brick; chimneys; the same eye-level dressing in a lighter set.
- **Alleys and gardens:** the 4 m alleys between the rows of the Via Lata and the street before the Pantheon are closed at the street by a wall with an arched passage, or, at a fountain, by the back of its recess, so that the fronts run on; the blocks left empty are walled gardens (horti) with a pool, beds with box hedges, a pergola, a statue, planted with cypress, olive, fig, plane and pine; the alleys between blocks are paved, not planted [inferred].

## 4.10 The Baths of Agrippa walked into

- The first great public baths of Rome, founded by Agrippa in 25 BC; rebuilt after the fire of AD 80 and again under Hadrian; the Pantheon stood by them [stated: Wikipedia "Baths of Agrippa"]. The plan inside is the typical imperial one: frigidarium, tepidarium, caldarium on an axis, with palaestrae and changing rooms either side [inferred from the imperial type; the Agrippan plan is little known].
- **Built** (`baths_agrippa`): outer wall with three gates and tall arched windows; two palaestrae with a colonnade, sand, a pool and statues; the block: frigidarium 60 × 32 m with eight granite columns, two cold pools and statues in bays, a clerestory; tepidarium with two warm tubs; two apodyteria with benches and shelves; the round caldarium of 28 m under a dome with an oculus, the great basin on a baluster, four hot tubs, a bench round the wall; marble facing inside, coffered and painted ceilings; bathers; steam from the oculus.

## 4.11 The Saepta Julia as a market

- Marble-walled enclosure with porticoes, in the late Republic a voting enclosure, in Hadrian's day a place for shops and the dealers in luxuries [stated: Wikipedia "Saepta Julia"; the shops: inferred]. **Built** (`saepta`): marble and brick walls with gates, a deep portico on plain columns, a paved court with a coloured strip, rows of statues, fountains, benches; shops (`taberna_row`) against the long walls under the portico; stalls, buyers.

## 4.12 The Tiber

- The Tiber's banks in the city were walled in stone quays with flights of steps down to the water, mooring stones with rings, treadwheel cranes (the relief of the Haterii shows one); goods came up the river on barges to the warehouses [stated: general; Haterii relief; details inferred]. **Built:** a quay of travertine ashlar along the east bank with a paved platform 10 m wide, four landings with steps into the water, two treadwheel cranes with a pallet of amphorae, warehouses with their fronts to the water; moored barges loaded with amphorae and sacks, skiffs with fishermen, laundresses kneeling on the steps, porters; carp in the stream, herons on the quay. The river was made nearer a straight course in the drawn part only by the circle of the radius; the far bank lies outside it.

## 4.15 Sculpture, modelled (round 4)

## 4.17 The visitor's walk, opened (round 7: `nightlight.js`, `keepers.js`, `town.js`)

- **The walk.** From the arrival (0, -120) in the Pantheon's forecourt: south across the forecourt to the portico and the rotunda; north to the street before the forecourt (z = -175) and its rows of tabernae on the north side; the passage at x -42..-12 to the atrium house with its thermopolium, the two inns and the fuller's behind the rows; west along the street to the forum and the Temple of Matidia; east along it to the Via Lata (x = 175) with its rows on both sides, the Saepta Julia beside it, the baths to the south [inferred: the order is ours; the places are §2].
- **Finding.** The shops of the rows were walked into but shown from the street behind a dark panel (the `opening` material filling every doorway), so from the street no shop could be seen open: a visitor saw a black door. [stated: shops of Ostia and Pompeii stood open to the street by day, shut at night with boards in a groove in the threshold: §4.5 sources.] **Built:** the doorway panels are left out of the near copy of every row (`lit(…, 'row')`, a wrapper of the shared builder in the style `rome-town`, a copy of `roman`; the shared style is untouched), so the counter, the dolia, the goods and the joists of each shop show from the street; shut shops keep their boards.
- **Keepers.** [inferred] The keeper of a shop with a counter in its door (wine and oil, hot food, butcher, baker) stood behind it. **Built:** a street-life figure behind each such counter, found by building the row once into a recording bag and reading the counter tops; small plans of their own, shown within 30 m of their row, for the twelve rows nearest the street before the forecourt and the Via Lata's first rows (`keepers.js`).

## 4.18 Rome by night (`nightlight.js`)

- **What lit Rome at night, about AD 128.** [stated] No public street lighting: a man going to dinner by night is escorted by slaves with torches ("plenty of flambeaux and a bronzed candelabrum" before a rich man), and the street was dangerous (falling potsherds from the high windows; Juvenal, *Satire* 3, https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/juvenal3.asp); fire in the tenements spread upward and the third storey could be blazing (same). [stated] The vigiles, founded by Augustus in AD 6, patrolled by night for unattended fires and thieves, seven cohorts of two regions each, the first with its station on the east side of the Via Lata opposite the Saepta Julia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigiles). [inferred] Terracotta and bronze oil lamps in shops open late, taverns and the thermopolium; the lamp before the household gods; the glow of the baker's oven (a pistrinum worked before dawn), the smith's forge and the kitchen hearth; the lamp of a room seen through its shutters. [inferred] The baths were shut at night: the guarding of the baths by the vigiles was added only under Alexander Severus (same page), and Hadrian's rule kept bathing to the afternoon; no lamps are shown in them.
- **Built.** One additive `halo` material (the engine's, as at Suzhou; day view unchanged, checked by the surface check and a render): a pane of lamp light over about a half of the doorways of the insulae's fronts (none where boards stand over them), over three in ten of the windows; in the open shops of the rows, six in ten lit: a hanging lamp's glow and a pool on the shop floor within, a pool of light across the doorstep; a glow ball for every plan light of the forges, ovens, hearths and lamps of the elements (`flame`, `glass_lamp`, plan lights: the oven of the bakery, the kitchens, the thermopolium's lamp, the atrium's lararium), within near copies only; the household gods' lamp on the shelf of each crossroads shrine; a torch (pine, iron cup, flame, its pool on the street) carried before the litter. The far copies of the tiles and the baths, the stadium, the Saepta and the Pantheon are not lit.
- **Not done:** the Pantheon's lamps, the temple's cella, the watch's lanterns (a still watchman would stand in the street all day).

## 4.19 Rome heard (`soundmap.js`, `src/engine/voices-rome.js`)

- **The baths.** [stated] Seneca, living over a bathhouse, lists what he hears: the strong men exercising with lead weights, grunting and hissing as they breathe out; the slap of the masseur's hand on a shoulder, a different sound flat or cupped; the ball-player counting his score; the quarrelsome and the thief caught; the man who likes the sound of his own voice in the bath; the plunge and splash of the swimmers; the hair-plucker's thin shrill cry, now and then silent while another armpit pays for it; and the cries of the pastry-cook, sausage-seller and other food-sellers (*Letter* 56, https://www.romanletters.org/ , fetched this pass). **Built:** `bathNoise` draws three or four of these (grunt and breath, slap, the count, the plunge, the plucker) in a hall of voices, at two points over the palaestrae, from the thirteenth hour to the eighteenth. [inferred: the hours; the baths opened for the afternoon, the eighth hour, about one o'clock at the equinox.] `bathGong`: three strokes of bronze at the opening [stated: Martial 14.163 has "aes thermarum", the baths' bronze, from memory, not re-checked here].
- **Water.** [stated] Fountains (lacus) at the street corners and basins fed by the aqueducts ran day and night; no wells are placed (`WELLS = []`) because the Campus Martius was served by the Aqua Virgo [inferred]. **Built:** a trickle-and-fall `fountain` at each of the nine fountains of `layout.js` (always), and the Tiber's lapping (the engine's `lap`) at the quay.
- **Work.** [stated] Fullers trod cloth in vats of stale urine (World History Encyclopedia, "The Fullers of Ancient Rome", already in Sources): `fullerTread`, a wet slow tread with the splash, 6:30 to 17:00. [stated] Mills turned by mules in Pompeii's bakeries: `millstone`, the stone's grinding, a slow beat, the mule's hoofs. Laundresses beating cloth at the landings (`clothBeat`) and the treadwheel crane's creak at the quay (`craneCreak`) [stated: treadwheel cranes on Roman relief, e.g. the Haterii tomb, from memory, not re-checked here; the placing on the quay is inferred]. The caupona, thermopolium and latrine have their own small sounds (cups, the kitchen, murmur) from the engine's.
- **Street.** [stated] Wheeled traffic was kept out of Rome by day and came at night, with its noise a famous cause of sleeplessness (Juvenal, *Satire* 3, fetched this pass; the day ban of the Lex Julia municipalis rests on the VRoma litter page in Sources, the one primary page I could not fetch). **Built:** `cartPass`, iron tyres and hoofs on basalt, 19:00 to 4:30 at three stations on the Via Lata and the street. [stated] Litters by day (VRoma): `streetTrack` serves the engine's single walker-by-day track (the sedan) and nothing else; Suzhou's vendor and watch tracks are not made here (they carry Chinese cries and clappers). `hawkerRome`: a short cry on a falling third, by day, at nine stations; [inferred] the pitch and the length. [stated] the first cohort's station of the vigiles and their night rounds (Wikipedia, Vigiles): `vigilesPatrol`, hobnailed march on stone with a clink of buckets, by night; the station's place is [inferred].
- **Rites.** [stated, from memory, not re-checked: Pliny, *Natural History* 28.11] A flute-player (tibicen) played at a sacrifice so that no ill-omened word was heard. **Built:** `tibia`, a reed pipe, a short phrase in a Phrygian-leaning mode, by the temple of the forum in the morning [inferred: the mode and the hours].
- **Rooms.** The Pantheon's rotunda (a hush, long air; rain falls through the oculus), the baths' halls, the atrium house, an inn, the fuller's, the mill, the latrine, the shops of the rows and the upper floors each have a tone, a roof and small works of their own (`ROOMS`, `UPPER`).
- **Not verified.** No one has listened to any of this; the voices were measured with `tools/sound-render.mjs` (spectrum, envelope, onsets) and a test makes every one of them against a stand-in audio context. Whether they sound like Rome is untested. The Latin of the cries is not used: the hawkers cry on pitches only (no words are synthesised).

## 4.20 Animals with a rig (`fauna.js`, `critters`)

- **Dog at the door.** [stated, from memory: the mosaic *cave canem* of the House of the Tragic Poet at Pompeii, and Petronius, *Satyricon* 29, a chained dog painted at the door] **Built:** a dog (`dog`) by the jamb of the atrium house's street door, 0.8 m off the door's 1.6 m, lying at a distance and standing and facing the visitor within 3.5 m. - **Cats.** [inferred] The domestic cat was kept in Roman houses and shops by the second century (the evidence is thin: mosaics and a few epitaphs; stated here from memory only). **Built:** up to six cats (`cat_tabby`, `cat_orange`) curled at the foot of a pier between two shops of a row, on the pavement, out of the 1.5 m width of every door, chosen by the position of the row (one in five); one asleep on the tiled coping (2.95 m) of the south wall of the garden nearest the arrival.

## 4.21 Writing and wear (`lettering.js`, `writing.js`)

- **Why Latin and what.** [stated] The Pantheon's frieze carries Agrippa's dedication, kept on Hadrian's façade (`docs/buildings/pantheon.md` §8d); it is built there with its own letter table. The town's other texts needed the whole alphabet: `lettering.js` has the 23 capitals of the Roman alphabet of the Empire (A B C D E F G H I K L M N O P Q R S T V X Y Z; no J, U or W, V standing for U) and the interpunct, strokes of thick (0.13) and thin (0.07) weight with serifs on the stems that meet the foot or the top, the letter forms after the Pantheon's [inferred: the proportions are ours, not a copy of any inscription]. **Verified** by a rendered sheet drawn from the same ink the 3D letters are built from (`ROME_SHEET=/path.png node --test tests/rome-lettering.test.mjs`) and looked at letter by letter: every letter reads as itself (a first sheet showed a stray bar over G and Y, a serif put on a stem that does not reach the top; fixed).
- **What the town writes.** Only common words of the language, so that no person is invented: CAVPONA, FVLLONICA and PISTRINVM in white over the red sign band that the inn, the fuller's and the bakery already had over their doors (letters 0.26 m high at most, raised 2 cm); SALVE and CAVE CANEM in black raised tesserae on the floor of the atrium house's fauces (0.2 and 0.17 m high), the first met by a visitor coming in from the street. [stated, from memory, not re-checked this pass: the mosaics SALVE and *cave canem* at the doors of houses of Pompeii; fullers, bakers and inns of Pompeii and Ostia marked their trades by signs] [inferred: the use of these words as signs, at Rome, in this form; the white on red].
- **Not written.** No names of persons or magistrates: election notices (*programmata*) are of Pompeii before AD 79 and would be an anachronism at Rome in 128, where the Senate had chosen the magistrates since AD 14 [stated: Tacitus, *Annals* 1.15, from memory]. Sign bands of the rows' shops are not written on (the trades are shown by their goods). The honorific statues' bases are left plain: no text of theirs is known.
- **Wear.** [stated, from memory] The thresholds of the shops of Pompeii and Ostia are hollowed by use, with the groove for the boards. **Built:** the front lip of every shop's threshold in the rows has a pale polished strip across the door's width (a thin slab of the grey of weathered whitewash, 4 mm above the travertine): a hint of wear, not a modelled hollow. Not done: cart ruts in the basalt of the Via Lata, stairs worn in the middle, stains under the lamps.

- **Measured** (`tools/draw-count.mjs rome`, before this round and after; the "before" is the tree at the start of the round): main draws at the arrival 361 → 363 and main triangles 2.70 M → 2.74 M; in the street before the forecourt (at -20, -177 facing east) 382 → 385 draws, 2.05 M → 2.08 M; in the Via Lata (at 176, -100 facing north) 365 → 373 draws, 2.09 M → 2.13 M. `tools/geo-one.mjs`: 85.7 MB, 2434 meshes → 89.3 MB, 2570 meshes. 160 shop bays in 32 rows are opened (the door panels of the rows' near copies are left out), 27 keepers stand in 12 near plans, eleven walks from the street into shops (1.6 m of open door each) are in `walks.js` (`SHOP_WALKS`), walked by `tools/walk-check.mjs`.

## 4.23 Third pass: forecourt, the Pantheon at night, upper floors (`forecourt.js`, `ruts.js`, `pantheon-night.js`, `flats.js`)

### Job 1: the forecourt by day
- **Paving.** [stated] The Hadrianic pavement of the Pantheon's square is of travertine blocks of about 80 x 90 cm and 30 cm thick, laid aligned, about 2.5 m below the present street; first found in the 1990s, more exposed after a sinkhole in 2020 (Turismo Roma, https://www.turismoroma.it/en/news/pantheon-hadrians-imperial-pavement-comes-light, fetched this pass; archaeology.org, 6 May 2020, gives the material only and dates the first pavement to 27-25 BC). **Built:** nothing new: the travertine of the palette already shows courses (1.0 x 1.5 m in the shader); a joint grid of 0.8 x 0.9 would fight with them and the palette is not ours. The sources record no pattern beyond "aligned", no inlays, no altar, fountain or statue in the square: none are built.
- **Statues and benches.** [inferred] Porticoes were hung with honorific statues and had seats against the back wall (no source for this square; the porticoes of the Campus Martius were promenades, Strabo 5.3.8 from memory, not re-checked). **Built:** five statues on moulded plinths against the back wall of each long portico (togate, cuirassed, matron, genius: the forum's `rome_statue`, registered into the Pantheon's own style), three marble benches on each side. Plinths carry no text.
- **Sellers and idlers.** [inferred] A temple forecourt drew sellers of offerings (garlands, lamps, small pots) and idlers. **Built** in two small near plans (36 m from x = +-41, z = -97, hidden from the arrival): per side two trestle stalls with goods, the seller behind, a buyer before; two benches with two sitters each; a pair talking. The west portico's walk (x = -42.5, z -100 to -60) is kept clear.
- **Cart ruts.** [stated, general] Carts wore ruts in the paving of Roman streets (Pompeii); [inferred] here a polished band 0.16 m wide, 4 mm proud, in the gaps of the stepping stones (1.9 m gauge of `crossing`) on the Via Lata (four lines) and the street before the forecourt, in the drain stripe's granite grey. Seen at 2 m: faint but readable.
