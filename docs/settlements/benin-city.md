# Benin City (Edo kingdom), c. 1600 — dossier

Settlement id: `benin-city` · Style: `benin` (new) · Region: `guinea` (the forest belt of southern Nigeria; life kit `africa`) · Code: `src/settlements/benin-city/`, `src/styles/benin/`
Status: new settlement (L0 to the Suzhou standard): the Oba's palace (landmark) with its courts, the broad straight streets, the earthwork rampart and ditch (Iya) with its gates, the guild quarters (brass casters, ivory and wood carvers), the Oba market, the red-earth compounds with impluvia.

## 1. The moment and the real example

- **Moment shown:** about 1600, the reign of Oba Ehengbuda (c. 1578–1606), after the Golden Age of Esigie (1504–1550) and Orhogbua, when the kingdom was at its widest and the city had traded with the Portuguese for a century [inferred date of the shown view; stated: Wikipedia "Benin City" — the Portuguese came about 1485 and the city grew rich "in the 16th and 17th centuries" on pepper and ivory; Wikipedia "Benin Bronzes" — Esigie reigned in one of two golden ages of the metalwork]. Oba Ewuare (c. 1440) is credited with the great rebuilding, the planned streets and the Inner City Iya (c. 1460) [stated: Wikipedia "Walls of Benin": built around 1460 AD; inferred: Ewuare as the builder, a widespread tradition].
- **Sources:** Wikipedia "Benin City" (Lourenço Pinto 1691: the streets "run straight and as far as the eye can see", the king's house "richly decorated" with "fine columns", the houses with no doors; David van Nyendael 1701/02: the palace with turreted roofs decorated with brass snakes, handsome houses) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_City ; Wikipedia "Walls of Benin" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Benin ; Olfert Dapper, *Naukeurige Beschrijvinge der Afrikaensche Gewesten* (1668), as quoted at https://stolenbeninbronzes.blogspot.com/2021/12/4-olfert-dapper-was-dutch-physician-and.html and https://archive.artic.edu/benin/palace ; Wikipedia "Benin Bronzes" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Bronzes ; Wikipedia "Igun Street" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igun_Street ; Pitt-Rivers, *Antique Works of Art from Benin* (1900; Galloway's 1892 account of the impluvium, the ju-ju houses and tusk-bearing heads) — https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/44014/pg44014-images.html ; This is Africa, "African Marvels: The Walls of Benin" — https://thisisafrica.me/politics-and-society/african-marvels-the-walls-of-benin/ .

## 3. The Oba's palace (the landmark)

- **Whole:** "divided into many palaces, houses, and courtiers' apartments", with "fair and long Galleries, one larger than the other, but all supported on Pillars of Wood", the pillars "from top to bottom covered with cast copper" and engraved with the exploits of war [stated: Dapper, archive.artic.edu/benin/palace]. Hundreds of rectangular brass plaques, about 3 mm thick, were hung on the palace pillars by nails [stated: Wikipedia "Benin Bronzes"]. Pinto: "fine columns" [stated]. Every corner a turret "rising high with a Spire", and "every roof is adorned with a small spired tower, on which casted copper birds are standing" [stated: Dapper as quoted]; van Nyendael: turreted roofs decorated with brass snakes [stated].
- **Courts:** the Edo house is built round open courts with a rectangular pool, the impluvium, under an opening in the roof: "The houses have a sort of impluvium in the centre of the rooms" [stated: Galloway 1892 in Pitt-Rivers]; the one surviving old house has "a courtyard, horizontally fluted walls, pillars, a central impluvium and carved decorations" [stated: This is Africa, on Chief Aikoriogie's house, Obasagbon]. The palace is [inferred] a very large set of such courts: an outer court of audience before the great gallery of the Oba, inner courts with galleries on pillars round impluvia, the courts of the queen mother and of the chiefs, the shrine courts.
- **Altars:** the ancestral altar (a platform of red clay) carries bronze commemorative heads of the dead Obas, each with a carved elephant tusk set in its top, bells, staffs and figures; a new Oba set up an altar for his predecessor [stated: Wikipedia "Benin Bronzes"; Pitt-Rivers: bronze heads as stands for carved tusks "in the Ju-Ju houses", the antiquities found "mostly in the king's compound and the Juju houses"]. About 170 heads survive, the oldest of the twelfth century [stated].
- **Walls and finish:** the walls were of red clay, polished smooth, and the storeys above the same clay [stated: Dapper, This is Africa: "divided into rooms by red clay walls", "polished smooth"]. Walls of horizontal ridges (fluting) [stated: the surviving house]. Roofs of timber shingles and palm-leaf thatch [inferred: Roth and the 1897 photographs; not stated by the fetched sources].
- **Measures used here (inferred):** outer wall of clay 3.6 m high and 0.9 m thick; gallery pillars 4.4 m high, 0.42 m across, 3.2 m apart; gallery floors raised 0.3 m on a clay plinth; the audience gallery 11 m deep; the great tower 17 m with the snake down its face and the bird on its point.

## 4. The guild quarters

- **Brass and bronze casters (Igun Eronmwon):** a guild of the Oba, living in a quarter in the palace precincts under his direct control; only the descendants of the seven families may cast [stated: Wikipedia "Benin Bronzes"; "Igun Street"]. Casting is lost-wax: a core of clay, a wax model, a clay shell, the wax run out and brass poured in (the technique is secret; the steps are [inferred] from the general method). Brass came as manillas from the Portuguese and was melted [stated: "Benin Bronzes"]. Under Esigie the work became elaborate, with plaques, and the guild's monopoly was settled [stated: "Igun Street"].
- **Yards [inferred]:** an open shed on posts under palm thatch, a clay furnace with a bellows pot and a charcoal heap, clay moulds drying in rows, wax models on a board, finished heads and plaques hanging on the wall; the caster at the fire, an apprentice at the bellows.
- **Carvers (Igbesanmwan):** ivory and wood, the Oba monopolizing ivory [stated: "Benin Bronzes"]; carved tusks stood on the heads [stated].

## 5. Streets, houses, the market

- **Houses (compound, [inferred] from the sources in §3):** a gabled front range on the street with its door open (Pinto: no doors), a passage to the court, rooms on three sides under lean-to roofs sloping to the impluvium, the back range higher; red clay walls polished, ridged in bands, a plinth step at the door; roofs of palm thatch or shingle; a well in the yard (Dapper). Upper storeys of clay for chiefs [stated: Dapper].
- **Street furniture:** large metal street lamps are reported on the streets [stated, weakly: This is Africa]; they are not built.
- **Market (Oba Market) [inferred]:** rows of mat-roofed stalls: yams, plantains, palm oil, pepper, cloth, brass wares, ivory, kola, goats, fowls; women sellers in wrappers, men in wrappers and the chiefs' coral beads.
- **Trade:** pepper and ivory sold to the Portuguese [stated: "Benin City"].

## 6. Materials and climate

- Red laterite earth for everything [stated: red clay]; brass and bronze; palm thatch, palm-trunk and timber posts; ivory; coral beads for the dress of chiefs [inferred]. Palette names used: `tuff_red`, `stucco_red`, `scoria_red`, `terracotta`, `laterite`, `kokowai` (red-ochre timber), `thatch_palm`, `roof_bark`, `palm_trunk`, `timber`, `wood_dark`, `bronze`, `gilt_bronze`, `ivory`, `iron`, `water`, `cloth_*`.
- Hot, humid, rain-forest zone: soil red, grass and oil palm and silk-cotton trees [inferred: Benin lies at 6.3° N in the forest belt]; damp rises on the clay, algae on the north faces, rain streaks; hazy sky, a bright wet-season light. `damp` 0.9, no waterline.

## 7. People and sound

- **People [inferred]:** the Edo: men in the wrapper (a wide cloth to the knee or ankle), chiefs in a long wrapper and bands of coral beads at the neck and wrists, bare chest; women in wrappers with a second cloth over the shoulder, hair in a crest or plaits; casters bare-chested in loincloth or short wrapper; palace pages and guards with a staff; porters with loads on the head; traders; children. Skin dark; the red earth on feet and hems.
- **Sound:** the log drum and the bells at the palace, the beat of the hammer and the bellows in the guild yard, women's voices at the market, the cry of the dove and the hornbill, cicadas and frogs after rain, goats, fowls.

## 8. Life, smoke, fauna

- **Walkers:** by hour on the streets, at the market and in the guild lane; crossing the great street. **Smoke:** cooking smoke at the three meals from the compounds; the casters' furnaces, thin pale smoke by day, a glow at night (not built); incense at the shrine. **Fauna:** doves and weaverbirds on the roofs, a kite over the market, hornbills over the rampart trees, goats and fowls in the yards, lizards.

## 10. Deepening pass (to the Suzhou standard)

- **Fill of the city [inferred]:** the compounds now fill each run between lanes and zones (15–25 m wide, about 150 lots), not only a few between the lanes; Dapper's houses stand "alongside the streets in good order, close together" [stated: §2]. Fronts are of four kinds: the plain gable with one open door (Pinto: houses without doors) [stated: §1]; a verandah on palm posts over the street with a clay seat; a trading front, the front room open behind a clay counter (the market's and the streets' sellers are women, Dapper/This is Africa [inferred]); a chief's front with an upper storey [stated: Dapper, chiefs' clay upper storeys, §5], here with two windows on the street and a stair from the court.
- **House furnishing [inferred]:** clay beds with reed mats and hides, stools, timber chests, the three-stone hearth with a black pot, wooden mortar and pestle for yam, water jars, calabashes on a shelf, cloth on pegs, racks drying pepper and cassava, washing lines, a plantain clump; the impluvium and well as before [stated: Galloway 1892; Dapper: each house has a well].
- **Ancestral altars [stated: Wikipedia "Benin ancestral altars"]:** a commoner's altar is a mud rectangle with a row of rattle staffs (ukhurue) at the back and a brass bell (eroro) in the middle; a chief's adds a wooden commemorative head; royal altars are round, of white clay, with cast heads topped with coral crowns carrying carved tusks, a brass tableau, bells at the front, staffs at the back. The houses now carry the first kind; the palace the royal kind (round, white).
- **Leopards [stated: Art Institute of Chicago, "Pair of Leopards", archive.artic.edu/benin/artwork/189491]:** brass leopards stood for the Oba's power, "the kingly counterpart in the wilderness"; a pair may have stood on either side of the throne. Built: a cast pair at the palace gate and one at each side of the dais steps.
- **Palace [inferred]:** two strips inside the palace wall beside the outer court are filled with ranges of single rooms for servants and stores (tusks, chests, kitchens, sleeping rooms, the casters' stock of heads on shelves), opening on lanes behind the outer court's galleries, whose doors lead to them; clay seats along the galleries, stools, water jars; the great gallery has leopard skins on the dais, stools of the chiefs in two rows, mats, clay seats along the walls, hanging bells; the gatehouse has the guards' clay seats and a rack of staves.
- **Street furniture:** lamps on posts along the great and cross streets (the large metal street lamps [stated, weakly: This is Africa; here a timber post with a brass bowl of palm oil, inferred]); in the king's square rows of clay seats and stools, a slit log drum on forked posts and a staff of bells [inferred: the palace drum and the bell of the Oba]; firewood and water jars at the lane mouths; silk-cotton and oil palms along the verges and in the market [inferred: forest-belt trees; stand-ins `banyan`, `date_palm`].
- **Guild yards [inferred]:** the casters' wax bench with wax heads, an anvil stone and hammer, the Ogun shrine (a clay mound with iron staves; Ogun the god of iron, inferred for the casters); the carvers' stools for sale, billets and a rack of tools; the weavers' warp threads on the frame, bolts of cloth, vats of indigo. Charcoal baskets, pots and firewood at the yards' fronts.
- **Gates [inferred]:** a gate shrine (clay mound with iron staves, chalked ring), toll goods (baskets, yam), guards with staves and travellers with loads at all eight gates.
- **Weathering [inferred: humid forest belt]:** splash band at the foot of every clay wall, damp and algae patches, rain streaks washed from the eaves, patches of mended clay (all drawn as thin relief, near only).
- **Street life:** sellers on mats along the verges of the great street and on the open floor of the Oba market with fowls and goats, people on the seats of the verandahs, hens and goats at the doors, guards and travellers at the gates, children; sparrows (weaverbird stand-ins) on the thatch ridges and the palace wall's coping.

## 11. Deepening pass 2: the palace seen from the king's square, and the life at the arrival

- **What the first view showed:** from the square the palace was a low brown block behind a wall with two small turrets; in rain the square looked empty because the walkers thin out and the static life stood far off. [inferred: the review screenshot of the arrival]
- **Roofs [stated]:** shingled roofs were introduced under Oba Esigie in the 16th century (corrugated sheet came only in the 19th) — https://access.thebrightcontinent.org/items/show/2 . Dapper (1668): "fair and long galleries ... all supported on pillars of wood" covered with cast copper, and "every roof adorned with a small spired tower on which cast copper birds stand" (§3). So the palace is drawn with very steep shingle roofs (about 50°) on long galleries, with small spired towers along every ridge. The pitch is [inferred] from the engraving and the plaques, which show steep roofs; the exact slopes are not recorded.
- **Turrets [stated]:** towers rose above the perimeter wall and made the palace more imposing than any other building; cast brass snake heads hung over the lower part of each tower, two to a tower, from the 16th century; the bird on top has outstretched wings; the tower of the Dapper engraving is pointed, but the plaque and a brass box show a flat-topped pyramid — https://access.thebrightcontinent.org/items/show/2 ; the British Museum plaque Af1898,0115.46 (a palace with a high shingled roof, a turret with a pyramid at its centre and a snake down its front head first) — https://digitalbenin.org/catalogue/5_Af1898011546 . Built: the turrets have a battered clay body, an open lantern, a skirt roof and a truncated pyramid of shingle with a flat top; a python of new bright (gilt) brass winds down the pyramid head first; two cast heads hang over the clay body; the bird is exaggerated to a 2.4 m span so that it reads from 90 m [inferred]. Two great turrets stand at the ends of the audience gallery and two flank the gate; the four at the corners are lower.
- **The front of the compound [inferred]:** behind the 3.8 m south wall stand two long front galleries (44 m each) on a double row of brass pillars with plaques, open to the outer court, with a steep shingle roof whose slope rises above the wall and whose ridge carries the small spired towers; the plaques hang on the wall's inner face. The wall's outer face has buttresses every 7.5 m and a frieze of plaques between them near the gate [inferred: Dapper's plaques "nailed to the pillars"; the wall's plaques are a stand-in for the same cast panels]. The audience gallery is raised to a 6 m wall and a ridge at about 14.5 m, the gate to a 6.4 m wall and a ridge at about 13 m.
- **The gate [inferred]:** the gate is not described; it is built as a gabled gatehouse over a 5 m passage with two wings (rooms with a clay bed, chest, guards' bench), a small spired tower with the great bird on its ridge, and a portico of six pillars of bright brass hung with larger plaques under a skirt roof on a clay plinth with steps, the pair of cast leopards on low plinths at the foot of the steps (Art Institute: leopards stood for the Oba's power), braziers of incense on the porch.
- **The waiting sheds [inferred]:** two gabled sheds of palm thatch on trunk posts, and fifteen bays of thatch leaning on the palace wall either side of the gate, with clay seats, mats, jars and cooking fires, where petitioners, sellers and idle people sit; the sources say nothing of them, but the king's square was the place of audiences and a place of waiting is the least that follows. They exist so that the arrival shows sheltered life in rain and shade.
- **Static life [inferred]:** seated petitioners on the sheds' seats and mats, a goat and fowls at each shed, women with pots, a cook at each fire, two ranks of sellers on mats before the sheds, chiefs in talk, a kneeling petitioner and a dog on the way to the gate, guards between the porch pillars, courtiers on the clay seats of the porch. These are placed static (they do not depend on the weather); the walkers thin to half in rain.
- **Not built:** the true plan behind the gate; the great doors; the sacrificial and ritual places of the square.
