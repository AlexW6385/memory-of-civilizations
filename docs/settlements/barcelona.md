## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Sagrada Família, begun in 1882, with Gaudí in charge from 1883. In 1910 the crypt and the apse walls stood, and the Nativity façade was rising. Its first bell tower was finished in 1925 [stated: Wikipedia "Sagrada Família"].
- **The Eixample:** Ildefons Cerdà's plan of 1859, a grid of blocks about 113 m square with chamfered corners [stated: Wikipedia "Eixample"].
- **Gaudí's houses:** Casa Milà (La Pedrera, 1906–1912) with its undulating stone front, and Casa Batlló (remodelled 1904–1906) on the Passeig de Gràcia [stated: Wikipedia "Casa Milà"; "Casa Batlló"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1910.

### The Sagrada Família as it stood about 1910

- The crypt was finished in 1889, and the apse walls were built from then until 1894 [stated: UPC IAHC, "Sagrada Familia – Nativity facade"]; the apse was dated 1894 and the schools 1909 [stated: Catalan News timeline]. The apse has seven chapels in a horseshoe round an ambulatory [stated: Wikipedia "Sagrada Família"]. Built: a horseshoe of seven polygonal chapel walls (outer radius 16 m, 19 m high) with two lancets and a rose each, hood moulds, a crocketed gable and finial over each chapel, stepped buttresses between them each carrying a pinnacle, and two straight presbytery walls running south. Inside, a floor over the crypt. The walls are left open to the sky because the apse vault, a hyperboloid reaching 75 m, was built much later [stated: the vault; inferred: that in 1910 it was open]. The pinnacles' crocketed shafts and finials of stacked buds are composed in the manner of the neo-Gothic apse [inferred]; their sizes are ours [inferred].
- **Nativity façade:** work on it began in 1891–1892 (foundations 1892) [stated: Sagrada Família, "History of the temple"; UPC IAHC]. The first tower (Barnabas, 98 m) was finished in November 1925 and the other three (Simon, Jude Thaddeus, Matthias) in 1930; the cypress pinnacle and the lanterns of the Hope and Faith portals were done before 1935 [stated: UPC IAHC; Sagrada Família Booklet 06]. One timeline puts the completion of the bell towers' lower parts ("basements") in 1918 [stated: Preceden timeline — a weak source]. The Portal of the Rosary on the cloister side was finished in 1899 [stated: Booklet 06]. Period photographs of 1905, 1906 and 1915 show the façade in timber scaffolding [stated: Bridgeman "View of the Fachada del Nacimiento … in 1905"; Rare Historical Photos captions]; we could not open the images themselves, so the 1910 state below is reasoned from the dates.
  - **What the façade is** [stated: Booklet 06; Sagrada Família blog "Nativity façade"; Wikipedia "Sagrada Família"]: on the transept's east end (Carrer de la Marina); three portals, left to right as one faces it — Hope (Joseph; the sea end, south), Charity (Jesus; the middle) and Faith (Mary; the mountain end, north); framed by stalactites "expressing the cold temperatures at Christmas"; full of plants (roses, almond, cherry, lilies, passion flowers). Two columns separate the portals, topped with palm fronds, each resting on a turtle: a sea turtle under Joseph's column (south) and a land tortoise under Mary's (between Charity and Faith). The four bell towers start from a square base and become round after a few metres. Charity, from the top down: the Coronation of Mary (crowned by Jesus, Joseph at the left), the Annunciation (by Jaume Busquets), the star of Bethlehem with its trail, the Nativity over a column's capital with the ox and the mule (Busquets), the adorations of the kings and of the shepherds at the sides (Joaquim Ros i Bofarull), child angels singing and six angel musicians; the central column bears Jesus' genealogy [stated: Booklet 06] and the serpent; 33 figures in all. Hope: the flight into Egypt, the massacre of the Innocents, the wedding of Mary and Joseph, Joseph sailing a boat. Faith: the Visitation, Jesus as a carpenter, the Presentation, the Immaculate Conception.
  - **Built (1910):** the four towers from square bases (7.2 m, string courses, slits) to 24 m, a collar turning them round, round hollow shafts (r 2.65 m) with staggered narrow openings, their last course broken off at 30.5, 37.5, 40 and 32.5 m, each in timber scaffolding standing on its base [inferred: the 1925/1930 completions and "basements … 1918" put the towers well short of their height in 1910; heights ours]. The three portals as deep parabolic porches (Charity 8.4 m wide and 21 m high, the sides 6.8 × 17 m) splayed back to the doorway in three stepped orders, rough as ice and rock, the mouths hung with stalactites; over each a sculpted mass (Charity's to 36 m, the sides' to 28.5 m) grown with knots of leaves and flowers and set with the scenes above on leafy corbels, the figures of life size and over [stated: the programme; inferred: the forms, sizes and positions]. Charity's trumeau with the bands of the genealogy and the serpent coiled up it, the Nativity on its capital (Mary seated, Joseph, the manger, the ox and the mule), the star with its trail, the Annunciation, the Coronation, the shepherds and the kings, six angels. The palm columns (13 m, ringed with leaf scars, a crown of eleven fronds) on the sea turtle (flat shell, flippers) and the tortoise (domed shell, legs). Charity's top left flat and in scaffolding, blocks waiting where the cypress (1930s) would go; the side portals' tops capped, their pinnacles (1935) not yet [stated: the dates; inferred: the state]. Not built: the cypress and its doves, the pelican, the chameleons, the rosary windows [the cypress and lanterns are after 1910; the others left for later].
- **Schools (Escoles de la Sagrada Família):** opened on 15 November 1909 on ground meant for the Glory façade. The plan is 10 × 20 m with three classrooms. The walls are brick in three overlapping layers, and both the walls and the roof wave [stated: Wikipedia "Sagrada Família Schools"]. Built: the long walls waving in plan (three waves, ±0.45 m). The roof is a conoid of tiles: its rulings run straight from a level central beam to the walls' waving heads (±0.75 m) [stated: the conoid roof, Google Arts & Culture "Conoid roof of the Sagrada Família School"; amplitudes inferred].
- **The works:** a long timber workshop shed under a lean-to tile roof, the stone yard's cut blocks, and a guyed timber derrick lifting toward the façade [inferred: the period's site practice; Gaudí's workshop was on the site, Sagrada Família blog]. The block's ground is bare soil, with no trees.
- Removed: the 90 m stone slab that stood for the nave (not built in 1910), the finished-looking towers to 100 m, and the steel crane.

### Casa Milà and Casa Batlló

- **Casa Milà** (92 Passeig de Gràcia): built 1906–1912, completed December 1910; nine storeys including basement, ground floor, mezzanine, main floor, four upper floors and attic; Garraf limestone; floors set back with different curves, "similar to waves"; twisting wrought-iron balconies; an attic on 270 parabolic vaults; roof with skylights, stair exits and chimneys ("the garden of warriors") [stated: Wikipedia "Casa Milà"]. Built on the north-west corner of its block (the Passeig and Provença — the corner site from the plan, inferred): the front following the property line round the cut corner, smoothed; each floor's edge a rounded stone band whose projection waves along the front with a phase that shifts floor to floor; piers bending from band to band (heavier on the ground floor); dark windows behind; iron balconies of twisting bars on about two openings in three; the attic's small windows under an undulating crest; eleven chimneys and stair exits on the roof, the three large ones white [inferred: proportions and counts].
- **Casa Batlló** (remodelled 1904–1906): the main floor's façade entirely of sandstone, its bay on columns like the bones of a limb, the openings like an open mouth; above, a mosaic of broken ceramic tiles shading from golden orange to greenish blue with glass discs and 330 rounds of polychrome pottery; the roof's arched profile like a dragon's spine with ceramic scales, and a tower topped with a cross whose four arms point to the cardinal directions [stated: Wikipedia "Casa Batlló"]. Built on the Passeig's west side, 15 m wide: the sandstone ground and principal floors, the bay on six bone-like columns under a scalloped lintel, glazing behind; the mosaic front waving gently, set with ~150 ceramic discs; the windows in stone frames behind mask-shaped balconies (bent plates with two eyes); a cornice; the scaled roof back with its spine of round tiles, rising toward the far end; the turret with its bulb and four-armed cross [inferred: width, floor heights, mask shape and the balconies' metal colour; the single turquoise mosaic material stands for the gradient].

### The Eixample's houses

- Square blocks with chamfered (45°) corners round an internal courtyard; streets 20–60 m wide [stated: Barcelona Bus Turístic "Eixample"; Wikipedia "Eixample"]. Built: each block a ring of houses on plots of 12–19 m along the four streets, a house on each cut corner with a short return; every house with a granite plinth and coursed stone ground floor of shops (wooden fronts and fascias) and a portal, an entresol, the principal floor with its long iron balcony on stone brackets, three or four floors with a balcony of iron to each window, green or brown louvred shutters open, half-drawn or closed, lintels, a cornice and a parapet round a flat roof terrace with its stair house and chimneys; the corner houses with a glazed tribune up the middle of the cut; the court sides with plain windows and laundry [inferred: the house type of the period — floor heights 4.6/2.8/3.8/3.3 m, bay 3.4 m — composed by us]. Facades in stone, cream, ochre, pale and rose stucco.

### 5a. Street life (about 1910)

- **Trams.** The first tram (1872, horse-drawn, four horses) ran from the Drassanes up the Rambla and the Passeig de Gràcia to Gràcia; electric traction came in 1899 and by 1905 the other lines were being rebuilt for it; steam trams ran 1877–1902; the operator was the Barcelona Tramways Company Limited [stated: TMB, "150è aniversari del primer tramvia"]. Closed car no. 58 entered service in 1899: 8.90 m long, 2.00 m wide, 3.59 m high, two wooden benches inside, two doors [stated: Fundació TMB, "Tramvia No. 58"]. The open-sided "jardinera" no. 129 of 1906 (built by Can Girona, Poblenou): 8.90 × 2.00 × 3.59 m, no sides, a roof; restored in its original colour, which the page implies was red [stated: Fundació TMB, "Open slided gardener tram"; the red inferred].
  Built: two double-track lines — down the Passeig de Gràcia (from Gràcia toward the old city) and one turning off it round a cut corner, east along a cross street and round another corner up the Carrer de la Marina past the Nativity façade. The rails (1.435 m apart, a flangeway beside each) in bands of granite setts; iron poles at both kerbs every 30 m (every 6 m round the curves, where the pole inside the curve gives way to a bracket arm), span wires across with the trolley wires hung over each track at 5.9 m. Trams on them: closed saloon cars (red panels, cream waist and letter boards, six windows a side, clerestory, rounded platform dashes with lamp and number board, the truck's wheels on the rails, life guards) and open jardineres (eight cross benches with reversible backs under the roof on posts, running boards, curtains rolled under the eaves); each with its trolley pole on the wire, a motorman in cap and blue on the front platform, the conductor behind, passengers seated in the open cars [inferred: the line, livery details, gauge, pole spacing and wire height are ours; car sizes stated].
- **The cut corners.** Cerdà's 45° chamfer was designed to accommodate the tram's turning radius; he believed the steam tram would dominate the city's transport [stated: Failed Architecture, "Behind four walls: Barcelona's lost utopia"]. Built: the line sweeps round two chamfered corners on a 14 m radius [inferred].
- **Carriages.** Hackney carriages (a victoria: a low black body between a small fore carriage and large back wheels, folding hood up or down, red cushions, the driver in black with a hat on his box, lamps, one horse in the shafts) at a rank on the Passeig and here and there at the kerbs, keeping right [inferred: the type is the period's common cab; positions ours]. Carts, horses, figures, lamps and shop signs come from the shared life pass (kit "industrial").
- **Planes** along both kerbs of every street, clear of the crossings and the tram poles [stated: planes; inferred: spacing].

### 5b. The blocks' courts

Cerdà planned the block's middle as a green court for sunlight and ventilation, part of a citywide green belt; most blocks were instead built up on all four sides and their courts later closed and built over [stated: Failed Architecture]. Built (1910, before the courts filled with workshops): each house's garden runs from its back into the court between party walls carried on from the plot lines, the four sides' gardens meeting on walls along the court's diagonals (the usual subdivision of a Cerdà block — inferred); in each garden a gravel path from the back door, a round stone basin with water or a well head with its iron hoop and pulley at the far end, on some a timber pergola by the house, terracotta pots along the walls; planted with date palms, oleanders, figs, cypresses and grass [inferred: contents and species].
