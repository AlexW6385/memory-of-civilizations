## 1. The moment and the real place

- **Moment shown:** the autumn of 210 BC, weeks after the First Emperor's death at Shaqiu (Sima Qian, *Shiji* 6, "Annals of the First Emperor"): the tomb is being closed, the pit roofed. [stated: the pits "lie about 1,500 m east of the mound" — Wikipedia "Terracotta Army"; the tomb's construction began on his accession, c. 246 BC, and used 700,000 workers, per Sima Qian — general] We show the pit's last stage: the army stands finished and painted; the roof of logs is laid over the whole pit, the earth cover is heaped over the western part and not yet over the eastern. [inferred: the pit was roofed and buried; the unfinished eastern cover is our device to make the beams visible from outside]
- **Two places, one disc.** The pits stand on the plain east of Mount Li near modern Lintong, about 40 km south-east of Xianyang, the capital on the Wei river. [stated: general; Xianyang's palace ruins are 72 km² surveyed, "more than 230 sites" — Wikipedia "Xianyang Palace"] The planet's disc is the mausoleum's (lat 34.38, lon 109.25); we draw the capital's palace and market beside the army, compressed, as the planet does for every place that is two sites. [inferred]
- **Why this moment:** the unified empire (221 BC) is at its height: one script, one coinage, one measure, the roads. The Qin fall in 206 BC; the pits were burned and collapsed in the rising, which is why the army was found broken. [stated: general; Wikipedia "Terracotta Army"]

## 3. Ground and climate

The Guanzhong plain: loess, flat, the Wei valley with Mount Li (Qinling) as a blue wall to the south — we leave the mountain to the far view. Dry-winter continental climate, dust and haze, summer rain. [stated: general] Millet and wheat fields, Chinese pine (*Pinus tabuliformis*), elm, jujube, mulberry, poplar and willow by the water. [inferred: regional flora of the Guanzhong plain] Loess dust everywhere: the ground is pale yellow-brown. Damp low (0.1).

## 4. Pit 1 — the army as it was

- **The figures:** "life-sized, typically about 1.75 m to 2 m. Officers are typically taller"; kneeling and standing archers, infantry, charioteers, officers and generals; "a peaked cap indicates a civil official of the eighth or higher rank. A flat, split-peak cap is usually a general's"; "the whole body was made in one piece [in the reconstruction], and only the head could be lifted off"; the torso coiled hollow, legs from a solid lump, heads and hands moulded (Zhang Wenli) — competing theories. [stated: Met Museum; Wikipedia "Terracotta Army"] Each 150–200 kg, fired 6–10 hours. [stated: Met Museum]
- **Colour:** "practically every warrior and horse was painted"; on a base of raw lacquer; pigments cinnabar, malachite, azurite, ground bone white, charcoal, iron oxide, "Han purple" (synthetic barium copper silicate); "faces were pink, with black eyebrows and facial hair"; "dozens of colors … soft green, vermillion, claret red, pink, azure, and white"; "generals had more complicated colors". [stated: chinahighlights.com "The Original Colors of the Terracotta Army"; Wikipedia] Ours: robes in green, red, blue, purple, white and pink with the collars and hems in a second colour; armour plates dark brown (lacquered leather) with coloured lacings [inferred: the armour's colour; the lacings follow the traces on excavated plates].
- **Dress and weapons:** the infantry in a knee-length belted coat, leg bindings, square-toed shoes, the hair in a topknot twisted to one side under a cap or bare; armoured soldiers in lamellar coats with shoulder pieces; the archers with crossbows, the spearmen with bronze-headed spears and halberds, swords at the belt; "over 40,000 bronze weapon items have been recovered, mostly arrowheads … found in bundles of 100". [stated: Wikipedia] The weapons were taken in the rising; we restore them. [inferred]
- **Formation:** the army faces east; the front rank of archers; behind them the infantry in the corridors, two files of ours (the real corridors held three or four abreast; ours leave a walking aisle) with the chariots, each of four horses abreast under a wooden carriage with a driver and two soldiers, at intervals. [inferred]
- **Light:** none from outside; the corridors are roofed. Torches at the partitions and the ramp's light. [inferred]

## 5. Style vocabulary (style `qin`)

- **Rammed earth (hangtu):** walls and platforms of earth beaten in layers; "buildings were mainly on high platforms of rammed earth"; murals on a "three-layer structure: a mud-based plaster layer, a white ground layer, and a paint layer"; "hollow bricks … used as platform stepping stones and as barrier bricks for corridors, often with dragon or geometric patterns". [stated: Wikipedia "Xianyang Palace"]
- **Timber hall:** the Qin hall is the early tai-xie type: a rammed-earth core with timber rooms round it, round columns, simple bracket blocks (ludou) rather than the later complex dougong, a hipped roof of grey semi-cylindrical tiles ending in round tile-ends. [inferred: general Qin–Han architecture]
- **Black and red:** Qin took water as its element and so black as its colour; flags and robes black, the palace in black and vermilion. [stated: general, *Shiji* 6; the palace's actual colours inferred]
- **Weights and measures:** units "based on Qin's existing standards: the chi (about 23.1 cm), sheng (about 200 mL), and dan (about 60 kg). Bronze prototypes were distributed to every commandery and county. Officials had to display the standards publicly and use them for tax collection, grain storage, and market trade"; bronze weights carry an edict of 221 BC. [stated: historyrise.com; general]
- **Axles and coins:** "all carts had to use the same wheel spacing" (six chi, about 1.39 m) [stated: historyrise.com]; the round ban liang coin with a square hole, strung on cords. [stated: same]
- **Script:** Li Si's small seal script on the standards and stelae. [stated: same]

## 6. The town

- **Market:** the walled square with its tower; stalls in rows under awnings: grain in sheng measures, bolts of cloth, pottery, salt, lacquer ware, bronze, the balance-beam and the stone weights on every counter, the market officer's office at the tower with the edict on a board. [inferred]
- **Houses:** rammed-earth walls, flat roofs of beams and clay or gabled roofs of grey tile or thatch; a gate in a street wall leading to a courtyard; a raised granary (cang) on posts. [inferred: Qin bamboo-slip and excavated houses]
- **Figure-makers' quarter:** clay pits and a pug-yard, hollow torsos rising on a turntable 10 cm a day, heads from two-part moulds hung on racks, bodies drying in the shade under damp cloth, the kiln (a round updraught kiln of grey brick), the painting shed with lacquer pots and pigment dishes, the finished figures standing in rows to be carried to the pit on sledges. [inferred from the sources of §4]
- **People:** officials in black robes and tall caps; soldiers; labourers in short coats and trousers; women in the long wrapped robe (shenyi) with the long sleeves and a wide sash; convict labourers in grey. [inferred]
- **The road:** carts of one gauge; ox carts with solid wheels; the chariots of officials with canopies; pines at the edge. [inferred]
