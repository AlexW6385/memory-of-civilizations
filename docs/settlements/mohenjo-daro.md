# Mohenjo-daro, c. 2500 BC — dossier

Settlement id: `mohenjo-daro` · Styles: `indus` (own) and `life` · Region: the Indus valley (Sindh, Pakistan) · Code: `src/settlements/mohenjo-daro/`, `src/styles/indus/`
Status: built from nothing in this batch: the Citadel mound with the Great Bath (enterable: court, stairs, pool, gallery, cells and well), the granary / great hall, the Pillared Hall, the College of Priests; the Lower Town grid of baked-brick houses with covered drains, wells, flat roofs, lane doors, furnished houses and bead, seal, pottery, bronze and shell workshops; the central market with its well; the town wall and tower on the south; the Indus with its landing and reed-and-plank boats; carts with zebu; people in the dress of the figurines.

## 1. The moment and the real place

- **Moment shown:** about 2500 BC, the mature Indus (Harappan) phase, when the city is at its height. [stated: Wikipedia "Mohenjo-daro": "Built c. 2500 BCE … abandoned c. 1700 BCE"]
- **Place:** on the right (west) bank of the lower Indus, on a Pleistocene ridge in the floodplain, Larkana District, Sindh; about 28 km from Larkana. [stated: Wikipedia "Mohenjo-daro"] Latitude 27.33° N, longitude 68.14° E.
- **Why this moment:** the one great ancient river civilization otherwise missing from the set; the undeciphered seal script, the "priest-king", the bronze "dancing girl". [stated: DESIGN §7] The city shows the whole system at once: grid streets, covered drains, a public bath, a granary or hall, fired-brick houses, seal and bead workshops.
- **Size:** "250 ha … covered area about 300 ha", "population at least 40,000 by one estimate", which the Oxford Handbook calls weak. [stated: Wikipedia] Other accounts say 30,000–60,000 for Mohenjo-daro and Harappa each. [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"]
- **The river:** the Indus ran close to the city on the east in antiquity (its bed has since moved about 5 km east); the city rose on platforms against its floods. [inferred from general accounts: the river's present course is farther away; Wheeler's view that the city was flooded and silted up to six times is [stated] in Wikipedia "Mohenjo-daro"]

## 3. Ground, climate, sky

Flat alluvial plain, the grey-buff silt of the Indus, dust in the streets, packed mud in the lanes; very hot, dry, hazy; the river's banks soft, reeds and tamarisk at the water; the plain beyond in barley and wheat fields (winter crops) with date palms and acacia. [inferred, general] Sun high (lat 27°), warm light, `damp` very low. The Indus at its summer height is wide and brown; we show a wide channel with a low bank. [inferred] Crops: "winter crops were wheat, barley and pulses; summer crops rice, millets and beans; peas, sesame, dates, cotton" [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"]. At 2500 BC the climate was somewhat wetter than today's desert [inferred], so the country has trees.

## 4. The Citadel and the Great Bath (the landmark)

See §2. The platform is built of mud brick with a baked-brick facing and buttress towers on its edge, entered by a broad stair from the town. On the top: the Bath's court is the centre of the northern half, the granary at its western end, the Pillared Hall at the eastern end, the College to the south; houses and lanes fill the corners. The Bath is entered from the south doors into the gallery, round the court to the pool, down the north or south stair to the ledge. Brick paving everywhere on the top, mud plaster on the walls above the facing. [inferred: the arrangement of the buildings on the top; the Bath's own details are stated above]

## 5. Style vocabulary (style `indus`)

- **Baked brick** in English bond, thin dark joints, a red-buff colour with burnt headers; **mud brick** plastered with mud, buff. Bricks in the 4:2:1 ratio. [stated: anantamias.com]
- **Flat roofs:** timber beams and reed or matting covered with a thick layer of mud, a low parapet and waterspouts (the roofs of the Lower Town are lost; flat mud roofs are [inferred] from the drains from bathrooms, the stairs to the roof and the continuity with later South Asian practice).
- **Plain street fronts:** blank walls with a single door on the lane; windows high, small, of brick lattice or timber (clay models show windows) [inferred].
- **Stairs** of brick, ending at the roof; **corbelled arches** of brick for drains. [stated for the corbelled arch: Wikipedia "Great Bath"]
- **Brick piers** with stepped edges for the Bath gallery and the Pillared Hall. [stated for the Bath: "preserved columns have stepped edges"]

## 6. The town

- **The grid:** "cities were laid out in a uniform grid, with the ground divided into two levels" [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"].
- **Workshops:** shell working, ceramics, agate and glazed steatite bead making; "carnelian and terracotta beads were made at Mohenjo-daro, and glazed faience beads found"; "steatite seals (2 to 4 cm square) were used to stamp clay on trade goods; thousands have been recovered"; bronze alloyed with tin, arsenic and lead; copper from Khetri and the Aravalli. [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"] "Copper tablets, balance-scales and weights, seals, gold and jasper jewellery, children's toys, pottery braziers." [stated: Wikipedia "Mohenjo-daro"] "Chert cubical weights in a 5:2:1 ratio; one unit about 28 g". [stated] Ours: a bead workshop (bow drills, grinding stones, a small kiln, strings of carnelian and steatite), a seal workshop (benches, copper burins, blank steatite squares, a kiln for glazing), a potter's yard (a wheel, two updraft kilns, rows of drying jars), a coppersmith's with a crucible hearth, a shell-bangle workshop with conch shells.
- **Carts and boats:** "bullock carts may have looked like those still used across South Asia" and "boats were mostly small, flat-bottomed craft, perhaps sail-driven"; "plank-built sea craft with a single central mast and a sail of woven rushes or cloth" [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"]; the terracotta cart models show two solid wheels and a platform. [inferred] Ours: two-wheeled ox carts with a plank bed and solid disc wheels, zebu in the yoke (humped cattle on the seals [stated]); flat-bottomed river boats of plank and reed with an awning.
- **Animals:** "cattle, buffalo, goat, pig and chicken"; "humped zebu-type cattle appear often on seals"; "water buffalo domesticated" [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"]; dogs.
- **The market:** the central market place with its large central well [stated]; stalls of grain, dates, pots, beads, cloth. [inferred]

## 7. People (dress, bearing)

The "priest-king" (soapstone, 17.5 cm) wears a cloak drawn over the left shoulder, patterned with trefoils, a headband with a central ornament and an armlet, a short trimmed beard, the hair combed back; the "dancing girl" (bronze, 10.5 cm) is nude but for a necklace and a great many bangles on one arm, the hair in a bun. [stated: Wikipedia "Mohenjo-daro" for the dimensions and the find; the dress [inferred] from the figurines] Ours: men in a wrapped cloth (dhoti-like) with a shawl over one shoulder, bare above, the hair in a bun or tied back, some bearded; women in a short wrapped skirt, a long necklace and bangles, the hair in a bun or long; porters with headloads and shoulder poles; craftsmen bare-chested at their benches; children bare. Skin medium to dark.

## 8. Sound

The river and its birds, the creak of ox-cart axles on solid wheels, the hiss of kilns, the tapping at the bead workers' benches, the drip of the Bath's drain, bells of the oxen (clay models show bells [inferred]), goats and dogs, crows and kites, cicadas, a drum. No instruments are certain; clay rattles and whistles are known from the sites and we use a plucked string, a flute and a drum as stand-ins. [inferred] Music: a modal drone with a reed pipe and a hand drum.

## 9. Weathering

Dust and the salt that rises in the baked brick (efflorescence at the foot), mud plaster cracked and flaking in patches, the bitumen black at the Bath's waterline, soot over the kilns, brick spalled at the corners. `damp` very low; the waterline at the Bath's level and the river's. [inferred; "groundwater salinity" [stated: Wikipedia] is the real site's enemy]

## 11. The deepening pass: what was added and on what grounds

- **House fronts [inferred]:** the street faces are blank walls of baked brick under mud brick [stated: anantamias.com]; the dressing is what the climate does to them (efflorescence at the foot of baked brick, "groundwater salinity" [stated: Wikipedia]; flaking mud plaster) and what clay models and later South Asian practice suggest (timber door frames and leaves, projecting rafter ends under a flat mud roof, small lattice windows [clay models show windows], sitting benches of mud brick beside the door, mat shades, lamp niches blackened by soot).
- **Rooms by use [inferred]:** sleeping rooms (rope-net beds, mats, lamps), kitchens (hearth, saddle quern, firewood), stores (jars, sacks, shelves), bathrooms of baked brick with a drain channel [stated: baked bricks for bathrooms and drains, anantamias.com], work rooms (loom frame, baskets: cotton is among the crops [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"]); an upper room over one back room in about a third of the houses ("some had two stories" [stated: Wikipedia]).
- **Roofs [inferred]:** flat mud roofs used in the heat: reed shades, rope cots, grain mats, jars, fuel.
- **The street [inferred]:** soak jars and brick stacks at the walls, dust drifts, ruts of solid-wheeled carts, dung cakes (fuel), water-stands with jars; covered drains and soakage jars at junctions are [stated: anantamias.com].
- **The market [inferred]:** stalls of grain, pots, beads, cloth, baskets, fish and dates ("peas, sesame, dates" [stated: Wikipedia "Indus Valley Civilisation"]); mat pavilions for shade.
- **The landing [inferred]:** warehouse sheds with balances and stone weights (chert weights in a 5:2:1 ratio, one unit about 28 g [stated: Wikipedia]), seals pressed at the harbour office, nets drying, fish in baskets, washing; fish stakes in the shallows; reed boats poled by fishermen, plank boats with a steersman.
- **The Great Bath [stated + inferred]:** wooden screens in alternate bays of the gallery ("stepped edges that may have held wooden screens" [stated: Wikipedia "Great Bath"]); bathers on the steps are [inferred] (ritual bathing is the usual reading, not proven).
- **The College [stated + inferred]:** "78 rooms, thought to be a priestly residence" [stated: Wikipedia]; the cells, stores, work room and the shrine with a seated stone figure are [inferred] (the "priest-king" is soapstone [stated]).
- **The granary hall [inferred]:** whether granary or great hall is argued [stated]; ours shows the sacks, jars, a balance, a tally board and a ladder, which fit either reading.
- **Not built:** the flood-defence platforms and the west wall's second tower row; the Stupa; individual DK and VS layouts.

## 12. The Citadel deepening pass (browser review fixes)

- **The Great Bath's water [inferred, from the brief's review]:** the pool is 2.4 m deep with a bitumen lining [stated: Wikipedia "Great Bath, Mohenjo-daro"]; the water now stands about 1 m below the coping so that at a visitor's grazing view the black bitumen lining and the wet steps read as a tank, not as paving. The steps of the two stairs that stand in the water are dark (wet brick), those within half a metre above it are damp, the rest dry baked brick. The bathers stand waist-deep on the third step from the bed and sit hip-deep on the fifth.
- **Drains of the platform [stated for the city: covered brick drains with manholes, Wikipedia; anantamias.com]:** the platform's streets and lanes get the same covered channel at their own height (the Lower Town's were drawn at the plain), falling east to a spout through the parapet and a salt-stained run down the facing [inferred: the outfall's placement].
- **Wells [stated: "over 700 wells, roughly one for every three houses"; the wells are lined with wedge-shaped bricks, Wikipedia]:** street wells on the platform and at the mouths of some lanes in the Lower Town, each with a paved apron, trough, rope and pot [inferred: rope and pot; no windlass is attested].
- **The pipal [stated: seals show a deity among the branches of a pipal (Ficus religiosa), Wikipedia "Pashupati seal" and general accounts; inferred: a tree on a brick seat in the Citadel]:** two trees on round brick seats with elders sitting under them.

## 5b. Four street fronts and the households within (round 5)

[inferred] The excavated houses present blank baked-brick fronts to the lanes, but the lanes were not all alike: shops and workshops at the bazaar opened to the street under mat awnings with a counter at the door, some fronts were plastered in mud and limewashed over the brick, and parapets were finished in openwork of brick. Each lot now takes one of four fronts (indus house `variant`): plain baked brick over mud brick; shop front (awning on posts, mud-brick counter with the trade's goods, strings under the eave; always for the workshops); limewashed lower front with a red-ochre dado; a parapet screen of brick pillars. Twenty open houses lie within 120 m of the arrival (thirteen furnished and lit on the Citadel's quarters), every room furnished by use; a person now sits or stands just within the door of the first rooms of each.
