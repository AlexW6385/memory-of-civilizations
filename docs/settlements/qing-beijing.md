## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Forbidden City (Zijin Cheng), Beijing, the imperial palace from 1420 to 1912; about 961 × 753 m, surrounded by a wall and a moat. [stated: Wikipedia; imaginoso spec]
- **Part shown:** the Outer Court on the central axis: the Meridian Gate (Wumen), the Inner Golden Water River, the Gate of Supreme Harmony (Taihemen, the largest freestanding gate of the palace), and the three great halls on a three-tiered white marble terrace. [stated: Wikipedia; MIT Dome image records]
- **Moment shown:** after the Hall of Supreme Harmony's rebuilding of 1695–97, which reduced it from about 95 × 48 m to its present 64 × 37 m [stated: Wikipedia — "after a reconstruction in the 16th century" is given there; the present hall dates from 1695–97, our inference from the hall's history], i.e. the Qing palace on the Ming plan.
- **Townscape:** the lanes (hutong) and courtyard houses (siheyuan) of the Inner City round the Imperial City. [stated: Wikipedia, Siheyuan; Beijing siheyuan]

## 3. Orientation

Everything faces south: the halls, the gates, the courtyard houses' main halls. [stated: general; Beijing siheyuan]

## 4. The palace's buildings and objects

- **Halls:** vermilion columns on marble bases; beams painted blue and green; red lattice doors and windows; yellow glazed tiles; double eaves on the greatest halls. The Hall of Supreme Harmony's roof is a double-eaved hip (wudian), the highest rank. [stated: general]
- **Terrace:** white marble, three tiers, balustrades with posts and rails all round, stairs in three flights at the front and back, the middle one a single carved slab (the imperial way). [stated: Britannica — "a marble terrace … above the marble balustrades"; the ramp: benjielayug]
- **Bronzes:** on the terrace before the hall stand bronze cauldrons, cranes, tortoises, a sundial and a grain measure [stated: Britannica]; we place tripods and vats; gilt bronze vats along the courts (water against fire) [stated: general]; bronze lions before the gates [stated: general].
- **Galleries:** covered walks along the east and west sides of the courtyard, the Pavilions of Embodying Benevolence and Spreading Righteousness between them [stated: general].

## 5. The courtyard houses (siheyuan)

- Grey brick walls; the gate at the south-east corner, vermilion; a screen wall inside it; the main house on the north side facing south; side houses east and west; the "opposite house" along the lane facing north. [stated: Wikipedia, Siheyuan]
- Simple roofs of grey tiles with plain ridges. [stated: Wikipedia, Beijing siheyuan — "relatively simple Qingshui roof ridges"]
- A tree in the courtyard, often a goldfish tank. [stated: Beijing Siheyuan (wiki mirror)] We plant a scholar tree (an elm stands in) or a jujube, and set a vat in some.

## 6. Ground and water

Level ground at 0; moat water 1.6 m down over a bed at 3.6 m; the Golden Water River 1.2 m down over 3 m; both with stone walls and marble balustrades. Paving of grey brick inside the palace, marble on the axis; lanes of brick and bluestone. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

No trees in the palace's courts [stated: the Outer Court was kept bare — general]; willows along the moat; a tree in each courtyard; grass and a few trees at the town's edge.

## 8. Sky and weather

A clear autumn: high blue sky, dry air, a north-west wind, now and then dust from the north. Latitude 40° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

The court's ceremonial music (bell and stone chimes, slow, on the gong mode) in the open; mouth organs and flute in the halls; a plucked lute for the lanes. [inferred; timbres from our sound library]
