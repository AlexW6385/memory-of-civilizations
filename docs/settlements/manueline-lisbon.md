## 1. The moment and the real example

- **Moment shown:** about 1520, the last years of Manuel I "the Fortunate" (r. 1495–1521). Vasco da Gama had sailed from Lisbon for India in 1497 (the Wikipedia page for Lisbon gives 1497 and 1498 in different places) and the spice trade made the city "Europe's commercial hub linking Africa, India, the Far East" [stated: Wikipedia "Lisbon"]. The city has about 70,000 people in 1500 [stated: Wikipedia "Lisbon"]. The Manueline style, "active roughly 1490 to 1520" under Manuel [stated: Wikipedia "Manueline"], is at its height: the Belém Tower is just finished (1514–1519) and the Jerónimos is rising (begun 6 January 1501).
- **Landmarks (one settlement, three set pieces):** the Jerónimos church and cloister; the Belém Tower; the Ribeira palace with its square and the Ribeira das Naus.
- **Compression** [inferred; DESIGN §4.2]: the true places lie along 7 km of the Tagus (Belém is about 6 km west of the Terreiro do Paço, Alfama just east of it). The disc holds them in their true order, west to east: the Tower and the Jerónimos in the west, the Terreiro do Paço and the shipyard in the middle, the Alfama hill with the Sé and the castle in the north-east, the river along the south. About 1:10 along the shore.

## 3. The Jerónimos church (the first landmark)

- **Facts** [stated: Wikipedia "Jerónimos Monastery"]: begun 6 January 1501; Diogo de Boitaca drew the plans and laid out the church; João de Castilho took over about 1517 and completed the vault and the cloister; Nicolau Chanterene made the west portal in 1517. Three aisles of equal height (a hall church), a single ribbed vault over a church 19 m wide; **six slender octagonal columns, each 25 m high**, carved by Castilho; the transept vault (1522) has no supports, so it appears to float. The main chapel and the choir are Torralva's of 1550 on, so in 1520 the east end is Boitaca's [inferred].
- **South portal** [stated: same]: Castilho's, 32 m high and 12 m wide over two storeys; a statue of Henry the Navigator between the two doors; a tympanum with two scenes from the life of Saint Jerome, with Manuel's arms and the cross of the Order of Christ. Built here as a deep portal of orders of carved colonnettes and niches with saints under canopies, the Navigator on the pier, the arms of Manuel over the doors, pinnacles and a high ogee gable [inferred for the form].
- **Stone** [stated: same]: calcário de lioz, a gold-coloured limestone from Ajuda, the Alcântara valley and Laveiras: the palette's `limestone_yellow`; carved parts `limestone_carved`.
- **Vocabulary** [stated: Wikipedia "Manueline"]: the armillary sphere (Manuel's personal emblem); anchors, ropes, cables, spheres; the cross of the Order of Christ; columns carved like twisted rope; semicircular and multi-lobed arches in place of pointed ones; eight-sided capitals; conical pinnacles and bevelled crenellations; shells, pearls and strings of seaweed; laurel, oak, acorns, poppy capsules, corncobs, thistles; deliberate asymmetry. Coral is also a Manueline motif (the cloister's and the Tomar window's branching forms) [inferred; not in the pages read].
- **Built:** a hall church of 4 bays between 6 octagonal columns (19.5 m to the capital, the vault to 25 m); a low vault under the choir gallery at the west end (the choir loft over it) [inferred: Boitaca's plan]; a raised main chapel with the altar and a retable; net vaults, the ribs in stars; tall round-headed windows in the aisle walls; the columns carved with rope spirals and armillary spheres on the capitals; the floor of limestone slabs; lamps and candles; tombs [inferred: Manuel's tomb is later; here the king's founding tomb on the chancel wall is an empty niche only].
- **Dimensions used:** outer 72 × 25 m; interior 20 m across (nave 8 m, aisles 6 m); bays 10 m.

## 4. The cloister

- 55 × 55 m, square; each wing has six bays with tracery vaults; Boitaca's round columns were replaced by rectangular piers by Castilho, who gave the lower storey a classical overlay and set the upper storey back [stated: Wikipedia "Jerónimos Monastery"]. Armillary spheres, coats of arms and the cross of the Order of Christ in the cloister and the portals [stated: same]. The two storeys are 7 m and 6 m [inferred]; the court a garden of orange trees round a basin [inferred: Portuguese monastic cloisters].
- **Built:** the lower arcade of six round arches a wing, on piers carved with rope and spheres; the upper gallery set back, of paired round arches with a rope-twisted mullion and an armillary medallion in each bay; the ribbed vaults; a stair at a corner; the court with orange trees, a cross and a basin; the walls closed on the outside (the ranges of the monastery, plain, with tile roofs).

## 5. The Belém Tower

- **Facts** [stated: Wikipedia "Belém Tower"]: begun about 1514, finished 1519, designed by Francisco de Arruda; stone arrived by 1516. About 12 m wide and 30 m tall, four storeys; a rectangular tower on the north side of an irregular hexagonal bastion that projects into the river; the bastion has embrasures for 17 guns; the first Portuguese fortification with a two-level gun emplacement; the terrace has bartizans with domed roofs, their bases carved with animals including a rhinoceros (perhaps the one Manuel sent the Pope in 1515); crosses of the Order of Christ on shields and on the parapet; twisted rope and armillary spheres; a loggia of seven arches on the south side of the tower with St Vincent and the Archangel Michael in niches; a Virgin and Child on the terrace. Ground floor a vaulted cistern; first floor the octagonal Governor's Hall; fourth floor a chapel with a ribbed vault. The Grande Nau, a ship of about 1,000 tons, guarded the river until the fort was ready. The tower marked the return of ships from Asia.
- **Built:** the hexagonal bastion in ashlar with its row of embrasures, a parapet with battlements carrying shields, the tower of four storeys with a window on each, the loggia, the corner bartizans, the cross shields, the rope moulding at every string course; a stone gangway from the shore [inferred].

## 6. The Ribeira palace, the Terreiro do Paço, the shipyard

- The palace: groundbreaking 1498, the court in by 1502, extended 1508–10 (Diogo de Arruda); the main loggia faces the Terreiro do Paço; the "Tower of the King" in the south wing housed the Casa da Índia, with a large royal coat of arms on its river face [stated: Wikipedia "Ribeira Palace"]. From a second-floor balcony the king could watch ships leave [stated: fapesp]. The loggia follows Manuel's other palaces (Évora) [stated: Wikipedia]. Form [inferred]: a long two-storey front in plastered masonry with stone window frames, an arcaded loggia on the first floor, the tower of three storeys at the corner with a loggia under a hipped roof.
- **The Ribeira das Naus:** shipbuilding "a leading sector, requiring timber, pitch, tallow, oakum, nail iron, rope, and sailcloth, with the best wood imported" [stated: fapesp]; warehouses, markets and bazaars beside the palace [stated: same]. Built: slipways with hulls on the stocks (rib frames and part-planked), timber stacks, a rope-walk shed, barrels of pitch, a crane [inferred].
- **The Rua Nova dos Mercadores** [stated: Art Newspaper]: goods sold: Ming porcelain, parrots, ivories, jewellery, lacquerware, Asian textiles, rock crystal; Asian fans (19,000 in one ship's inventory); Chinese silk (over 2,000 kg in a 1518 cargo). Built: arched shop fronts of tall merchants' houses, the goods on stalls and benches (jars, cloth, sacks, crates), awnings.

## 7. The ships

- The carrack (nau): three or four masts, foremast and mainmast with square sails, mizzen with a lateen, a small square sail on the bowsprit; forecastle and sterncastle; length to beam about 2:1 in the article, usually nearer 3:1 for Portuguese naus [stated: worldhistory.org "Carrack"; the 3:1 is [inferred]]. The cross of the Order of Christ on the sails [stated: Wikipedia "Manueline"]. The caravel: small, lateen-rigged, two or three masts [inferred, general]. Built: hull with a high stern castle and a forecastle, a main course with the red cross, topsails, a lateen mizzen, flags; plus caravels, and the local boats (a lateen fishing boat, the barge).

## 8. Houses, streets, people

- **Houses** [inferred; the pages read give no building detail]: tall narrow merchants' houses of three to four storeys with a shop at the street (rounded arch openings), whitewashed or tinted plaster (white, ochre, pink, yellow), stone door and window frames, timber lattice balconies (Moorish heritage), tile roofs of low pitch with wide eaves; poorer houses in the Alfama of one to three storeys, narrow, stepped up the hill; fishermen's huts on the beach at Belém. Streets: beaten earth and cobbles; stone quays at the Ribeira; a beach at Belém.
- **People** [stated: fapesp, Art Newspaper]: highly diverse; about 140,000–150,000 African slaves entered Portugal from the mid-15th to the early 16th century; Jews forced to convert (cristãos novos) after 1497, 1506 massacre; foreign merchants from Flanders, Italy, England, Germany; sailors, shipwrights, porters, fishwives, friars (Hieronymite monks in white habits with black cloaks [inferred]), nobles in doublets and capes, women in long dress with headcloth or mantle [inferred].
