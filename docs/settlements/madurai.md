# Madurai (the Meenakshi temple), about 1650 — dossier

Settlement id: `madurai` · Style: `dravidian` · Region: India (Tamil Nadu, the Vaigai plain) · Code: `src/settlements/madurai/`
Status of this dossier: L2 (the way in through the gopurams open, the tank and its cloister, the Ashta Shakti Mandapam, the temple car and elephants — §10; the outer wall and its four great gopurams, the Sundareswarar and Meenakshi enclosures with their gopurams and gilded vimanas, the Golden Lotus tank in its colonnade, the Thousand-Pillar Hall, pillared halls and flagstaffs; the city's concentric square streets with single-storey houses; ground, planting, weather, music). ## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Meenakshi Sundareswarar temple at the heart of Madurai. 14 gopurams, 45–50 m high, the southern one tallest at 51.9 m, and two gilded vimanas over the sanctums of the main deities [stated: Wikipedia; Britannica gives the south gopura as more than 52 m]. The present form was rebuilt by the Nayakas in the 16th–17th centuries, who added the great gopurams, the Thousand-Pillar Hall, the Golden Lotus tank and the outer enclosures [stated: anantamias]. The tank, Porthamarai Kulam, is 50 × 37 m, stepped, with a pillared corridor round it [stated: visiearth, scribd]. The Thousand-Pillar Hall has 985 carved pillars [stated: scribd/Wikipedia text]. The outer gopurams are covered in brightly painted stucco figures [stated: anantamias]. The compound is roughly 240 × 255 m with fortified walls [stated: praveenmusafir].
- **Moment shown:** about 1650, at the end of Tirumala Nayaka's rebuilding.

## 3. Orientation

The temple and both shrines face east; the city's square streets run with the cardinal directions. [stated/inferred]

## 4. The style

Granite bases with the gate passage, storeys of brick and painted stucco stepping in, crowned by a barrel-vaulted roof and a row of finials (kalasha); granite enclosure walls; flat-roofed pillared halls (mandapa); gilded vimanas; the stepped tank. Colours shown as bands of sky blue, rose, saffron, green and cream (the figures on each storey are L3). [stated: painted stucco; inferred: banding]

**The Thousand-Pillar Hall inside (L3, built).** Built in 1569 by Ariyanatha Mudaliar, general of Vishwanatha Nayaka; 985 pillars, each carved as its own composition (yalis, deities, portraits) [stated: anantamias]. Two rows of pillars carry yalis, the lion-bodied, elephant-headed beast of the Nayakas [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]; about 110 yali pillars [stated: come2india]. A Nataraja stands in the hall [stated: esamskriti; lotussculpture]; the ceiling is painted [stated: backpackersunited]; the musical pillars are just outside to the west [stated: scribd text]. Ours: about 290 pillars on a 2.6 m grid (fewer than the real 985: the hall is smaller), each in square blocks with relief panels between octagonal bands under a lotus-bud bracket capital (pushpapotika) [inferred: the Nayaka pillar type]; the two yali rows face across a widened central aisle; a lotus medallion painted in every bay; standing brass lamps down the aisle (the hall's 1008 lamp holders [stated: come2india], abridged); the bronze Nataraja in his ring of fire on a lotus pedestal at the north end [inferred: placement].

**The gopurams' storeys and crown (L2–L3, built).** A gopuram is a "truncated pyramid of receding storeys", "crowned not by a point but by a long barrel-vaulted roof, the wagon-topped shala", whose ridge is "studded with pot-and-flame finials (kalasams)"; each tier "crowded with painted stucco figures" [stated: Studio Matrx]; the outer towers have nine storeys, the south 170 ft 6 in high with 1,511 stucco figures [stated: panneer-madurai]; the tower is "usually topped with a barrel-vaulted roof crowned with finials (kalasam)" [stated: Wikipedia "Gopuram"].
Ours: on every storey a window on the axis of both long faces, a dark opening in a cream frame within a horseshoe surround (nāsī) [inferred: the gopuram type as photographed]; on every cornice a parapet (hāra) of miniature shrines — a domed kūṭa at each corner, apsidal pañjaras with barrel roofs along the sides, each with its pot finial, joined by a low wall [inferred: the Dravidian hāra]; the crowning vault with a great horseshoe gable (mahānāsī) at either end, a painted field and a dark opening in it and a lion face (kīrtimukha) at its crown, a horseshoe dormer on each long side, and a ridge carrying an odd number of gilded pots, about 1.35 m apart (nine on the great gopurams) [inferred: count not found in the sources]; the pots in their full form — foot ring, belly, neck, lid and flame tip. The ceiling medallions of the Thousand-Pillar Hall now stand clear of one another (they shared a plane before).

## 5. The town

Single-storey houses of plastered brick with a raised pillared verandah (thinnai) on the street and tiled roofs, in blocks between the ring streets. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat red-earth plain; the temple courts paved; the streets beaten earth; coconut palms (date palms stand in until a coconut is added), neem and banyan in the yards. The only water inside is the tank. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Hot and bright, hazy; monsoon clouds sometimes; a passing shower. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the south up the axial street through the south gopuram into the outer court, past the tank. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

The nadaswaram (a double reed) and the tavil drum are the temple's music; a Carnatic raga, Mayamalavagowla, over a drone. [inferred; reed, drone and plucked string stand in]

## 10. Detail added (2026-11 pass: the way in, the tank, the goddesses' hall, the festival)

- **The gopurams' passages.** The moulded granite plinth of every gopuram ran straight across its passage at 1.2 m, so that no one could walk into the temple at all (the tank and the Thousand-Pillar Hall were unreachable). The plinth now stops at the jambs and the passage is paved at the court's level [inferred: the gateway's threshold as built].
- **The Golden Lotus tank (Porthamarai Kulam).** 165 × 120 ft (50 × 37 m) [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]; Nayaka-period painting of the 17th–18th c. on the portico by the tank, "a vivid red background" with black line and white, green and ochre [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]; devotees bathe in it on new-moon and other days [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]. Ours: the tank was under the flat ground before (its steps and water invisible); the ground now drops away inside it, and the water cannot be waded. Sixteen steps of 25 cm with 35 cm treads on all four sides down to the floor at 4 m, the water 2.4 m down; the cloister round it rebuilt — a granite plinth, a row of carved Nayaka pillars on the tank side, the outer wall with an opening in the middle of each side and a painted panel in every bay inside (a red ground in a cream frame, an ochre shrine-frame, a couple seated under a tree — the god blue-faced), beams to the wall, painted ceiling panels with lotus medallions, the flat roof with cream parapets on both edges [inferred: the cloister's form, the murals' composition after the stated palette]. The gilt "lamp pillar" in the water is gone (no source for it in 1650).
- **The Ashta Shakti Mandapam.** Built by Rudrapathi Ammal and Tholimamai, consorts of Tirumala Nayaka, to feed pilgrims; its entrance "twenty-five feet high and eighteen feet broad"; statues of the eight Shaktis, four on the left and four on the right, Ganapathi and Muruga, and coloured sculptures of the Tiruvilaiyadal (the god's sacred sports) [stated: templesyatra]. Ours: a hall 24 × 12 m inside the outer east wall south of the east gopuram, entered through its own opening in the wall: the tall entrance (7.6 × 5.5 m) under a cream lintel with a painted panel over it; a nave 7 m wide between two rows of five carved pillars, a goddess standing against each of the four inner pillars of each row facing across the nave (her garment painted, four arms, a gilt crown); Ganesha and Muruga in niches either side of the west doorway; a barrel vault over the nave painted in coloured bands between ribs at each bay [inferred: the vault after photographs]; the aisles' walls carrying painted panels for the sacred sports [inferred: their form].
- **The festival and the elephants.** Tirumala Nayaka instituted the Chithirai festival, moving the god's wedding to Chithirai, and "built two huge Thers, one for Swami and another for Amman", drawn round the four Masi streets by great crowds; on the eighth day the king rode the state elephant in procession [stated: Swarajya]; elephants are tied along the Adi Veedhi, the street inside the outer wall [stated: templesyatra]. Ours (`town.js`, the festival plan): one car on the South Masi street being drawn west — four solid wheels with iron tyres, a carved wooden body in four tiers stepping out with friezes of little figures, the god's platform with gilt posts, the festival images on their seat under a cloth canopy with a striped valance, the cloth tower of nine coloured bands drawing in to a red dome, the gilt pot and a white parasol, two wooden horses springing from the front with the charioteer, the ropes running 34 m ahead with the crowd hauling on them [inferred: dimensions and form after the cars as they are drawn today]; two temple elephants inside the south gate by the wall, chained at a foot, each with the sacred marks painted on the forehead, a brass bell on a rope, a red cloth with gilt hems over the back, rings at the ankles, their keepers by them; worshippers bathing on the tank's steps and in the goddesses' hall [inferred].

## 11. Detail added (2026-11 L3 pass: the Pudhu Mandapam, the things of worship, the temple streets)

- **The Pudhu Mandapam.** "Built by King Thirumalai Nayakkar between the years of 1628 to 1635" on the temple's eastern side; "with a height of 25 feet, length of 330 feet and breadth of 105 feet, this mandapam is built on a pedestal"; "there are 124 pillars"; "the pillars on the sides of central passage have been decorated with carvings of 14 rulers and their consorts of Nayaka dynasty"; today a market of tailors, cloth and bangle sellers, and still a venue for the temple's festivals [stated: maduraitourism]. Ours (`pudhu_mandapa`, a plan of its own in `town.js`): 100.6 × 32 m on a moulded granite pedestal 1.2 m high across the street from the east gopuram, the axial street running through it; four rows of 31 carved Nayaka pillars (the passage 9 m wide on the axis, an aisle either side), the beams, the flat roof with its cream parapet, a flight up the pedestal at either end; against seven pillars on each side of the passage a Nayaka king carved in the round with joined hands, the tall cap and the dagger at his waist, his queen beside him [stated: dimensions, pillar count, the fourteen rulers; inferred: the rows, the passage width, the figures' forms]. In 1650, fifteen years after it was finished, it is shown without the later shops; the unfinished Raya Gopuram east of it is left for later.
- **The gopurams' gods.** Every third stucco figure on the tiers now has four arms, the upper pair raised with gilded emblems, and every figure stands on a small cream pedestal on the ledge [inferred: the many-armed deities of the gopuram stucco].
- **The things of worship.** Pūjā offers flowers and garlands, incense, the waved lamp and food [stated: encyclopedia.com, "Pūjā"]. Ours (element `rite`): before the passage of each shrine's gopuram (Sundareswarar's and Meenakshi's) two bronze lamp stands of the Tamil kind (kuttuvilakku) with five lit wicks, a bronze bell hung in the passage, an offering of a lamp, flowers on a leaf and a water pot; a jasmine garland round each gilded flagstaff and a row of lamps before it; a row of clay lamps along the top step on the north and south sides of the Golden Lotus tank; lamp stands and an offering before the Nataraja in the Thousand-Pillar Hall [inferred: the placing]. No lotus or other growth in the tank: "neither fish or other lives are found in this miraculous sacred pond" [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism].
- **The temple streets.** Jasmine has "a unique link with Madurai, since 300 BC or even earlier", and the goddess is adorned with it [stated: krishijagran]. Ours: five women selling jasmine (and red oleander) from mats along the Chitrai street either side of the south gopuram, buyers and passers-by; at each shrine's gopuram a priest — bare-chested in a white veshti, the hair in a tuft — and devotees with joined hands, one kneeling; a priest before the Nataraja; pilgrims walking through the Pudhu Mandapam's passage, a man sitting by a pillar, a cow lying there; zebu cows lying and standing in the streets [inferred]. Marigolds, from the Americas via Spanish and Portuguese traders [stated: National Geographic], are not shown.
- **Plants and weathering.** Oleander (also offered at the shrines; standing in for jasmine bushes, not in the species list) with neem and palms in the yards of the first ring of blocks [inferred]; the climate set damp (0.55) for the materials' streaks and moss [inferred].

## 12. The city's houses and shops (2026-11 density pass)

- **Streets and fronts.** The city is laid out round the temple in concentric streets (Chithirai, Aadi, Masi, Avani Moola), each "marking a calibrated ritual distance from the sacred core", their widths "directly informed by the movement of temple chariots, deities, and accompanying crowds"; artisans, traders, flower sellers, metalworkers and textile merchants sat along the processional routes; "continuous street frontages and thinnai and adaptable ground-floor spaces" supported ritual visibility and commerce [stated: Negotiated Sacred Urbanism, aimsr.edu.in]. Ours (`layout.js`): rows of lots with continuous fronts on both sides of each of the three ring streets and of the four axial streets (about 1,900 lots), back to back between the streets with a 1.6 m alley, a back lane (4 m) cutting the wide outer block; beyond the last row a scatter of huts and yards. The name-to-ring assignment (Chithirai the ring against the wall, Masi the outer) follows the festival car's route [inferred].
- **The thinnai.** "A raised masonry platform or porch flanking the front door" [stated: Studio Matrx, Vernacular architecture of Tamil Nadu]; a raised platform near the entrance that "creates connectivity between the public and private zones", its form varying by region, occupation and religion [stated: IJTK 3343, Mangalrevu]. Ours (`tamil_house`): a granite platform 0.55 m high across the whole front, a central step, wooden octagonal posts on granite blocks with bracket capitals under a lean-to of tiles with a timber fascia, a front wall with a carved door (cream frame, dark leaves with brass studs, a painted panel of the goddess over it) and shuttered windows, a red-ochre dado band and a cream cornice, the main roof a gable of terracotta tiles with a ridge cap [inferred: the type as photographed; the dado after the red-and-white banding of Tamil walls [inferred]]. Rice-flour kolam dots on the street before a part of the doors [inferred].
- **Agraharam rows.** "Two parallel rows of row-houses... a temple closing one end of the street"; each house "narrow-fronted, deep, sharing party walls with its neighbours" [stated: Studio Matrx]. Ours: the two inner rows of the outer block are narrow lots (5–5.8 m) with a higher platform [inferred].
- **Shops.** Open fronts on a platform 0.62 m high, a palm-mat or tiled awning on posts over the street edge, shelves with vessels on the back wall, shutter planks above, the goods of the trade before the seller: flowers (heaps of white jasmine, red oleander and pink lotus in baskets, garlands hung under the awning; no marigold, as §11), brass (vessels in stacks, standing lamps), cloth (bolts on a white sheet, saris hung from the bar), grain and spices (baskets of pulses, chillies, turmeric, a balance), pooja goods (coconuts, bananas, small brass lamps), sweets (trays and a cauldron on a hearth), coconuts [inferred: trades from the textile, flower and metal trades of the temple streets, aimsr.edu.in]. A seller sits on every shop's platform; one house in six has someone on its thinnai [inferred].
- **A merchant's courtyard house (enterable, four of them by the south gate's street).** The courtyard (mitham/mutram) of a house with pillared halls round it, the first court the most formal and the rear the most private; carved teak portals; lime-washed walls; roofs sloping inward to the court [stated: Studio Matrx, for Chettinad and the agraharam; the Madurai merchant's version inferred]. Ours (`court_house`, 13–16 m wide): the thinnai and door; a vestibule with a hanging lamp; the counting room on the left (reed mat, low desk with ledgers, chests, palm-leaf bundles, a standing lamp, the merchant seated cross-legged); the open court in a pillared verandah with the tulsi pedestal, water jars, a grindstone, a swing (oonjal) on chains and a kolam; at the back the puja room with a granite shelf, three framed gods, two lit standing lamps and garlands. Lamps give point light.
- **Hot humid weathering.** Handled by the climate (`damp` 0.55) and the engine's wall streaks and moss [inferred].

## 13. The living streets

- **People.** Men in the white veshti bare-chested with a sash, merchants in turbans with a shoulder cloth, priests with the tuft of hair, women in cotton saris with the end over the shoulder, devotees with baskets of flowers or coconuts and brass pots, porters; 40 by day, 5 by night, more in the market streets (`movers.js`) [inferred: dress as in the festival plans of §10–11].
- **Animals.** Zebu and buffalo in the streets, a few dogs, bullock carts (`town.js`); rock pigeons and swifts round the gopurams, house crows over the roofs, sparrows on the paving, none over the tank's water but swifts (`fauna.js`) [inferred].
- **Smoke.** Kitchen smoke through the tiles of a third of the houses at the three meals, the sweet-makers' steam by day, incense at the two shrines' gopurams (`smoke.js`) [inferred].
