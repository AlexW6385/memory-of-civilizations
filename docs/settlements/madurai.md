## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Meenakshi Sundareswarar temple at the heart of Madurai. 14 gopurams, 45–50 m high, the southern one tallest at 51.9 m, and two gilded vimanas over the sanctums of the main deities [stated: Wikipedia; Britannica gives the south gopura as more than 52 m]. The present form was rebuilt by the Nayakas in the 16th–17th centuries, who added the great gopurams, the Thousand-Pillar Hall, the Golden Lotus tank and the outer enclosures [stated: anantamias]. The tank, Porthamarai Kulam, is 50 × 37 m, stepped, with a pillared corridor round it [stated: visiearth, scribd]. The Thousand-Pillar Hall has 985 carved pillars [stated: scribd/Wikipedia text]. The outer gopurams are covered in brightly painted stucco figures [stated: anantamias]. The compound is roughly 240 × 255 m with fortified walls [stated: praveenmusafir].
- **Moment shown:** about 1650, at the end of Tirumala Nayaka's rebuilding.

## 3. Orientation

The temple and both shrines face east; the city's square streets run with the cardinal directions. [stated/inferred]

## 4. The style

Granite bases with the gate passage, storeys of brick and painted stucco stepping in, crowned by a barrel-vaulted roof and a row of finials (kalasha); granite enclosure walls; flat-roofed pillared halls (mandapa); gilded vimanas; the stepped tank. Colours shown as bands of sky blue, rose, saffron, green and cream (the figures on each storey are L3). [stated: painted stucco; inferred: banding]

**The Thousand-Pillar Hall inside (L3, built).** Built in 1569 by Ariyanatha Mudaliar, general of Vishwanatha Nayaka; 985 pillars, each carved as its own composition (yalis, deities, portraits) [stated: anantamias]. Two rows of pillars carry yalis, the lion-bodied, elephant-headed beast of the Nayakas [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]; about 110 yali pillars [stated: come2india]. A Nataraja stands in the hall [stated: esamskriti; lotussculpture]; the ceiling is painted [stated: backpackersunited]; the musical pillars are just outside to the west [stated: scribd text]. Ours: about 290 pillars on a 2.6 m grid (fewer than the real 985: the hall is smaller), each in square blocks with relief panels between octagonal bands under a lotus-bud bracket capital (pushpapotika) [inferred: the Nayaka pillar type]; the two yali rows face across a widened central aisle; a lotus medallion painted in every bay; standing brass lamps down the aisle (the hall's 1008 lamp holders [stated: come2india], abridged); the bronze Nataraja in his ring of fire on a lotus pedestal at the north end [inferred: placement].

**The gopurams' storeys and crown (L2–L3, built).** A gopuram is a "truncated pyramid of receding storeys", "crowned not by a point but by a long barrel-vaulted roof, the wagon-topped shala", whose ridge is "studded with pot-and-flame finials (kalasams)"; each tier "crowded with painted stucco figures" [stated: Studio Matrx]; the outer towers have nine storeys, the south 170 ft 6 in high with 1,511 stucco figures [stated: panneer-madurai]; the tower is "usually topped with a barrel-vaulted roof crowned with finials (kalasam)" [stated: Wikipedia "Gopuram"].
Ours: on every storey a window on the axis of both long faces, a dark opening in a cream frame within a horseshoe surround (nāsī) [inferred: the gopuram type as photographed]; on every cornice a parapet (hāra) of miniature shrines — a domed kūṭa at each corner, apsidal pañjaras with barrel roofs along the sides, each with its pot finial, joined by a low wall [inferred: the Dravidian hāra]; the crowning vault with a great horseshoe gable (mahānāsī) at either end, a painted field and a dark opening in it and a lion face (kīrtimukha) at its crown, a horseshoe dormer on each long side, and a ridge carrying an odd number of gilded pots, about 1.35 m apart (nine on the great gopurams) [inferred: count not found in the sources]; the pots in their full form — foot ring, belly, neck, lid and flame tip. The ceiling medallions of the Thousand-Pillar Hall now stand clear of one another (they shared a plane before).

## 5. The town

Single-storey houses of plastered brick with a raised pillared verandah (thinnai) on the street and tiled roofs, in blocks between the ring streets. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat red-earth plain; the temple courts paved; the streets beaten earth; coconut palms (date palms stand in until a coconut is added), neem and banyan in the yards. The only water inside is the tank. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

Hot and bright, hazy; monsoon clouds sometimes; a passing shower. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the south up the axial street through the south gopuram into the outer court, past the tank. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

The nadaswaram (a double reed) and the tavil drum are the temple's music; a Carnatic raga, Mayamalavagowla, over a drone. [inferred; reed, drone and plucked string stand in]

## 10. Detail added (2026-11 pass: the way in, the tank, the goddesses' hall, the festival)

- **The gopurams' passages.** The moulded granite plinth of every gopuram ran straight across its passage at 1.2 m, so that no one could walk into the temple at all (the tank and the Thousand-Pillar Hall were unreachable). The plinth now stops at the jambs and the passage is paved at the court's level [inferred: the gateway's threshold as built].
- **The Golden Lotus tank (Porthamarai Kulam).** 165 × 120 ft (50 × 37 m) [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]; Nayaka-period painting of the 17th–18th c. on the portico by the tank, "a vivid red background" with black line and white, green and ochre [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]; devotees bathe in it on new-moon and other days [stated: tamilnadu-favtourism]. Ours: the tank was under the flat ground before (its steps and water invisible); the ground now drops away inside it, and the water cannot be waded. Sixteen steps of 25 cm with 35 cm treads on all four sides down to the floor at 4 m, the water 2.4 m down; the cloister round it rebuilt — a granite plinth, a row of carved Nayaka pillars on the tank side, the outer wall with an opening in the middle of each side and a painted panel in every bay inside (a red ground in a cream frame, an ochre shrine-frame, a couple seated under a tree — the god blue-faced), beams to the wall, painted ceiling panels with lotus medallions, the flat roof with cream parapets on both edges [inferred: the cloister's form, the murals' composition after the stated palette]. The gilt "lamp pillar" in the water is gone (no source for it in 1650).
- **The Ashta Shakti Mandapam.** Built by Rudrapathi Ammal and Tholimamai, consorts of Tirumala Nayaka, to feed pilgrims; its entrance "twenty-five feet high and eighteen feet broad"; statues of the eight Shaktis, four on the left and four on the right, Ganapathi and Muruga, and coloured sculptures of the Tiruvilaiyadal (the god's sacred sports) [stated: templesyatra]. Ours: a hall 24 × 12 m inside the outer east wall south of the east gopuram, entered through its own opening in the wall: the tall entrance (7.6 × 5.5 m) under a cream lintel with a painted panel over it; a nave 7 m wide between two rows of five carved pillars, a goddess standing against each of the four inner pillars of each row facing across the nave (her garment painted, four arms, a gilt crown); Ganesha and Muruga in niches either side of the west doorway; a barrel vault over the nave painted in coloured bands between ribs at each bay [inferred: the vault after photographs]; the aisles' walls carrying painted panels for the sacred sports [inferred: their form].
- **The festival and the elephants.** Tirumala Nayaka instituted the Chithirai festival, moving the god's wedding to Chithirai, and "built two huge Thers, one for Swami and another for Amman", drawn round the four Masi streets by great crowds; on the eighth day the king rode the state elephant in procession [stated: Swarajya]; elephants are tied along the Adi Veedhi, the street inside the outer wall [stated: templesyatra]. Ours (`town.js`, the festival plan): one car on the South Masi street being drawn west — four solid wheels with iron tyres, a carved wooden body in four tiers stepping out with friezes of little figures, the god's platform with gilt posts, the festival images on their seat under a cloth canopy with a striped valance, the cloth tower of nine coloured bands drawing in to a red dome, the gilt pot and a white parasol, two wooden horses springing from the front with the charioteer, the ropes running 34 m ahead with the crowd hauling on them [inferred: dimensions and form after the cars as they are drawn today]; two temple elephants inside the south gate by the wall, chained at a foot, each with the sacred marks painted on the forehead, a brass bell on a rope, a red cloth with gilt hems over the back, rings at the ankles, their keepers by them; worshippers bathing on the tank's steps and in the goddesses' hall [inferred].
