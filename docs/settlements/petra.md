## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Khazneh (the Treasury), cut in the rock at the end of the Siq, about 39–40 m high and 25 m wide [stated: Bananas for Scale (39.1 × 25.3 m); wondermondo]. It is probably the mausoleum of Aretas IV, 1st century AD [stated: pelago].
- **The Siq:** about 1.2 km long, 3–12 m wide, with walls up to 80 m [stated: Live Science]. Wondermondo gives about 1.5 km and 70 m deep.
- **The Street of Façades:** tombs in the southern cliff past the Treasury [stated: Live Science].
- **The theatre:** 1st century AD, 7,000–8,500 seats [stated: ermakvagus], 45 rows [stated: chefindisguise].
- **The Royal Tombs:** a group of four in the cliff above the colonnaded street [stated: illustrarch].
- **The colonnaded street:** leads to Qasr al-Bint [stated: ermakvagus]. Qasr al-Bint is 23 m high [stated: Live Science].
- **Moment shown:** about AD 50, under the Nabataean kings. The colonnaded street is shown although it may be later. [inferred]

## 3. Orientation

The Siq runs roughly east–west; the colonnaded street runs east–west, ending at Qasr al-Bint in the west. [stated: plans]

## 4. The style

Façades cut into rose sandstone: the Khazneh's Hellenistic two storeys (six columns and a pediment below; a tholos between broken half-pediments above, crowned by an urn); tombs with pilasters, a cornice and crow-step merlons; a palace tomb in stacked storeys; ashlar temples on podiums with a portico in antis. [stated: Hellenistic columns with eastern motifs — bestopics; inferred: details]

## 5. The town

Flat-roofed houses of rose ashlar on the valley's slopes. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

Rock everywhere; sandy floors in the gorges; oleander and tamarisk in the valley, scrub on the heights. Water came through channels and cisterns (L3). [inferred; water system stated — wondermondo]

## 7. Sky and climate

Dry and clear, sometimes dusty. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the east down the wadi into the Siq, out into the square facing the Khazneh, on along the Outer Siq past the façades and the theatre, into the valley and along the colonnaded street. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Caravan music of Arabia: lyres, pipes and frame drums, little known. A reed pipe, a lyre and a drone stand in. [inferred]

## 10. Detail (2026-11-06)

**The Khazneh.** The façade is 24.9 m wide and 38.77 m high [stated: Smarthistory]. Corinthian columns throughout; on the lower level the Dioscuri, Castor and Pollux; on the upper level a broken pediment and a central tholos, Amazons and Victories flanking a central female figure, probably Isis-Tyche; eagles, vines, kantharoi, acroteria and rosettes; above the broken pediments the bases of two obelisks run up into the rock [stated: Smarthistory]. Two large eagles atop the broken pediments; Isis on the tholos below the urn; two winged Victories in the recesses behind; four figures holding axes aloft; two lions on the entablature between the storeys; the mounted riders Castor and Pollux at ground level; the entrance portico flanked by rooms with round windows above their doors; inside, a blank square chamber with smaller rooms off it [stated: Rough Guides]. Ours: three steps; six columns 12.45 m high on Attic bases with floral capitals (bell, two rows of eight leaves, corner volutes, abacus), the middle four before a portico 5.5 m deep, the outer two against side bays where each Dioscurus stands before his horse in high relief; the architrave in three fasciae, a frieze of griffins and kantharoi, the cornice on dentils; the pediment over the middle with a kantharos and vine scrolls in the tympanum, its raking cornices and acroteria; the lions on the cornice over the bays; above, an attic, the two pavilions of two columns each with an Amazon raising her axe between them, a Victory in each recess, the broken half-pediments rising toward the middle with an eagle on each and the obelisk bases above; the tholos of six engaged columns round its drum, Isis-Tyche with a cornucopia before it, the conical roof, a floral capital and the urn to 38.77 m; in the portico the great door under its pediment and a side door with a round window over it in each side wall [inferred: the heights of the parts, the poses and sizes of the figures, the griffin frieze; the riders shown standing by their horses].

**The Siq.** Channels cut in the walls carried water into Petra, and some 2,000-year-old terracotta pipes are still in place; niches in the walls held baetyls of Dushara; on the left wall a weathered carving of a camel and caravan man with the water channel running behind it; a Nabataean dam of about AD 50 kept Wadi Musa's floods out of the Siq [stated: Lonely Planet "Siq"]. It is not water-cut but a block rent apart by tectonic forces [stated: Lonely Planet]. Ours: the walls are built (as Lalibela's pits are) over the edge of the ground, sheer and uneven, from the east down to the square, the floor running on under them so that the ground's slope never shows; along the foot of the south (left) wall a kerb with the open channel and its water; along the north wall a ledge with the terracotta pipe and its collars [inferred: which side has which]; four niches with framed panels, a crow-stepped lintel and a plain baetyl; the camel and its man on a dressed panel over the channel. The Roman paving is later and left out.

## 11. Detail (2026-11-06, second pass): the rock, the tombs, the theatre

**The rock of the gorges.** The Siq is "a narrow winding cleft in the rock made by water cutting of the fracture system"; its top is the white Disi sandstone, below it the thick, cross-bedded, very hard Umm Ishrin sandstone, the formation of all Petra's famous rose-coloured rock; the Khazneh and the Street of Façades are cut in the same thick red cross-bedded facies; the sandstones are "spectacularly color-banded in yellow, orange, red, gray, brown and mauve", partly by Liesegang banding (iron and manganese compounds deposited from water in the rock); the Umm Ishrin has vertical fractures and joints [stated: GeoExpro]. The Umm Ishrin is reddish-brown with shades of red, orange, yellow and white, the red and yellow from iron oxides (limonite), the white from quartz [stated: geocaching GCAN2KH]. The Siq is not a water-cut gorge but was rent by tectonic forces; in places so narrow that one can touch both walls with arms outstretched [stated: Amusing Planet]. Ours: the walls of the Siq, of the square before the Khazneh, of the Outer Siq, behind the theatre and of the valley's east cliff are all built by one builder (`src/styles/nabataean/siq.js`) as displaced rock faces, every 0.5 m along and bed by bed up: about seventy beds from below the floor to above the rim, rose, red, mauve, ochre and a little yellow and grey, white and yellow above 44 m for the Disi; each bed's boundary runs at the same height on both walls (the two halves of one block), dipping 1.2 % to the west and swelling and pinching along its length; the thin beds weather back into grooves (0.2–0.75 m) and the thick stand out, so that every bed's foot is a ledge or a small overhang; vertical joints every 5–18 m, 0.5–1.6 m deep, wandering a little as they rise; a coarse and a fine roughness over all; the faces lean in by 0.8–3.4 m toward the top so that the Siq closes overhead; the lowest two metres, scoured by the floods, are smooth [inferred: the bed thicknesses and colours' proportions, the lean, the joint spacing; the Siq is kept 12 m wide, wider than the real 3–12 m, for walking]. Fallen blocks of the bed colours lie at the foot of the Siq's walls in front of the channel's kerb and the pipe's ledge, each with its rubble, none at the mouth (the view of the Khazneh) nor by the niches and the panel [inferred]. The ground runs on, flat, under every built face, so that the terrain's coarse slope never shows; the Khazneh's portico no longer fills with it.

**The square.** No market: the forecourt is a place where a few people stand; nothing is set out in the Siq [inferred]. The cliff round the square is built rock; the Khazneh stands in a recess cut back into it, its own dressed rock only 1.5 m beyond the façade on either side [inferred, after photographs].

**The Street of Façades.** The tombs are "neatly arranged in ascending street-like rows along the cliff face", fairly homogeneous, with crow-stepped attics and simple rectangular entrances, the style called Assyrian, 50 BC – AD 50 [stated: PDTRA]. Over 80 % of Petra's tombs carry some form of the crow-step; the double-pylon tombs of the street have two rows of it [stated: Encircle Photos]. A Hegra tomb of the type has two half-crow-steps above an Egyptian entablature (a cornice of gorge and torus over an architrave), its door flanked by pilasters with pseudo-Doric capitals [stated: Hegra Conservation, IGN 40]. Ours (`src/styles/nabataean/tombs.js`): eight façades of three kinds side by side at the foot of the Outer Siq's west wall, each square to the wall: *pylon* (one row of three-stepped merlons), *step* (a lower row of two-stepped merlons before a plain attic, its fillet, an upper row; plain corner pilasters) and *hegr* (corner pilasters with horned Nabataean capitals, architrave in two fasciae and frieze, an attic with its pilasters, two great half-merlons stepping down to the middle); each on a plinth, the door in a recess with jambs and lintel standing out, the Egyptian cornice (fillet, torus, gorge, crowning fillet) across the top of the front; the cliff rises from the top of each tomb's dressed rock, set back 1.6 m behind its crow-steps [inferred: the sizes, the sequence of kinds, the proportions]. Only five fit before the theatre.

**The theatre.** First century AD under Aretas IV; a substantial part carved out of the solid rock, the scaena and outer wall built; three horizontal sections of seats separated by passageways, seven stairways; about 8,500 seats; floral capitals [stated: Wikipedia "Petra Theater"]. Ours: thirty rows (kept from the first pass) in three sections with two passages 1.8 m wide, each row solid down to the rock, seven stairways with an intermediate step on each row, a parapet at the back with the cut cliff rising from it, stepped walls closing the ends, the orchestra paved; the stage platform 1.4 m high and the scaenae frons of ashlar, 14.5 m high, in two storeys of eight columns on pedestals with floral capitals, three doors (the middle one wider) and framed niches between the columns [inferred: the frons's design, its height, the niches]. The cavea's rock is cut away behind it (the ground used to bury most of it).

**The Royal Tombs.** In the west face of Jabal al-Khubtha, overlooking the centre: the Palace, Urn, Silk and Corinthian Tombs; the Urn Tomb's façade about 26 × 16.5 m with two engaged half columns between corner pilasters, Nabataean capitals, four busts in the frieze and a lidded urn on the pediment, on vaults carrying a terrace; the Silk Tomb named for the swirls of pink, white and yellow in its rock; the Corinthian Tomb 27.55 m wide and 28 m high in three orders, eight half columns in the lowest, modelled on the Khazneh [stated: Madain Project]. The Palace Tomb is 49 m wide and 46 m high in three storeys; the first has four doors, the middle two almost twice as wide as the outer, under triangular pediments, the outer under semicircular ones; eighteen engaged columns in the second; the top left corner of the third built of ashlar, not carved; end of the first century AD [stated: Wikipedia "Palace Tomb"]. Ours: from the north, the Palace Tomb where the cliff runs straightest, the Corinthian Tomb, the Silk Tomb (a *hegr* façade) and the Urn Tomb, side by side, each square to the curving cliff and standing on the valley floor where it meets it (they stood 10 m below it before); the Urn Tomb with its pilasters, engaged columns, framed door under a pediment, the frieze with four busts, an attic of short pilasters, the pediment and its urn; the Corinthian Tomb with eight engaged columns with floral capitals, three doors, a pediment over the middle, and above a tholos of five columns with its conical roof and urn between two pavilions; the Palace Tomb with thirteen pilasters and the four doors below, eighteen engaged columns in the second storey, pilasters in the third and its masonry corner in coursed ashlar. The cliff is built round and above them [inferred: the order along the cliff, the Urn Tomb's terrace left out (L3), proportions]. They used to stand inside the theatre's cavea. The theatre is moved 14 m to the east-south-east, its stage now against the Outer Siq's east side instead of across the gorge (it used to block the way into the city: the plan check failed), and the Royal Tombs begin south of it; the valley floor is kept level round the theatre.

## 12. Detail (2026-11-06, third pass): the colonnaded street, the Great Temple, Qasr al-Bint

**The colonnaded street.** It runs from the Nymphaeum to the Temenos Gate; a double row of columns lined it; commercial shops stood along its south side; the Romans paved it and narrowed it in the 2nd century, replacing an earlier Nabataean street along the Wadi Musa [stated: Visit Petra "Colonnaded Street"]; it was rebuilt 6 m wide, lined with colonnaded porticoes, with stairs on the south side up to a courtyard once called a market [stated: Madain Project "Decumanus"]. The Nymphaeum (about AD 106) and the triple-arched Temenos Gate (about AD 130) are later than the moment shown and are left out [stated dates: Madain Project]. Ours: the paving 6 m wide from the Outer Siq's mouth to the temenos; on both sides three steps of 15 cm up to a portico 5 m deep, columns of 6 m with floral capitals 3.6 m apart under an architrave and cornice, a timber ceiling and a flat roof; behind the south portico a row of shops 4.6 m wide, each with its doorway, lintel and threshold, a stone counter inside and partitions between; behind the north portico a wall toward the wadi; no shops where the Great Temple's propylaeum opens [inferred: the porticoes' depth and steps, the shops' size and counters, the street kept at its Nabataean place and shown in its later form].

**The Great Temple.** South of the colonnaded street, on an artificial terrace: a propylaeum; the lower temenos with triple colonnades east and west (96–120 columns) leading to semicircular buttressed exedrae, paved with large white hexagonal pavers over drains; a capital with Asian elephant heads found near the West Exedra; monumental east and west stairways up to the upper temenos; the temple tetrastyle in antis, the middle columns about 7 m apart and the outer about 5 m beyond, the porch columns about 15 m high (at least 19 m to the pediment), two pronaos columns of 1.5 m, eight cella-wall and six rear columns of 1.2 m, the podium on a forecourt of hexagonal pavers; a theatron inside; columns and walls stuccoed red and white; built in the last quarter of the 1st century BC, enlarged in the 1st AD [stated: Brown University, Petra Great Temple excavations]. The east exedra: 12.4 m wide outside, 6.7 m inside, five niches about 2 m wide between four buttresses, two columns at its mouth, a bench inside, 5.8 m preserved [stated: Brown University, Lower Temenos]. Ours (`great_temple`, 56 × 79 m): the propylaeum's stair up from the southern portico to the lower temenos 1.5 m up, four columns at its head under an entablature between antae; the lower temenos paved in white hexagons (1.1 m across); three rows of columns down each side, roofed, the inner row's capitals with four elephant heads (head, ears, curled trunk); an exedra at the south end of each, half-round with its niches, buttresses, bench, two columns and cornice; the retaining wall with two stairs up to the upper temenos 4.7 m up, paved the same; the temple 35 × 35 m on a podium 1.2 m high, its stair; four columns of 1.5 m and 15 m in the front (7 m and 5 m apart), two more in the pronaos, the antae; the cella's front wall with a door 7 × 10 m; inside four columns along each wall and six across the back carrying a ceiling, the theatron of nine rows in a half-round facing the door, its orchestra paved; the entablature, a stuccoed frieze, the pediment and a tiled gable roof; every column stuccoed white with its foot red [inferred: the precinct's size, the levels, the cella's length shortened from 42.5 m, the theatron's size and place, the roof, how the red and white were shared].

**Qasr al-Bint.** About 27.90 × 27.62 m and 23 m high, on a podium; the north front with four Corinthian columns between pilasters (stuccoed); a flight of 26 marble steps; at the back three compartments, the middle with the platform for the god's images, the others with balcony terraces [stated: Visit Petra]. A monumental marble stair of 27 steps divided by a landing; the vestibule framed by four columns with Corinthian capitals; square in plan: pronaos, naos and the tripartite adyton; the cella's baetyl on a base faced with gold; the side chambers' upper rooms reached by stairs hidden in the thick walls; wooden string courses of Lebanese cedar along the walls; the walls plastered inside and out [stated: Madain Project]. The temple stands in a large paved temenos whose peribolos had seats added under Aretas IV; an altar for sacrifices faces the temple on the north; stucco with imitation ashlar, relief panels and vegetal scrolls; a marble dado about 70 cm high inside [stated: PDTRA]; the podium 3 m high [stated: Madain Project "Decumanus"]; the city's most important temple, probably built under Aretas IV [stated: Smarthistory]. Ours (`qasr`): the temenos paved from the street's west end to the temple, its south wall with a bench along it, a wall on the west; the altar on the temple's axis before it, 12 × 10 m and 3 m high on a moulded base with a crowning moulding, a marble stair up its west side, a stone hearth with a bronze plate on top; the podium 3 m high with its moulding, the marble stair in two flights of 13 steps with a landing between cheeks; the temple 28 m square: antae and four columns with floral capitals in the porch, stuccoed pilasters along the flanks and the back, three courses of cedar laid in the walls; the pronaos wall with a door 6 × 10 m between stuccoed jambs; the naos with its marble dado and floor; the adyton in three, the middle open with its platform 1.6 m high, a stair up to it and the baetyl (a plain dark block) on a gilded base, the side rooms walled with doors and balcony floors; a cedar ceiling over all; the architrave, a stuccoed Doric frieze of triglyphs and rosette metopes, the cornice, the pediment and a tiled roof to 23 m [inferred: the altar's size and stair, the frieze (after its surviving fragments as commonly described), the roof, the temenos's extent].

**The theatre's side.** Left on the Outer Siq's east side (maps put it on the façades' side). Moving it needs the Outer Siq's walls, the floor's levels and the Street of Façades' run all laid out again (the façades fill the west side to the valley); recorded for a later pass.
