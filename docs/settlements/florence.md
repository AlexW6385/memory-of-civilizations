# Renaissance Florence, about 1480 — dossier

Settlement id: `florence` · Style: `renaissance` (with `vernacular` houses and bridge) · Region: Italy (Tuscany, the Arno) · Code: `src/settlements/florence/`

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Florence Cathedral, Santa Maria del Fiore. Brunelleschi's dome, built 1420–1436, is about 45.5 m across inside, and the lantern's top stands about 114 m up [stated: Wikipedia "Florence Cathedral"].
- **Giotto's Campanile:** about 84.7 m high and 14.45 m square, in white, green and red marble [stated: Wikipedia "Giotto's Campanile"].
- **The Baptistery of San Giovanni:** octagonal, before the cathedral's west front [stated: Wikipedia "Florence Baptistery"].
- **The Signoria:** the Palazzo della Signoria (Palazzo Vecchio), its tower 94 m high, stands on the Piazza della Signoria with the Loggia dei Lanzi (1376–1382) [stated: Wikipedia "Palazzo Vecchio"; "Loggia dei Lanzi"].
- **The Ponte Vecchio:** across the Arno, lined with shops [stated: Wikipedia "Ponte Vecchio"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1480, under Lorenzo de' Medici.

## 4. The style

White, green and pink marble on the cathedral, campanile and Baptistery; red tile on the dome and roofs; rusticated stone on the Palazzo; plastered houses under tiled roofs. [stated]

**Santa Maria del Fiore inside (L3, built).** A basilica with a wide nave of four square bays and an aisle either side, separated by wide pointed arches on composite piers [stated: Wikipedia]; the clerestory windows are round [stated: Wikipedia]; the interior cold and grey with pietra serena [stated: Horner 1877]. The polychrome marble floor dates from the 16th–17th centuries [stated: buffaloah], so in 1480 a brick floor is shown [inferred]. On the inner façade, Paolo Uccello's painted 24-hour clock of 1443, between the mosaic of the Coronation and the rose window [stated: Opera del Duomo]. The eight round windows of the drum were glazed 1434–45 to designs by Donatello, Ghiberti, Andrea del Castagno and Uccello [stated: Opera del Duomo], and Brunelleschi's choir enclosure stood under the dome [stated: Opera del Duomo]; the Last Judgment in the dome was painted only in 1572–79 [inferred: after our date], so the shell is shown white between its ribs. Ours: the four bays (oblong in our smaller building), piers of pietra serena with half-columns and leaf capitals, the arcades, a walkway on corbels at the springing [inferred from the ballatoio inside], rib vaults, lancets in the aisles where the outer skin has windows; the central west door open; under the dome an octagonal wooden choir round the high altar; the three tribunes with five chapels each, lit by lancets, under ribbed half-domes. Recorded for later: Uccello's Hawkwood (1436) and Castagno's Niccolò da Tolentino (1456) on the north aisle wall, the cantorie of Luca della Robbia and Donatello, the sacristies' doors.

**The Piazza della Signoria (L2–L3, built).**
- *Palazzo della Signoria.* Rusticated ashlar of pietraforte [stated: ermakvagus]; ours the block in courses of projecting blocks, stronger on the ground storey, with string courses under the windows [inferred]. Two storeys of two-light windows with trefoil heads under round arches [stated: ermakvagus — the present marble ones are 18th-century replacements of the originals; we show the type]; a column between the lights, an oculus in the tympanum, rusticated voussoirs [inferred]. The gallery projects on corbels carrying round arches, some with machicolations [stated]; the corbels are triple [inferred from photographs]. Under its arches coats of arms were painted in 1353, nine repeated along the front [stated]; ours four designs in turn — the lily, the cross, the parted shield, the keys [inferred: arms of the commune, the people, Florence-and-Fiesole and the Church]. Square (Guelph) merlons on the palace, swallowtail (Ghibelline) merlons on the tower [stated]; a pent roof over the gallery on the merlons [inferred]. Stone marzocchi (lions) at the four corners of the gallery [stated]; ours seated lions with a shield [inferred]. The tower (94 m) stands off-centre on the front and rests partly on the gallery [stated]; its own gallery on corbels with pointed arches, then an aedicule of round arches on four massive columns (the belfry) [stated]; the bell, the cap and the Marzocco vane [stated: the vane is a Marzocco; inferred: its form]. The ringhiera, the raised platform before the front, with steps [inferred: it stood until the 19th century]. Judith (1495), David (1504) and the later statues are after our date and are not shown [stated: ermakvagus — the earliest pre-1500 sculpture there is the Judith of 1494–95]; the clock on the tower (1667) likewise [inferred].
- *Loggia of the Signoria* (1376–82, Benci di Cione and Simone Talenti): three semicircular arches on compound piers with Corinthian capitals, of pietraforte with pietra serena details; the four cardinal virtues by Agnolo Gaddi (1383–86) above, on a blue-enamel ground; a cornice with modillions and dentils and a parapet of small trefoil arches; wide steps [stated: ermakvagus]. The roof terrace is Buontalenti's (late 16th c.), so a low tiled roof is shown behind the parapet [stated; inferred]; the Medici lions (1598) and the sculptures inside are later and not shown [stated]. Ours: three arches on the front, one on each side, the Virtues in green-framed quatrefoils on a dark ground [inferred: the blue enamel shown dark for want of a blue material].
- *Ponte Vecchio.* Three shallow arches, the middle spanning 30 m and the sides 27 m, a span about 6.5 times the rise; the shops given to butchers and greengrocers in 1442; the back shops projecting over the water on corbels; an open middle over the central arch; the Vasari corridor only from 1565 [stated: ancient-history-sites]. Ours: segmental arches with voussoirs, two piers with cutwaters, shops of stone with stone-framed fronts, counters and propped shutters, the back shops on timber brackets, roofs falling to the river; the arches flatter than the real ones because our banks are low (span ≈ 9 × rise) [inferred]; plaster colours are not shown (stone and timber only, to stay within the mesh budget) [inferred].

## 5. The town

About 1,500 houses and palaces. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): an osteria (tables and benches, the counter with jugs, wine barrels, the hearth, hams hanging) and two homes (table and stools, hearth, chest, bed, a cupboard of crockery); a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

## 6. Ground and water

Stone paving; the Arno and its banks; cypresses and pines in gardens. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

The clear Tuscan light; cloud and rain sometimes. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

From the Baptistery's square along the cathedral to the Signoria and down to the Ponte Vecchio. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Polyphony in the cathedral; the lute and the viol; carnival songs. [inferred]
