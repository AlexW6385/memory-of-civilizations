# Zagwe Lalibela, c. 1220 — dossier

Settlement id: `lalibela` · Style: `zagwe` · Region: Ethiopia (the Lasta highlands) · Code: `src/settlements/lalibela/`
Status of this dossier: L2 (interiors of Bete Medhane Alem and Bete Maryam, the Lasta tukuls and the liturgy in §11; Bete Giyorgis, the cross-shaped church in its pit; the northern group with Bete Medhane Alem and its colonnade, Bete Maryam and Bete Golgotha; the rock-cut Jordan dividing it from the south-eastern group — Bete Amanuel and Bete Gabriel-Rufael; the trenches leading down; the village of round stone houses with conical thatch on the slopes; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 recorded for later: the interiors, paintings and carved crosses, the tunnels and hermit caves, Abba Libanos in its cliff, Bete Lehem, Mercoreus, the baptismal pools, people, priests and processions.

Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning, written next to it.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the eleven monolithic churches of Lalibela, cut from volcanic tuff under the Zagwe king Gebre Meskel Lalibela (1181–1221) [stated: ancient-history-sites; Guinness]; in two groups divided by the rock-cut Jordan, with Bete Giyorgis alone about 300 m away [stated: wldu; brilliant-ethiopia].
- **Moment shown:** about 1220, the churches newly hewn.

## 3. Orientation

The churches face east–west (altars east); the groups north and south of the Jordan, Bete Giyorgis to the south-west. [inferred / stated for the groups]

## 4. The style

Red volcanic tuff, hewn smooth; Aksumite banding — walls in recessed and projecting courses with the ends of beams ("monkey heads") — on Medhane Alem's kin; windows of Aksumite and cross forms; flat roofs, Giyorgis' carved with nested crosses. [stated: Aksumite influence, dustanddynasty; inferred: details]

## 5. The town

Round two-storey houses of stone (the Lasta tukul) with conical thatched roofs, single-storey round huts, yards with low stone walls. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

A sloped plateau at about 2,500 m among escarpments [stated: wldu]; ours: the church precinct level, the slopes rising round it. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

Juniper, wild olive and acacia; grass and terraced fields on the slopes. (Eucalyptus came only in the 1890s.) [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

Highland: clear dry season, cool mornings, cloud on the escarpments. Latitude 12° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Chant in the ge'ez mode with the kebero drum and sistrum, the begena's buzzing strings (a plucked string, a drone and the ney stand in). [inferred]

## 10. Detail added (L2–L3)

- **Aksumite windows:** Aksumite openings were "framed with rounded cross beams, nicknamed 'monkey heads'", and the Lalibela churches carry these forms on [stated: The Met, Aksumite architecture]. Ours: on Medhane Alem and the Aksumite churches, each window framed by squared beams standing proud of the wall — jambs, sill and lintel running past them — with the cross-beam ends jutting at the corners, a transom with its own heads in the taller windows.
- **Bete Giyorgis:** "the lower windows of the building are in the Axumite style", "the upper windows are in the form of floral ogive windows" [stated: Senses Atlas]; the Greek cross appears "three times on the roof" [stated: Senses Atlas; Pilgrimaps: triple cross]. Ours: Aksumite lower windows, pointed (ogee) upper windows with raised frames, the roof's three crosses nested and stepped up one within another.
- **Caves in the pit:** "Its vertical walls have a number of caves", used as lodgings for priests and as tombs [stated: Senses Atlas]. Ours: nine cells cut into the pit's faces, some at the floor and some higher with a rock step below.
- **People:** pilgrims "dressed in white"; priests "are the only people to wear color" [stated: myjoyonline (pilgrimage to Lalibela)]. Ours: about forty still figures — pilgrims wrapped in white (most with the shawl over the head) in the pits' corners, sitting on the rim above Bete Giyorgis, filing down the trenches; four priests in white or ochre with white turbans, each holding a brass hand cross in the raised hand [hand crosses inferred from photographs].

- **Bete Giyorgis re-proportioned (third pass).** The church stands in a shaft about 25 m by 25 m cut into the tuff, some 3,400 m³ of rock removed round it; it is reached "by a descending trench and a tunnel, which leads to a sunken courtyard surrounding the building" [stated: Senses Atlas]; "a very narrow man-made canyon, spiralling downwards", becomes a tunnel close to the church [stated: Church of Saint George — Wikipedia]; its roof at ground level [stated: Amusing Planet]. Ours: the plinth had stood within 0.2 m of the pit's faces and the roof's crosses 0.4 m above the ground; now the pit is 42 m square (36 m inside, a court of about 5 m round the plinth — wider than the sources' shaft, which we read as the church's own extent [inferred]), the body stops 0.42 m below the ground so that the top of the triple cross lies flush with it, and the trench from the west runs into a tunnel through the last 8 m of rock (4 m wide, its ceiling 7.4 m below the ground, a hewn band over each mouth) opening into the court beside the west arm [inferred: the tunnel's size and place]. The nine cells in the pit's faces keep their places, stretched to the larger pit.

## 11. Detail added (fourth pass: interiors, the village, the liturgy)

- **Bete Medhane Alem hollowed.** Three entrances on the west, north and south [stated: ancient-history-sites, Biete Medhane Alem]; "five naves", "twenty-eight pillars that rise toward semicircular ceiling arches"; "the interior has no paintings" [stated: same]. Ours: the body is now hollow — walls 1.2 m thick, the lower row of Aksumite windows open, the upper blind; doors in the middle of the west, north and south walls with plank leaves folded back inside, a hewn frame round each, steps up onto the plinth; inside, four rows of seven square pillars (28) with plain block capitals divide five naves (the middle the widest) into eight bays, round arches along the rows and across the naves; the aisles' flat ceilings lower than the nave's; unpainted. The outer ring now 12 × 7 pillars, the 36 outside the sources give [stated: dustanddynasty]. Layout of the rows, the widths of the naves, the arches across [inferred].
- **Bete Maryam, its own element.** "Approximately 15–16 metres in length, 9–11 metres in width, and 9 metres in height"; "three projecting porches extend from the south, west, and north sides", the western the main facade; on the east "a central opening containing a carved cross", the lower openings with "monkey heads"; "five rectangular pillars on each side separate the aisles from the nave, with two additional supports in the sanctuary"; "one sanctuary pillar is permanently veiled"; "the flat ceiling is painted to imitate coffering"; "the undersides of the arches are carved and painted mainly in red, yellow, and green" [stated: ancient-history-sites, Bete Maryam]; "three naves", the decoration with "Greek meanders" [stated: Pilgrimaps, Biete Maryam]. Ours: a body 15.6 × 9.2 m on a low plinth, three porches each with a round-arched mouth and steps, cross-shaped windows with raised hewn borders in the long walls, on the east a large cross over two Aksumite windows; inside, two rows of six pillars (the last pair in the sanctuary, the northern veiled in white cloth with a red hem), capitals with a red cross, round arches whose soffits are painted in turn red, ochre and green, a painted meander frieze over the arcades, the nave's ceiling painted, the aisles' coffered; a red curtain hung across the sanctuary [inferred: curtain, from Orthodox practice]. Its reliefs (the horsemen and dragon on the west porch) recorded for later.
- **Lasta tukuls.** The village round the churches has "two storey round houses, constructed of local red stone, and known as the Lasta Tukuls", "with interior staircases and thatched roofs" [stated: UNESCO, Rock-Hewn Churches, Lalibela]. Ours: drums of red stone, a little battered, with thin proud courses; plank doors in timber frames; in the two-storey houses the joist ends of the upper floor out through the wall, an upper door over a stone ledge and small framed windows; the thatch in a deep skirt and a cone, a clay pot upturned on the apex with a stick through it; low piled-stone walls round some yards in the level precinct [inferred: pot finial, yard walls, from photographs of Amhara tukuls].
- **The liturgy's instruments and people.** "A large version of the kebero is also used in Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Christian liturgical music", a drum of a hollowed trunk with hide over each end [stated: Wikipedia, Kebero]; the tsenatsel, a sistrum [stated: Wikipedia, Orthodox Tewahedo music], of brass, about 25 cm high [stated: The Met, Ṣanāṣel]. Ours: in Bete Maryam's north court six deacons in white turbans before the church, five shaking sistra (a wooden grip, a brass frame, three rods of loose discs), one with the kebero slung before him, a priest leading them; inside Medhane Alem rush mats down the nave, ten pilgrims seated on them facing the sanctuary curtain, a priest with his cross before it, two drums laid by a pillar [inferred: placement]. The kebero's heads of unequal size [inferred, from photographs].
- **Trench walls.** The rock walls now reach 2 m outside each cut's edge under the ground and stand a hand proud of it as a hewn kerb, and the ground drops inside the walls, so that no seam or dip shows where the ground meets the cut [inferred: technical].

## 12. Detail added (fifth pass: the hewn faces, the water)

- **How the cuts were made.** "The churches have been carved top-down ... using chisels, axes, and other blades"; "workers first traced the perimeter of the structure on the rock face, then isolated the main structure of the church" [stated: The Met, The Rock-hewn Churches of Lalibela]. Ours: on every face of every pit and trench toward the cut, the tuff's beds as level bands a little paler than the rock round them, at the same depths across the whole site, and every 1.1 m down a thin ledge where one lift of the cutting stopped and the next began [inferred: the bands from the layered volcanic rock, the working levels from the top-down method].
- **Drainage.** "To avoid flooding from underground rivers and water tables, the church builders excavated drainage canals and trenches" [stated: The Met]. Ours: round Bete Giyorgis and Bete Medhane Alem a channel 0.35 m wide and 0.3 m deep cut in the pit's floor, a little out from the plinth and its steps [inferred: size and place].
- **Bete Maryam's pools.** "Additional hydraulic systems filled cisterns and baptismal pools, including the three pools in the courtyard of Biete Mariam" [stated: The Met]. Ours: three rectangular basins 2.4 × 2.6 m sunk 1.1 m into the court's floor, lined with fresh-hewn rock, water standing in them [inferred: sizes and places].

## 13. Bete Giyorgis hollowed (sixth pass)

- **Interior.** The interior is "its flat ceilings and its dome" (R. Sauter); "each arm of the cross is cut by a semicircular arch" cut into "the extension of the pilasters which rise at the four corners of the central space"; the sanctuary is "all decorated with crosses in relief"; no paintings or sculptures "likely to distract the eye" [stated: dragontravelmag, House of Saint George]. "The hollowed interior contains a simple shrine to St. George"; "behind a curtain (forbidden to view apart from priests) lies a replica of the Ark of the Covenant" [stated: Wikipedia, Church of Saint George]. Ours: the cross is hollow, walls 1.6 m thick, a crossing 7.6 m square with an arch at each arm's mouth (span 6 m), the arms' flat ceilings 6.4 m over the floor, the crossing roofed by a stepped dome of four square rings and a cap [inferred: stepped, from the Aksumite corbelled tradition], doors in the west, north and south arms with a flight of steps from the pit's floor, the lower Aksumite windows open and the upper ogee ones blind, a red curtain across the east arm before a hewn altar with a white cloth and a bronze cross, six crosses in relief on the sanctuary wall, rush mats and seated pilgrims in the west arm, a priest before the curtain [inferred: sizes, mats, placement]. Two interior rooms (halls) added; the plan's rooms are now listed halls first, since a position goes to the first room holding it.

## 14. Seventh pass: the house you can enter, tunnels, the procession, living systems

- **Tukul interior.** Interior staircases and thatched roofs [stated: UNESCO]; a household around "the cooking fire in the middle of the house" and a central pole in the related highland house [stated: core.ac.uk, Konso housing study; used only for the pole and the fire]. Ours (`hut`, beside the arrival): a drum 3.9 m outside, 0.55 m walls, one low door with a timber frame, the thatch lined and its rafters and purlin rings showing, a central post, a hearth with three stones and the clay griddle (mitad) for injera, a raised mud bed with hide, two great clay jars and a water pot, a woven basket table (mesob), a three-legged stool, a goatskin bag and a hide on pegs [inferred: all furniture, from photographs of Amhara houses]. A room `hut` (quiet music zone).
- **Tunnels.** "A hole in the floor ... leads to a long, subterranean tunnel (one of three) that connects the church [Amanuel] to Bet Merkorios and Bet Gabriel-Rufael"; Merkorios is "reached via a long, narrow and pitch-black tunnel that starts from Bet Gabriel-Rufael"; "a labyrinth of passageways, tunnels, hidden entrances and carved channels" [stated: The Velvet Rocket]. Ours: the trenches from the Jordan to Gabriel-Rufael and to Amanuel are roofed for 12 m with the rock left over them (ceiling 5.3 m under the ground), each mouth under a hewn band [inferred: places and lengths; the real tunnels run from the churches themselves].
- **Processional furniture.** Ethiopian crosses are "cast in brass using the lost-wax casting technique"; a trefoil cross in a quatrefoil frame with small crosses budding from it, with a socket so that it "could be mounted on a staff and carried during services or processions" [stated: The Met, Processional Cross 2011.159]; processions go "with colourful umbrellas" [stated: Alamy caption, Axum procession; colours not given]. Ours: a procession in Medhane Alem's south court — the cross-bearer, two priests under velvet umbrellas (crimson with ochre fringe, blue with white) held by deacons, two deacons with sistra — and the same umbrellas on a share of the walking priests [inferred: colours, gilt rim and finial].
- **Living systems.** Pilgrims in white cotton, women with jars and baskets, farmers with sacks, priests in turbans, boys, walking the courts, trenches, the Jordan and the paths out to the village (`movers.js`: courts and trenches as ways; day 44, night 4); swallows over the pits, ravens high over the plateau, sparrows on the rims and roofs (`fauna.js`); kitchen smoke through the thatch at the three meals, incense in the church doors, mist in the Jordan at dawn (`smoke.js`) [inferred: all counts and hours]. Soundscape was already in the engine.
- **Not built.** Eucalyptus: it came to Ethiopia only in the 1890s, so the c. 1220 planting stays juniper, wild olive and acacia.

## 15. Eighth pass: the village as a settlement (deepening toward the Suzhou standard)

- **Compounds.** The 420 scattered houses are replaced by about 107 walled compounds in 30 hamlets on the level precinct between the pits, each: a yard walled with piled stone (55 %) or fenced with wattle and posts, a gate gap with two posts and a pole laid by it, the family's round house at the back of the yard (some two-storey), a smaller kitchen house, in half the yards a store, a granary on stone piers, a pen, a threshing floor, haystacks on poles, firewood, water pots by the door, a stone seat, a grindstone, a washing line of white shawls, hanging log beehives; footpaths from every gate to the hamlet's lane and from the hamlet to the nearest road [inferred: the plan, from the UNESCO description of the Lasta tukuls and common highland farmstead practice; the single-family walled yard with outbuildings is typical of Ethiopian highland farmsteads, FAO/PMC landscape studies, not verified house by house].
- **Terraced slopes.** Beyond 262 m the ground steps up in nine terraces (9 m a ring, a 1.4 m riser each) with a dry-stone retaining wall on each riser; the treads in plots of wheat/barley, fallow and grass [inferred: northern Ethiopia's terraced landscapes are old and well documented, https://www.fao.org/4/x5523e/x5523e04.htm and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4190142 (landscape studies; their age at c. 1220 not verified)]. The village houses stand only on the level ground and the first slope.
- **Houses to enter.** Nine main houses near the visitor's ways (and the original one) are furnished as a household, a weaver's (frame loom with warp and the cloth woven up), a brewer's (fermenting jars, cups on a shelf, gesho bundle), a scribe-priest's (book stand with a parchment manuscript between wooden boards, hand cross, prayer stick, lamp) and a farmer's store (sacks, mud grain bins, plough and yoke leaning on the wall, hides). Every house has smoke gourds and peppers hung over the hearth [inferred]. Eucalyptus and coffee-ceremony furniture are left out as later.
- **Still life.** Goats, sheep and some zebu in the pens, hens, a donkey, a dog, a woman squatting at her work by the kitchen door, a man on the stone seat, a child in the yard [inferred].

## 16. Eighth pass: the churches' inside and the courts

- **The Aksumite-manner churches hollowed.** Bete Golgotha, Bete Amanuel and Bete Gabriel-Rufael are now enterable: a west door with steps, thick walls pierced by open windows, rows of square pillars under a flat ceiling, the sanctuary screened by a wall and a red curtain, an altar with the tabot's casket, a rush mat, hanging lamps. Bete Golgotha is "the one church in Lalibela that women are prohibited from entering", its Selassie chapel "off limits to all visitors" [stated: Rough Guides, https://www.roughguides.com/destinations/africa/ethiopia/lalibela-around/lalibela/lalibela-church-complex/the-southeast-cluster-2/ ]; Bete Amanuel "the most precisely worked church of the cluster" [stated: same]. The seven saints in relief on Golgotha's walls are widely reported [inferred: not verified in a page read here]; ours has eight robed figures with haloes in relief on its nave walls. The Amanuel and Gabriel interiors are not described in the sources read: ours is the same plan as Golgotha's without the saints [inferred].
- **Weathering.** Dark rain streaks down every cut face from the rim, pale lichen patches, damp at the foot [inferred]. Whitewashed-grey damp band round each house's base.
- **Courts.** Sandals left in rows at the church doors (the pilgrims go in barefoot), staffs leaning at the rock walls' foot, white bundles, water jars, and rock seats left by the masons [inferred from pilgrim accounts, myjoyonline, §10].
- **Descents.** The long ramps of the trenches (to Bete Giyorgis, to Medhane Alem, to Golgotha, the Jordan's two ends) have steps cut in their floors, 0.9 m runs [inferred; the sources describe a "descending trench"].
- **The Jordan's pool.** A kerbed basin of 30 × 3.6 m in the river-bed's floor with water in it; washerwomen at its kerb, a priest with his cross at its steps, pilgrims waiting to be blessed with its water, washed shawls spread on the stones [inferred: the Jordan's baptismal use, Met essay §12: "baptismal pools"].
- **Lamps.** Seventeen hanging brass lamps with a warm light in the five great churches' naves [inferred].
