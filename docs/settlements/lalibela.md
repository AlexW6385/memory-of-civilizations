# Zagwe Lalibela, c. 1220 — dossier

Settlement id: `lalibela` · Style: `zagwe` · Region: Ethiopia (the Lasta highlands) · Code: `src/settlements/lalibela/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (Bete Giyorgis, the cross-shaped church in its pit; the northern group with Bete Medhane Alem and its colonnade, Bete Maryam and Bete Golgotha; the rock-cut Jordan dividing it from the south-eastern group — Bete Amanuel and Bete Gabriel-Rufael; the trenches leading down; the village of round stone houses with conical thatch on the slopes; ground, planting, weather, music). ## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the eleven monolithic churches of Lalibela, cut from volcanic tuff under the Zagwe king Gebre Meskel Lalibela (1181–1221) [stated: ancient-history-sites; Guinness]; in two groups divided by the rock-cut Jordan, with Bete Giyorgis alone about 300 m away [stated: wldu; brilliant-ethiopia].
- **Moment shown:** about 1220, the churches newly hewn.

## 3. Orientation

The churches face east–west (altars east); the groups north and south of the Jordan, Bete Giyorgis to the south-west. [inferred / stated for the groups]

## 4. The style

Red volcanic tuff, hewn smooth; Aksumite banding — walls in recessed and projecting courses with the ends of beams ("monkey heads") — on Medhane Alem's kin; windows of Aksumite and cross forms; flat roofs, Giyorgis' carved with nested crosses. [stated: Aksumite influence, dustanddynasty; inferred: details]

## 5. The town

Round two-storey houses of stone (the Lasta tukul) with conical thatched roofs, single-storey round huts, yards with low stone walls. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

A sloped plateau at about 2,500 m among escarpments [stated: wldu]; ours: the church precinct level, the slopes rising round it. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

Juniper, wild olive and acacia; grass and terraced fields on the slopes. (Eucalyptus came only in the 1890s.) [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

Highland: clear dry season, cool mornings, cloud on the escarpments. Latitude 12° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Chant in the ge'ez mode with the kebero drum and sistrum, the begena's buzzing strings (a plucked string, a drone and the ney stand in). [inferred]

## 10. Detail added (L2–L3)

- **Aksumite windows:** Aksumite openings were "framed with rounded cross beams, nicknamed 'monkey heads'", and the Lalibela churches carry these forms on [stated: The Met, Aksumite architecture]. Ours: on Medhane Alem and the Aksumite churches, each window framed by squared beams standing proud of the wall — jambs, sill and lintel running past them — with the cross-beam ends jutting at the corners, a transom with its own heads in the taller windows.
- **Bete Giyorgis:** "the lower windows of the building are in the Axumite style", "the upper windows are in the form of floral ogive windows" [stated: Senses Atlas]; the Greek cross appears "three times on the roof" [stated: Senses Atlas; Pilgrimaps: triple cross]. Ours: Aksumite lower windows, pointed (ogee) upper windows with raised frames, the roof's three crosses nested and stepped up one within another.
- **Caves in the pit:** "Its vertical walls have a number of caves", used as lodgings for priests and as tombs [stated: Senses Atlas]. Ours: nine cells cut into the pit's faces, some at the floor and some higher with a rock step below.
- **People:** pilgrims "dressed in white"; priests "are the only people to wear color" [stated: myjoyonline (pilgrimage to Lalibela)]. Ours: about forty still figures — pilgrims wrapped in white (most with the shawl over the head) in the pits' corners, sitting on the rim above Bete Giyorgis, filing down the trenches; four priests in white or ochre with white turbans, each holding a brass hand cross in the raised hand [hand crosses inferred from photographs].

- **Bete Giyorgis re-proportioned (third pass).** The church stands in a shaft about 25 m by 25 m cut into the tuff, some 3,400 m³ of rock removed round it; it is reached "by a descending trench and a tunnel, which leads to a sunken courtyard surrounding the building" [stated: Senses Atlas]; "a very narrow man-made canyon, spiralling downwards", becomes a tunnel close to the church [stated: Church of Saint George — Wikipedia]; its roof at ground level [stated: Amusing Planet]. Ours: the plinth had stood within 0.2 m of the pit's faces and the roof's crosses 0.4 m above the ground; now the pit is 42 m square (36 m inside, a court of about 5 m round the plinth — wider than the sources' shaft, which we read as the church's own extent [inferred]), the body stops 0.42 m below the ground so that the top of the triple cross lies flush with it, and the trench from the west runs into a tunnel through the last 8 m of rock (4 m wide, its ceiling 7.4 m below the ground, a hewn band over each mouth) opening into the court beside the west arm [inferred: the tunnel's size and place]. The nine cells in the pit's faces keep their places, stretched to the larger pit.
