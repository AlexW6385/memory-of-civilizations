# Ottoman Istanbul (the Süleymaniye), about 1560 — dossier

Settlement id: `ottoman-istanbul` · Style: `ottoman` (with `vernacular` houses) · Region: the Bosphorus (the third hill above the Golden Horn) · Code: `src/settlements/ottoman-istanbul/`
Status of this dossier: L3 in the prayer hall (§4.10); L1–L2 elsewhere (the Süleymaniye on its terrace — the central dome on its drum with semidomes and cascading domes, weight towers, the domed courtyard arcades and fountain, four minarets; the outer court's wall; the külliye round it — four medreses with porticoed courts, chimneyed cells and domed dershanes, the vaulted shops before them, the darüşşifa, the imaret, the hamam and the dar'ül-hadis (§4.4–§4.9); the terrace's retaining wall; timber houses down the slopes; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 recorded for later: the tomb of Süleyman (1566, after our moment), the Tıp medresesi, the tabhane and caravanserai, the sıbyan mektebi and dârülkurrâ, the Golden Horn itself with its boats, the Grand Bazaar, people.

Tags: **[stated]** by a cited source; **[inferred]** our reasoning, written next to it.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Süleymaniye Mosque, built by Mimar Sinan for Süleyman the Magnificent, 1550–1557. Its central dome is 26.5 m across and 53 m high, buttressed by two semidomes on the qibla axis. It has four minarets and a courtyard with a fountain [stated: Wikipedia "Süleymaniye Mosque"].
- **The külliye:** madrasas, a hospital, a soup kitchen (imaret), baths and the tombs of Süleyman and Hürrem Sultan [stated: Wikipedia "Süleymaniye Mosque"].
- **The site:** the third hill, with views over the Golden Horn [stated: Wikipedia "Süleymaniye Mosque"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1560.

## 3. Orientation

The qibla wall faces Mecca (south-east; ours on +z). [stated]

## 4. The style

Cut stone; lead domes; pencil minarets with balconies and lead cones; domed arcades; timber houses jettied over the lanes under tiled roofs. [stated: materials; inferred: houses]

### 4.1 The courtyard (L3, built)

- A cloister of 24 arcades on 24 columns: the two nearest the mosque's door of porphyry, twelve of pink granite and ten of white marble, all with white marble capitals [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; columns of marble, granite and porphyry [stated: Wikipedia]. Domes over the gallery, the largest midway along the porch before the mosque's door, with marble muqarnas pendentives [stated: ktb.gov.tr gives 24 domes]. Ours: a ring of 9 × 7 square bays (5.44 m) — the plan that 24 columns on the inner line make — so 28 domes [inferred; the plan does not allow 24 domes with 24 columns at a uniform bay, recorded here], the one before the door raised on a drum; muqarnas capitals of four tiers of cells; pointed arches along the court and across each bay to the outer wall, their voussoirs red and white in turn [inferred: the Süleymaniye's courtyard arches; not in the cited sources]. The court is now 38 m deep (was 48) to keep the bays square [inferred].
- The paving of large white marble flagstones; a round porphyry slab about two metres across before the main door [stated: ktb.gov.tr].
- A small central fountain (şadırvan), roughly a parallelogram, with a zinc roof, an emerald-green metal grille and an openwork lattice with a marble frieze [stated: ktb.gov.tr]. Ours: an oblong marble basin 5.2 × 3.6 m on a step, bronze taps and marble stools round it, the green grille between marble corner posts, the frieze, a hipped roof in lead (for zinc) on a timber soffit, a gilt finial [inferred: sizes, taps and stools].
- The outer walls north, east and west with two rows of windows per bay — the lower rectangular with marble frames and iron grilles, the upper pointed — the side doors in the middle bays, and the portal in the north wall: a block standing out and above the wall, its niche with a hood of muqarnas, the door under an inscription panel [inferred: the Ottoman courtyard type].

### 4.2 The minarets (L3, built)

- Four minarets at the corners of the courtyard [stated: Wikipedia; ktb.gov.tr]; the two at the far end (by the mosque) with three balconies each and the two on the entrance side with two, ten balconies in all [stated: ktb.gov.tr; Wikipedia: the taller two have three]; the balconies on stalactite (muqarnas) corbelling [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; the taller two 63.8 m without their lead caps and 76 m with them [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: the taller 76 m, the shorter 56 m [inferred]; a square base up past the court's roof, a chamfered transition, a sixteen-fluted shaft, under each balcony four tiers of muqarnas cells, a parapet of carved marble panels between posts with a rail, a door from the stair onto each balcony, the lead cone and a gilt alem [inferred: the Sinan pencil minaret].

### 4.3 The tomb of Hürrem Sultan and the burial garden (L3, built)

- Behind the qibla wall, in a walled enclosure: the tombs of Süleyman and of Hürrem Sultan [stated: Wikipedia]. Süleyman's, dated 1566, is after the moment shown (about 1560) and is not built. Hürrem's, dated 1558: octagonal outside, sixteen-sided inside, seven rectangular windows each under a tiled lunette and an epigraphic panel, Iznik tiles inside, eight mihrab-like hooded niches between the windows; the ceiling now whitewashed, probably once painted [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: 8.8 m across inside, walls 1.2 m thick and 7.6 m high on a marble plinth, the door on the north face toward the mosque, small pointed windows above the seven lower ones, the tiles (an Iznik dado of white ground with cobalt, turquoise and red, a blue band of script at the window heads) inside, the niches as hooded panels across the inner corners, a drum with sixteen windows and a lead dome, the plastered dome inside; the cenotaph under a green cloth within a wooden rail, a carpet, a lamp [inferred: sizes, the drum, the furnishing; the sixteen-sided interior is simplified to eight].
- The cemetery on the qibla side with rose bushes and tombs [stated: ktb.gov.tr]. Ours in 1560: a dozen graves only, each a low marble kerb round earth with a headstone (a turban carved on a man's, a rounded top on a woman's) and a footstone, the long axis across the qibla; rose bushes; cypresses among them [inferred: number, the cypresses].

### 4.4 The medreses (L3, built)

- Four law medreses, each a central court surrounded by an arcade opening onto small cells, the cells and arcade bays under small domes, a larger domed dershane [stated: Archnet 2024]. Evvel and Sani west of the mosque: near-square, of cut stone, symmetrical, divided by a narrow street; the court porticoed on three sides; on the mosque side an eyvan of three arched openings under three domes; the cells round the porticoes domed; on the far side, on the axis, the dershane standing out of the building and cutting the portico, entered from both sides under a large dome [stated: TDV İA]. Salis and Rabi east, stepped down the slope toward the Golden Horn, cells round the court on three sides, the dershanes on the slope [stated: TDV İA; Archnet 2024]. Rabi: 21 cells of about 14 m², square, each with a hearth niche and cupboards, opening on the portico in front and outside through a rectangular jambed window; a square court of 718 m²; a dershane of 61 m², the tallest part; cut stone [stated: TÜBA "Râbi Madrasa"].
- From Sinan's other medreses: student cells about 3.5 m square under domes, each with two lower windows and one upper window on its outer face and a stove niche by the door; the dershane square under a dome about 9.5 m across on an octagonal drum, three windows in each of two walls [stated: Archnet 3738, Sokullu Mehmed Paşa Medresesi, Eyüp]; each room with a furnace, "square-shaped chimneys with hipped roofs located between the domes over the rooms"; the room domes slightly raised on octagonal drums [stated: Archnet 2016, Rüstem Paşa Medresesi].
- Ours (one builder for all four, mirrored for the east pair): a square of 11 modules of 4.2 m (46.2 m); a court of 7 × 7 modules (29.4 m, about 860 m² against Rabi's 718); porticoes of domed bays north, south and on the far side, pointed arches on marble columns with stepped (muqarnas) capitals toward the court and across each bay; 24 student cells (9 north, 9 south, 6 on the far side) each with a door and a grilled window on the portico, two grilled windows and a pointed light outside, a dome on a low octagonal drum and a square chimney with a pyramidal lead cap at the outer wall between the domes; closed corner rooms; the dershane on the far side, 9.6 m square inside under a dome on an octagonal drum of eight windows, entered from the portico bays on both sides, three windows to the outside, one in each side face and two to the court, inside a carpet, the teacher's raised seat by the far wall, six reading stands and a lamp; on the mosque side the eyvan of three domed bays open to the court by three arches, the gate behind it under a portal with a hood of muqarnas and an inscription panel, and two larger and four smaller closed rooms either side; a marble pool in the court [inferred: the module, the count of cells (between Rabi's 21 and the 30 the grid would take), the corner rooms, the pool, the furnishing; the porticoes all domed where TDV gives Evvel and Sani cross-vaulted porticoes north and south; the east pair not stepped, the terrace being level].

### 4.5 The darüşşifa (hospital) (L3, built)

- Two rectangular courts side by side, both porticoed; rooms on both sides of the front court; in the rear court a pool in the middle and rooms on three sides; all units domed; nine vaulted rooms in a lower storey on the north because of the slope [stated: TDV İA]. Two courts with domed arcades, the entrance into the outer court, larger domed rooms along its sides, the inner court with domed cells on three of four sides [stated: Archnet 2024].
- Ours: 12 × 10 modules of 4.4 m; the front court by the gate (east, on the avenue) with three larger rooms 8.8 m square each side under single domes; the rear court with twenty domed cells (chimneyed) on three sides round an octagonal marble pool with a fountain; porticoes of domed bays round both courts, the wall between them with a door; the gate under a portal [inferred: the module, the counts, the pool's shape; the lower storey omitted].

### 4.6 The imaret (soup kitchen) (L3, built)

- Square in plan, rooms of different sizes round a porticoed court, all domed; two ovens at the north corner, where the kitchen is; the five domed units at the west corner have domes with lanterns ("aydınlık fenerli"); the north and south walls of cut stone, the others of alternating stone and brick [stated: TDV İA]. Larger domed spaces on three sides for storage and cooking [stated: Archnet 2024].
- Ours: 10 × 10 modules of 4.4 m; five kitchen units 8.8 m square along the north, each dome with a lantern of posts under a lead cap; the bakery at the east corner, its two ovens shown by two chimneys; store and refectory units 8.8 m square on the east and south; porter's rooms and the gate on the west (the avenue); a well in the court; all walls of cut stone [inferred: the kitchens moved from the west corner to the north side, the banded east and west walls not built].

### 4.7 The dar'ül-hadis (L3, built)

- Rooms in a single row along a bent line, porticoed in front, a narrow court before them; a small dershane on top of the building toward the mosque; walls of "one course of stone, two courses of brick", brick sawtooth eaves (kirpi saçak) [stated: TDV İA]. An arcaded, domed portico of 18 bays with 22 cells, four of them past the end of the portico; it faces an enclosure wall with several gates [stated: Archnet 2024].
- Ours: a module of 4.0 m; 18 cells behind 18 domed portico bays along the south edge, four more turning north at the east end; the small domed dershane at the west end, a storey's height above the cells; walls in bands of stone (0.36 m) and brick (0.2 m), a sawtooth course of bricks set on edge under the cornice; the court's north wall with two gates [inferred: the module, the band heights, the dershane at ground level with taller walls].

### 4.8 The hamam (L3, built)

- Rectangular, the front slightly chamfered; the dressing hall (soyunmalık) square and domed; the warm room (ılıklık) rectangular and domed; the hot room (sıcaklık) of four eyvans with four halvets at the corners, the centre domed, the eyvans vaulted, the halvets under small domes [stated: TDV İA]. Completed in 1557, arched and domed [stated: Wikipedia "Süleymaniye Hamam"].
- Ours: 16 m wide; the dressing hall 16 m square, its two front corners cut back 2.2 m, two rows of windows, the door under a portal, a dome on a tall octagonal drum with a lantern; the warm room 6.4 m deep under a dome with two smaller domes beside it; the hot room 15 m square, 3 × 3 (the central dome on a drum, four pointed vaults under lead, four halvet domes); glass bulbs (fil gözü) in rings on every dome over the warm and hot rooms; behind, the furnace and water tank with a brick chimney, the furnace door and a woodpile [inferred: sizes, the lantern, the bulbs, the furnace — the Ottoman bath type; the chamfer applied to the dressing hall's corners].

### 4.9 The shops (arasta) (L3, built)

- Vaulted shops: on the west below the sıbyan mektebi, Evvel and Sani and the Tıp medresesi, known as Tiryaki Çarşısı; on the east below the court wall and the dar'ül-hadis, known as Dökmeciler Çarşısı [stated: TDV İA]. A row of shops fronts madrasas 3 and 4, lining the avenue between them and the enclosure wall [stated: Archnet 2024].
- Ours: rows before the medreses' mosque sides on both avenues, broken at the medreses' gates and the alley between them; each shop 3.3 m wide and 4.4 deep under a pointed barrel vault opening by a pointed arch, a raised stone floor with a plank top where the shopkeeper sits, a beam across the arch with a wooden lattice above it, the upper shutter leaf propped out as an awning and the lower let down as a counter on brackets; bolts of cloth on shelves on the west, copper and brass vessels and pans on the east (the founders', dökmeci); a flat lead roof over the row [inferred: the level terrace puts them in front of, not under, the medreses; sizes, shutters, goods].

### 4.10 The prayer hall inside (L3, built)

- **The space.** Almost a square, 59 × 58 m, a single vast space; the dome 26.5 m across and 53 m high, flanked by semidomes on the qibla axis; across the other axis arches with tympana filled with windows [stated: Wikipedia]. The interior about 58.5 × 57.5 m; the dome-arches rise from four great irregularly shaped piers; between the piers on the tympanum sides triple arcades on two monolithic columns; on the inside a single gallery only, screening the buttresses [stated: Freely, *Strolling through Istanbul*]. "A very large dome supported by four arches resting in turn on massive pillars"; aisles of five bays each under a smaller dome [stated: Archnet 2024]. The colossal vault on four gigantic piers [stated: ktb.gov.tr]. Granite monoliths, some brought from elsewhere in the empire (Baalbek), with muqarnas capitals [stated: Smarthistory]; Wikipedia calls the tympanum columns porphyry monoliths — ours follow Smarthistory, red granite. No exedrae: flat tympanum walls close the square across [stated: Studio Matrx].
- **Ours.** The hall 58.3 m square inside the walls' outer faces (was 58.3 × 53; the prayer hall now reaches 2.65 m further north, the courtyard moved with it); the piers 5.2 m square at the corners of the 26.5 m square, with a marble plinth and mouldings; four pointed great arches springing at 21 m to the drum's foot at 37.1 m; the pendentives as curved surfaces from the arches to the ring; the drum of 32 windows [inferred: the count, a common one; not in the cited sources] under the dome, painted inside with a band of script at its foot, a gilt ring and a blue medallion with gilt rays at the crown (blue, white and gold [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; red the later dominant colour [stated: Wikipedia] — left out); a roundel of script on each pendentive [inferred]. The semidomes north and south spring at 21 m under the great arches, each pierced by six windows at its foot within a low drum, carried on the piers, side arches to the outer walls and curved fills at the corners [inferred: the corner supports]. The tympana across: triple arcades of red and white voussoirs on two red granite monoliths (9.5 m to the capital, four tiers of muqarnas cells, the top tier gilt), the walls above with windows in three rows within the arch above the aisles' roofs [inferred: counts]. The aisles 11.5 m wide, five bays each under a dome on a flat ceiling with transverse arches [inferred: the gallery over the aisles is not built].
- **The qibla wall.** White marble mihrab with stalactite carving gilded in gold, the niche about 8 m with its base [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; mihrab and minbar of Proconnesian marble, of great simplicity [stated: Freely]; Iznik tiles only round the mihrab: a stencil-like floral pattern on a white ground, mainly blue with turquoise, red and black; on either side of the mihrab large calligraphic roundels (Al-Fatiha) [stated: Wikipedia] — two large rosettes of glazed tiles, white letters on royal blue [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; the stained-glass windows of the qibla wall by Sarhoş (the Drunkard) İbrahim [stated: Freely; Smarthistory], stained glass restricted to the qibla wall [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: the mihrab a marble frame 6 × 9 m standing out of the wall, a half-octagonal niche 1.2 m deep with carved panels, its pointed head, a hood of four tiers of muqarnas cells gilded in turn, a band of gilt script on blue, a cresting of palmettes; two great bronze candlesticks [inferred]; tile panels 6.6 × 5.7 m either side in lapis borders under a blue band of white script; the roundels 3.2 m across; six and five windows of coloured glass in a lattice of white plaster bars in two rows; plain grilled windows beyond [inferred: sizes and counts].
- **Furnishings.** The minbar of white marble beside the mihrab, of four marble plates [stated: ktb.gov.tr, "to the left of the mihrab"]; ours to the right as seen from the hall, where Ottoman minbars stand [inferred: ktb's "left" read as from the mihrab]: the gate with an inscription and a crown of palmettes, fourteen steps between side plates pierced with a lozenge lattice, the kiosk on four colonnettes under an octagonal cone with a gilt finial. The sultan's loge: white marble on porphyry columns with gilded white marble capitals, its door carved with geometric rosettes, on the right side of the nave [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; ours in the south-east corner (left of the mihrab as seen from the hall, the same reading) 3.4 m up on five porphyry colonnettes, a parapet of carved panels with a gilt rail, a door from outside [inferred]. The muezzins' platform on the pillar at the opposite end, simpler [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; ours by the north-west pier on marble columns with its stair. A walnut kürsü beside the pillar nearest the imperial loge, open-work and carved [stated: ktb.gov.tr]; woodwork inlaid with ivory and mother-of-pearl [stated: Freely]; ours a high seat with steps, ivory inlays.
- **Lamps and carpets.** Hanging mosque lamps and reflective metal balls [stated: Smarthistory]; the soot of hundreds of oil lamps drawn into a chamber over the entrance [stated: Studio Matrx]. Ours: a great iron ring of 64 glass lamps 4.2 m above the floor with an inner ring of 32, on sixteen long chains to the drum's foot, gilt balls hung among them; rings of lamps in the semidome bays and the aisles [inferred: the heights and counts]. The floor covered in carpets laid in rows of prayer places [inferred: the period's furnishing].

## 5. The town

About 700 timber houses. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): three houses with the sedir (the raised divan) round the walls, rugs, the brass tray on its stand, a hanging lamp, a painted chest; a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

**House detail (vernacular `house_med`).** The cumba as a closed bay on stepped brackets with a row of sash windows round it, their lower parts behind wooden lattice (kafes), a cornice board above; casement frames; iron-grated ground-floor windows; cornices as deep as the eaves [inferred].
