# Muscovy (the Kremlin and Red Square), about 1600 — dossier

Settlement id: `muscovy` · Style: `muscovite` · Region: Russia · Code: `src/settlements/muscovy/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2. Built: the Kremlin's triangle of red-brick walls with swallowtail merlons, its towers (round at the three corners), the Spassky gate and the Borovitsky gate; Cathedral Square with the Dormition (five helmet domes, zakomary, five apses), the Archangel, the Annunciation, Ivan the Great's bell tower in stacked octagons (≈ 81 m) and the Palace of Facets; Red Square with the Lobnoye Mesto and the trading rows; St Basil's (the raised gallery, the central tented church, four great and four small chapels with onion domes); the Moskva; log houses with plank roofs inside the Kremlin and in the posad; ground, planting, weather, music. Recorded for L3, later: the Kremlin's towers in their exact sequence and shapes; the moat (Alevizov ditch) along Red Square; the Neglinnaya; St Basil's inner chapels and painted galleries; the Terem Palace; the Kitai-gorod wall; bridges over the Moskva; true birch and lime species (stand-ins now: `poplar`, `elm`).

## 1. The real example

- **The Kremlin's walls and towers:** Italian masters built them in red brick between 1485 and 1495. They have swallowtail merlons. Twenty towers stand on a triangle, and the corner towers are round [stated: Wikipedia "Moscow Kremlin Wall"].
- **Cathedral of the Dormition:** by Aristotele Fioravanti, 1475–1479, in white stone with five domes [stated: Wikipedia "Cathedral of the Dormition, Moscow"].
- **Cathedral of the Archangel:** 1505–1508. **Annunciation Cathedral:** 1484–1489 [stated: Wikipedia articles of those names].
- **Ivan the Great Bell Tower:** built 1505–1508 and raised to 81 m under Boris Godunov in 1600 [stated: Wikipedia "Ivan the Great Bell Tower"].
- **St Basil's (the Cathedral of the Intercession on the Moat):** built 1555–1561 for Ivan IV. Nine churches stand on one base: a central tented church among eight chapels [stated: Wikipedia "Saint Basil's Cathedral"]. The bright colours of its domes are later, from the 17th to 19th centuries [stated: same]. The domes here are part gold and part green, which is our guess [inferred].
- **Red Square:** the market square under the east wall, with the Lobnoye Mesto (a stone platform from the 16th century) [stated: Wikipedia "Red Square"; "Lobnoye Mesto"].
- **Moment shown:** 1600, as Ivan the Great reaches its full height.

## 3. Orientation

The churches' altars face east. St Basil's central church rises above the square's south end. [stated]

## 4. The style

Red brick with white-stone trim; white-stone cathedrals with zakomary and helmet domes; gilded domes; onion domes on St Basil's; log houses under plank roofs. [stated]

## 5. The town

The posad of Kitai-gorod and Zamoskvorechye: about 1,000 log houses in their yards. Inside the Kremlin are the boyars' courts. [stated: timber houses; count ours — inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The square is beaten earth with log paving. There are gardens and orchards behind the courts, and the Moskva's banks are low with willows. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

The light of a summer at 56° N, sometimes overcast. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

Arrival is in Red Square, looking south to St Basil's; the walk goes past the Lobnoye Mesto. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Znamenny chant and the bells; the gusli and the songs of the skomorokhi in the market. [stated: Wikipedia "Znamenny chant"; "Skomorokh"]
