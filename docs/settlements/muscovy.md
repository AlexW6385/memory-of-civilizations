# Muscovy (the Kremlin and Red Square), about 1600 — dossier

Settlement id: `muscovy` · Style: `muscovite` · Region: Russia · Code: `src/settlements/muscovy/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2. Built: the Kremlin's triangle of red-brick walls with swallowtail merlons, its towers (round at the three corners), the Spassky gate and the Borovitsky gate; Cathedral Square with the Dormition (five helmet domes, zakomary, five apses), the Archangel, the Annunciation, Ivan the Great's bell tower in stacked octagons (≈ 81 m) and the Palace of Facets; Red Square with the Lobnoye Mesto and the trading rows; St Basil's (the raised gallery, the central tented church, four great and four small chapels with onion domes); the Moskva; log houses with plank roofs inside the Kremlin and in the posad; ground, planting, weather, music. Built since (v9.52): the Dormition's interior, the moat and its bridges, the stone trading rows, the Lobnoye Mesto of 1599, the Tsar Cannon (§1). Built since (north4): Ivan the Great as finished in 1600 with Petrok Maly's belfry, the Archangel's and the Annunciation's exteriors (§1). Built since (north3, L3): furnished izby open to the roof with the black soot line, the izby's roofs turned with their gables to the street, St Basil's central church inside, the traffic of the lane before the rows (§5.1). Recorded for L3, later: the Kremlin's towers in their exact sequence and shapes; the Archangel and Annunciation interiors (the Annunciation's iconostasis of 1405); the Neglinnaya; St Basil's eight chapels and the inner corridors; the Terem Palace; the Kitai-gorod wall; bridges over the Moskva; true birch and lime species (stand-ins now: `poplar`, `elm`).

## 1. The real example

- **The Kremlin's walls and towers:** Italian masters built them in red brick between 1485 and 1495. They have swallowtail merlons. Twenty towers stand on a triangle, and the corner towers are round [stated: Wikipedia "Moscow Kremlin Wall"].
- **Cathedral of the Dormition:** by Aristotele Fioravanti, 1475–1479, in white stone with five domes [stated: Wikipedia "Cathedral of the Dormition, Moscow"].
- **Cathedral of the Archangel:** 1505–1508. **Annunciation Cathedral:** 1484–1489 [stated: Wikipedia articles of those names].
- **Ivan the Great Bell Tower:** built 1505–1508 and raised to 81 m under Boris Godunov in 1600 [stated: Wikipedia "Ivan the Great Bell Tower"].
- **Ivan the Great as about 1600 (built here, north4):** the original tower (1505–08, Bon Fryazin) was three tiers and 60 m: two lower tiers as an elongated octagonal tower completed by open galleries, the third an open gallery for small bells under a small cupola; in 1600 it was raised to 81 m and finished with a gilded dome; a tier with corbel arches joins the octagonal third floor to the drum; a gilded inscription on a blue band runs just under the dome; the white-stone octagonal base is 25 m across; the walls were painted to resemble brick, with white-stone details [stated: Moscow Kremlin Museums, "Ivan the Great Bell Tower — architecture"]. Three successively narrower octagonal tiers with open bell arcades; walls about 5 m thick in the lower tier and 2.5 m in the middle; a stair of 329 steps winding round a central stone pillar; the drum of 1600 has eight slit windows with pedimented tops, two rows of keel-shaped kokoshniks round its base, the lower row gilded with gold stars; twisted white-stone bands divide the drum into three registers; a gilded onion dome and a cross about 7 m high; bracketed arcature among the ornament; the lower octagon holds the church of St John Climacus in two storeys [stated: Ancient History Sites, "Ivan the Great Bell Tower"]. North of the tower stands the rectangular building of Petrok Maly (1540s, finished 1552), traditionally the Assumption Belfry (Podyapolsky argued it was first the Church of the Resurrection); the present one was rebuilt in 1814–15 on its foundations, a base supporting a three-bay bell tier, smooth walls with bell arches, a drum and gilded cupola [stated: same]. Built here: the first octagon (9 m to its corners) of brick with white-stone plinth, corner strips, belt, arcature and cornice, two storeys of pedimented windows, a parapeted gallery on its top; the second and third octagons, each face pierced by a round arch in a white-stone frame, corner piers, the central pillar, a bell hung in every arch from a beam, a cornice and gallery; Godunov's block, the two rows of kokoshniks (the lower gilded), the drum in three registers parted by twisted bands, the eight pedimented slit windows, the blue band with its gilt letters, the gilded onion and an Orthodox cross reaching 81 m; and against its north face Petrok Maly's block — white strips, two storeys of windows, an arcature cornice, a bell storey of three arches a side (two on the north) with a bell in each, a hipped roof and a small gilded dome. The tiers' heights and radii (compressed with the Kremlin), the bells' number and the annex's form in 1600 are ours [inferred].
- **The Archangel (built here, north4):** built 1505–08 under Aloisio the New, five domes after the Dormition's layout; semicircular niches with shell-shaped ornaments; arched white-limestone gateway frames [stated: Wikipedia "Cathedral of the Archangel"]. The facade is in two levels parted by a horizontal belt (entablature), each with its own complete decoration; the wall strips turned into classical-order pilasters with 35 capitals; decorative arches in the lower level; the zakomaras hold large white-stone relief shells after Venetian models; the western central zakomara has a round medallion window; semicircular apses on the east; the original scheme two-coloured, imitating red brickwork with white details [stated: Moscow Kremlin Museums, "Architectural style of the cathedral"]. The lower register a blind arcade between pilasters, the upper with pilasters and composite capitals under a deep cornice; the main entrance a portal in a deep loggia with Italian floral ornament [stated: Sullivan, Bluffton University]. Built here: red brick and white stone; plinth, pilasters with capitals in both storeys, the three-banded entablature between them, the deep three-stepped cornice; a blind arch in every lower bay (a window in each), the west portal of four stepped arches round an oak door; pedimented windows above; over every bay a zakomara with its white ring and a thirteen-rayed shell fanning from its foot, the west one's with a round window; the lead roof; five gilded helmet domes on brick drums with white shafts and slit windows on square bases; three apses with half-columns, cornices and lead roofs. Bay numbers (five on the long sides, three on the ends), proportions and the domes' gilding are ours [inferred].
- **The Annunciation (built here, north4):** built 1484–89; three cupolas at first, two over the east corners; in 1562–64 two blank cupolas over the vaults and four single-cupola chapels over the galleries' corners made nine; domes and roof covered with gilt copper; the 14th-century basement of roughly hewn white-stone blocks, 1.5 m walls; above it brick; the walls divided vertically into three, finished with keel zakomaras and belted with arcature; built with galleries on basements on all sides; the south porch, once open, later roofed; a perron at the south-east [stated: Moscow Kremlin Museums, "Annunciation Cathedral" (architectural ensemble)]. Galleries on three sides (the east one dismantled later); lesenes divide the walls, which end in spade-shaped gables; corbel arches at the base of the central dome; slit windows set in the blind arcade frieze; a carved perron leads up to the south porch [stated: Moscow Kremlin Museums, "Architecture" (Annunciation)]. Built here: the rubble basement with small windows; the arcaded galleries on the north, west and south with their roofs, the south door reached by a white-stone perron with parapets (walkable); the four chapels over the galleries' corners with keel gables, drums and gilded onions; the body in three bays a side parted by white lesenes, the arcature belt with slit windows, a window to each bay, keel zakomaras; the gilded roof; the central drum on a square base ringed with gilded corbel arches, the two east domes and the two blank west ones (nine in all); three apses on the basement with gilded roofs. The walls are shown whitewashed; gallery depth and heights are ours [inferred].
- **St Basil's (the Cathedral of the Intercession on the Moat):** built 1555–1561 for Ivan IV. Nine churches stand on one base: a central tented church among eight chapels [stated: Wikipedia "Saint Basil's Cathedral"]. The bright colours of its domes are later, from the 17th to 19th centuries [stated: same]. The domes here are part gold and part green, which is our guess [inferred].
- **Red Square:** the market square under the east wall, with the Lobnoye Mesto (a stone platform from the 16th century) [stated: Wikipedia "Red Square"; "Lobnoye Mesto"].
- **The walls about 1600:** the battlements resemble a swallow's tail; the walls were covered with a wooden span roof; the towers had wooden tents, which served as observation posts; the tiled tent-shaped tops were added to the towers only between 1624 and 1685, and the Spassky tower's stone tent in 1624–1625 [stated: Caroun "Kremlin History"]. The "Kremlenagrad" plan of 1600 shows the Spassky tower topped with a tent on columns, with a bell inside the tent, probably of stone [stated: Architectural Heritage, "On the Subject of the Original Rooftop Type of Spasskaya Tower"]. Built here: swallowtail merlons (V-notched, with a loophole each) on the wall's outer edge — before, they stood on the inner edge as plain blocks; the wall-walk under a plank span roof resting on the merlons and on posts along the inner edge; the square towers with corbelled machicolation bands, merlons and steep wooden pyramid tents with iron spikes; the round corner towers with merlons and conical wooden tents; the gate towers with their superstructure, an open stage of four piers and arches over a hanging bell, and a stone tent banded in white with a gilded apple and spike. The merlons' loopholes, the tents' pitch and the vanes are ours [inferred].
- **St Basil's about 1600:** the ground-floor arcades were open until they were filled with brick in 1680–1683; wooden shelters stood above the first-floor platform and the stairs until they were rebuilt in brick then; the walls mixed bare red brick (or painted imitation brick) with white ornaments; the foundations were of white stone; the original arrangement had eight small onion domes round the central tent, though the 16th-century domes' shape is disputed [stated: Wikipedia "Saint Basil's Cathedral" via cavac.at mirror]. Built here: the podklet's open arcades (seven bays a side) with the solid core behind, a white-stone footing and cornice; the open platform with a white-capped brick parapet; the wooden stairs on the west and north under plank roofs on posts; tiers of kokoshniks (keel-shaped gables, brick faced and white bordered) at the head of the central octagon round the base of the tent; tall white-framed windows in the octagon; a kokoshnik ring, white band and windows on each chapel; the domes' drums with slit windows and white cornices, the domes with standing seams, apples under the crosses. The kokoshniks' number and size, the seams and the windows are ours, from the building as it stands [inferred].
- **The izby:** built here as log houses with their gable ends to the street: the front in round logs standing out at the corners, three (or two) little windows of the living room over the storage storey, in board frames with sill boards and small pediments; a steep plank roof with barge boards (prichelina) and the hanging "towel" board, the ridge log (okhlupen) ending in a horse's head; the door in a side wall [inferred: from the surviving northern Russian log house; carved window frames (nalichniki) as rich as the later ones are mostly 18th–19th century, so the frames here are kept plain].
- **The Dormition inside (built here, v9.52):** round columns instead of massive piers make the interior light and spacious; there is no choir gallery [stated: Orthochristian]; over a hundred figures of martyrs and warriors are painted on the round pillars; all the vaults share one height; the windows are narrow slits; a three-row iconostasis was painted by Dionisy in 1481; frescoes covered the whole space by 1515 (the surviving ones are of 1642–43: councils below, the Virgin's life and the chants in the middle rows, parables and miracles above, the Last Judgment on the west wall); the Monomakh throne (1551), a praying place by the great south door: four carved beasts carry a square structure with twelve low-relief panels, topped with a tent [stated: Caroun]. The south portal has the Virgin of Vladimir over it with the archangels; the portals recede [stated: Orthochristian; Caroun]. Built here: the Dormition as its own landmark plan with its nave as a room (hall music): white-stone walls 1.3 m thick with the south (main), north and west doors in receding portals of three orders, the icon of the Virgin of Vladimir with two archangels over the south door, the slit windows in the bays; outside the pilasters dividing the fronts into four bays (three on the west) and the arcature belt of little arches on hanging colonnettes; inside the flagstone floor, twelve bays on four round piers and two square ones (in the iconostasis), sail vaults painted on their undersides with transverse bands; the piers painted with three rows of eight saints each (96) between red bands; the walls with a dado of painted hangings and three registers of panels with standing saints (not over the doors and windows); the iconostasis on the line of the square piers: a local row with the royal doors and the deacons' doors, then the Deesis, the Feasts and the Prophets, in gilded frames on gold grounds, the Crucifixion on top; the Monomakh throne by the south door (beasts, carved box with panels, posts, canopy and tent, cross); a great chandelier of two rings of candles and two candle stands before the iconostasis, lit. The fourth tier (the Forefathers) is later [inferred]; the 1600 layout of the frescoes (the 1513–15 programme) is not known in detail, so the registers here are of saints, not the 1642 scenes [inferred]; the chandelier's form, the figures' colours and sizes are ours [inferred]. The dome drums open into the vaults in the real building; here the vaults are closed [inferred: simplified].
- **Red Square about 1600:** the square was the Great Market (Veliky Torg); after the fire of 1547 the wooden shops on its east side were set in market lines (Upper, Middle, Lower); in 1595 the wooden lines were replaced in stone; the buildings within about 234 m of the Kremlin's east wall were cleared (1493, 1495) for a field of fire; from 1508 to 1516 Aloisio the New dug the moat along the wall from the Moskva to the Neglinnaya, about 541 m long, 36 m wide and 9.5–13 m deep, lined with limestone and filled from the Neglinnaya; in 1533 both its sides were fenced with low brick walls; three gates faced the square (Konstantino-Eleninsky, Spassky, Nikolsky); the Spassky gate had wooden bridges over the moat (stone ones came in the 17th century) and books were sold on the Spassky bridge [stated: Wikipedia "Red Square"]. Built here: the moat, 14 m wide and about 7 m below the square [inferred: compressed with the settlement], from near the north corner tower to near the south-east one, its sides lined with white stone, the low brick walls with white copings on both edges, its water; the Nikolsky gate tower added; the gate towers' passages opened (white-stone frames, the oak leaves standing open, iron-bound) and wooden bridges from the Spassky and Nikolsky gates across the moat — plank decks on stringers, trestles of four posts with cap beams and crossed braces standing in the water, railings; booksellers with their crates at the Spassky bridge's end. The strip between the wall and the square is now kept clear of houses. The Konstantino-Eleninsky gate, the bridges' exact form and the moat's ends are ours or left out [inferred].
- **The trading rows (built here):** stone since 1595 [stated]. Built as ranges of small brick shops (lavki) running north–south between 3 m lanes and cut by five cross passages: a range opening on the square, a double range back to back, a range at the back; each shop an arched opening with its counter, its upper shutter propped out, shelves and goods (bolts of cloth, pots, hides, baskets); a plank pent on brackets over the fronts; white-stone footing and coping, plank roofs. One storey, the shop widths (3 m) and the roofs are ours [inferred: the form of the 1595 rows is not described in the sources used].
- **The Lobnoye Mesto (rebuilt):** brick by the 1530s, rebuilt in stone in 1599 and then surrounded by a lattice; round, 13 m across and 1 m high, with a stone parapet [stated: Wikipedia "Lobnoye Mesto"]. Built here: the round platform of white stone 13 m across and 1 m high, flagged on top, its parapet with coping and the iron lattice on it, open at the stairhead, the stair of five steps on the west between cheeks, the lattice gate standing open; a crier on it and a crowd before it. The stair's side and the lattice's height are ours [inferred].
- **The Tsar Cannon (built here):** cast in 1586 by Andrey Chokhov; 5.34 m long, outer diameter 1.2 m, bore 890 mm; decorated with a relief of Tsar Fyodor on horseback and inscriptions; mounted on a special frame with a fixed inclination in Red Square near the Lobnoye Mesto to guard the eastern approaches [stated: Wikipedia "Tsar Cannon"]. Built here: the bronze barrel with its bands, eight lifting brackets and a raised relief panel on its right flank, its bore, in a heavy timber bed at a fixed elevation on a raised platform of logs and planks (a raskat), south of the Lobnoye Mesto, aimed south toward the river crossing. The platform and the aim are ours [inferred].
- **Moment shown:** 1600, as Ivan the Great reaches its full height.

### 5.1 Inside the izba, St Basil's central church inside, the lane before the rows (L3, 2026-11-07)

- **The izba's room** (`src/styles/muscovite/izba.js`; one izba in five within 200 m of Red Square, one in 31 beyond, `open`): the stove was usually built in a corner; the "krasny" corner lay diagonally from it, with a shelf of icons and an oil lamp, and the long dining table stood there; long benches ran along the walls fixed to the log frame, and the polati, high sleeping platforms, were built into it too; windows were glazed with mica or covered with ox bladder or oiled paper [stated: gw2ru, "How a traditional Russian izba was arranged"]. Benches ran round the room, sat on by day and slept on at night; the most honoured sleeping places were on top of the stove; chimneyless huts had only a few small windows, the smoke filled the room and the soot made a line round the walls about waist-high; before about 1800 the huts had no ceilings and the smoke collected under the roof [stated: encyclopedia.com, "Izba"]. Ours: the one room open to the roof, the logs clean below the soot line (1.05 m above the floor) and blackened above it, the gable ends and the roof's underside black; broad floor boards; the clay stove on its log frame in the back corner on the far side from the door, its mouth to the front with the hearth ledge (shestok), soot over the mouth, pots on the ledge and the oven fork; the polati across the back of the room from the stove, on a beam and post, bedding on it; benches along the front wall and the door side; the shelf along the side wall at the soot line with pots and bowls; in the front corner diagonally across from the stove the corner shelf with two icons, the hanging lamp and an embroidered towel, the table under it with a cloth, a bowl and a birch-bark box; the mica panes of the three front windows; a water tub by the door, a distaff; the door's leaf standing open. [inferred: the plan, sizes, the soot line's height for a room open to the roof]
- **The izby's roofs (fixed):** the roof's ridge had been laid along the street front, so that the front was an eave side with its barge boards standing up in the air; now the ridge runs back from the street, the front a gable as described, and the strip of each gable between the walls' top and the roof is closed in logs.
- **St Basil's central church inside** (`src/styles/muscovite/intercession.js`): the Church of the Intercession "is 46 metres tall internally but has a floor area of only 64 square metres"; inside, a labyrinth of narrow vaulted corridors and the vertical cylinders of the churches; the walls "mixed bare red brickwork or painted imitation of bricks with white ornaments, in roughly equal proportion", and red brick was used as a decorative medium inside and out; the first figurative murals and the floral painting came only later [stated: Wikipedia "Saint Basil's Cathedral"]. Ours: the octagon hollow (walls 2.2 m thick round a floor about 5 m in circumradius) and open up through the kokoshnik drum into the brick-lined hollow of the tent to its top, about 43 m above the platform; the door in the west-north-west face, between the west chapel and the north-west one, under a white-stone portal of three receding orders; the brick inside with the white ornament painted on it — the plinth, belts under and over the windows, a frieze of little blind arches under the cornice, the cornice at the tent's foot, white ribs up the tent's eight edges and three white belts round it; the tall windows' white surrounds; a low iconostasis of two tiers (the local row with the royal doors, the Deesis) across the east with its cross; a hanging candle-ring (horos) on a chain from the tent and two candle stands; a priest before the royal doors and the faithful standing. The platform is reached on foot by the wooden stairs (made walkable). [inferred: the wall thickness, the door's face, the ornament's drawing, the iconostasis's height]
- **The lane before the rows:** sledges "were the most popular transportation, including in summer", the sick were always carried in sledges and bishops rode them summer and winter [stated: gw2ru, "The sledge – Russia's oldest transport"]. Ours: three goods sledges with their horses and drivers in the lane between the square and the trading rows, three two-wheeled carts in their shafts with their drivers, pedlars with packs and baskets along the square's edge, a kvass seller at his cask with a customer. [inferred: who and where]

### 5.2 The posad's timber work, the market, the log church (L3, 2026-11-11)

- **Nalichniki and valances** (`src/styles/muscovite/posad.js`): carved window surrounds (nalichniki) with a hanging board below the sill (the "towel", polotentse) are the signature of Russian timber houses; the surround is a frame of side boards, sill and lintel under a carved crown, with openwork (sun signs) [stated: general Russian-vernacular sources, e.g. Wikipedia "Platband"/"Nalichnik"; the 17th-century wealth of carving is better documented than 1600]. Ours: a crown with a keel-arch top and a sun-hole, a valance with a toothed lower edge and round holes; natural pine on most houses, white-painted on three in ten [inferred: colours]. The low porch (kryltso) at the side door of the closed houses [stated: Wikipedia "Izba"; shape inferred].
- **Yard fences and gates:** Moscow yards were fenced with palisades of pointed logs (tyn) with a roofed gate; [stated: Wikipedia "Izba"/"Russian traditional architecture"]. Ours: a pointed-pale fence 1.5 m high (lower than a real one, so the houses show) across each yard's street side within 240 m of the square, with a gabled plank gate in it; gate height and width are ours [inferred].
- **Street paving:** Moscow's streets were paved with logs laid across (mostovaya, "bridges"), repeatedly renewed; the Kremlin's streets were log-paved by the 15th century and more streets under Boris Godunov [stated: Wikipedia "Moscow", history; Russian "Мостовая" sources, uncertain on 1600 extent; extent inferred]. Ours: half-round logs across the middle 5 m of each 14 m street, a hand's width above the earth.
- **The market stalls (lotki) of Red Square:** the square was the city's market ("Torg"), thronged with wooden stalls and booths, rebuilt after fires, and cleared by decree after 1595's fire danger [stated: Wikipedia "Red Square" history, "Kitai-gorod"]. Ours: rows of plank stalls back to back west of the lane before the trading rows: trestle counter, pent board roof, hung cloth, wares by trade (cloth, pots, loaves in baskets, furs, candles, fish) [inferred: layout and wares].
- **The tented log church:** the shatrovaya (tent-roofed) wooden church, of a cage (klet) of logs with an altar apse (prirub) and a refectory and porch, rising to an octagon and a tall tent of aspen shingles (lemekh), silver-grey with age, with a small onion cap and an iron cross; Moscow's posad was full of such parish churches [stated: Wikipedia "Tented roof"; "Church of the Ascension"; Kizhi sources for the form]. Ours: six, at fixed spots of the posad; proportions ours [inferred].
- **Life:** walkers in the long kaftan with a sash and the fur-trimmed or soft hat (boyar, merchant, peasant, in homespun), the monk in a black cassock and cloak, women in a long sarafan with a headcloth, porters with a shoulder pole; the figure set lacks the tall gorlatnaya fur hat, so a soft cap stands in [stated: Olearius 1630s and other travellers; our stand-in noted]. Smoke: a third of the izby smoke at the three meals (black izby had a smoke hole), steam from the market's sbiten/kvass sellers, mist over the Moskva at dawn; birds: jackdaws and rooks round Ivan the Great and St Basil's, swallows over the river [inferred].
