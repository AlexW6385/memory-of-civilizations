# Muscovy (the Kremlin and Red Square), about 1600 — dossier

Settlement id: `muscovy` · Style: `muscovite` · Region: Russia · Code: `src/settlements/muscovy/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2. Built: the Kremlin's triangle of red-brick walls with swallowtail merlons, its towers (round at the three corners), the Spassky gate and the Borovitsky gate; Cathedral Square with the Dormition (five helmet domes, zakomary, five apses), the Archangel, the Annunciation, Ivan the Great's bell tower in stacked octagons (≈ 81 m) and the Palace of Facets; Red Square with the Lobnoye Mesto and the trading rows; St Basil's (the raised gallery, the central tented church, four great and four small chapels with onion domes); the Moskva; log houses with plank roofs inside the Kremlin and in the posad; ground, planting, weather, music. Built since (v9.52): the Dormition's interior, the moat and its bridges, the stone trading rows, the Lobnoye Mesto of 1599, the Tsar Cannon (§1). Recorded for L3, later: the Kremlin's towers in their exact sequence and shapes; the Archangel and Annunciation interiors; the Neglinnaya; St Basil's inner chapels and painted galleries; the Terem Palace; the Kitai-gorod wall; bridges over the Moskva; true birch and lime species (stand-ins now: `poplar`, `elm`).

## 1. The real example

- **The Kremlin's walls and towers:** Italian masters built them in red brick between 1485 and 1495. They have swallowtail merlons. Twenty towers stand on a triangle, and the corner towers are round [stated: Wikipedia "Moscow Kremlin Wall"].
- **Cathedral of the Dormition:** by Aristotele Fioravanti, 1475–1479, in white stone with five domes [stated: Wikipedia "Cathedral of the Dormition, Moscow"].
- **Cathedral of the Archangel:** 1505–1508. **Annunciation Cathedral:** 1484–1489 [stated: Wikipedia articles of those names].
- **Ivan the Great Bell Tower:** built 1505–1508 and raised to 81 m under Boris Godunov in 1600 [stated: Wikipedia "Ivan the Great Bell Tower"].
- **St Basil's (the Cathedral of the Intercession on the Moat):** built 1555–1561 for Ivan IV. Nine churches stand on one base: a central tented church among eight chapels [stated: Wikipedia "Saint Basil's Cathedral"]. The bright colours of its domes are later, from the 17th to 19th centuries [stated: same]. The domes here are part gold and part green, which is our guess [inferred].
- **Red Square:** the market square under the east wall, with the Lobnoye Mesto (a stone platform from the 16th century) [stated: Wikipedia "Red Square"; "Lobnoye Mesto"].
- **The walls about 1600:** the battlements resemble a swallow's tail; the walls were covered with a wooden span roof; the towers had wooden tents, which served as observation posts; the tiled tent-shaped tops were added to the towers only between 1624 and 1685, and the Spassky tower's stone tent in 1624–1625 [stated: Caroun "Kremlin History"]. The "Kremlenagrad" plan of 1600 shows the Spassky tower topped with a tent on columns, with a bell inside the tent, probably of stone [stated: Architectural Heritage, "On the Subject of the Original Rooftop Type of Spasskaya Tower"]. Built here: swallowtail merlons (V-notched, with a loophole each) on the wall's outer edge — before, they stood on the inner edge as plain blocks; the wall-walk under a plank span roof resting on the merlons and on posts along the inner edge; the square towers with corbelled machicolation bands, merlons and steep wooden pyramid tents with iron spikes; the round corner towers with merlons and conical wooden tents; the gate towers with their superstructure, an open stage of four piers and arches over a hanging bell, and a stone tent banded in white with a gilded apple and spike. The merlons' loopholes, the tents' pitch and the vanes are ours [inferred].
- **St Basil's about 1600:** the ground-floor arcades were open until they were filled with brick in 1680–1683; wooden shelters stood above the first-floor platform and the stairs until they were rebuilt in brick then; the walls mixed bare red brick (or painted imitation brick) with white ornaments; the foundations were of white stone; the original arrangement had eight small onion domes round the central tent, though the 16th-century domes' shape is disputed [stated: Wikipedia "Saint Basil's Cathedral" via cavac.at mirror]. Built here: the podklet's open arcades (seven bays a side) with the solid core behind, a white-stone footing and cornice; the open platform with a white-capped brick parapet; the wooden stairs on the west and north under plank roofs on posts; tiers of kokoshniks (keel-shaped gables, brick faced and white bordered) at the head of the central octagon round the base of the tent; tall white-framed windows in the octagon; a kokoshnik ring, white band and windows on each chapel; the domes' drums with slit windows and white cornices, the domes with standing seams, apples under the crosses. The kokoshniks' number and size, the seams and the windows are ours, from the building as it stands [inferred].
- **The izby:** built here as log houses with their gable ends to the street: the front in round logs standing out at the corners, three (or two) little windows of the living room over the storage storey, in board frames with sill boards and small pediments; a steep plank roof with barge boards (prichelina) and the hanging "towel" board, the ridge log (okhlupen) ending in a horse's head; the door in a side wall [inferred: from the surviving northern Russian log house; carved window frames (nalichniki) as rich as the later ones are mostly 18th–19th century, so the frames here are kept plain].
- **The Dormition inside (built here, v9.52):** round columns instead of massive piers make the interior light and spacious; there is no choir gallery [stated: Orthochristian]; over a hundred figures of martyrs and warriors are painted on the round pillars; all the vaults share one height; the windows are narrow slits; a three-row iconostasis was painted by Dionisy in 1481; frescoes covered the whole space by 1515 (the surviving ones are of 1642–43: councils below, the Virgin's life and the chants in the middle rows, parables and miracles above, the Last Judgment on the west wall); the Monomakh throne (1551), a praying place by the great south door: four carved beasts carry a square structure with twelve low-relief panels, topped with a tent [stated: Caroun]. The south portal has the Virgin of Vladimir over it with the archangels; the portals recede [stated: Orthochristian; Caroun]. Built here: the Dormition as its own landmark plan with its nave as a room (hall music): white-stone walls 1.3 m thick with the south (main), north and west doors in receding portals of three orders, the icon of the Virgin of Vladimir with two archangels over the south door, the slit windows in the bays; outside the pilasters dividing the fronts into four bays (three on the west) and the arcature belt of little arches on hanging colonnettes; inside the flagstone floor, twelve bays on four round piers and two square ones (in the iconostasis), sail vaults painted on their undersides with transverse bands; the piers painted with three rows of eight saints each (96) between red bands; the walls with a dado of painted hangings and three registers of panels with standing saints (not over the doors and windows); the iconostasis on the line of the square piers: a local row with the royal doors and the deacons' doors, then the Deesis, the Feasts and the Prophets, in gilded frames on gold grounds, the Crucifixion on top; the Monomakh throne by the south door (beasts, carved box with panels, posts, canopy and tent, cross); a great chandelier of two rings of candles and two candle stands before the iconostasis, lit. The fourth tier (the Forefathers) is later [inferred]; the 1600 layout of the frescoes (the 1513–15 programme) is not known in detail, so the registers here are of saints, not the 1642 scenes [inferred]; the chandelier's form, the figures' colours and sizes are ours [inferred]. The dome drums open into the vaults in the real building; here the vaults are closed [inferred: simplified].
- **Red Square about 1600:** the square was the Great Market (Veliky Torg); after the fire of 1547 the wooden shops on its east side were set in market lines (Upper, Middle, Lower); in 1595 the wooden lines were replaced in stone; the buildings within about 234 m of the Kremlin's east wall were cleared (1493, 1495) for a field of fire; from 1508 to 1516 Aloisio the New dug the moat along the wall from the Moskva to the Neglinnaya, about 541 m long, 36 m wide and 9.5–13 m deep, lined with limestone and filled from the Neglinnaya; in 1533 both its sides were fenced with low brick walls; three gates faced the square (Konstantino-Eleninsky, Spassky, Nikolsky); the Spassky gate had wooden bridges over the moat (stone ones came in the 17th century) and books were sold on the Spassky bridge [stated: Wikipedia "Red Square"]. Built here: the moat, 14 m wide and about 7 m below the square [inferred: compressed with the settlement], from near the north corner tower to near the south-east one, its sides lined with white stone, the low brick walls with white copings on both edges, its water; the Nikolsky gate tower added; the gate towers' passages opened (white-stone frames, the oak leaves standing open, iron-bound) and wooden bridges from the Spassky and Nikolsky gates across the moat — plank decks on stringers, trestles of four posts with cap beams and crossed braces standing in the water, railings; booksellers with their crates at the Spassky bridge's end. The strip between the wall and the square is now kept clear of houses. The Konstantino-Eleninsky gate, the bridges' exact form and the moat's ends are ours or left out [inferred].
- **The trading rows (built here):** stone since 1595 [stated]. Built as ranges of small brick shops (lavki) running north–south between 3 m lanes and cut by five cross passages: a range opening on the square, a double range back to back, a range at the back; each shop an arched opening with its counter, its upper shutter propped out, shelves and goods (bolts of cloth, pots, hides, baskets); a plank pent on brackets over the fronts; white-stone footing and coping, plank roofs. One storey, the shop widths (3 m) and the roofs are ours [inferred: the form of the 1595 rows is not described in the sources used].
- **The Lobnoye Mesto (rebuilt):** brick by the 1530s, rebuilt in stone in 1599 and then surrounded by a lattice; round, 13 m across and 1 m high, with a stone parapet [stated: Wikipedia "Lobnoye Mesto"]. Built here: the round platform of white stone 13 m across and 1 m high, flagged on top, its parapet with coping and the iron lattice on it, open at the stairhead, the stair of five steps on the west between cheeks, the lattice gate standing open; a crier on it and a crowd before it. The stair's side and the lattice's height are ours [inferred].
- **The Tsar Cannon (built here):** cast in 1586 by Andrey Chokhov; 5.34 m long, outer diameter 1.2 m, bore 890 mm; decorated with a relief of Tsar Fyodor on horseback and inscriptions; mounted on a special frame with a fixed inclination in Red Square near the Lobnoye Mesto to guard the eastern approaches [stated: Wikipedia "Tsar Cannon"]. Built here: the bronze barrel with its bands, eight lifting brackets and a raised relief panel on its right flank, its bore, in a heavy timber bed at a fixed elevation on a raised platform of logs and planks (a raskat), south of the Lobnoye Mesto, aimed south toward the river crossing. The platform and the aim are ours [inferred].
- **Moment shown:** 1600, as Ivan the Great reaches its full height.

## 3. Orientation

The churches' altars face east. St Basil's central church rises above the square's south end. [stated]

## 4. The style

Red brick with white-stone trim; white-stone cathedrals with zakomary and helmet domes; gilded domes; onion domes on St Basil's; log houses under plank roofs. [stated]

## 5. The town

The posad of Kitai-gorod and Zamoskvorechye: about 1,000 log houses in their yards. Inside the Kremlin are the boyars' courts. [stated: timber houses; count ours — inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The square is beaten earth with log paving. There are gardens and orchards behind the courts, and the Moskva's banks are low with willows. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

The light of a summer at 56° N, sometimes overcast. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

Arrival is in Red Square, looking south to St Basil's; the walk goes past the Lobnoye Mesto. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Znamenny chant and the bells; the gusli and the songs of the skomorokhi in the market. [stated: Wikipedia "Znamenny chant"; "Skomorokh"]

## 10. Life

The booksellers on the Spassky bridge, merchants and buyers in the trading rows, a crier and a crowd at the Lobnoye Mesto, a priest and the faithful in the Dormition, as still figures (`town.js`), besides the market life placed over the square by the shared life pass.
