## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Temple of Apollo and the oracle of the Pythia, in the sanctuary of Apollo on the south-west slope of Mount Parnassus [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi"; "Temple of Apollo (Delphi)"].
- **Moment shown:** about 400 BC. The temple standing then is the fifth, begun at the end of the 6th century and paid for by the Alcmaeonid family, "fully functioning by 490 BC" [stated: Wikipedia, "Temple of Apollo (Delphi)"]; it stood until the destruction of 373 BC, so the temple of Spintharos that stands today is NOT yet built [inferred from the temple's sequence: the one visible now is the 4th-century rebuilding]. Socrates is said to have been named the wisest of men by this oracle in his lifetime (the answer given to Chaerephon) [inferred from the tradition; Plato, Apology 21a]; the Peloponnesian War ends in 404. Pindar's odes (d. after 446) celebrate the Pythian games [stated: tradition].
- **Why it is here (DESIGN §7):** "the centre of the Greek world and its oracle" — the navel of the earth (omphalos), "Know thyself" on the temple front. Every Greek city sends questions and gifts; the treasuries line the Sacred Way.
- **Location:** 38.48 N, 22.50 E, Phocis [stated]. The sanctuary lies on a steep south-facing slope, about 500 m below the cliff-top scarp, above the Pleistos gorge [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi": the scarp rises up to about 500 m above the valley].
- **Today:** a ruin; the Athenian Treasury restored (1903–06), the Chians' altar restored (1920) [stated]. We show the place as it stood, painted marble and poros, crowded with offerings.

## 2. Layout (radius 190 m; DESIGN S class)

The sanctuary is compressed to the disc: the real Sacred Way, theatre and stadium lie farther apart than 190 m allows, so distances are shortened and the climb is steeper than real. [inferred: compression; the order of the monuments is kept]

- **Frame:** +x east, +z south, +y up. The slope rises toward the north-west; the Phaedriades cliffs close the north. The Pleistos valley falls away to the south. [stated: the sanctuary faces south over the valley; Wikipedia]
- **Entrance and Sacred Way:** one entered the peribolos at its south-east corner and the Sacred Way climbed in zigzags to the temple terrace, lined with votive statues and treasuries [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi": "from the upper entrance, the Sacred Way climbs almost to the Temple of Apollo. Votive statues and treasuries line it"]. The Roman agora outside the gate is NOT built (Roman) [stated: Roman and Late Antique]; in its place a rough open forecourt with stalls selling votive goods [inferred: sellers of offerings, tripods, wreaths stood at the gate].
- **The temple terrace:** the temple stands on a levelled terrace on the slope; its uphill (north-west) side is held by the **polygonal wall**, a retaining wall of polygonal masonry built c. 548–505 BC for the terrace of the temple [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi"]. At c. 400 BC it is still bare; the manumission inscriptions are from c. 200 BC and later and are NOT cut [stated: "from about 200 BC, the stones were inscribed with manumission contracts"]. The Athenian Stoa runs along the base of the polygonal wall and opens on the Sacred Way [stated].
- **Above the temple:** the theatre (first built in the 4th century BC; seats about 4,500 later) [stated: Wikipedia, "Theatre of Delphi"], and farther up the hill the stadium (track about 177.55 m long, 25.5 m wide; starting lines of stone) [stated: Wikipedia, "Stadium of Delphi"].
- **The Castalian spring:** in the ravine between the Phaedriades cliffs, east of the sanctuary, outside the peribolos; everyone who came washed and drank there [stated: Wikipedia, "Castalian Spring"].
- **Athena Pronaia** (Marmaria) lies lower, to the east; its tholos is 380–360 BC and is NOT built (after the date) [stated]. Not shown within our disc except as the old poros temple [inferred: omitted].

### 2.1 What we change to fit [inferred]
- The theatre and the stadium: at c. 400 BC the stone theatre (4th c.) and the stadium's stone phases do not yet exist; Wikipedia gives the stadium's first construction in the second half of the 4th century or the mid-3rd, while older studies put it in the 5th [stated: Wikipedia, "Stadium of Delphi"]. We show the first form of each, so the visitor sees what the games used: a **theatre of earth with timber benches** cut in the slope (the greek style's `theatron`, the same as Athens), and a **stadium as an earthen track with stone starting sills** at its east end. Both are marked as the earliest phases. The Pythian games were held here every four years, with music and, from 582, athletic contests at the stadium [inferred from tradition; stadium site in the Krisaean plain before].
- Track length shortened (to about 110 m) so that it stays inside the disc; the true length is 177.55 m. [inferred]

## 4. The buildings (what is built)

### 4.1 The Temple of Apollo (the fifth temple)
- **Plan:** Doric peripteral, 6 columns by 15, a cella with pronaos and adyton [stated: Wikipedia, "Temple of Apollo (Delphi)": "Doric, with a six-by-fifteen column peristyle"]. Stylobate about 21.7 × 58.2 m [inferred from the figures commonly given for the temple on the 6th-century foundations]. Columns here about 7.6 m high, 1.35 m at the base [inferred: Doric proportion 5.6 : 1].
- **Materials:** the contract called for limestone (poros); the Alcmaeonidae, paying more, faced the front with white Parian marble [stated: Wikipedia, citing Herodotus 5.62: "they used Parian marble in the front instead of limestone"]. We build the colonnade and the sides of poros (`limestone`) and the east front (steps, east columns, entablature, pediment) of `marble`. [inferred: split]
- **Sculpture:** the east pediment showed Apollo, Artemis and Leto; the west Dionysus among the Thyiades; the metopes showed gods and heroes overcoming monsters [stated: Wikipedia]. The pediment groups are NOT built (the greek programmes are the Parthenon's, not Delphi's); the east and west metopes carry the greek style's Gigantomachy and Amazonomachy groups as stand-ins for the gods and heroes overcoming monsters, a palmette acroterion crowns each gable. [inferred: simplification]
- **Pronaos:** a column (or wall) bore the three maxims "Know thyself", "Nothing in excess", "Surety brings ruin", and the enigmatic E [stated: Wikipedia]. We cut the maxims as lines of incised stone on the pronaos wall (as the Athenian stele's cut lines) and set the E in bronze on the architrave. [inferred: placement]
- **Cella:** pilgrims waited here; the floor of blue-grey limestone; walls with paintings attributed to Aristoclides [stated: Wikipedia]. The hearth of Apollo (the eternal fire of pine wood, with the hearth of Hestia where the goat was tested) stood in the cella [stated: Wikipedia, the goat is sprinkled "at the hearth of Hestia"; Pindar mentions a hearth, statues of Zeus and Apollo and the iron chair]. Plutarch: the fire was fed with pine and the temple's laurel [stated in tradition; Plutarch, "On the E at Delphi"].
- **Adyton:** sunk below the cella floor at the west end, closed to all but the priests and the Pythia; it held the omphalos, the golden statue of Apollo, the Pythia's tripod on its cauldron, and an altar with a fire of pine and burnt laurel and barley [stated: Wikipedia: "likely held a golden statue of Apollo, the omphalos, sacred armour, and an altar"; "the Pythia sat on a tripod in a chamber below the temple"]. The omphalos was a stone egg wrapped in a net of wool (agrenon) [stated in tradition; Pausanias 10.16.3, the stone was "a stone of white marble", not in our fetched sources — inferred: marble with a carved net]. The chasm and fumes are in the ancient accounts, and modern research finds no chasm [stated: Wikipedia, "Temple of Apollo (Delphi)"], so we show a stone-lined cleft in the floor under the tripod, covered with a grating. [inferred]
- **Ceiling:** timber beams and coffers painted [inferred: Doric temple practice]; cella roof a timber roof with terracotta tiles [inferred].
- **The altar of the Chians:** a great altar before the temple, black marble except for its base and cornice [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi"]; dated by its inscription to the 5th century. We build it at its true place east of the temple, about 5 × 3 m [inferred: dimensions], with a step platform.
- **The Serpent Column:** dedicated in 478 BC, three intertwined bronze serpents about 8 m high carrying a golden tripod, standing beside the altar of Apollo [stated: Wikipedia, "Serpent Column"]; the golden tripod is lost only in 354 BC (Third Sacred War), so it stands in 400 BC [stated]. Built here as a coiled column with a gilt tripod.
- **The Sphinx of the Naxians:** about 560 BC, a Naxian-marble sphinx on an Ionic column about 10 m high, beside the temple near the Halos [stated: Wikipedia, "Sphinx of Naxos"]. Built.

### 4.2 The treasuries on the Sacred Way
- **Athenian Treasury:** Parian marble throughout, a distyle in antis, 6.68 m wide (the page's "diameter"), 9.75 m high, 30 metopes (nine on each long side, six on each short side) of Theseus and of Herakles [stated: Wikipedia, "Athenian Treasury"]. The base carries the dedication "from the Persians, first fruits of Marathon" [stated]. Dated 507 BC or after 490 [stated: disputed]. Directly below the temple on the Sacred Way [stated]. Built at about 6.7 × 9.7 m footprint [inferred: 8.7 m long], with the metopes painted (`egyptian_blue` bands) and the long base. This is the second landmark of the town.
- **Siphnian Treasury:** about 525 BC, 8.27 m long, 6.09 m wide, a pronaos distyle in antis with two caryatids (korai) instead of columns, Ionic frieze painted green, blue, red and gold [stated: Wikipedia, "Siphnian Treasury"]; first religious building wholly of marble [stated]. Built with two maidens; the frieze's subjects (gods at Troy, Gigantomachy) as a painted band. [inferred: not in relief]
- **Other treasuries named on the Way:** the Sicyonian, Boeotian, Thebans, Massaliot and Cnidian [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi"]. The Argive treasury is c. 380 BC and is NOT built [stated]. We build the Sicyonian, Theban, Corinthian and Cnidian as smaller Doric or Ionic boxes of poros and marble in antis, each a different colour [inferred: forms of the archaic treasuries are all of the in-antis type].
- **The Athenian Stoa:** built for the naval victory of 478 BC over the Persians (the inscription names it); seven fluted monolithic Ionic columns, runs along the foot of the polygonal wall and opens onto the Sacred Way [stated: Wikipedia, "Delphi"]. Built (`ionic` columns, seven, with the polygonal wall behind).
- **The Bull of Corcyra, Arcadian and Argive kings, the Marathon offerings (the Athenian dedication by Miltiades' statues):** votive statues "line the Way" [stated]; built as groups of bronze statues on stone bases, using the greek `votive` kinds (stele, tripod) and statues on bases. [inferred: sets]

### 4.3 The theatre and the stadium
- See §2.1. Theatre: an orchestra a full circle 7 m across [stated: Wikipedia, "Theatre of Delphi"], the cavea cut in the slope above and a little north-west of the temple, giving a view of the whole sanctuary and the valley [stated]. Stadium: a long earthen track on the terrace above, with stone start sills (aphesis) at the east end [stated: the starting lines were of stone].

### 4.4 The Castalian spring
- The archaic fountain: a marble-lined basin with benches about it, water from a short aqueduct, lion-headed spouts [stated: Wikipedia, "Castalian Spring"]. The Roman fountain 50 m higher, with niches, is NOT built [stated: 1st c. BC]. Built: a fountain house against the foot of the cliff with a marble basin, benches and three lion-head spouts, a paved court; the Phaedriades as rock cliff in the ground.
- Use: pilgrims, athletes and the Pythia washed and drank there; the Pythia bathed in the Castalian water before consulting [stated: Wikipedia, "Pythia"].

### 4.5 The village
- The town of Delphi lay east and west of the sanctuary, the houses of the Delphians along terraces [inferred: ancient Delphi is known mainly from inscriptions; the houses were small, of rubble and mudbrick with flat or low pitched tile roofs]. We build the Greek courtyard house (the greek `greek_house`, simple form) in terraced rows on the slope west of the sanctuary and a few lodgings (xenones) near the gate, and shops of votive goods at the forecourt. [inferred]

## 5. Style vocabulary

- The Doric order of the archaic temple: heavier columns than the Parthenon, spacing about 4 m axis to axis for 1.35 m diameter [inferred: ratio of the archaic temple]; the Ionic of the Naxian column, the Athenian Stoa and the Siphnian korai. Materials: `limestone` (poros, painted), `marble` (Parian), `marble_dark` for the altar of the Chians (black), `egyptian_blue` for triglyphs and tympana, `terracotta`-red `roof_tile`. [inferred: palette]
- Polygonal masonry: `cyclopean`-like, but of finely fitted limestone polygons; built with the greek `cyclopean` element as an approximation (rough boulders) — a known difference. [inferred]

## 7. Sound

- The Pythia is consulted on the seventh day of the month in the nine warm months; Apollo "is absent in winter" [stated: Wikipedia, "Pythia"]. Sound: wind among the cliffs, jackdaws (choughs) on the Phaedriades [inferred: the cliffs' birds today are choughs and ravens], goat bells on the slope, the spring, voices of pilgrims, a kithara and aulos from the processions; the Dorian species of the Parthenon music (the greek buildings' music).
