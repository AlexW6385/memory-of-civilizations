## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Phoenix Hall (Amida-dō) of Byōdō-in, dedicated in 1053, facing east across its pond [stated: `docs/buildings/byodo-in-phoenix-hall.md` and its sources].
- **The setting:** Byōdō-in stands on the west bank of the Uji River. It was built as a Fujiwara villa turned temple [stated: Wikipedia "Byōdō-in"]. The Uji Bridge, first built in 646, crosses the river nearby [stated: Wikipedia "Uji Bridge"]. The Ujigami Shrine stands on the far bank [stated: Wikipedia "Ujigami Shrine"].
- **Moment shown:** 1053.

## 4. The style

Heian timber architecture (see the hall's dossier); village houses of timber on stones, walls of planks or white plaster, gables of cypress bark or thatch; a timber-and-stone bridge (stone stands in). [inferred]

**The phoenixes (L3, built).** A pair of bronze phoenixes (hōō), once gilt, stand on the ridge; made with the hall in the 11th century, the originals are in the temple museum since their designation as National Treasures (1973) and replicas stand on the roof; the north one is 4 cm taller, the south one 10 cm wider; the wings spread and the chest thrust out as if about to take flight [stated: MLIT "Phoenix (Hō-ō) Statues"; Wikipedia "Byōdō-in"]. Ours (1053, so new and gilt): about 1 m, on a small round base, the legs, the body with the chest out, the neck curving up to a crested head with an open beak and a wattle, each wing of five long feathers raised and spread over coverts, three tail plumes streaming back and curling up [inferred: the form]; facing outward along the ridge as before [inferred].

**The Uji Bridge (L3, built).** Counted among Japan's three oldest bridges, first built in the 640s; a small projection a little west of its middle, the san-no-ma, is said to be the remnant of a shrine to the guardian Hashihime (later, water for Hideyoshi's tea was drawn there) [stated: GLTJP "Ujibashi Bridge"]. Ours: a timber bridge 78 m long and 6 m wide in a gentle arch, on rows of four round piles every 5.4 m tied by cap beams and braces, stringers, a plank deck, railings of posts and two rails with bronze onion finials (giboshi) at the ends and every third post, and the san-no-ma as a railed platform projecting upstream from the third bay from the west [inferred: the Heian form; the finials are of later bridges and stand in].

## 5. The town

About 200 houses. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): furnished as in Nara: the raised floor, the sunken hearth, mats, a low table, a chest, a shelf; a step up to the door, the leaf open inside, a ceiling of boards on beams [inferred: the period's village interiors].

**House detail (vernacular `house_japan`).** The timber frame shown: corner posts, posts between the bays, the sill beam on the stones and the head beam; windows and a gable vent of wooden lattice (shitomi-like); deep eaves with their boards underneath; thatch ridges with crossed poles; some roofs of boards held down by poles and stones — early town roofs were of shingles weighted with stones or of thatch [stated: JAANUS, "machiya"; inferred: the details].

## 6. Ground and water

Gravel and moss in the garden; the pond; the pebbled banks of the swift river; cedar, maple and pine on the hills. [inferred]

**The pond's edges (L3, built).** Byōdō-in's garden is the oldest Pure Land garden completed in the Heian period, its Aji pond, the Uji River and the hills behind making the setting of the Western Paradise; the Sakuteiki describes the ideal of such a garden [stated: MLIT "Byōdō-in garden"]; the shallow Aji pond round the Phoenix Hall has a pebble beach (suhama) laid with gravel, and two bridges, a flat one (hirabashi) and an arched one (sorihashi), cross from the north shore to the north wing's side [stated: Travel Watch]; at Mujōkō-in, built after the Phoenix Hall, the central island was joined to the shore by a bridge by way of a small north island [stated: MLIT "Mujōkō-in: Nakajima"]. Ours: the pebble beach — about 20,000 rounded stones 0.18–0.4 m across, laid thick from 0.4 m above the water's edge to 0.3 m under it along every shore of the pond and round the small island; a dozen larger set stones at the beach's points and on the island; the small island (koshima) in the pond's north arm, about 10 × 6 m above the water, its middle in turf; an arched bridge (rise 1.3 m) from the hall's island to it and a flat one on to the north shore, each 2 m wide [inferred: sizes and stone counts; the arrangement after the stated bridges and Mujōkō-in's copy].

## 10. Detail (L3, second pass)

- **The canopy.** In Japan canopies hung over statues were of metal or wood, square, hexagonal, octagonal or round, often hung with jewelled nets; until the Fujiwara period decorated with arabesque flowers (hōsōge) in openwork or relief; the Phoenix Hall's (1053) "consists of a round, openwork, hōsōge decorative panel inside a box-shaped outer structure (inlaid lacquer)" [stated: JAANUS "tengai"]; floral motifs with mother-of-pearl inlay once covered the interior [stated: MLIT "Phoenix Hall decorations and door paintings"]. Ours (in `src/buildings/byodo-in-phoenix-hall`): a box 3.7 m square and 0.62 m deep hung from the high ceiling over Amida, above the mandorla's tip: boards of black lacquer bound top and bottom in gilt, a row of mother-of-pearl rosettes on each face, a valance of gilt pendants and strings of gilt beads; inside, the round canopy: a rim and two rings joined by C-scrolls with curls, leaves in the inner field, a lotus of twelve petals round a bronze mirror at the centre [inferred: sizes, the valance, the beads, the scroll pattern].
- **The mirrors.** Sixty-six bronze mirrors in the ceiling catch the light [stated: MLIT]. Ours: discs 0.24 m across with a rim, their faces down — thirty on the high ceiling round the canopy, thirty-six on the lower ceilings either side [inferred: the placing].
- **The paintings.** The walls and doors bear the *kuhon raigō-zu*, the nine forms of Amida's welcome of the dead after the Contemplation Sutra, in landscapes of the four seasons, the earliest surviving raigō paintings in Japan [stated: MLIT "Art of Byōdōin"]; the interior was in full colour, with wall, door and pillar paintings [stated: Niigata Bandaijima Art Museum, Byōdō-in exhibition release]. Ours: six wall panels (the side walls' four bays, the back wall's two side bays) between 0.35 and 3.55 m above the floor, under the bodhisattvas on clouds, and the open door leaves' inner faces: far and near green hills (snowy in winter), a winding river, pines and the season's trees (blossom, red maples), a house with the dying believer, and the white cloud trailing down from the upper corner with Amida in his halo and his bodhisattvas, each layer a face a little in front of the last [inferred: the composition; the seasons assigned round the hall].
- **The bell tower.** Byōdō-in's bronze bell, cast in the Heian period, about 2.5 t, a National Treasure; three relief bands, dragons near the top, heavenly figures in the middle, lions at the base; four panels of knobs near the top; a hook of two dragons; it hung in the bell tower across the pond from the Phoenix Hall (a replica hangs there now) [stated: MLIT "Temple Bell (Bonsho)"]; counted among Japan's three famous bells, "the Byōdō-in bell of form" [stated: tabi-mag]. Ours: east of the pond at (50, 46): a stone platform, four posts splayed slightly inward, tie beams low and high, bearing blocks, head beams, the hanging beam, a hip-and-gable roof of tiles; the bell about 1.95 m high with its bands, the four vertical bands, five-by-five knobs in each upper panel, the relief bands as raised forms, two lotus striking seats and the two-dragon hook, its lip about 2 m above the floor; the striking log on ropes [inferred: the tower's form, the bell's size and its relief simplified; the bell taken as of the hall's time].
- **The fishing weirs.** *Ajirogi*, fish-trap stakes, stood in the shallows of the Uji River in autumn and winter, familiar to the Heian court — Fujiwara no Sadayori's poem (Hyakunin isshu 64) sees them appear as the morning mist lifts; the Uji chapters of the Tale of Genji mention them [stated: One Thousand Summers, poem 64]. Ours: two weirs above the bridge, each two converging lines of stakes every 0.6 m with a wattle screen between them, pointing downstream to a slatted trap under a plank platform where a fisherman stands with his pole [inferred: the form].
- **Boats and people.** A roofed boat moored by the west bank, two fishing boats in the stream, a barge poled upstream; on the bridge a courtier in black with a lacquered cap, two women under veiled hats, travellers with a basket and a carrying pole, an ox cart of hay with its driver [inferred].

- **Amida's dais (third pass).** "The whole interior is decorated with the motif of an imaginary flower, hōsōge"; "all these decorations shone with mother-of-pearl inlay"; the walls, ceiling, pillars and beams had vivid original colours [stated: MLIT 001553422]. Ours (`src/styles/japanese/dais.js`, replacing the plain lacquered block): a sumeru dais 3.9 × 4.0 m and 1 m high in black lacquer — a plinth, a step in, the recessed waist, a step out, the cap and a top board; the waist divided by posts into panels about 0.6 m wide, each with a cusped kōzama outline in gilt and a hōsōge rosette of eight mother-of-pearl petals and four leaves round a gilt heart; gilt bands round the plinth, the cap and the top board; gilt plates bent over the corners [inferred: the form after Heian shumidan in general; the rosettes after the stated hōsōge and raden].
- **Worship (third pass).** "When you see the hall from across the pond, the face of Amida Buddha can be seen just through the round window of the hall" [stated: Kyoto Private Guides]; the garden made, with the pond, the river and the hills, the setting of the Western Paradise [stated: MLIT "Byōdō-in garden"]. Ours: on the east bank, either side of the axis (so the view from the arrival stays clear), two mats of rushes on the gravel and on them a courtier's party kneeling toward the hall, palms joined — a courtier in black with his lacquered cap, ladies in red, green, ochre and maroon with long hair — the monk who leads them standing at the water with his staff, a servant behind; in the hall two monks kneeling before the dais [inferred: the people and their places].
- **Weathering.** The climate's dampness set to 0.7 (the humid basin, mist and rain off the river): moss on the shaded foot of walls and on north-facing roofs, rain streaks [inferred].

## 11. Houses of the commoners (batch pass)

- **Roofs.** Roofs of boards held down by stones instead of nails (*ishioki itabuki*): shingles laid from the eaves to the ridge, head-sized round stones set close over horizontal timbers, a gabled roof of very shallow pitch (3–3.5 in 10), thick bargeboards and fascias; medieval handscrolls show it already in use (details differing) [stated: JAANUS "ishioki itabuki"]. Thatch and cypress bark were the other coverings of the period (the shinden's roof is laminated cypress bark, not tile) [stated: Wikipedia "Shinden-zukuri"]. Ours (`heian_house`, `src/styles/japanese/heian.js`): by the lot's seed, a stone-weighted board roof (pitch 0.34 of the half-span, four rows of stones on each slope), a thatch roof (pitch ~0.95, thick, with crossed poles at the ridge ends) or a bark roof (0.55), gabled, the ridge along or across the front [inferred: the proportions; the weights as rows of stones].
- **Walls, floor, hearth.** A timber frame of dark posts on stones, walls of planks with battens or white plaster, a plank door, a lattice window with a reed blind half lowered; the house raised 0.45 m on stones. The three open houses are hollow: an earth floor with the clay stove (*kamado*) and a water jar, a step board, a raised plank floor with two straw mats, the sunken hearth (*irori*) with its kettle on a pole, a chest, a bench, a hanging shelf of jars [inferred: after the period's village houses and later minka].
- **Yards.** A wattle fence of posts and woven panels behind and at one side, a woodpile, jars, a rack of drying rice sheaves on poles, on larger lots a granary on four posts under thatch [inferred]. The lanes and yards of the village are beaten earth in the ground's cover.

## 12. The great houses (batch pass)

- **The shinden-zukuri.** A mansion stood on a one-*chō* (about 109 m) square; the shinden faced south over an open court; two tai-no-ya east and west joined to it by the sukiwatadono and watadono corridors, with narrow corridors running on south to the tsuridono pavilions; a stream fed the large south pond with islets, bridges, trees and rocks "to evoke the Land of Amida". Roof of laminated cypress bark. Floors raised on stilts, mats laid separately on polished boards; a core (*moya*) wrapped in a one-ken aisle (*hisashi*); few fixed walls, openings closed by doors, shitomi (hinged shutters) and sudare (blinds); portable screens divided the room; a plastered sleeping room (*nurigome*) with the canopied bed (*chōdai*); pillars left natural [stated: Wikipedia "Shinden-zukuri"]. Byōdō-in itself was a Fujiwara villa: built by Minamoto no Shigenobu in 998, bought by Fujiwara no Michinaga, turned into a temple by his son Yorimichi in 1052 [stated: Wikipedia "Byōdō-in"].
- **Ours** (`heian_hall`, `corridor`, `tsuijibei`, `yakumon`, `src/settlements/heian-uji/compound.js`): a villa west of the garden, 72 × 80 m: the shinden of 7 × 4 ken (ken 2.4 m: 16.8 × 9.6 m), its floor 1.0 m up on round cypress posts on stone bases, a veranda along the south with a red-lacquer railing and a seven-step stair in the middle bay; the aisle's outer line closed by shitomi (the lower leaf lattice, the upper leaf hung up under the beam) and half-lowered sudare with green borders, the middle bay open under a red valance; the roof an `irimoya` of bark with deep eaves; a tai-no-ya (3 × 3 ken) either side joined by covered corridors; the earthen wall (*tsuijibei*) round it, battered, with white plaster bands and a tiled coping, and a four-post gate (*yakumon*) with a bark gable in the south; the south court of white gravel; servants' houses inside. A smaller nobleman's house (a tai-no-ya type hall with its wall and gate) stands to the north [inferred: the sizes; the stair and railing after the hall's own; the wall's profile after later tsuijibei].
- **Inside the shinden** (furnished, walkable): two thick mats (*agedatami*) with red borders and a cushion, a gilt six-panel folding screen (*byōbu*) with a black frame behind them, two curtain stands (*kichō*: a T-frame of black lacquer, white silk with red cords), round mats and long mats with green or black borders, a low lacquer table with jars, a lamp stand (*tōdai*), a black-lacquer shelf (*zushi-dana*) with a mirror stand and a jar, a koto on its stands, and a plastered *nurigome* room at the end with the canopied bed [stated: the forms in §12's source; inferred: the placing]. In it a lord in the black lacquered cap (*eboshi*), two ladies on mats in layered robes with long hair, a courtier at the koto, an attendant kneeling.

## 13. Life (batch pass)

- **Pleasure boats of the Aji pond.** The pleasure boats of a shinden garden came in a pair, one with a dragon head at the bow, the other a cock- or fish-hawk-head (*geki-shu*); Chinese (tōgaku) music was played on the dragon boat, Korean on the other [stated: Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, "Cock-and-dragon boats"]. Ours (`ryuto_boat`, `pier`): a pier on piles from the east shore at z = −30 and the pair moored either side of it, each 9 m, narrow, in red lacquer, a high curved prow with its gilt carved head, a four-post pavilion with a pyramid roof of bark over a mat, a pennant pole at the stern; four players seated in each under the pavilion with flute, lute, harp and drum [inferred: the size and form; the players' number].
- **People.** Courtiers (black cap and robe), ladies in layered robes under the veiled hat, monks with a staff, villagers in short jacket and trousers with the soft cap or straw hat, porters with a pole, farmers, children; in rain the straw hat and the palm-fibre cape (`movers.js`) [inferred]. They walk the gaps of the house grid, the road to the Uji Bridge, the bridge itself, the pond's east bank and south path. The river's boats stand (no moving boats: the river is crossed by the bridge's piles and the weirs).
- **Animals** (`fauna.js`): carp and red carp in the pond, dark carp in the river, swallows over the pond and the river, crows over the village, sparrows on the gravel, a heron and an egret at the shores [inferred]. **Smoke** (`smoke.js`): kitchen smoke from about two in five houses at the three meals and the villa's kitchen, incense before Amida in the hall, mist over river and pond at dawn [inferred].

## 15. Deepening pass (street network, trades, market, landing)

- **Streets and fronts.** The village is laid on fifteen runs of lane (`STREETS` in `layout.js`): the road to the Uji Bridge, the south road and the temple approach, the road along the river, cross-lanes; houses stand on both sides with their fronts to the lane, 5.6–12.5 m wide, about 2 m between eaves (filled with wattle fences), no empty cells in the core and thinning toward the rim [inferred: Uji was a river port and post stage on the Kyoto–Nara road; the plan is ours].
- **Trades.** Farmers, fishers (nets on poles, basket traps; ayu and ajiro fishing are attested for Uji's river, §10), weavers (hemp skeins, indigo vat, a loom inside), potters (jars on a rack), carpenters (planks, logs), porters (carrying poles, bundles), woodmen (brushwood and logs), brewers (rows of lidded jars), each with its eye-level dressing: straw rain cape and hat on pegs, bundles under the eave, plank bench, doorstep stone, damp at the foot and rain streaks on plaster, a lean-to on a third [inferred from Heian-period scrolls and the dossier's §11 sources].
- **Interiors.** The 90 houses nearest the arrival, the market and the temple's north road are open and furnished by trade; wide ones on the roads are inns with bedding rolls and bundles.
- **Market (ichi).** Two rows of cloth-roofed stalls and mats of goods on the south approach, sellers and buyers, an ox cart, a well [inferred; markets (ichi) stood at the crossings of the roads].
- **Landing.** A plank stage on piles on the west bank with mooring posts, a barge and two rowboats, washers kneeling at the water, sacks and baskets, rafts of timber floated down the Uji [inferred].
- **Precinct.** An earthen wall (tsuijibei) round the garden with gates on the south approach, the north and the west, the east open to the river [inferred].
- **Planting.** Persimmon and mulberry in the yards behind houses, willows and reeds on the river banks, as before pines and maples in the garden and cedar on the hills.
