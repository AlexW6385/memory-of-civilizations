## 1. The moment and the real place

- **Moment shown:** a day of divination in the reign of Wu Ding's successors, c. 1200 BC (the Late Shang, Yinxu phase II): the king's diviners are heating turtle plastrons in the court of the ancestral halls, the foundries south of the moat are casting, and the tomb of Fu Hao, Wu Ding's consort, has just been closed beneath its small hall. [stated: "The Late Shang period is dated c. 1250 to c. 1046 BCE"; Fu Hao's tomb "dated to 1250 BCE" — Wikipedia "Yinxu"] The date c. 1200 BC is our choice inside that span [inferred].
- **The place.** Yin was the last Shang capital, on both banks of the Huan river at modern Anyang; the palace-and-ancestral-temple zone lay at Xiaotun, on the south bank, in a bend of the river. [stated: Wikipedia "Yinxu"; chiculture.org.hk: "The palaces and ancestral temple were at Xiaotun village, on both sides of the Huan River"] The whole site is "about 30 square kilometers" along the river with a core of 414 ha. [stated: Wikipedia; ancient-history-sites.com]
- **Why this moment:** the first Chinese city known from its own writing: more than 100,000 oracle bones were recovered in 1928–37, about 150,000 in all, with more than 5,000 distinct signs. [stated: Wikipedia; ancient-history-sites.com]

## 3. Ground and climate

The alluvial plain of the Huan river at the foot of the Taihang: flat, loess-silt, the river on the north and east. [stated: general] The Shang climate was warmer and wetter than today, with elephants, rhinoceros and bamboo recorded in the oracle bones [inferred from general knowledge]. Millet, hemp, mulberry (silk), elm, jujube, willow and poplar by the water, reeds on the banks. Sky: monsoon summer, dry hazy winter; `damp` low (0.18).

## 4. The ancestral hall (the landmark)

- **Construction:** "Mostly timber posts, compacted-earth walls, and thatched roofs, raised on rammed-earth platforms." [stated: ancient-history-sites.com] The platforms were beaten in layers of 5–10 cm, 0.5–1.3 m high; posts stood on pebble or bronze-capped bases in rammed pits; walls of wattle and daub or rammed earth [inferred from the excavation reports].
- **Form:** a long hall of odd bays, 9 here (4 m each, 36 m), 3 deep, with a colonnade round it; a hipped thatched roof (the "four-sloped" *siā* of the Kaogongji's account of the Shang "double-eaved" hall *zhongwu*) [inferred]; the front wall open in the middle bays.
- **Inside:** the floor beaten and plastered, the walls plastered white with a red dado of cinnabar [inferred]; mats of reed; at the back the ancestral altar with its row of bronze vessels (*ding* tripods, *gui*, *jue* cups, *gu* beakers, *zun*, *lei*) on a low lacquered table; a stone chime (the tiger chime of Wuguancun, Yinxu); a drum with crocodile-skin heads; a bronze bell set; the king's mat and the diviners' mats; the fire and the bronze tongs for the plastron [inferred: bronzes and chime stated in finds; arrangement ours].
- **Divination:** a hole drilled or chiselled in the back of a turtle plastron or ox scapula, heated until it cracked; the crack read; the charge and the verdict later engraved beside it [stated: general; "oracle bones" — Wikipedia "Yinxu"].

## 5. The townscape

- **Compounds:** a main house of rammed foundation (0.4–0.6 m) with wattle walls and a thatched gable roof; beside it semi-subterranean pit houses (floor 0.6–1.0 m down, steps, a gabled thatch roof reaching nearly to the ground), storage pits, a well, a drying rack for hemp and millet, a pen for pigs. [inferred from the excavated house foundations; "rectangular" and rammed, chiculture.org.hk]
- **Foundry:** a yard of open sheds on posts under thatch; rows of piece-mould sections drying; clay-lined furnaces (crucible furnaces) with bellows; a pouring pit; charcoal; finished vessels cooling; the casters in short coats and bare arms [inferred; the section-mould method — Wikipedia: "The Houmuwu ding was cast using a section-mold process"].
- **Oracle-bone workshop and archive:** a shed where plastrons are sawn, scraped and drilled, stacks of shells and scapulae, bone saws and bronze drills; the covered archive pit with its stair [inferred].
- **Pit houses** — see Compounds; modelled bermed (floor at ground level, earth banked 0.75 m round three sides, the door in a low front wall, a gabled thatch roof reaching nearly to the ground) because the ground mesh is too coarse to sink a 4 m floor [inferred: a modelling choice]. **Chariot pits** beside Fu Hao's tomb: six, each a chariot and two horses [stated].

## 6. People

Kings, diviners (*zhen* men), officials, soldiers, bronze-casters, potters, bone-workers, farmers, women weaving hemp and silk. Dress: the short-skirted robe or jacket with a sash and trousers or a long skirt, closed to the right, hair in a topknot or bun held with a bone or jade pin [inferred from jade figures of Fu Hao's tomb]. Hemp cloth in brown, ochre and grey; the great in red and black silk.

## 7. Animals and sound

Oxen, horses (two-horse chariots), pigs, dogs, chickens; swallows, crows, kites; fish in the Huan river; elephants and rhinoceros are in the records but not shown [inferred]. Sound: bronze bells and the stone chime in the halls, drums, the ocarina (*xun*) in a pentatonic mode; in the foundry quarter hammering and the bellows; the river; cicadas in summer [inferred]. Music: tonic 50, scale 0 3 5 7 10 (gong, zhi, yu), the drone of the bells, a harp stand-in for the stone chime.

## 5a. Deepening pass: the dressed town (second pass)

- **Lots fill their cells.** The lineage compounds now run to the lane's edge (28 × 26 m lots in 34 × 32 m cells, lanes 5 m), so the fronts are continuous: wattle fences with posts and thatch caps, gateways of two tall posts with a hood, or the wattle house fronts themselves on the lane. [inferred: "above-ground courtyard houses stood among storage or refuse pits, wells, drainage features, streets, and cemeteries" — dailylifehistory.pages.dev, Anyang c. 1200 BCE; the packing is ours]
- **House types by lot.** `court`: main house on a rammed foundation at the back with a porch of posts on stone bases, two wing houses facing the yard, pit houses by the gate, granary, well, hearth, garden, pen, a tree; `pits`: pit houses round a hearth yard; `row`: three wattle houses on the lane with an alley to a yard behind; `workshop`: a shed over benches of the craft. [inferred from the excavated plans]
- **Pit houses.** "Semi-subterranean (pit) houses had one to five rooms, and one-room houses were most common … roofed areas ranged from about 5 to 25 square meters … earthen benches, hearths, wall niches, and pottery were found inside." [stated: dailylifehistory.pages.dev, on Xiaomintun] One pit house in each pit yard (and one of the casters') is enterable: earth bench to sleep on, hearth, jars, bundles hung from the rafters.
- **Crops and animals.** "Foxtail and broomcorn millet formed the dietary base"; pigs, cattle, sheep or goats, dogs and deer are in the bone record; horses in chariot burials only. [stated: same] So: ridged beds of millet, hemp and beans (hemp is our addition from the textile traces, [inferred]); shocks of millet; pigs in the pens, dogs and hens in the yards, oxen, horses only with the chariots.
- **Crafts in the dwelling quarter.** "Some residential areas doubled as workspaces, with craft debris linked to bronze, bone, pottery, stone, or shell working"; the Tiesanlu bone workshop made standardized pins, awls and arrowheads; spindle whorls and needles attest textile work. [stated: same] Hence looms in the houses, grindstones, bone blanks leaned on the workshop fences.
- **Weathering.** Plaster walls with a splashed foot of earth and rain streaks under the eaves; the ridge of the thatch as a dark bound bundle; soot and ash about the furnaces; wet mud at the water troughs. [inferred]
- **Court of the ancestral halls.** Braziers with plastrons before the diviners, two great dings on rammed plinths, standards with pennants, drums on stands, tripod lamps along the axis, the two chariots parked at the court's sides with their teams (horses attested in chariot burials), stone bases of a vanished colonnade, a stone drain. [inferred; the ding and the chariot team from the finds]
- **Hall interior.** Cinnabar dado, a frieze of red and black squares, tie-beams and a ridge-beam in the roof, tablets of the ancestors on the altar, rows of mats for the officials, tables of food vessels, wine jars on the walls, banners on the posts. [inferred]

## 5b. The Huan with its people

Landings of planks on piles at intervals along both reaches, each with a net hung to dry, a rack of split carp, wicker traps, a washing stone, stakes of a weir; dugout canoes and reed rafts moored at them and one hauled up on the bank; women washing, fishers, ducks, egrets. Boats and fishing are not described in the sources read (the text names the Huan only as organising movement through the settlement); they are [inferred] from the river itself, the carp of the oracle inscriptions and the dugout tradition.
