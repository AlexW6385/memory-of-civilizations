## 1. The real example

- **St Peter's:** Michelangelo designed the dome and the Greek-cross plan, from 1547. Giacomo della Porta finished the dome in 1590. Carlo Maderno added the nave and the façade between 1607 and 1614. The church was consecrated in 1626. The dome rises 136.6 m to the top of the cross [stated: Wikipedia "St. Peter's Basilica"].
- **The piazza:** Bernini laid it out between 1656 and 1667. It has two parts: a trapezoid before the church and an oval about 240 m across. The oval is held by colonnades four columns deep, with 140 statues of saints on top. The Egyptian obelisk was set at the centre in 1586. The fountains are Maderno's (1613) and a twin of 1675 [stated: Wikipedia "St. Peter's Square"].
- **Castel Sant'Angelo:** Hadrian's mausoleum, later made a papal fortress. It is joined to the Vatican by the Passetto di Borgo, a raised wall-walk [stated: Wikipedia "Castel Sant'Angelo"; "Passetto di Borgo"].
- **The Borgo:** the quarter between the Vatican and the river. Its two streets, the Borgo Vecchio and the Borgo Nuovo, ran either side of the "spina" of houses, until the Via della Conciliazione replaced them in 1936–1950 [stated: Wikipedia "Borgo (rione of Rome)"; "Via della Conciliazione"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1670, with the colonnade just finished.

## 4. The style

Travertine walls, lead roofs and a ribbed lead dome; Tuscan columns in the colonnade; statues on the attics. [stated]

**The piazza's sculpture and water (L2–L3, built).**
- *The obelisk:* 25.31 m high on a base 8.25 m wide, re-erected by Domenico Fontana for Sixtus V in 1586; it rests on four couchant bronze lions, each with two bodies whose tails intertwine, on a high pedestal; on top a bronze emblem of Sixtus with a relic of the Cross, the cross above [stated: stpetersbasilica.info]; 41 m with the pedestal [stated: aviewoncities]. Ours: steps, plinth, die with framed panels and cornice, the lions with one head at each corner and a body along each side, the emblem as the three mounts and the star of the Peretti arms [inferred], the cross.
- *The fountain:* Maderno's of 1614 — an octagonal base with dragons and Borghese eagles, the old granite lower basin reused, all set in a much larger, slightly oval basin with a multilinear profile; height about 8 m, upper basin about 16 m round [stated: vatican.va]. Bernini rebuilt it a few metres forward in line with the obelisk (1667); its twin was inaugurated in 1677 [stated: vatican.va; aviewoncities] — so at our date only the north fountain stood; the twin is shown as in the existing plan [inferred: a known anachronism, left for the owner to decide]. Ours: the lobed basin, the octagonal pedestal, the granite bowl, the upturned bowl, the water falling from both rims in streams [inferred: the form from photographs].
- *The colonnade:* four rows, 284 Doric columns and 88 pilasters, finished 1660; 140 statues by Bernini and his pupils on top [stated: aviewoncities]. Ours: a balustrade along the inner edge of the entablature and a saint on a pedestal over every bay, in five poses with a cross, a book, a palm or a staff [inferred: the attributes].
- *Ponte Sant'Angelo:* five arches, the three middle ones Roman [stated: ancient-history-sites]; ours three great arches and a small one at each end, segmental because our banks are low [inferred]. In 1669 Clement IX had Bernini design a new parapet; ten angels with the instruments of the Passion, commissioned 1667, were installed in 1670 — the column, the scourges, the crown of thorns, the veil, the garment and dice, the nails, the cross, the title, the sponge, the lance; at the end opposite the castle, Peter with the keys and Paul with the sword (1535) [stated: ancient-history-sites]. Ours: travertine balustrades between pedestals [stated: the balustrades], the angels five a side facing across the road [inferred: placing], Peter and Paul at the city end.
- *The Vatican ranges:* the palace as stuccoed storeys over a travertine base with framed windows under alternating pediments and a crowning cornice; the Sistine Chapel as a plain brick hall with its row of tall round-headed windows [inferred: from views; replaces the Florentine palace block used before].

**Maderno's front (L2–L3, built).** Designed by Carlo Maderno and built 1607–14 (first stone February 1608), of travertine; 114.69 m wide and 45.55 m high [stated: stpetersbasilica.info] (116 m and 45.50 m without the statues [stated: vatican.va]). Eight columns [stated: stpetersbasilica.info], Corinthian, as are the pilasters; the columns 27.48 m high and 2.77 m across; the pediment 30 m long and 7.5 m high [stated: vatican.va]. In the lower order five entrances to the atrium with rectangular windows over them, the central window replaced by Buonvicino's high relief of Christ giving the keys; in the upper order five balconies with small columns, balustrades, pediments and brackets, interrupted by four smaller niches [stated: vatican.va]; the central balcony the Loggia of the Blessings [stated: stpetersbasilica.info]. The inscription to Paul V on the entablature, its letters about a metre high [stated: stpetersbasilica.info]: IN HONOREM PRINCIPIS APOST PAVLVS V BVRGHESIVS ROMANVS PONT MAX AN MDCXII PONT VII [inferred: the wording as commonly transcribed]. The attic with eight square windows framed by small pilasters under the balustrade; on it thirteen travertine statues, Christ the Redeemer (6 m, with a cross topped in copper), St John the Baptist and eleven apostles (5.65 m), 1612–14, by Ambrogio Buonvicino and others [stated: vatican.va; stpetersbasilica.info]. Valadier's two clocks in the arches of the unbuilt bell towers are of 1790 [stated] and not shown; Bernini narrowed the front steps after 1667 [stated: vatican.va]; under Alexander VII he made a "great sloping entrance" to the basilica's threshold, and the statues of Peter and Paul, set at the foot of the marble staircase of the old basilica under Pius II (1458–64) and moved forward when the front was finished in 1612, stood at the foot of the stairs (replaced in 1847) [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, St Peter statue]. Ours: the church's floor 3 m above the piazza on a travertine socle with a 0.6 m plinth round all its walls; the sagrato 9 m deep along the whole front; three flights of six steps (rise 1/6 m, tread 0.42 m) 68 m wide with landings of 2.4 m, cheek walls with copings, balustrades at the landing's ends; the old Peter (keys) and Paul (sword) on pedestals at the foot [inferred: the height, the step count and the widths, from photographs]. Ours: the composition scaled by 1.055 so that the balustrade stands at the block's 48 m [inferred]; the eight columns paired about the four niche bays, ±5.8, ±10.2, ±20.8 and ±25.2 m from the axis, pilasters beyond them at ±35.8 and ±40.2 m and at the ends, the end bays with the arches of the towers' feet [inferred: the rhythm from photographs]; the middle (±12.6 m) breaking forward 1.5 m under the pediment; each column on its pedestal with an Attic base, a tapering shaft and a capital of two rows of acanthus with corner volutes; the entablature of three fasciae, the frieze with the inscription in raised dark letters (a stroke alphabet of Roman capitals, in three parts so that no letter crosses the break), modillions under the cornice; the balconies on brackets with balusters, small columns at their windows and pediments triangular and segmental by turns; a stone of the papal arms in the pediment; Christ and the twelve on pedestals over the supports [inferred].

**Inside St Peter's (L2–L3, built).** The Baldacchino: designed by Bernini in 1624 for Urban VIII and finished in 1633, its twisted columns recalling the "Holy Column"; bronze drapes joined to the columns, curved ribs, palm branches, angels that seem to bear the structure, Urban's bees throughout, a cross on a gilded globe at the top [stated: Vatican News 2024]. The Altar of the Chair: commissioned by Alexander VII, composed by Bernini 1657–66 [stated: Vatican News] (1647–53 in smarthistory's listing; either way before our date); a great bronze throne enclosing the old wooden chair, with reliefs of "Feed my sheep", the Washing of the Feet and the Delivery of the Keys; flanked by Ambrose and Augustine (the West) and Athanasius and John Chrysostom (the East), who seem to lift it; above, the dove of the Holy Spirit in light amid clouds and flying angels [stated: Vatican News]; the dove in a stained-glass window, golden rays, gilded bronze, gilded stucco and glass; behind the Baldacchino on the same axis, in the apse [stated: smarthistory]. The nave's coffered barrel vault, the crossing's four piers with their niches and statues and the balconies above them, the drum's band with TV ES PETRVS ET SVPER HANC PETRAM AEDIFICABO ECCLESIAM MEAM ET TIBI DABO CLAVES REGNI CAELORVM, the gold of the pendentives and the dome [inferred: from the building as it is; not fetched here]. Ours: the arms 27.5 m wide under barrel vaults springing at 30 m, coffered with gilt ribs and rosettes; the nave of five bays with paired pilasters, gilt capitals, the entablature on modillions, an arch in every bay to a chapel 5.5 m deep with its altar and altarpiece; the inner front with Filarete's bronze door (1445, from the old basilica) open, the portico beyond it with its coffered ceiling; the crossing's arches 21 m from its centre and the piers' faces between them, each with a niche, a colossal saint (Longinus with his lance, Helena with the cross) and a relic balcony between two twisted columns; the pendentives as the sphere through the arches' crowns and the piers' corners, in gold mosaic with a roundel each; the drum 42 m across with the inscription in blue letters on gold, sixteen windows between paired pilasters; the dome in gold mosaic with sixteen gilt ribs and three rings, the oculus with the lantern's light; the Baldacchino on its platform — marble plinths with the Barberini arms in coloured marble, four twisted bronze columns 11 m high wound with gilt laurel, the entablature and the valance with its tassels, an angel on each corner, the four scrolls rising to the gilt globe and the cross; short transepts ending in altars under columns and a pediment (the transepts' apses since opened, below); the west arm ending in a flat wall instead of the apse, with the Cathedra — the altar, the four Doctors in bronze on pedestals of coloured marble (the two in front mitred), the gilt throne above their hands, the oval window of alabaster (topaz glass) with the gilt dove, the gilt sunburst, forty rays, clouds and cherubs, the glory breaking through the cornice [inferred: simplifications for the model: the aisles' domed bays are shown as chapels; the other altars not yet placed].

**The coloured marbles and the sculpture of the nave (L3, built).** Innocent X commissioned Bernini in 1645 to complete the pilasters and chapels; the nave is bordered by pairs of massive piers with Corinthian pilaster strips, between them niches (their 39 statues of founders date from the early 18th century, so the niches are shown empty); Bernini's workshop made the cherubs, the doves and the papal symbols, the tiara and the keys; sixteen Virtues line the walls, eight a side [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Nave]. Bernini replaced the earlier flat, multicoloured incrustation with sculpture: white marble medallions of sainted popes on gilded grounds, set against polychrome marble pilaster surfaces, borne aloft by pairs of winged putti with the tiara and the keys, marked throughout by Innocent's emblem, the dove with an olive branch in its beak; the Virtues recline on the arches of the arcade like Victories on a triumphal arch [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Bernini at St Peter's ch. 4]; the medallions of 56 early popes run on the piers of the nave and the sides of the chapels [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Pope medallions]. The pavement: begun by Giacomo della Porta, continued by Maderno and completed by Bernini, "a succession of geometric games", in white Carrara, green and red porphyry, portasanta, broccatello, Sicilian jasper and other marbles [stated: michelangelobuonarrotietornato.com, 2025]; a great disc of red porphyry from the old basilica near the entrance, where Charlemagne knelt to be crowned in 800 [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Nave]. Ours: the pilasters of grey-white veined marble (proconnesian), fluted (eight flutes between fillets) on pedestals with Attic bases, the capitals gilt with two rows of acanthus, corner volutes, the flower and the abacus; the piers' faces within each arch (the chapels' sides) revetted — a green dado, a red field (africano shown as breccia) in a giallo antico frame with a green fillet — each with two medallions: a white profile on a gilt ground in a white ring, two winged putti bearing it, the tiara of three gilt crowns and the crossed keys above, the dove with the olive branch below; between the paired pilasters two arched niches lined in green with red shells and gilt rays, framed in giallo, and framed panels; on each arch two reclining Virtues; the transept walls in bays of fluted pilasters with coloured fields, niches and medallions; the pavement in each bay a great roundel (a giallo rim, a red ring, white, a giallo ring, a green heart with a porphyry eye), green lozenges and giallo bands between the bays, porphyry discs at the sides, green borders; the crossing ringed in giallo, red and green with sixteen rays; the porphyry disc inside the middle door [inferred: the composition, the colours and the counts, from photographs; the palette has no africano].
- *The bronze St Peter:* seated, in the philosopher's stole, the keys held at the breast in the left hand, the right raised in blessing, on an early-Renaissance marble seat, against the pier of St Longinus; late 13th century, attributed to Arnolfo di Cambio; the alabaster base is of 1757 and the mosaic behind of 1871 [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, St Peter statue] — so ours stands on a plain pedestal of coloured marble, before the pilaster at the nave's end by the Longinus pier [inferred: its place in 1670 is not given by the source].
- *The tombs flanking the Cathedra:* Urban VIII's (Bernini, finished 1647), flanking the tribune on the right: the pope in dark, partly gilded bronze blessing on a tall white marble pedestal, the black marble sarcophagus with bronze ornaments, Death as a skeleton writing the name on a scroll, Charity with children and Justice with sword and scales in white marble [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Urban VIII]. Paul III's (Guglielmo della Porta), moved by Bernini in 1628 to the left of the Altar of the Chair to balance it: the seated bronze pope crowning a pyramidal monument, Justice and Prudence reclining below, a base of africano marble and a mask of giallo and black [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Paul III]. Ours: each in an arched niche lined in red and framed in giallo on the west arm's side walls (the apse itself is not opened: the Cathedra's glory rises above our springing), Urban's on the north (the right as one faces the Cathedra), Paul's on the south [inferred: placing].
- *The transept apses (opened):* each transept runs on to a half-cylinder apse of its own width under a coffered half-dome with gilt ribs and rosettes, the entablature running round, fluted pilasters and niches on the curve, the altar with its columns, pediment and altarpiece at the back [inferred: the form from the building as it is].
- *Not shown, later than 1670:* the holy-water stoups with the giant putti (designed by Agostino Cornacchini, 1722–25; the putti by Moderati and De Rossi, the basins by Lironi) [stated: michelangelobuonarrotietornato.com, 2019]; the founders' statues in the niches (18th century); the mosaic copies of the altarpieces (the altarpieces stay paintings) [inferred: the mosaics replaced the paintings in the 18th century]. Noted for the owner: the nave vault's coffers are said to have been added under Pius VI in the late 18th century [stated: stpetersbasilica.info, Nave] — the existing coffered vault is left as it is.

## 5. The town

The Borgo's stuccoed houses of three or four storeys with tiled roofs, between the two Borgo streets and in the lanes. [stated: the houses; their number ours — inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): an osteria and two homes, furnished as in Florence; a step up from the street, the door leaf open inside, the ceiling's beams overhead [inferred: the period's furnishing of such rooms].

**Coaches, a sedan chair and pilgrims (L3, built: `src/styles/baroque/street.js`, `src/settlements/baroque-rome/street.js`).** Jubilees came every 25 years and brought pilgrims in great numbers: some 400,000 in the year after the 1574 opening, when the city had about 80,000 people, and some 536,000 in 1600; a print of Bertelli shows the pilgrim with his staff over his shoulder [stated: History Today "Pilgrims and Poverty in Renaissance Rome"]. Ours (1670, between the Holy Years of 1650 and 1675): groups of pilgrims about the obelisk, the fountains and the foot of the steps, with the staff, the broad felt hat and the short cape (the pilgrim's pelerine), some veiled women, a few kneeling toward the church; a file walking in from the Borgo; three beggars sitting on the ground by the colonnade; a seller of rosaries and medals at a stall; pigeons, a dog [inferred: dress from period prints in general, not one checked here]. A cardinal's coach waiting before the steps with its coachman and two footmen in red livery, an ambassador's painted coach crossing the oval with grey horses, a coach in the Borgo Nuovo: each a body swelling at the foot, hung on four leather braces from the standards of a long perch carriage, hind wheels 1.56 m and fore wheels 1.04 m across, the coachman's box under a fringed hammercloth, the footmen's board behind, a leather roof with gilt vases, gilt mouldings, curtains at the windows, velvet seats, the pole and traces to a pair of horses [inferred: after Roman gala coaches of the later 17th century, generalised]. A closed sedan chair set down on its poles near the steps, its two bearers beside it [inferred].

**The worn steps (L3, built: `sagrato.js`).** Each of Bernini's front steps worn in the middle 24 m of the flight, where most people climb: the tread dished 2–3.2 cm (deeper on the lower flights) and the nosing rounded back, the ends of the flight left sharp [inferred].

**The Borgo's fronts (L3, built: `src/styles/baroque/borgo.js`, rows in `layout.js`).** East of the oval, three rows of palazzi and narrow houses replace the generic lots: the spina's two rows back to back with a 3 m alley between them, facing the Borgo Nuovo (north) and the Borgo Vecchio (south), and the Vecchio's south side; two lanes cross the spina, one leads south [inferred: the number and place; the real Borgo ran 900 m]. The vocabulary is that of Roman palazzi of the Cinquecento and Seicento: a rusticated ground storey with a round portal whose voussoirs are rusticated, a keystone, and a balcony over it on corbels; kneeling windows (finestre inginocchiate) with iron grilles and small mezzanine windows over them; a piano nobile with tall windows under alternating triangular and segmental pediments; plainer, lower upper storeys; a modillion cornice; a roof loggia (altana) on some [stated: general Roman palace typology, Wikipedia "Palazzo", "Roman architecture" survey; Palazzo Farnese/Barberini are the type; no single palace modelled — inferred]. The houses: a ground-floor bottega under a round stone arch whose lower leaves fold down as a counter, a door to the stair beside it, shuttered windows (persiane/scuri) open or closed, pots and iron balconettes, a hanging painted sign [inferred]. Ours: 3–4 storeys of 5.0/5.4/4.6/3.4 m (palazzi) and 3.9/3.4/3.2/2.9 m (houses), walls in the five stuccoed ochres and rose of the palette, travertine dressings, shutters in green, brown and grey, widths 17–24 m for a palazzo and 7–10 m for a house, odd bay counts (3–7) so the portal stands in the middle bay [inferred].
- *Madonnelle:* painted images of the Virgin set in niches at the corners and storey lines of Roman streets, with a lantern and flowers, a mark of the popular devotion of Rome from the 16th century; the lanterns lit by votive oil [stated: general; Wikipedia "Edicola sacra" and "Madonnella"]. Ours: on about two in five houses, a framed blue field with the Virgin and a gilt halo, a pedimented travertine head, a shelf with two pots and a candle, an iron bracket with a glass lantern [inferred].
- *Wall fountains:* Rome's streets were watered by wall fountains (mostre) of travertine fed by the restored aqueducts: a niche, a mascherone spouting into a trough [stated: Wikipedia "Fountains in Rome"; Sixtus V and Paul V restored the Acqua Felice and Acqua Paola]. Ours: in two houses a scallop-headed niche with a pale marble field, a bronze mask, a falling jet and a travertine trough, two women at it [inferred: form generalised].
- *Laundry:* washing hung on ropes across alleys and lanes from the windows of facing houses, moving in the wind [inferred: the common Italian street sight; period prints show it].
- *Botteghe to enter:* the first house of the north row and of the Vecchio's south side open their shop: a barrel-vaulted room, a counter across it with scales, four shelves of jars, bottles and cheeses along the back (bolts of cloth in the draper's), barrels and sacks, hams hung from an iron rail, a lantern, the keeper behind the counter and a customer before it [inferred].
- *Life:* Romans in the black of the seicento (ferraiolo, broad hat), artisans in apron and cap, veiled women, secular priests in black, Capuchins in brown, a monsignore in violet and a rare cardinal in red, pilgrims with staff, porters with the pole, boys; swallows and swifts over the roofs, pigeons on the paving, kitchen smoke and bakers' ovens at meals [inferred; Capuchin dress and Roman clerical colours stated by general sources: Wikipedia "Ecclesiastical dress", "Capuchins"].

## 6. Ground and water

The piazza is paved in travertine and the streets are cobbled. The Tiber's banks were unembanked in 1670 (the embankments date from after 1870), so they are muddy here. The Vatican gardens rise to the west, with umbrella pines and cypresses. [stated: the embankments; inferred: the planting]
