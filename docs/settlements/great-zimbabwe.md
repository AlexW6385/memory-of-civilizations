Settlement id: `great-zimbabwe` · Style: `zimbabwe` · Region: Zimbabwe (the plateau) · Code: `src/settlements/great-zimbabwe/`

## 1. The real example

- **The Great Enclosure:** an elliptical outer wall about 250 m round, up to 11 m high and 5 m thick, of mortarless granite blocks, with a chevron pattern near its top on the north-east. An inner wall forms a narrow parallel passage, and a solid conical tower about 10 m high stands inside [stated: Wikipedia "Great Zimbabwe"].
- **The Hill Complex:** walls built among the granite boulders of the hill to the north [stated: Wikipedia].
- **The Valley Ruins:** lie between the two [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Houses:** daga (clay) houses with conical thatched roofs [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Moment shown:** about 1350, the height of the city.

## 10. Detail added (L2–L3)

- **Conical tower:** "nearly 10 metres high", "plain and has sloping sides swelling slightly"; "the top of the Conical Tower once had a dentelle pattern which is now gone" [stated: Zimbabwe Field Guide]. 5.5 m in diameter, 9 m high [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours: 5.6 m across, 10 m, a slight swell in the batter, the dentelle course (blocks set with gaps) between two plain courses 0.9 m below the flat top [position of the band inferred].
- **Platform:** beside the tower "a platform 7.6 metres (25 ft) across, the largest built at Great Zimbabwe" [stated: Zimbabwe Field Guide]. Ours: a round, battered stone platform with a daga top and a step.
- **Eastern Enclosure and the birds:** eight soapstone birds, carved "from a micaceous schist (soapstone) on the tops of monoliths the height of a person"; slots in a platform in the Eastern Enclosure appear designed to hold monoliths; their original positions cannot be determined [stated: Wikipedia, Great Zimbabwe]. Birds about 40 cm tall on columns over 90 cm; Bent recorded six large and two small [stated: Wikipedia, Zimbabwe Bird]. Ours: an enclosure wall at the hill's east end, a stone platform, eight birds (two smaller) on columns with a raised zigzag, folded wings, legs with toes on a perch ring, lips rather than a beak; six plain granite monoliths behind. Row order, zigzag and the birds' features from museum photographs [inferred].
- **Boulders:** granite boulders among the Hill Complex walls (18, 3–8 m), set into the slope clear of the walls [stated: walls among boulders; placement inferred].
- **Daga houses:** "Dhaka once formed interior platforms and floors of huts"; courtyards paved with dhaka "often with raised kerbs" [stated: Zimbabwe Field Guide]. Ours: each house on a kerbed daga floor, the wall a little battered, a doorway with a raised sill and lintel, the thatch in two lifts; the larger houses with a veranda of eight posts under the eave [veranda inferred from Shona houses].

## 11. The Hill Complex (2026-11-06)

Source: Zimbabwe Field Guide, "The Great Zimbabwe Hill Complex in photographs" [stated items], Smarthistory "Great Zimbabwe".
- **Western Enclosure:** "bounded by two curved walls over 30 feet (9 metres) high, the most substantial erection on the hill"; the Great West Wall "nearly 8 metres high and 4.2 metres wide at their widest"; "capped by turrets and monoliths" — Mabel Bent photographed seven turrets, four remain; the floor levelled with dhaka, deposits 4.3 m deep, eleven hut floors, huts "just a metre apart"; a second original entrance at the south end of the West Wall [stated]. Ours: an enclosure 48 × 34 m at the hill's west end (`layout.js` WEST); the Great West Wall on its west side 8.5 m high and 4.2 m thick with four small turrets on its outer half and monoliths standing along its northern part; the second curved wall (7 m, 2.6 m thick) round the north and east, joining it; the entrance on the south between the two walls' rounded ends; the floor levelled in the ground's height (the hill's undulation blended out over the enclosure); six daga houses on it about a metre apart, their doors toward the court [house count and placement inferred].
- **Recess Enclosure:** "four slots built within the walls that may have held wooden posts" carrying a thatched roof; perhaps a gathering place or small shrine [stated]. Ours: a small walled space (14 × 11 m) open to the north, four posts standing in its walls, a ring beam and a low cone of thatch over it (seen from beneath too) [form of the roof inferred].
- **Ancient Ascent:** "The remaining 20 metre portion that rises through the narrow cleft" is probably original [stated]. Ours: granite steps (42 cm going) up the south face from the valley to the ridge, low dry-stone walls either side on the steep part, passing between two boulders below the top and through a gap in the ridge wall [route inferred].
- **Eastern Enclosure:** probably terraced to hold "groups of circular stone platforms" that carried monoliths; its south wall "once capped by a dentelle-patterned frieze" [stated]. Ours: three round stone platforms on the slope, each with a monolith, beside the bird platform; a dentelle frieze (a plain course, teeth with gaps, a plain course) near the top of the wall's southern part.
- Not built: the Balcony (three walls on a great boulder), the covered stairway with its twist, the narrow passages among the small enclosures, the cave.

## 12. The Valley Ruins (2026-11-06)

The Valley Ruins "include a number of structures that offer evidence that the site served as a hub for commercial exchange and long distance trade" — Chinese porcelain, south-east Asian beads, copper ingots [stated: Smarthistory]; houses of daga with interior platforms and floors, courtyards paved with dhaka "often with raised kerbs" [stated: Zimbabwe Field Guide, §10]. Ours (`layout.js` VALLEY): four walled enclosures, each with its own entrance, a court paved with daga and kerbed round its edge, three daga houses set opposite the entrance with their doors to the court; the scattered houses of the valley no longer stand in or on the enclosures' walls [arrangement inferred]. Trade goods not yet placed.

## 13. A fix: the walls seen from inside (2026-11-06)

The dry walls' faces were turned by the centre of the whole wall, so the inner face of every curved wall (the Great Enclosure's included) faced away and was invisible from inside. Each face is now turned by its own side of the wall (outer, top, inner); the chevron's blocks each by their own centre.

## 14. Inside the houses, cattle and the coast trade (L3, 2026-11-07)

- **The furnished daga house.** A Zimbabwean kitchen house has "a central hearth where cooking fire was ignited"; "The hearth comes in the form of a shallow depression within which are lodged three hearth stones", a pot set over them; the interior walls carry shelves for plates and cups [stated: Herald, "Iziko: linking the hearth and human sexuality"]. A traditional kitchen holds "a short 'rukuva' (a seeming round hut curved rectangular prism bench)" about half a metre high, on it clay pots "on top of each other"; "'rukukwe' (a traditional sitting or sleeping reed woven mat)"; "'guyo nehuyo'", the lower and upper grinding stones [stated: Herald, "Inside women's traditional space" — a modern rural kitchen shown in the National Gallery]. Ours (`daga_hut` with `open`): the daga wall 0.22 m thick with a real doorway (lintel at 2.05 m), the hearth's kerbed hollow with ash, three stones, a cooking pot and sticks, the fire glowing between the stones; the rukuva moulded against the back wall, three pairs of pots on it; the mat, the grinding stones, a water pot on its grass ring inside the door; the thatch seen from within blackened by smoke, fourteen rafters and two rings of withies. That the 14th-century houses were furnished as the ethnographic kitchen is [inferred]. All the houses of the Western Enclosure and the Valley Ruins and one in six in the valley are open and furnished.
- **Cattle.** "Cattle, in particular, were a key source of wealth and could also be used as draft power" [stated: Met, "Great Zimbabwe"]. Ours (`life.js`): four herds of six to nine head (humpless long-horned and humped beasts, for the Sanga type) grazing in the open grass, each with a herd boy and his stick; three kraals of close-set rough posts bound by withy rails, a gate with bars laid by it, the trodden, dunged ground inside, four beasts in each [kraal form inferred from Shona homesteads].
- **The coast trade.** The Valley Ruins held Chinese porcelain, south-east Asian beads and copper ingots [stated: Smarthistory]; glass beads, porcelain, gold foil, and crucibles used "to melt gold, copper, bronze, and brass" [stated: Met]. Ours (`trade_goods`): in the first Valley court, on a mat, coils of blue, green and yellow glass beads, two celadon bowls and a white one, a crosswise stack of cast copper bars, coils of gold wire, two iron hoes, a crucible; a trader in a white cloth sitting by them [the ingots' form and the arrangement inferred].
- **People.** Women kneeling at the grinding stones and carrying water pots in the Valley courts; a man in a hide cape and a woman with a basket in the Western Enclosure; two men in the Great Enclosure [inferred].

## 15. Walls, homesteads and the living valley (2026-11-11)

Sources: Zimbabwe Field Guide, "The Great Enclosure in photographs" (zimfieldguide.com/node/710); Ebsco Research Starters, "Great Zimbabwe Urbanism and Architecture".
- **Coursing.** Granite "splits fairly easily into parallel face slabs", slabs about 3 to 8 inches thick, laid without mortar, "each layer slightly recessed in comparison to the successive layers below"; the eastern end of the Great Outer Wall has "the most regular stone coursing achieved at Great Zimbabwe" [stated]. Ours: every dry wall's faces step in a little at each 0.42 m course (a stair, not a plane), so the treads take light and shadow; the chevron and dentelle bands follow the stair [recess per course inferred from the total batter].
- **Drains.** "Six original drainage outlets in the north-east side of the Great Outer Wall", five roofed with stone [stated]. Ours: six outlets along the NE stretch of the outer wall, each a dark opening between jamb stones under a lintel slab.
- **Weathering.** The field guide notes weathering and loss of the dhaka finishes; lichen is not mentioned [lichen inferred: grey-green and a few yellow-orange crustose patches on the granite, more on the lower courses]. Ours: flat patches on single courses of both faces of every wall.
- **Homesteads.** Commoners' daga dwellings formed an outer ring round the royal compounds (Ebsco); huts of "pole and dhaka"; Hall's work destroyed "store huts and grain bins" in the enclosure (zimfieldguide) [stated]. Ours: 78 compounds in the valley, each a low rubble wall (about 1.1 m) round a yard with a gate, two to four daga houses on a ring with doors to the yard, a granary on stones between two of them, firewood and two seats; a few houses stand free in the Great Enclosure [form of the compound and granary inferred from later Shona homesteads]. Thatch is bound with two withy rings; open houses gain a daga shelf with bowls over the rukuva [shelf stated: Herald, §14].
- **Granary.** A plastered drum on a ring of stones and a pole floor, a deep-eaved thatch roof, a small high door with a notched ladder pole [inferred].
- **Life.** Women with pots and baskets, men in hide capes, herd boys, traders on paths from every gate to the Great Enclosure's entrance, the Valley courts and the Ascent; swallows, crows over the hill, sparrows on the compounds; hearth smoke from a share of the houses at the morning and evening meals [inferred].
- Not built: the Balcony, the covered stairway, the cave; no new materials were needed (lichen uses plaster_damp and paint_ochre).
