# Mughal Agra, 1632–1653 — dossier

Settlement id: `mughal-agra` · Style: `mughal` · Region: India (the Yamuna plain) · Code: `src/settlements/mughal-agra/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the Taj Mahal complex: riverfront terrace, plinth, mausoleum, minarets, mosque and jawab, charbagh with channels and pool, walls and towers, great gate, forecourt; the town of Taj Ganj; ground, planting, weather, music). ## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Taj Mahal, the mausoleum of Mumtaz Mahal on the southern bank of the Yamuna at Agra, built 1632–1653 for Shah Jahan [stated: Wikipedia; visiearth]; the complex about 580 × 300 m with a garden of 300 × 300 m [stated: archetravel].
- **Moment shown:** completed, 1653.

## 3. Orientation

The axis runs north–south: the tomb on the river at the north end, the gate on the south. [stated]

## 4. The style

White Makrana marble for the tomb [stated: visiearth]; red sandstone for the terrace, walls, gate, mosque and jawab; bulbous double dome with lotus crown and brass finial [stated: dream-it24]; chhatris; minarets leaning slightly outward [stated: Wikipedia] — ours stand straight.

**The tomb chamber inside (L3, built).** An octagon of 7.3 m sides, entered from each face in design but only by the south door from the garden; the walls about 25 m high under a false inner dome with a sun motif [stated: middleheight; thetilesofindia]. Double-height, each face with two storeys of niches; the niches on the axes have jali screens on the outer face that let in light; muqarnas at the upper level turn the octagon toward the dome's ring [stated: Archnet]. The dado frames and spandrels carry pietra dura of yellow marble, jasper and jade [stated: slideshare after Koch]. The octagonal marble screen was set up in 1643 in place of the first one of enamelled gold; each side has three panels, one opening to the cenotaph [stated: tajmahal.gov.in; Archnet]. Mumtaz's cenotaph is exactly in the centre of the hall [stated: tajmahal.gov.in]; Shah Jahan's was added after his death (1666), so in 1653 only hers stands [inferred from the dates]. Ours: walls to 20 m and a flat ring to the inner dome (our mausoleum is a little smaller); the axial niches glazed with light behind a marble lattice; carved dado panels with flower bosses; inlay of red, green, gold and blue stones in the spandrels; the screen as a grid of slender bars between posts; a vestibule from the south iwan's door to the chamber.

**The screen and the cenotaph (third pass, built).** "The screen is octagonal. Each side of the octagon is divided into three by marble frames"; "The corners are fortified by posts ending in kalasha finials"; the frames are filled with jalis — the only ones in the Taj pierced in plant arabesques rather than geometric forms; it is "topped with ornamental crenellations, kanguras", of vase-shaped elements and acanthus volutes; its flowers are "inlaid in semi-precious stones"; "the entrance arch in the centre of the south side" rises above the screen, with a matching closed arch on the north. "Each cenotaph consists of a single block of stone, shaped like a sarcophagus, set on a stepped plinth", the plinth on a wider platform whose upper surface has "a framed flowery scrollwork pattern"; on Mumtaz's, "inscribed on the top and the sides of the block are Quran'ic verses in formal Sulus script" [stated: tajmahal.gov.in, Interior decoration]. No hanging lamp is recorded for 1653 (the lamp hanging there now was given by Lord Curzon in the twentieth century), so none is built. Ours: the screen 1.9 m high, each panel a slab of marble pierced with staggered almond-shaped openings and little lozenges between them, so that what stands reads as a net of stems and leaves; a plinth rail and a top rail on each panel, with a row of inlaid flowers (carnelian, jade, lapis, yellow, jade leaves) along both faces of each; the corner posts turned, with a carnelian ring and a kalasha (a pot with a lid and a bud) on top; a cresting of vase-shaped kanguras; in the middle of the south side the opening under a seven-lobed cusped arch on tall stiles rising 0.75 m above the screen with a finial, the north side's twin over a jali. The cenotaph: a platform 2.7 × 4.5 m framed by a dark line, a scroll of inlaid flowers round it; three steps, each face with a row of flower sprays (four petals round a yellow heart, jade leaves and stem); the block with a foot, sloping sides and a flat top bearing a raised tablet, a band of black letters in a frame on each long side and rows of letters on the tablet [inferred: the heights, the piercing pattern, the cresting's outline, the steps' sizes, the letters as abstract strokes]. Shah Jahan's cenotaph (1666) is still left out.

**The faces' inlay, the mosque and jawab, the fountains (L2–L3, built).** "The calligraphy is made by jasper inlaid in white marble panels"; "higher panels are written slightly larger to reduce the skewing effect when viewing from below"; the archway spandrels carry "pietra dura inlays of highly stylised" vines, flowers and fruits; the lower walls have "white marble dados that have been sculpted with realistic bas relief depictions of flowers and vines"; the inlay stones are "yellow marble, jasper and jade" [stated: indianmarvels]. The mosque is of red sandstone, its outside "covered in pietra dura work, especially across its spandrels, and calligraphy", with four octagonal towers and three domes on its roof; on the jawab "instead of Koranic inscriptions, there are wonderful flower designs", in "white marble on the red sandstone background" [stated: ednet student portfolio — a weak source, kept for the facade only]. At the crossing of the walks is "a raised platform (chabutra) of white marble with an ornamental pool (hauz) containing five fountains" [stated: tajmahal.gov.in]; "five fountains are located within the tank, one at each of its four corners and one in its center", and "a line of equidistant water fountains runs down the center of the nahr" [stated: CPREEC ecoheritage].
Ours: on every pishtaq face of the tomb and the great gate, an inscription band framing the great arch — white marble field (on the red gate), dark borders, black letters (baselines, uprights, bowls and dots) growing larger with height; a vine of inlaid stones following the arch's extrados with flowers of red, yellow and blue stone and jade leaves; a rosette of red and green stones in each spandrel with a spray of stems ending in flowers; the dado in panels framed in dark stone, each with a flowering plant in white relief, between the openings [inferred: composition after photographs]. The mosque's facade with the inscription band round its central arch, the jawab's with a scroll of flowers instead, both with inlaid spandrels, the dado, white marble frames round the side arches and a white band under the parapet; their arches now open into bays with dark doors at the back (the hall's mass filled them before). The central tank is now a rim round a basin (it was a solid block that hid the water) with its five fountains; fifteen jets at 8 m intervals down each half of the north–south channel [inferred: count and spacing]; each fountain a marble lotus with a copper nozzle and its jet, the jet of broken white water, part translucent (`water_jet`; a white paint before). The inlay of the spandrels, the bands of flowers, the chamber's niches, the screen's rails and panels and the cenotaph is now in the palette's inlay stones — carnelian (red), jade (green), yellow jasper, lapis (blue) — in place of the coloured plasters that stood in for them, after "yellow marble, jasper and jade" [stated: indianmarvels]; the gilt and painted sun of the inner dome stays as it was [inferred].

**The iwans' recesses (second wave, built).** The arched recesses of the pishtaqs carry muqarnas [stated: MIT Dome, G. E. Kidder Smith collection, "Taj Mahal, exterior, detail of muqarnas motifs on arched recess, or pishtaq"]. Ours: every iwan and niche of the tomb and the great gate is now a closed recess, half an octagon in plan (the back narrower than the opening), its walls rising to the springing, two tiers of muqarnas cells along the springing, a pointed half-dome meeting the arch at the face with a net of thin ribs over it; on the back wall a wooden door with gilt bosses below and a marble jali (over darkness) above, framed in dark stone (the raised niches have the jali alone); a blind panel framed in dark stone on each slanting side; the south iwan, the way in, is a rectangular vaulted recess before its door [inferred: the plan, the proportions and the net pattern after photographs; before, the recesses opened onto the dark interior].

## 5. The town

Flat-roofed houses of two or three storeys, plastered brick or red sandstone, with a jharokha balcony over the door. [inferred]

**The forecourt's bazaar streets and gates (third pass, built).** "The Taj complex is now entered through one of three gates leading into the Jilaukhana, or forecourt"; "Two identical bazaar streets lead from the east and west gates to the Jilaukhana"; "The streets are lined with rows of small unconnected rectangular cells without windows", "fronted by an arcaded verandah with multi-cusped arches supported by columns of distinctive Shah Jahan type"; the Khawasspuras (attendants' quarters) on the north against the garden wall and the two tomb complexes (Saheli Burj) on the south; outside, a short flight up to the Taj Ganj [stated: tajmahal.gov.in, Outlying buildings]. Visitors "would dismount from their horses or elephants" there; the cells "were used to sell goods from when the Taj was built until 1996"; a caravanserai on the north-west was "Katra Omar Khan" [stated: Wikipedia, Origins and architecture]. Ours: in our shortened forecourt (250 × 56 m) a bazaar street 9 m wide runs along its middle from a gate in each end wall to 48 m from the axis, lined on both sides by 22 cells 3.2 m wide and 4 m deep, each with a door 1.4 × 2.5 m in a plastered front, its wooden leaves folded back, dark inside; before them a verandah 3 m deep on a plinth of 0.35 m with two steps, its front an arcade of seven-lobed cusped pointed arches on Shah Jahani columns (a cusped square base, a faceted tapering shaft with two rings, a bell capital, an impost block), a white marble line and two rosettes over each arch, a chhajja on brackets and a crenellated parapet; the east, west and south gates of red sandstone 20 m wide and 11 m high, a pointed arch one walks through on each face framed by a scroll of inlaid flowers in white marble, the passage between solid piers under a plastered ceiling, merlons and four domed kiosks on the roof; a paved street outward from each side gate [inferred: the cell, verandah and gate dimensions, the lobes, the column's profile, the gates' form]. The Khawasspuras, the Saheli Burj and the caravanserais are left for later.

**Life (third pass, built).** The forecourt was where visitors "would dismount from their horses or elephants and assemble in style", and its cells were used "to sell goods" [stated: Wikipedia, Origins and architecture]. Ours (`life.js`): on about two-thirds of the verandahs goods set out before the doors — clusters of water pots and dark pots, sacks of grain, baskets of cloth — and on most of them the keeper sitting cross-legged; buyers and passers-by in the streets, the men in the jama (a long robe) with a sash and a turban, the women robed and veiled; six saddled horses held by grooms in the forecourt and a few visitors before the great gate; thatch-roofed barges and rowing boats on the Yamuna below the riverfront terrace [inferred: the goods, the dress after Mughal paintings, the boats; no elephant figure exists yet].

## 6. Ground and water

The level plain; the riverbank falling to the Yamuna on the north. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

The flowerbeds are "divided by stone-paved raised pathways", sixteen to each quarter; the trees are "either that of Cyprus (signifying death) or of the fruit bearing type (signifying life)", arranged symmetrically [stated: tajmahal.org.uk "Taj Garden"]. **Built (second wave):** raised walks of red sandstone 3 m wide with marble kerbs, 0.55 m above the beds, three each way in every quarter (at 46, 80 and 114 m from the axes) so that each quarter holds sixteen sunken beds, and a walk 4 m wide round the inside of the walls; a line of cypresses along the edge of every walk, fruit trees in the beds behind them (apple trees stand in for the orchard), grass [inferred: the walks' height and the spacing of the planting]; neem and banyan in the town.
**The riverfront (second wave):** the terrace's face to the Yamuna built as an arcade of blind arches of red sandstone, crenellated, between the corner towers, from the river's bed to a parapet over the terrace [inferred: articulation after photographs of the riverfront].

## 8. Sky and weather

The north Indian winter: hazy sun, morning mist off the river. Latitude 27° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

A raga on plucked strings over a drone; the shehnai at the gate (an oud-like string, a drone and a reed stand in). [inferred]

## Placement

The settlement's latitude in `src/planet/sites.js` is set a little south and east of the true place (26.2°, 78.3°), so that the layout keeps its disc off the foothills the warped map puts north of Agra. [inferred]
