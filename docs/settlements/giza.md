# Giza, c. 2560 BC — dossier

Settlement id: `giza` · Style: `oldkingdom` · Region: Egypt (the Memphite plateau) · Code: `src/settlements/giza/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the Great Pyramid and its complex, the queens' pyramids, the causeway, the five sealed boat pits, the valley temple and harbour with the king's ship, the two cemeteries of mastabas, the Wall of the Crow and the workers' town with its bakeries; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 recorded for later: Khafre's and Menkaure's pyramids and the Sphinx (they are of the following reigns), the pyramid's interior (the Grand Gallery, the King's Chamber), the reliefs of the temples, people and the building ramps.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Great Pyramid of Khufu: a true pyramid, originally 146.6 m (280 cubits) high on a base of 230.33 m (440 cubits), a slope of 51°50′40″ [stated: Wikipedia]; cased in white limestone from Tura [stated: Wikipedia; Smarthistory], "slant-faced, but flat-topped, blocks of highly polished white limestone" [stated: Wikipedia (IPFS)]; a capstone that may have been gilded [stated: Smarthistory] — ours is gilded.
- **Moment shown:** completed, c. 2560 BC, before Khafre's pyramid. [stated: dates — Wikipedia]

## 3. Orientation

The pyramid's sides face the cardinal points within minutes of arc [stated: Wikipedia (IPFS)]; the temples and causeway on its east, toward the river.

## 4. The style

Fine white limestone casing; plain limestone walls; red granite pillars and black basalt pavements in the temples; battered mastabas with false doors on their east faces; mudbrick and mud plaster in the town. [stated: materials — Wikipedia, historyofegypt; inferred details]

### 4.2 The boat pits

Seven boat pits at Giza belong to Khufu's complex, five of them directly beside the Great Pyramid [stated: Wikipedia "Ancient Egyptian royal ships"]. The first ship lay dismantled in a pit cut in the rock immediately south of the pyramid, the second in a pit parallel and adjacent to it [stated: Madain Project]; each pit was limestone-lined and sealed with massive blocks after the burial [stated: Madain Project]; over the first, 41 blocks averaging about 14 tons, with key stones at its west end [stated: Waseda University Khufu Boat project]. The other three, boat-shaped, lie east of the pyramid — beside the mortuary temple and along the causeway [inferred: from plans of the site as generally published; positions approximate].
Ours, as sealed at the moment shown: the two southern pits (31 and 32.5 m) beyond the south enclosure wall, each covered by 40–41 limestone beams laid side by side, the joints stopped with gypsum, a kerb round the cutting; the three eastern pits (34–50 m) boat-shaped, their covering beams following the hull's outline — north of the mortuary temple, between it and the first queen's pyramid, and beside the causeway. [inferred: sizes of the covers and kerbs, the gypsum]

### 4.3 The king's ship in the harbour

The first Khufu ship: 43.6 m long, 5.9 m in the beam [stated: Wikipedia]; Lebanese cedar and Egyptian acacia, joined by mortise and tenon and lashed with cords [stated: Madain Project]; papyriform ends — the stem rising sharply, the sternpost higher still and curving toward the bow [stated: indigenousboats, after Landström]; a windowless deckhouse about 9 m long with an inner chamber about 2 m, a wooden framework for an awning extending from it and a second canopy in the prow; twelve oars, ten lashed amidships ahead of the deckhouse, two steering oars at the stern; the deck almost wholly covered, stringers along it to which the oars were lashed [stated: NOVA "Building Pharaoh's Ship"].
Ours: the ship afloat in the harbour beside the landing, as before her burial, bow to the north: the hull with its wale, the posts ending in papyrus umbels, the deckhouse with its anteroom and framed panels, the awning frame with a linen awning over its aft half, the bow canopy, ten oars with their blades in the water, the steering oars against their posts. That she lay at the harbour is [inferred]; her purpose is debated [stated: Madain Project].

## 5. The town

Long galleries (halls with a central colonnade and sleeping platforms), houses with a court, an oven and bins; flat roofs of logs and mud. [inferred]

**Bakeries.** Dozens of bakeries able to make thousands of loaves a day; thousands of heavy bell-shaped pots (bedja), heated in open fires, filled with dough and stacked [stated: The Archaeologist]; the bakery rooms excavated in 1991 had low stone walls, large vats and hearth platforms, attached to a large mudbrick building [stated: NOVA "Pyramids — the inside story"]. Ours: sixteen bakeries, four at the south end of each gallery block — a room of low stone walls, the hearth platform along the south wall with rows of bread pots bedded in ash (some lidded with an upturned pot), a stack of pots heating upturned over a fire in a corner, two vats in mudbrick rings. [inferred: placement, sizes, the arrangement of the pots]

## 6. Ground and water

The plateau level round the pyramid, falling 12 m east to the valley and 5 m south to the town; the harbour basin in the valley. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

None on the plateau; date palms and sycamore figs in the valley by the harbour. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

Clear and hot, haze now and then, the khamsin's dust in spring. Latitude 30° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound
