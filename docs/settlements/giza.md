## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Great Pyramid of Khufu: a true pyramid, originally 146.6 m (280 cubits) high on a base of 230.33 m (440 cubits), a slope of 51°50′40″ [stated: Wikipedia]; cased in white limestone from Tura [stated: Wikipedia; Smarthistory], "slant-faced, but flat-topped, blocks of highly polished white limestone" [stated: Wikipedia (IPFS)]; a capstone that may have been gilded [stated: Smarthistory] — ours is gilded.
- **Moment shown:** completed, c. 2560 BC, before Khafre's pyramid. [stated: dates — Wikipedia]

## 3. Orientation

The pyramid's sides face the cardinal points within minutes of arc [stated: Wikipedia (IPFS)]; the temples and causeway on its east, toward the river.

## 4. The style

Fine white limestone casing; plain limestone walls; red granite pillars and black basalt pavements in the temples; battered mastabas with false doors on their east faces; mudbrick and mud plaster in the town. [stated: materials — Wikipedia, historyofegypt; inferred details]

### 4.2 The boat pits

Seven boat pits at Giza belong to Khufu's complex, five of them directly beside the Great Pyramid [stated: Wikipedia "Ancient Egyptian royal ships"]. The first ship lay dismantled in a pit cut in the rock immediately south of the pyramid, the second in a pit parallel and adjacent to it [stated: Madain Project]; each pit was limestone-lined and sealed with massive blocks after the burial [stated: Madain Project]; over the first, 41 blocks averaging about 14 tons, with key stones at its west end [stated: Waseda University Khufu Boat project]. The other three, boat-shaped, lie east of the pyramid — beside the mortuary temple and along the causeway [inferred: from plans of the site as generally published; positions approximate].
Ours, as sealed at the moment shown: the two southern pits (31 and 32.5 m) beyond the south enclosure wall, each covered by 40–41 limestone beams laid side by side, the joints stopped with gypsum, a kerb round the cutting; the three eastern pits (34–50 m) boat-shaped, their covering beams following the hull's outline — north of the mortuary temple, between it and the first queen's pyramid, and beside the causeway. [inferred: sizes of the covers and kerbs, the gypsum]

### 4.3 The king's ship in the harbour

The first Khufu ship: 43.6 m long, 5.9 m in the beam [stated: Wikipedia]; Lebanese cedar and Egyptian acacia, joined by mortise and tenon and lashed with cords [stated: Madain Project]; papyriform ends — the stem rising sharply, the sternpost higher still and curving toward the bow [stated: indigenousboats, after Landström]; a windowless deckhouse about 9 m long with an inner chamber about 2 m, a wooden framework for an awning extending from it and a second canopy in the prow; twelve oars, ten lashed amidships ahead of the deckhouse, two steering oars at the stern; the deck almost wholly covered, stringers along it to which the oars were lashed [stated: NOVA "Building Pharaoh's Ship"].
Ours: the ship afloat in the harbour beside the landing, as before her burial, bow to the north: the hull with its wale, the posts ending in papyrus umbels, the deckhouse with its anteroom and framed panels, the awning frame with a linen awning over its aft half, the bow canopy, ten oars with their blades in the water, the steering oars against their posts. That she lay at the harbour is [inferred]; her purpose is debated [stated: Madain Project].

### 4.4 The mastaba streets and their chapels

- The Western Cemetery was "laid out in blocks separated by avenues running north to south and intersecting east to west"; its first mastabas were rectangular, cores of well-laid blocks or block-faced with rubble fill, without chapels or rooms inside; "slab stelae were placed into the southern end of their eastern walls", standing for the false door, and "the areas of the slab stelae were often enclosed within small mud-brick walls"; later casing and false doors replaced the slab stelae, and the mud-brick chapels were sometimes replaced or enveloped by stone [stated: emhotep, "Khufu's Western Cemetery"]. Khufu's family and officials lay in the Eastern Cemetery, with eight double mastabas for his children and their spouses [stated: Wikipedia draft "Pyramid complex of Khufu"]. Slab stelae show the owner seated at a table of loaves with the lists of offerings, and were painted (Nefertiabet's is the famous example) [stated: Wikipedia "Nefertiabet"; Harvard Giza Project "Slab stelae of the Giza necropolis"].
- **Ours:** the tombs in rows along streets running north–south (21 m apart) crossed by east–west ones (32 m), each 9–12 m east–west and 18–27 m north–south, 4.2–6.6 m high. In the West Field the core in stepped courses about 0.8 m high, each set back; in the East Field cased smooth in Tura stone. On every east face, 2.6 m from the south end, the slab stela (0.53 × 0.38 m) set in a frame in the second course at 1.05 m: in painted relief the owner on a lion-legged stool, red-brown, his white kilt and black wig, reaching to the table of half loaves, a jar beneath it, columns of the offering list in blue and green under a blue band. Round it a chapel of mud brick 2.9 × 3.4 m, 2.3 m high, its door (0.85 × 1.75 m, palm-trunk lintel) to the east, a roof of palm logs under mud, a beaten floor; before the stela a limestone offering basin with three loaves and a beer jar on it [inferred: the sizes, the stela's position in height, the chapel's form and roof, the furnishings].

### 4.5 The temples' walls and ceilings

- Khufu's mortuary temple: a large court took most of its area, its floor "plastered with irregular slabs of basalt"; "rectangular granite pillars supported the roof which surrounded the court"; "the limestone walls of the temple were richly decorated with painted reliefs and hieroglyphic inscriptions" [stated: Wikipedia draft]; fragments of the court's wall reliefs show the king's thirty-year jubilee, relief-carved and painted [stated: AERA, "The Great Pyramid Temple"].
- **Ours (all the pyramid temples):** on the inner faces of the walls under the roofed ambulatory, registers 1.95 m high of offering bearers in painted raised relief (red-brown skin, white kilts, black wigs) striding toward the middle of each wall, bringing a loaf, a beer jar, a lotus or a trussed fowl, on blue ground lines; under the roof a band of blue and a frieze of khekher knots; the roof's undersides painted as the night sky, blue with yellow stars, stopping short of the architraves [inferred: the subjects (offering bearers stand in for the jubilee scenes), the sizes, the star ceiling — known from later Old Kingdom buildings].

### 4.6 The pyramid's interior, as at the burial

The entrance in the north face 16.98 m above the base and 7.29 m east of the centre line; the descending passage at 26°31' and 1.04 m wide; the ascending passage at 26°02' from 28.2 m down it, 39.3 m long; the Grand Gallery 46.68 m long and 8.6 m high, 2.06 m wide over its benches (0.51 m wide, 0.53 m high), with 27 slots a side in the benches, the walls rising 2.29 m and then corbelled in seven courses of about 7.6 cm a side to 1.04 m at the roof, the roof slabs each set a little steeper than the floor in its own notch; the Great Step 0.9 m high at its head; the antechamber 2.97 m long and 3.78 m high with granite wainscots, the grooves of the granite leaf and three portcullis slabs, and the half-round hollows over them; the King's Chamber of red granite 10.47 × 5.234 m and 5.974 m high in five courses, roofed by nine granite beams, the mouths of the two shafts in the north and south walls about 0.91 m up; the lidless granite sarcophagus 2.28 × 0.98 × 1.05 m by the west wall, its rim cut back for a sliding lid [stated: Petrie 1883, ch. 7; Maragioglio and Rinaldi 1965]. The three granite plugs stored in the gallery on beams across the slots, to be slid down after the burial, and the portcullis slabs held up on ropes over logs [inferred: the plugs are still in the ascending passage and the slots and hollows fit this use, Lehner 1997, 112–113]; the timber block before the Great Step, the stair over the plugs, the lid laid out on timbers and the oil lamps [inferred]. The sloping and low passages are given a walker's headroom of 2.1 m (real 1.05–1.2 m) [inferred, a concession to the walker]; the descending passage ends in the dark 60 m in (the subterranean chamber is not built).

## 10. c. 2500 BC: the Sphinx and Khafre's valley temple (2026-11-11)

The moment moves on a generation, to the reign of Khafre (c. 2520–2494 BC): Khufu's pyramid stands complete; to the south-east, at the plateau's edge above the valley, the Sphinx lies in its pit with Khafre's valley temple before it. Khafre's own pyramid and upper temple lie south-west, beyond the settlement's edge (radius 420 m) and are not built; his causeway is shown only as its lower end.

- **Sphinx.** "73.5 m in length and in parts 20 m in height", cut from "a relatively soft, natural limestone, left over in the quarry used to build the Pyramids"; the forepaws made separately of blocks; traces of paint near one ear, the face probably dark red; the cobra (uraeus) and the ritual beard are missing today; credited to Khafre c. 2540 BC by most Egyptologists [stated: World History Encyclopedia, "The Mystery of the Great Sphinx"]. Width 19 m at the haunches [stated: Wikipedia, as commonly cited]. Ours: lying along x in a pit cut to a floor 8–9 m below the plateau, open to the valley on the east, the face looking east; the body a squared oval tapering from the haunches to the chest, thighs standing out, forelegs and paws 15 m forward with four toes each; head in the striped headcloth (nemes) in bands of blue and yellow with wings behind and lappets on the breast, brow band, painted brows and eyes, nose, lips, the plaited beard in blue, the gilded uraeus; the face red-brown, the body the lion's ochre; a bedrock course at its foot left unpainted [inferred: the colours of cloth and body, the restored nose, beard and uraeus (complete in the Old Kingdom), the proportions of the head, the toes].
- **Khafre's valley temple.** Date c. 2520–2494 BC; piers "of huge blocks of pink Aswan granite", the floor alabaster, the main hall T-shaped, light let in "through narrow slits in the roof", symmetrical side rooms on either side of the central hall holding 23 diorite statues of the king, "probably placed against the walls, as indentations in the alabaster floor suggest"; linked to the pyramid by a covered causeway [stated: Bluffton University, "Valley Temple of Khafre"]. Ours: 46 × 42 m, front to the east, granite-cased with courses of 1.25 m (joints staggered), two doors in the east front to lobbies and doors into the hall; the hall's crossbar (north–south, 9 × 34 m) and stem (running west, 21 × 12 m), 16 square granite monoliths (10 + 6) with granite architraves, a roof of slabs 1.3 m thick with slits over the crossbar; two statue rooms, 23 seated statues of dark diorite on alabaster bases (12 + 6 along the rooms' walls, 4 in the hall's ends, 1 at the stem's west end), each a throne with the falcon behind the head; a west door to the causeway [inferred: the plan's proportions, number and position of doors, pillar sizes, the room sizes, the height of 8 m; only the T-hall, the granite, the alabaster, the slits and the 23 statues are stated]. Material: "alabaster" is rendered with `marble_smooth`, the nearest in the palette; the diorite with `granite_black`.
- **Khafre's causeway** (lower end): the existing causeway element, level on the plateau, down the plateau's edge, into the temple's west door; it ends on the plateau in a blind wall, the rest being beyond the settlement [inferred].

## 11. The quarry and the ramps (2026-11-11)

- The Giza quarry is "horseshoe-shaped", lies "just south of Khufu's Great Pyramid and the Khafre pyramid causeway"; workers first cut channels "the size of hotel corridors" to isolate large blocks of bedrock, subdivided them with narrower channels "just wide enough for one quarryman", freed a block by inserting levers "as big as railroad ties" into sockets under it and prying it loose; blocks of 50–100 tons were roughed out and left attached to the bedrock between the quarry and the Sphinx; one- to two-ton pyramid blocks were cut in the basin; the floor is filled with limestone, sand, tafla and gypsum debris that Lehner thinks "might be remnants of the pyramid construction ramps" dumped back at the end [stated: AERA, "Great Pyramid Quarry"].
- Ramps: at Hatnub (Khufu's reign) alabaster blocks went up "very steep slopes of 20 percent or more" on a sledge with ropes made fast to wooden posts, a central ramp between two staircases with many post holes [stated: Live Science on the Hatnub find, 2018].
- Ours: south of the boat pits and north of the Wall of the Crow, an 88 × 37 m quarry cut in three benches (2.4 m each, 7.2 m deep), open to the east where the benches fade into the sloping road out; on the benches rows of blocks marked out (1.4 × 1.1 m, 0.9 m high) with the lever socket at the foot, some already taken, some levered loose with the lever left under them, heaps of chips, three roughed-out giants (4.4–5 m long, 2.2–2.6 m high) on the floor; the road with palm-log sleepers every 0.85 m and rope posts at the sides; a block on its sledge with a gang of ten on the rope and an overseer; quarrymen kneeling at the blocks, a water-carrier, a scribe; mallets, copper chisels, dolerite pounders, water jars [inferred: all sizes and numbers; the tools' kinds are general Old Kingdom practice]. The pit's depth is much less than the real 30 m.

- **Yards.** The lots that used to stand empty are yards: pens (a hip-high mudbrick wall, a trough, straw, goats, sheep or a donkey), brickyards (the clay pit, rows of bricks on edge, stacks laid crosswise, the mould, jars, a heap of chaff), silos (beehive granaries on a platform, a stone on each filling hole, a ladder), middens (ash, sherds, dung cakes stacked to dry) and threshing floors (a beaten disc, a heap of grain, winnowing mats, a threshing sledge, an ox). ## 14. The finishing works at the arrival (fix pass, 2026-11-12)

The visitor now arrives on the plateau's east side at (242, −50), 270 m from the apex, looking west over the mortuary temple, with the causeway's head in front, the first queen's pyramid at the left and the works at the right; before, the arrival lay 290 m north-east on bare plateau and showed the pyramid alone.
- **The ramp, half taken down.** A ramp of rubble and mud, 38 m long, 7 m wide, rising 4.8 m (1 : 8) to a sheer cut where its upper end has been removed, a skin of mud with palm-log sleepers across it, logs along its kerbs, the fallen rubble at its foot. At Hatnub, in Khufu's reign, stones went up "very steep slopes of 20 percent or more" on a ramp with post holes along it, and Lehner reads the Giza quarry's fill as "remnants of the pyramid construction ramps" dumped back (§11) [stated: §11]. Ours is gentler than Hatnub's (1 : 8, for a loaded sledge) and of rubble and mud [inferred]. That one of Khufu's was still being taken down while the casing was finished is our staging [inferred]; its position, length and slope are ours.
- **Hauling.** A casing block on its sledge on the ramp, five pairs of men on the rope, the overseer with his staff, a boy pouring water on the sand before the runners: wetting the sand lessens the sledge's drag by about half, and a man pours water before a sledge in the tomb painting of Djehutihotep (c. 1900 BC) [stated: Fall et al., Physical Review Letters 2014, as reported by phys.org and the Christian Science Monitor, 2014]. The sleeper road of the quarry (§11) continues here to the ramp's foot [inferred].

## 15. The casing, the temples' outsides, the camps (deepening pass, 2026-11-12)

- **The casing.** The outer casing used higher-quality limestone quarried at Tura [stated: Wikipedia, "Egyptian pyramid construction techniques"]; "slant-faced, but flat-topped, blocks of highly polished white limestone" [stated: Wikipedia, §1 above]; the capstone may have been gilded [stated: Smarthistory, §1]. Ours: the faces laid in runs of courses of two batches of Tura stone (the palette's `tura_limestone` and the smoother, glossier `revetment`) so that a face shows a faint banding and sheen from far off, its joints close up; the pyramidion 4.4 m high in gilded metal (`clay_gilt`). That the batches differed in tone is [inferred], as is their arrangement; the gypsum and rubble filling the core joints [stated: same Wikipedia page] is not visible.
- **Temples and causeway, outside.** Plinth of dark core stone, a cavetto cornice and a roll on the wall tops, a torus roll up each corner, a lintel with a carved panel over each doorway; the causeway with a plinth, a cornice along the roof's edge and the night sky painted under its roof; the enclosure wall in the palace-façade manner (pilasters every 3.2 m, cornice and roll) [inferred from Old Kingdom royal architecture as generally known; no source for the details at Khufu's complex].
- **The gangs' camps.** The workforce "camped near the pyramids" [stated: Wikipedia, same page]; those who did not live in the town slept where they worked [inferred]. Ours: three camps on the plateau east of the pyramid — shelters of two reed-mat slopes on a ridge pole, hearths of three stones with a pot, a row of jars in damp sand under a mat, sacks of grain, rollers and ropes, a mortar trough with hoes (gypsum mortar needed heat from wood [stated: same page]), sleeping mats with limestone headrests — with their people (sitting round the fires, kneeling at the huts, filling jars), dogs, hobbled donkeys, a sparrow at the grain, smoke from the hearths in the meals' hours. A block on its sledge on the sleeper road six metres from the arrival with a gang of twelve on the rope; blocks "likely lubricated by water" [stated: same page], hence the boy with the jar. A masons' yard south of the causeway, the priests waiting at its northern doorway. Chips of Tura limestone and rubble heaps scattered about the stacks, the ramp, the road and the camps; the ground trodden pale and hard at the works.
