## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the citadel of Mycenae on an acropolis 274 m above the sea, about 19 km inland, in the north-east corner of the Argive plain, commanding the routes to the Isthmus of Corinth; with the lower town it covered about 32 ha and held some 30,000 people at its peak around 1350 BC [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1250 BC, the middle of Late Helladic IIIB: the wall has just been extended on the west slope to take in Grave Circle A, and the Lion Gate has been built (LH IIIB, c. 1250 BC) [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae", "Lion Gate"]. The north-east extension with the secret cistern (c. 1200 BC) is later; we show its wall and the passage's door in outline only. [inferred: simplification of the date]
- **In Homer:** the city of Agamemnon, "rich in gold" (the *Iliad*; Aeschylus's *Oresteia*) [stated: DESIGN §7]. The shaft-grave gold is of the 16th century BC, kept in view in the 13th as the royal ancestors' burial place. [stated: Wikipedia, "Grave Circle A"]
- **Location:** 37.73 N, 22.76 E (the planet's table; the town of Mycenae is at 37.73 N, 22.76 E) [stated].

## 4. The style

### 4.1 The walls
- First built about 1350 BC in "Cyclopean" masonry: huge limestone blocks roughly shaped or left irregular, wedged with small stones and yellow clay; the largest, lintels and gate jambs, weigh over 20 tonnes [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"; ancient-history-sites.com, "Mycenaean Architecture and Building Methods"]. The Greeks of later times thought the Cyclopes had built them [stated, common knowledge; "Cyclopean" is the name's origin].
- The LH IIIB extension at the Lion Gate is of large conglomerate blocks dressed with a hammer and laid in more regular courses ("pseudo-ashlar") [stated: same; Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"]. In the model: the west front (bastion, gate, north stub) is dressed pale conglomerate in courses, the rest of the circuit rough blocks. [inferred: where each is used]
- Thickness and height: walls up to about 6–7 m thick and up to 12 m high in life [inferred, from memory of the plan; no figure on the pages read]; ours 6 m thick, to 8–9 m high on the outer face, the top about 2 m over the ground inside. [inferred]
- The bastion: pseudo-ashlar, 14.80 m long and 7.23 m wide; the approach between wall and bastion narrows to a court about 15 × 7.23 m; the bastion on the right as one comes up, so that defenders strike the attackers' unshielded side [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"].

### 4.2 The Lion Gate
- Opening 3.10 m wide and 2.95 m high at the threshold, narrowing to 2.78 m under the lintel; closed by double doors on a pivot beam [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"].
- The lintel 4.5 × 2.0 × 0.8 m [stated: same]. Above it a corbelled relieving triangle; in it a limestone slab 3.60 m wide and more than 3 m high with two lionesses (their heads, carved separately, lost) standing on either side of a central Minoan-type column, their forefeet on an altar-like platform; four discs above the column, read as rafter ends [stated: same]. In the model the lionesses' heads are shown whole, turned out to the visitor. [inferred: restoration; the heads are missing]
- Just inside, a covered court with a small chamber, probably a guard post [stated: same]; to the right, next to the wall, the granary where pithoi held carbonised wheat [stated: same].

### 4.3 Grave Circle A
- 27.5 m across, six shaft graves with 19 burials (8 men, 9 women, 2 children), gold death masks (five, one the "Mask of Agamemnon" from Grave V), swords, daggers, the Silver Siege Rhyton [stated: Wikipedia, "Grave Circle A, Mycenae"].
- About 1250 BC the circle was enclosed in the new wall and made a sacred precinct (temenos): a double-ring peribolos wall, a level area for ceremonies, the stelae re-erected over the graves (three with chariot scenes), a circular structure, perhaps an altar, over one grave [stated: same]. The ring's wall of upright slabs with horizontal slabs laid over them is how the circle is restored today. [inferred: restoration, from memory of the site; the page does not describe the slabs]
- The circle lies south of the gate, inside the new wall [stated: same].

### 4.4 The palace on the summit
- The megaron unit of porch, vestibule and throne room (hall) [stated: Dartmouth "Aegean Prehistory", lesson 20]; in the hall a large round hearth at the centre, four columns in a square about it, under a raised roof with an opening; the throne against the middle of the right-hand wall, "certain at Pylos and Tiryns and probably at Mycenae"; two columns between the antae of the porch [stated: same; Wikipedia, "Megaron"]. Plastered floors with painted patterns in hall, vestibule and porch; the walls fully decorated with frescoes [stated: Dartmouth].
- A large court in front of the megaron, with a columned portico [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"; Dartmouth]; a grand staircase from a terrace below up to the court [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"].
- Sizes in the model: hall 13 × 10 m inside, porch 6.5 m, vestibule 4 m, hearth 3.7 m across, the four columns about 4.3 m apart, the Great Court 26 × 26 m. [inferred: from memory of the plan (the hall of Mycenae about 13 × 11.5 m); the pages read give no dimensions]
- Walls: mudbrick (and rubble poured into a timber frame) plastered, on stone socles; timber columns tapering downward on stone bases; flat clay roofs on timber beams [stated, in part: ancient-history-sites.com — timber frames, stone foundations and thresholds, plaster; Wikipedia, "Megaron" — mudbrick with frescoes]. Columns tapering downward, red shafts with a black cushion capital: the Minoan type that the Lion Gate relief itself shows [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate" — "Minoan-type column"].
- The frescoes: processions of men and women, griffins and lions flanking the throne, as at Pylos [stated: Wikipedia, "Megaron" — Pylos frescoes of a procession; Dartmouth]. In the model, griffins either side of the throne and a procession on the north wall of the hall, from the Minoan fresco vocabulary (`src/styles/minoan`, read-only) [inferred: borrowed].
- The palace's wings round the Great Court are entered from the court: the magazines (rows of pithoi, the archive of clay tablets in baskets above), the suites (a workshop room, a guest room with a clay bath, a loft). [inferred: the use of the rooms from the Linear B tablets' trades — oil, wine, textiles, perfume — and from Pylos's bath; no plan of Mycenae's wings is given by the pages read]

### 4.5 The Treasury of Atreus (the tomb called of Atreus or of Agamemnon)
- The largest of nine tholos tombs at Mycenae, LH IIIB (c. 1300–1250 BC), outside the citadel [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae", "Treasury of Atreus"].
- Dromos 36 m long, 6 m wide, east–west; side walls of conglomerate blocks rising from 0.5 m at the open end to 10 m at the façade, thickness 2 m rising to 3 m [stated: Wikipedia, "Treasury of Atreus"].
- Façade 10.5 m high; doorway 5.4 m high, 2.7 m wide, 5.4 m deep, tapering inward; the innermost lintel block 8 × 5 × 1.2 m (about 120 tons); above it the empty relieving triangle (once closed with a decorated slab) [stated: same].
- Two half-columns of green stone with zigzag shafts either side of the door; two smaller beside the triangle; red stone (rosso antico) bands and a rosette frieze [stated: same].
- The tholos 14.5 m across and 13.2 m high, 33 courses of cut limestone laid as a corbelled dome; nail holes suggest rosettes (possibly of gold) fixed to the stone [stated: same].
- A side chamber, 6 m square, through a doorway 2.5 m high in the north of the tholos [stated: same].
- The dome covered by an earth mound held at its foot by a poros retaining wall; a terrace in front about 27 × 27 m [stated: same]. In the model the mound is the ground itself, a cone over the dome, cut by the dromos. [inferred]

## 5. The town
- The lower town round the citadel, houses and workshops on terraces; beyond Grave Circle B the House of Shields, the House of the Oil Merchant, the House of the Sphinxes and the West House, dwellings and workshops both [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"]. [inferred: forms]
- A postern gate (undecorated) in the north wall [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"].

## 6. Ground and water
- The acropolis a rocky ridge between ravines to the north and south; limestone and conglomerate; the soil dry, pale and red. The water: a secret cistern 15 m down, reached by a corbelled passage of about 99 steps and fed by a tunnel from a spring [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"]; not built (later than our date). No stream or pond is shown. [inferred]

## 11. The deepening pass (L2): what was added and why
- **Houses you can enter.** Every house, workshop, store and shrine of the lower town and the citadel has a door with its steps, a front room with the round hearth (a hole in the clay roof over it, a collar of plaster round the hole), a back room, and in two-storey houses an internal stair to a loft; the roof is a flat clay slab on beams with a parapet and a clay spout, the beam ends showing in the front wall. [inferred: forms; stated in part — Dartmouth lesson 20: ordinary houses of mudbrick on stone socles, flat roofs of timber and clay, upper floors reached by a stair; "Mycenaean Residential Architecture"] The furniture is of the kinds found in house deposits: pithoi and stirrup jars for oil and wine, saddle querns, benches of mudbrick, beds on platforms with hides, the warp-weighted loom with its clay weights. [stated: common to Aegean Late Bronze Age excavation reports; the loom weights are among the commonest finds] [inferred: placement]
- **Trades in the shops.** Workshops by trade: potter (wheel on a post, drying racks of jars and stirrup jars, a kiln in the forecourt), bronze-smith (clay forge with a bellows, an anvil stone, a quench trough, ingots), weaver (three looms, baskets of wool, dyed cloths on a pole), oil-presser (a stone basin, a weighted beam, pithoi), ivory-carver (a bench with tusks and plaques), perfume-maker (a bronze cauldron over a hearth, small jars, bundles of herbs). The houses "beyond Grave Circle B" — the House of the Oil Merchant, the House of Shields, the House of the Sphinxes, the West House — "may have been both residences and workshops" [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"]; the trades follow the finds' names (oil, ivory, perfume) and the Linear B tablets' craftsmen (bronze-smiths, potters, weavers, perfumers) [inferred].
- **Storehouses.** Rows of pithoi on either side of an aisle, sacks, a bench for the tally-keeper; the granary inside the gate held pithoi of carbonised wheat [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"].
- **The palace's wings** as in §4.4; the guard-room of the gate on the left of the passage inside [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate" — "a small chamber, probably a guard post"]: spears in a rack, shields on the wall, benches, a table with dice, a brazier. [inferred: furnishing]
- **Shrines.** The two plastered houses by the road in the middle of the citadel are shown as cult rooms (benches round the walls, a plastered offering table, a shelf of terracotta figurines): the cult centre of the citadel with its "Room of the Idols" [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"; the figurines' place is our reading].
- **The lower town** now 70 lots on 14 lanes: houses shoulder to shoulder with party walls, set on terraces whose floors are levelled to the lane at the door; workshops stand back behind a paved forecourt walled low with a gap on the lane. [inferred: layout; the lower town's plan is known only from a few excavated houses] The approach road is 7 m wide between its houses [inferred].
- **Dry-stone walls** edge the lanes where no house does (terrace and field walls of fitted rubble, capped with boulders) [inferred: common Aegean practice]; five **chamber tombs** are cut into the north-west slope, their dressed façades turned down the hill, the door blocked with stones — the cemeteries of chamber tombs are the usual burial place of the lower town's people [inferred: from memory of the site's cemeteries; the pages read name only shaft and tholos tombs]. A **spring basin** stands at the cross of the lanes near the approach [inferred: the town needed water; the citadel's own cistern is later].

Roofs carry jars, fuel, a drying mat and a ladder. Weathering at eye level: plaster fallen to show the brick, damp at the foot, rain streaks under the windows, soot over the doors. [inferred]
- **People and animals.** About 120 still figures by trade and place (the road's traffic, sentries on the bastion and the wall's end, workers at the shops, tally-keepers at the magazines, mourners in the dromos), about 40 walkers by day; donkeys with packs, an ox-cart, goats, sheep and pigs behind the houses, dogs at the doors; sparrows on every second parapet. [inferred]
- **Planting** thickened to what the terraces would carry: olive groves with figs between the walls about the lanes, cypress by the tombs, kermes-oak maquis, aleppo and stone pine on the slopes, plots of wheat at the edge of the disc. [inferred: the Argolid's flora; the carbonised wheat is stated]
- **Sound.** Cicadas, goat and sheep bells, donkeys, dogs, cocks and cattle; smoke rises over the hearth of every workshop and about half the houses at the meals.

## 13. Detail pass (2026-11-17): what was added and on what ground

- **The Treasury's façade and tholos.** The doorway was "originally sealed with massive wooden doors" and the relieving triangle was "filled with slabs of red and green marble" [stated: ancientworldmagazine.com, "The Treasury of Atreus"; Wikipedia, "Treasury of Atreus" — red stone bands, rosette frieze, nail holes in the dome]. In the model: the triangle is a slab of red stone with a field of spirals and a diagonal band, bounded by a green border; the frieze over the lintel alternates rosettes with red and green bands; the doorway's two timber leaves, studded with bronze, stand open against the passage walls; the dome carries rings of gilded bronze rosettes fixed in the nail holes. [inferred: the spiral pattern of the slab is taken from the fragments reconstructed in the British Museum, not read on a page here; the open doors are our choice so that the tomb can be walked; the rosettes are "possibly gold" in Wikipedia]
- **The Lion Gate's doors.** The opening was "closed by double doors on a pivot beam" [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"]. In the model: two leaves of planked timber with rails and bronze nails, swung back against the jambs inside the passage and leaning with them, each turning on its pivot stone. [inferred: the planking and the open position]
- **Grave Circle A's stelae.** At least 21 stelae of oolithic limestone, 12 with relief; most of the ornament is spirals (stele I two spiral columns, III four columns with circles between, VI a spiral frame), IV and V divided into horizontal panels with spirals above, and chariot scenes on I (a charioteer driving over a man, a lion chasing a quadruped below), IV, V, VIII and IX; VI shows four rearing horses stacked; the background cut away to leave raised relief, perhaps painted [stated: Wikipedia, "Grave stelai from Grave Circle A, Mycenae"]. In the model each of the six graves keeps a stele cut from that vocabulary: a spiral-columned, a spiral-framed, a panelled one with spirals above and a chariot below, a lion-chase, a stack of horses, a meander-columned; the others stand plain; each grave's cover is bounded by a kerb of slabs. [inferred: which stele stands where; dimensions]
- **Fronts of the houses.** The Panagia houses of the lower town had an entrance room and courtyard, a corridor and a ramp to the entrance, columns beside a hearth, storage jars, stairs to the upper rooms, a drain and a paved work area [stated: ancient-history-sites.com, "Mycenaean Housing and Living Conditions"]; the House of Columns is named for its columns [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"]. Fronts are varied by rule from the lot (width, storeys, seed): a porch of two timber posts on stone bases under a clay canopy (one-storey houses of 7.4 m or more); dressed quoins with a painted dado band (two-storey houses); a painted frieze of alternating blue and red blocks under the parapet; the plain front. [inferred: the porch and the quoins are forms of Mycenaean ordinary and palace building; which houses wore them is our rule]
- **Works of the lower town.** Threshing floors (a round of beaten and paved ground with a post, sheaves, a winnowing fan and a fork), clay bread-ovens with their wood, and vine trellises on the terraces. [inferred: the farming of the Argive plain, the carbonised wheat of the granary (stated) and the Linear B tablets' cereals, wine and figs; the Panagia houses' "paved area ... for shared work" is stated]
- **Placing of the works:** found by rule on level ground outside the walls, clear of lanes, houses, forecourts, tombs and the Treasury: five threshing-floor sites sought (three found), nine ovens, seven arbours (four found).
- **The throne** is given arms, a footstool and a painted base with a spiral band. [inferred: the throne of Pylos, plastered and painted]

### 16.1 The visitor's walk (walk.js, walkroutes.js)
- **The walk** [inferred]: from the arrival on the approach road east past the caravan halt to the Lion Gate, through the passage, up the stepped ramp past the guard-room, the granary and Grave Circle A, along the citadel's road to the propylon and the Great Court, across it to the megaron; a spur south off the ramp to the cult rooms, and a lane south at the halt into the first lots of the lower town.
- **Lots opened by day** [inferred]: every lot whose front lies within 14 m of that walk (thirteen houses, workshops, stores, shrines and the guard-room, the granary, and the four wings of the palace) has a door 1.8 m wide (a house) or 2.2 m (a shop, a store, the wings) under a lintel 2.45 m high, the hearth or the forge lit, and a route walked by `tools/walk-check.mjs` into its front room. Wide doors on shops: the open front of a Mediterranean workshop by day [inferred; the lower town's real shops are not known, §5].
- **The road's shops** [inferred]: the three lots at the halt are a potter's, a smith's and a caravan store (the trades are those of the Linear B tablets and of the finds, §11; a great house's gate drew trains of pack-animals, §12). Each open shop has a counter of mudbrick under a plank top beside the door with the trade's goods on it (jars, stirrup jars and bowls; bronze ingots; folded cloth; ivory plaques), and a tally board on the door-post (plain: the tablets' signs are not set, §16.5).
- **Kept clear of the doors:** dress, laundry lines, dry-stone walls and the jars beside the wings' doors were moved off every opened door's lane (they had narrowed it to 0.5 m).

### 16.2 Night (nightlight.js)
- **What lit the place** [inferred]: the round clay hearth of every house's front room, the forge of the smith, the megaron's great hearth ("a large round hearth at the centre", Dartmouth, Aegean Prehistory, lesson 20 [stated]), torches of pitch pine in cressets on brackets or stands (the pine is the hills' own), bronze braziers on three legs, small clay lamps of olive oil with a pinched spout (oil is the palace's trade, §11), the drovers' open fire at the halt.
- **Placed:** four torches on the faces of the approach court (two on the stub's, two on the bastion's), a brazier at the ramp, four braziers in the Great Court, two torches in the megaron's porch, the fire with its pot at the halt (12 m ahead of the arrival), a lamp by each wayside stall. Every light the style's own builders make (hearths, forge, megaron, tomb) gets a halo in the engine's additive `halo` material, so it shows at the low preset; the houses' windows glow by the hash of their own place (one in three of the lit houses, half of the workshops' and shrines'); the lit door throws a pool into the lane. The day view is unchanged.

### 16.3 Sound (soundmap.js, voices-mycenae.js)
- **Voices** (all synthesised, prefixed `my`) [inferred]: a seven-string lyre (the bard of the Pylos megaron fresco and the lyres of Mycenaean seals and vases), the saddle quern, the smith's bellows, the clay loom weights, the sentry's wordless call from the wall, a drover's call, winnowing (in the weeks of the harvest). The engine's hammer, wheel, clay, kitchen, jar lids, pouring, the well's bucket, goats, sheep bells, donkeys, dogs, the owl and the cicadas are used as they are.
- **Placed:** the lyre in the Great Court by day and in the megaron, the sentries on the bastion, the stub, the propylon, the drovers at the halt and on the road, the women at the spring's basin, the jars at the magazines, winnowing in the fields; each open house sounds its trade by hour; rooms (houses, shops, the shrines, the wings, the megaron's hall and porch, the gate's passage) hush the outdoors by kind. Nothing at night but the sentries and the owl.

### 16.4 Animals (fauna.js `critters`)
- Dogs at the doors of the open houses (the Tiryns boar-hunt fresco and Mycenaean vases show hunting dogs; a house-dog is our reading) [inferred]; goats tethered by the spring and at the foot of the gate's ramp (herds of goats and sheep are listed in the Linear B tablets of Pylos and Knossos [stated: common knowledge]); doves on the court's paving and at the shrines' steps (doves sit on shrine models and idols of the cult rooms [inferred]); swallows' nests under the eaves of six open houses [inferred]. No cats and no hens: neither is certain in the Aegean of the 13th century BC. (The street-life layer of town.js still draws hens before some houses, as before this pass; it should be revisited.)

### 16.5 Wear and writing (wear.js)
- A darker, polished patch in the middle of each house's threshold stone and a second by the jamb; the soot of each hearth, forge or lamp on the beams over it; two dark wheel ruts in the paving of the Lion Gate's passage [inferred].
- **Not written:** Linear B signs exist (clay tablets of the palace archive, Pylos and Knossos) but no sign was set on any wall or board: no glyph could be verified from this shell, so none is shown. The tally boards are plain.

### 17.4 The landmark's wear
- Cyclopean runs (not the dressed ones): a talus bank of rubble sloping from the wall's foot a metre out with a moss/lichen cap (plaster_damp) on its shoulder, in lumps of 1.6-3.4 m with gaps, by the hash of each run's place; the Lion Gate's wall face: thin damp and lichen patches along its foot either side of the opening and a few higher where rain runs from the lintel. No new material, no new draw. Seen at the wall: the banks read as low mounds, the moss caps hardly show at the low preset; the first version (boxes) looked like loose blocks and was replaced.
