## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the citadel of Mycenae on an acropolis 274 m above the sea, about 19 km inland, in the north-east corner of the Argive plain, commanding the routes to the Isthmus of Corinth; with the lower town it covered about 32 ha and held some 30,000 people at its peak around 1350 BC [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1250 BC, the middle of Late Helladic IIIB: the wall has just been extended on the west slope to take in Grave Circle A, and the Lion Gate has been built (LH IIIB, c. 1250 BC) [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae", "Lion Gate"]. The north-east extension with the secret cistern (c. 1200 BC) is later; we show its wall and the passage's door in outline only. [inferred: simplification of the date]
- **In Homer:** the city of Agamemnon, "rich in gold" (the *Iliad*; Aeschylus's *Oresteia*) [stated: DESIGN §7]. The shaft-grave gold is of the 16th century BC, kept in view in the 13th as the royal ancestors' burial place. [stated: Wikipedia, "Grave Circle A"]
- **Location:** 37.73 N, 22.76 E (the planet's table; the town of Mycenae is at 37.73 N, 22.76 E) [stated].

## 4. The style

### 4.1 The walls
- First built about 1350 BC in "Cyclopean" masonry: huge limestone blocks roughly shaped or left irregular, wedged with small stones and yellow clay; the largest, lintels and gate jambs, weigh over 20 tonnes [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"; ancient-history-sites.com, "Mycenaean Architecture and Building Methods"]. The Greeks of later times thought the Cyclopes had built them [stated, common knowledge; "Cyclopean" is the name's origin].
- The LH IIIB extension at the Lion Gate is of large conglomerate blocks dressed with a hammer and laid in more regular courses ("pseudo-ashlar") [stated: same; Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"]. In the model: the west front (bastion, gate, north stub) is dressed pale conglomerate in courses, the rest of the circuit rough blocks. [inferred: where each is used]
- Thickness and height: walls up to about 6–7 m thick and up to 12 m high in life [inferred, from memory of the plan; no figure on the pages read]; ours 6 m thick, to 8–9 m high on the outer face, the top about 2 m over the ground inside. [inferred]
- The bastion: pseudo-ashlar, 14.80 m long and 7.23 m wide; the approach between wall and bastion narrows to a court about 15 × 7.23 m; the bastion on the right as one comes up, so that defenders strike the attackers' unshielded side [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"].

### 4.2 The Lion Gate
- Opening 3.10 m wide and 2.95 m high at the threshold, narrowing to 2.78 m under the lintel; closed by double doors on a pivot beam [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate"].
- The lintel 4.5 × 2.0 × 0.8 m [stated: same]. Above it a corbelled relieving triangle; in it a limestone slab 3.60 m wide and more than 3 m high with two lionesses (their heads, carved separately, lost) standing on either side of a central Minoan-type column, their forefeet on an altar-like platform; four discs above the column, read as rafter ends [stated: same]. In the model the lionesses' heads are shown whole, turned out to the visitor. [inferred: restoration; the heads are missing]
- Just inside, a covered court with a small chamber, probably a guard post [stated: same]; to the right, next to the wall, the granary where pithoi held carbonised wheat [stated: same].

### 4.3 Grave Circle A
- 27.5 m across, six shaft graves with 19 burials (8 men, 9 women, 2 children), gold death masks (five, one the "Mask of Agamemnon" from Grave V), swords, daggers, the Silver Siege Rhyton [stated: Wikipedia, "Grave Circle A, Mycenae"].
- About 1250 BC the circle was enclosed in the new wall and made a sacred precinct (temenos): a double-ring peribolos wall, a level area for ceremonies, the stelae re-erected over the graves (three with chariot scenes), a circular structure, perhaps an altar, over one grave [stated: same]. The ring's wall of upright slabs with horizontal slabs laid over them is how the circle is restored today. [inferred: restoration, from memory of the site; the page does not describe the slabs]
- The circle lies south of the gate, inside the new wall [stated: same].

### 4.4 The palace on the summit
- The megaron unit of porch, vestibule and throne room (hall) [stated: Dartmouth "Aegean Prehistory", lesson 20]; in the hall a large round hearth at the centre, four columns in a square about it, under a raised roof with an opening; the throne against the middle of the right-hand wall, "certain at Pylos and Tiryns and probably at Mycenae"; two columns between the antae of the porch [stated: same; Wikipedia, "Megaron"]. Plastered floors with painted patterns in hall, vestibule and porch; the walls fully decorated with frescoes [stated: Dartmouth].
- A large court in front of the megaron, with a columned portico [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"; Dartmouth]; a grand staircase from a terrace below up to the court [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"].
- Sizes in the model: hall 13 × 10 m inside, porch 6.5 m, vestibule 4 m, hearth 3.7 m across, the four columns about 4.3 m apart, the Great Court 26 × 26 m. [inferred: from memory of the plan (the hall of Mycenae about 13 × 11.5 m); the pages read give no dimensions]
- Walls: mudbrick (and rubble poured into a timber frame) plastered, on stone socles; timber columns tapering downward on stone bases; flat clay roofs on timber beams [stated, in part: ancient-history-sites.com — timber frames, stone foundations and thresholds, plaster; Wikipedia, "Megaron" — mudbrick with frescoes]. Columns tapering downward, red shafts with a black cushion capital: the Minoan type that the Lion Gate relief itself shows [stated: Wikipedia, "Lion Gate" — "Minoan-type column"].
- The frescoes: processions of men and women, griffins and lions flanking the throne, as at Pylos [stated: Wikipedia, "Megaron" — Pylos frescoes of a procession; Dartmouth]. In the model, griffins either side of the throne and a procession on the north wall of the hall, from the Minoan fresco vocabulary (`src/styles/minoan`, read-only) [inferred: borrowed].
- The cult centre, the houses on the ramp, the workshops and stores round the court are shown as plastered blocks and houses, not interiors. [inferred: omission]

### 4.5 The Treasury of Atreus (the tomb called of Atreus or of Agamemnon)
- The largest of nine tholos tombs at Mycenae, LH IIIB (c. 1300–1250 BC), outside the citadel [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae", "Treasury of Atreus"].
- Dromos 36 m long, 6 m wide, east–west; side walls of conglomerate blocks rising from 0.5 m at the open end to 10 m at the façade, thickness 2 m rising to 3 m [stated: Wikipedia, "Treasury of Atreus"].
- Façade 10.5 m high; doorway 5.4 m high, 2.7 m wide, 5.4 m deep, tapering inward; the innermost lintel block 8 × 5 × 1.2 m (about 120 tons); above it the empty relieving triangle (once closed with a decorated slab) [stated: same].
- Two half-columns of green stone with zigzag shafts either side of the door; two smaller beside the triangle; red stone (rosso antico) bands and a rosette frieze [stated: same].
- The tholos 14.5 m across and 13.2 m high, 33 courses of cut limestone laid as a corbelled dome; nail holes suggest rosettes (possibly of gold) fixed to the stone [stated: same].
- A side chamber, 6 m square, through a doorway 2.5 m high in the north of the tholos [stated: same].
- The dome covered by an earth mound held at its foot by a poros retaining wall; a terrace in front about 27 × 27 m [stated: same]. In the model the mound is the ground itself, a cone over the dome, cut by the dromos. [inferred]

## 5. The town
- The lower town round the citadel, houses and workshops on terraces; beyond Grave Circle B the House of Shields, the House of the Oil Merchant, the House of the Sphinxes and the West House, dwellings and workshops both [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"]. Ivory-carving, oil, perfume and pottery are the known trades of such houses. [inferred: from the finds' names]
- In the model: houses of rubble socle and mudbrick, plastered, flat-roofed, one and two storeys, with a timber-framed door and small windows, set on terraces along three lanes on the west and south slopes; a granary just inside the gate; workshops with open yards (a potter's kiln, an oil-press yard with pithoi, a weaver's with a warp-weighted loom, a smith's). [inferred: forms]
- A postern gate (undecorated) in the north wall [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"].

## 6. Ground and water
- The acropolis a rocky ridge between ravines to the north and south; limestone and conglomerate; the soil dry, pale and red. The water: a secret cistern 15 m down, reached by a corbelled passage of about 99 steps and fed by a tunnel from a spring [stated: Wikipedia, "Mycenae"]; not built (later than our date). No stream or pond is shown. [inferred]

## 7. Planting
- Olive, fig, almond, pine and cypress on the hills; kermes oak and mastic maquis; wheat and barley in the plain, vines on the terraces [inferred: the Argolid's flora; the carbonised wheat is stated]. Nothing is planted inside the walls.

## 8. Sky and weather
- Lat 37.7: bright dry summer, a high sun, some cloud in spring; hot, a thin haze over the plain. [inferred]

## 9. People and sound
- Men in the kilt or the short tunic, bare-chested or with a cloak, long hair, some bearded; women in the flounced skirt and fitted bodice, hair long or bound; the dress of the Mycenaean frescoes and of the Minoan model they took over. Warriors with boar's-tusk helmets, spear, round or figure-eight shield. Priestesses at the circle. [inferred: from the frescoes and the Linear B tablets' trades; Knossos's dress reused]
- Trades: the *wanax* and his household, scribes, bronzesmiths, potters, weavers, oil and perfume makers, ivory carvers, porters with pithoi and sacks, herders. [stated: Linear B tablets, common knowledge; inferred as to numbers]
- Sound: dry wind, cicadas, goat and sheep bells, donkeys, dogs, a lyre. The lyre's four- or seven-string forms are shown in Mycenaean art. [inferred]
