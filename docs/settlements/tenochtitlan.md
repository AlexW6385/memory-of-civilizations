## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Templo Mayor (Huey Teocalli), founded 1325, enlarged at least seven times to 1519 [stated: Studio Matrx]; the sixth temple dedicated 19 December 1487, the precinct walled with serpent heads [stated: Wikipedia; ancient-history-sites].
- **Moment shown:** 1519, the seventh temple, as Cortés saw it [stated: Wikipedia].

## 3. Orientation

The Templo Mayor faces west, its east–west axis aligned to a sunset in early April [stated: ancient-history-sites]; ours due west.

## 4. The style

Stucco over stone and rubble, painted; the Tlaloc shrine blue and white, Huitzilopochtli's red and white [inferred from the codices and the excavated earlier stages]; serpent heads at the stairs' feet and along the wall; flat-roofed houses of adobe and stone plastered white. [inferred]

## 5. The town

One-storey houses of adobe round patios, the canals as streets with canoes, footbridges; chinampa plots with maize and willows (ahuejote) at the edges. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The island level a little above the lake; canals and the lake at the edge. (`ground.js`) [inferred]

## 7. Planting

Willows (ahuejote) along the chinampas and canals, maize fields; few trees in the centre. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

High valley (2,240 m): clear dry-season skies, afternoon thunderclouds in the rains. Latitude 19.4° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Drums (huehuetl, teponaztli), clay flutes and conch trumpets, rattles (a plucked string, the ney and a drone stand in). [inferred]

## 10. Detail (L2): the shrines, the sculpture, the canals and the market

- **The shrines.** Tlaloc's on the north painted with blue stripes, Huitzilopochtli's on the south painted red; each held a wooden image of its god [stated: World History Encyclopedia]. Ours: Tlaloc's walls white with upright blue stripes, Huitzilopochtli's red; framed doorways to the west; a tall roof stage over a cornice — Tlaloc's blue with rows of white rings (chalchihuitl, precious water), Huitzilopochtli's white carved with rows of skulls — and a crest of merlons [inferred: after the codex pictures of the twin temple].
- **The stairs**: Tlaloc's flight painted blue and white, Huitzilopochtli's bright red; both with sculpted snake heads — Tlaloc's with blinkers (goggles), Huitzilopochtli's adorned with feathers [stated: World History Encyclopedia]. Ours: Tlaloc's treads white with a blue band on each riser; Huitzilopochtli's red; the heads in plaster after the shared serpent carving (meso.js), goggled or crested.
- **The sculpture on the summit**: a chacmool from Tlaloc's shrine [stated: World History Encyclopedia] — ours before his door; the stone over which victims were stretched [stated: ibid.] — ours a techcatl before Huitzilopochtli's door [inferred: its form]; braziers beside the stairheads [inferred].
- **The Coyolxauhqui stone**, a massive round stone about 3.5 m across at the pyramid's base, where the bodies fell [stated: World History Encyclopedia] — ours 3.5 m, flat at the foot of Huitzilopochtli's stair, the dismembered goddess in low relief [inferred: composition].
- **Canals and bridges**: the lesser streets "half land and half water, navigated by canoes"; openings in the streets crossed by wide bridges "composed of large pieces of timber, of great strength and well put together" [stated: Cortés, Second Letter]. Ours: stone canal walls with a coping; at each avenue a timber bridge of five great beams under a plank deck with kerbs (was a Chinese stone arch); the bridges could be lifted [stated: ibid., the causeways' openings].
- **Canoes**: dugouts, flat-bottomed and square-ended [inferred: the acalli of the codices], poled along the canals or moored, laden with maize, chiles, pots, cloth or reeds (was a Chinese awninged sampan).
- **The market (tianguis)**: the great market was Tlatelolco's, set up every day, where each kind of merchandise had its own street or quarter and the sellers their marked seats, goods coming in by very many canoe trips — maize, beans, chiles, squash, pottery, metates, mats, baskets, cloth, firewood, charcoal, lime [stated: Cortés; Díaz, via Mexico News Daily; Wikipedia, Tianguis]. Ours: a neighbourhood market on the west canal south of the causeway's avenue [inferred: a calpulli market in the manner of the great one], its rows by kind of goods, each seller squatting on a reed mat by heaps of maize, chiles and beans, pots, baskets, folded cloth, firewood or reeds, some under awnings of matting on poles; two landing stairs down to the canal, canoes unloading.

## 11. Detail (L2+): the houses, the palace, the tzompantli, the ballcourt, the chinampas

- **The houses of the calpulli.** Most commoner houses in Tenochtitlan were small structures arranged in walled compounds; elsewhere houses stood on low platforms of cut stone a single course high filled with rocks and soil (about 40 cm), their adobe walls on two rows of foundation stones; lime-plaster floors are found; Sahagún mentions flat pole-and-beam roofs; houses formed patio groups, each structure a household [stated: Smith, Aztec houses (latinamericanstudies.org)]. Lords' houses had "others, both upper and lower, that contain conservatories of flowers" [stated: Cortés, Second Letter (Fordham)]. Ours (`calli`, src/styles/aztec/houses.js): a 0.42 m platform of andesite with a plaster top; two or three one-room houses on its sides (the back one always), adobe plastered white on two courses of stone, their doors on the patio with a timber lintel, a threshold stone and a rolled reed mat; roof beams (vigas) from the patio wall to the back wall projecting at both ends, the earth roof plastered with a parapet and a stone spout; a compound wall with its gate and step on the street closing the rest [inferred: arrangement]. In the patio a basalt metate with its mano and a pot, sometimes a woman kneeling at it; the great water jar; a jar-shaped clay maize granary (cuezcomatl) under a thatch cap, a small plastered house altar with a clay figure and a censer, or baskets; a ladder to the back roof; on many roofs a raised bed or clay pots of plants with marigold and dahlia flowers [inferred: the objects of a Mexica household; the flowers after Cortés].
- **The palace of Moctezuma (tecpan).** In the Codex Mendoza (f. 69r) the palace's rooms are spread over two floors, with a patio toward the viewer on the upper level; the buildings are white, their entryways framed with terracotta-coloured beams with yellow reed mats (petlatl) inside; the glosses name rooms for courts and war councils; Moctezuma sits on a mat seat in the upper middle room; concentric circles run across the upper facade, a sign associated with royal palaces (tecpan) [stated: Aztec Glyphs, tecpancalli (Mdz69r)]. Cortés: palaces "so wonderful that it is hardly possible to describe their beauty and extent"; "a large court-yard, paved with handsome flags, in the style of a chess-board"; "a beautiful garden with balconies extending over it, supported by marble columns"; a floor of jasper [stated: Cortés, Second Letter (Fordham)]. Ours (`tecpan`): a 1 m platform with its stair from the plaza on the west; solid ranges of rooms round two courts joined by passages framed in red beams — the first court paved in squares of basalt and pale stone, the second a garden of flower beds round a stone-rimmed pool; porticoes of square piers on stone bases under red beams along the courts, doorways between them framed in red beams with mats laid in them; a red dado outside and the band of concentric discs (a white ring on red, a white boss) across the upper facades; over the west range an upper storey set back behind a terrace reached by a stair from the first court, its middle room open on the terrace through five piers under a red beam, with reed mats over its floor, a plastered dais and the king's high-backed reed seat (icpalli) with a jaguar skin, two braziers [inferred: plan, proportions and the seat's form].
- **The Huey Tzompantli.** Excavated 2015–17 at República de Guatemala 24: the northeast end of a low platform about 70 cm high, oriented north–south, estimated 36–40 m long and 12–14 m wide; in its stucco surface holes about 25 cm across in lines 80 cm apart held the wooden posts that carried the rods on which the skulls were pierced through the temples; at its north end a circular structure of skulls bound with lime, set in concentric rows, some facing the centre and others outward, now 1.25–1.8 m high and about 1.56 m thick, believed originally taller; phases VI (1486–1502) and VII (1502–1521) [stated: INAH, Ventana arqueológica Huei Tzompantli]; the platform estimated at more than 34 × 12 m; the skulls cemented with lime, sand and volcanic gravel [stated: The History Blog]. Ours (`tzompantli`): a stuccoed platform 13 × 38 m, 0.7 m high, west of the Templo Mayor's south stair with a stair on its west side [inferred: place and stair]; three rows of posts 0.8 m apart, 4.6 m tall, capped with a beam, five poles strung with skulls between them [inferred: height and number of poles]; at the north end the tower of skulls in lime mortar, 6 m across with a 1.56 m wall and 2.4 m high, skulls facing in and out (instanced).
- **The ballcourt (tlachtli).** Was two banks and raised end blocks with the rings lying flat; now I-shaped: the alley between two structures with vertical inner faces, the wider end zones at court level closed by walls, a line across the middle, and the two stone rings set upright out of the middle of the side walls, their holes along the court, carved with a ring of bosses [inferred: after the codex pictures of the I-shaped court and the surviving rings].
- **The chinampas** now carry maize (was grass standing for it) with grass between the rows, ahuejote willows (a columnar willow, the poplar stands in) along their canals [inferred].
