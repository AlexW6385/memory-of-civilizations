# Khajuraho (the Western Group), about 1050 — dossier

Settlement id: `khajuraho` · Style: `nagara` · Region: India (Bundelkhand) · Code: `src/settlements/khajuraho/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the Western Group's five temples on their terraces — Kandariya Mahadeva, Jagadambi, Chitragupta, Lakshmana with its four corner shrines, Vishvanatha with its shrines — each a row of halls under stepped roofs rising to the sanctum's clustered tower; the Shivsagar tank; the village; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 recorded for later: the 84 spires of the Kandariya tower (ours: nine), the sculpture bands and the makara toranas, the sardula figures at the terrace corners, the Varaha pavilion, the Matangeshvara temple, the Eastern and Southern groups, festivals and dance.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Kandariya Mahadeva temple, built about 1025–1050 under Vidyadhara, the largest of the Western Group. It is about 31 m long and 20 m wide, its tower about 31 m high [stated: Wikipedia]. Elsewhere: 116.5 ft above the ground and 88 ft above the terrace (jagati), which is about 4 m high [stated: slideshare (Sontakke); hindutemples-india]. It faces east [stated: hindutemples-india]. The tower is made of 84 miniature spires (urushringas) [stated: Wikipedia; esamskriti]. The temple shares its terrace with the Mahadeva shrine and the Jagadambi temple [stated: inditales]. Its sandstone is pink and yellow, from Panna on the Ken [stated: inditales].
- **The group:** the Lakshmana, Vishvanatha and Chitragupta temples belong to the Western Group, with the Varaha pavilion [stated: esamskriti]. Panchayatana plans set four corner shrines on the terrace [stated: hindutemples-india].
- **Moment shown:** about 1050.

## 3. Orientation

The temples face east. [stated]

## 4. The style

Nagara: a high terrace; halls in a row under stepped pyramidal roofs rising to the sanctum's curvilinear tower clustered with smaller towers, each ending in an amalaka and a pot; bands of sculpture on the walls. [stated]

## 5. The town

About 200 village houses. [inferred]

**Houses that can be entered (L3, built).** The three houses nearest the arrival are open (DESIGN §4.3): the clay stove (chulha) by the back wall, water pots, two string beds (charpai), a clay grain bin; a step up to the door, the leaf open inside, a ceiling of boards on beams [inferred: the period's village interiors].
