## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Kandariya Mahadeva temple, built about 1025–1050 under Vidyadhara, the largest of the Western Group. It is about 31 m long and 20 m wide, its tower about 31 m high [stated: Wikipedia]. Elsewhere: 116.5 ft above the ground and 88 ft above the terrace (jagati), which is about 4 m high [stated: slideshare (Sontakke); hindutemples-india]. It faces east [stated: hindutemples-india]. The tower is made of 84 miniature spires (urushringas) [stated: Wikipedia; esamskriti]. The temple shares its terrace with the Mahadeva shrine and the Jagadambi temple [stated: inditales]. Its sandstone is pink and yellow, from Panna on the Ken [stated: inditales].
- **The group:** the Lakshmana, Vishvanatha and Chitragupta temples belong to the Western Group, with the Varaha pavilion [stated: esamskriti]. Panchayatana plans set four corner shrines on the terrace [stated: hindutemples-india].
- **Moment shown:** about 1050.

## 4. The style

Nagara: a high terrace; halls in a row under stepped pyramidal roofs rising to the sanctum's curvilinear tower clustered with smaller towers, each ending in an amalaka and a pot; bands of sculpture on the walls. [stated]

**The tower, roofs and porch in detail (L2–L3, built).** The sanctum's tower is a *sekhari* śikhara: a main rekha tower with subsidiary towers (uruśṛṅgas) springing from its sides, engaged with it except at the top, each with its own amalaka and kalasha; Kandariya has 84 of them [stated: Wikipedia "Urushringa"], "clustered against its faces and flanks, and climbing them in tier upon tier", the tower "a tall curvilinear cone that swells outward and then draws in to a ribbed stone disc", capped by "a pot-shaped finial" [stated: Studio Matrx]. The amalaka is "a segmented or notched stone disk, usually with ridges on the rim", crowned with the kalasha [stated: Wikipedia "Amalaka"]; the tower's bands are "a mesh of gavaksha (dormer window) motifs" and its corner bands carry aedicules (bhumikhandas) [stated: Jio Institute, Neelkanth Mahadev latina śikhara]. The halls have "pyramidal, stepped phamsana roofs of diminishing tiers"; the porch "carries a low roof"; the great hall has "lateral balcony windows" [stated: Studio Matrx]; at the entrance is a torana, "a very intricately carved garland which is sculpted from a single stone", reached by steep steps [stated: Wikipedia "Kandariya Mahadeva Temple"].
Ours: every course of the tower on the stepped ratha plan (central bhadra, intermediate pratiratha, corner karna) [inferred: the Chandela plan type], the curve vertical at the foot and drawing in to the shoulder; ribbed bhumi-amalakas on the corners at every storey line [inferred: the Nagara corner division]; dormer motifs in relief on the bhadra and pratiratha of the larger towers; the crown of shoulder, neck, ribbed amalaka, a second small amalaka, the pot and its citron finial [inferred: the usual Nagara crown]. Round the main tower 28 towers instead of 84 (abridged): on the north, south and west faces a large one engaged against the face, a second further out and two pairs of small ones flanking; two on each corner; on the east the large one only, behind a gable (śukanāsa) with a great dormer medallion over the vestibule [inferred: placement after photographs]. The hall roofs carry rows of little bell-roofed pavilions (ghaṇṭā kūṭas) along each ledge and small spires at the corners, crowned by a great bell (ghaṇṭā) with amalaka and pot [inferred: the Khajuraho phamsana roof]. The porch is open: four pillars square below, then octagonal and sixteen-sided with a garland band, under a ribbed pot capital and cross brackets; the seat with its sloping back (kakṣāsana) along both sides; the architraves; the makara torana arching in seven lobes between the front pillars, a makara at each end. The halls now fit on the plinth (the porch overhung its east end before), and a steep flight climbs from the terrace up the plinth to the porch [stated: steep steps; inferred: dimensions].

## 10. Detail added (2026-11 pass: Lakshmana's platform, its corner shrines, the Varaha pavilion)

- **Lakshmana's platform.** The temple faces east, about 30 × 14 m, "constructed on high raised jagati (platform) of 10 feet height which is approached by a fleet of steps"; "the only temple which preserves the sculpture panel on the platform", depicting "everyday life: a royal hunt, battle, dance, musicians, erotic scenes, traders" [stated: hindutemples-india]; friezes along the plinth "depict images of daily life, love, and war" [stated: Smarthistory]. Ours: the platform enlarged to 43 × 26 m and lowered to 3.1 m (it was the size of the temple's own terrace, too small for its shrines, which hung over its edges), its sides moulded — a projecting base, a coping, and between two fillets a sunk band carved all round with processions of small figures in the round: elephants with riders, horsemen, foot soldiers with shields, dancers, drummers, couples, porters with their loads [inferred: the band's height and the figures' forms and order].
- **The corner shrines.** Four subsidiary shrines at the corners of the jagati (the panchayatana plan) [stated: arch-heritage; Smarthistory], "like miniature temples with their own vimanas" [stated: Smarthistory]; "each subsidiary shrine is like a separate temple with a sanctum and entrance porch. The subsidiary shrine at the back faces east and the subsidiary shrines at the front faces each other" [stated: hindutemples-india]. Ours: each on its moulded base, the sanctum under its curvilinear tower, an entrance porch of two pillars under a small stepped roof with its bells and spires, four steps down to the platform; the two at the back facing east, the two at the front facing each other across the way to the temple's stair, all now standing wholly on the platform. Vishvanatha's platform is enlarged likewise for its shrines [inferred].
- **The Varaha pavilion.** Of c. 900–925, opposite the Lakshmana temple; "an oblong pavilion with a pyramidal roof of receding tiers, resting on fourteen plain pillars", on "a lofty plinth", built of sandstone; the boar "2.6 m long and 1.7 high", colossal and monolithic, "carved with numerous figures on its entire body" [stated: Varaha Temple, Khajuraho — Wikipedia (IPFS mirror)]. Ours: 12 × 10 m on a 2.6 m moulded plinth east of Lakshmana's stair, its own stair on the side facing the temple; fourteen square pillars (five along each long side, two more on each short side), a ceiling slab and a five-tiered stepped roof; the boar on its pedestal facing north, rows of small carved figures over its flanks, short tusks [inferred: plinth height, plan size, the boar's facing].

## 11. Detail added (2026-11 L3 pass: the full tower, the things of the pūjā, the life of the temples)

- **Kandariya's 84 spires.** The tower is made of 84 miniature spires [stated: Wikipedia; esamskriti], "clustered against its faces and flanks, and climbing them in tier upon tier" [stated: Studio Matrx]. Ours: the 28 of the earlier pass (§4) and 56 more climbing the main tower — on each face a column of three smaller towers stepping up the bhadra (on the east two, above the śukanāsa, and a small one higher), a pair flanking it at two levels; on each corner three climbing the karna, two small ones at its foot and two on its flanks — 84 with the main tower [stated: the count; inferred: the placement, after photographs of the north and south faces]. The other temples keep the abridged cluster.
- **The doorway.** The porch now leads through a doorway into the halls: a dark opening framed by jambs in three bands (śākhās) rising to a stepped lintel with a small image at its middle, a half-round moonstone before the sill; the hall's sculpture bands stop either side of it [inferred: the Khajuraho doorway type].
- **The things of the pūjā.** Pūjā lays flowers before the image or drapes garlands round it, burns incense, waves a burning lamp before the god and offers food; temple officiants are usually Brahmins [stated: encyclopedia.com, "Pūjā"]. Ours (`life.js`, element `rite`, `src/styles/indian/rites.js`): in each porch a bronze bell hung from the ceiling on the axis; two bronze lamp stands with five lit wicks at the door; a clay lamp, flowers heaped on a leaf and a water pot (kalaśa) with its sprig set down before it; rows of little clay lamps along the seats; two garlands swagged between the front pillars behind the torana; before the Varaha a row of lamps, an offering and a garland on its pedestal [inferred: the placing]. The flowers are white (jasmine) and red (oleander, karavīra): no marigolds, which came from the Americas with Spanish and Portuguese traders [stated: National Geographic].
- **The life of the temples.** In each porch a priest at the sanctum's door — bare-chested in a white wrap, the hair in a top-knot — and a devotee kneeling with joined hands; pilgrims walking round each temple on its terrace with the temple on their right; at the foot of each stair two women selling jasmine and oleander from mats on the ground, a pilgrim coming with a basket of flowers; zebu cows grazing and lying on the lawns, a herdsman sitting by one; at the tank's north margin bathers at the water's edge, women filling pots, a priest at his prayers seated cross-legged [inferred: dress after the temple sculpture; the rest the usage of a temple town].
- **The tank's lotus; plants.** Drifts of lotus pads, with now and then a pink flower on its stalk, along the tank's margins [inferred]; oleander in clumps round the terraces; a pipal (a fig) and neem by the tank [inferred].
- **Weathering.** The place's climate is set damp (0.6): the materials' rain streaks darken the sandstone's faces and moss greens the shaded foot of the walls [inferred: the monsoon; the sandstone of Khajuraho weathers dark].

## 12. Detail added (2026-11 batch pass: the halls and the sanctum as rooms, the living systems)

- **The halls inside.** Kandariya Mahadeva's plan runs porch (ardhamandapa), mandapa, great hall (mahamandapa) with lateral balconied windows, vestibule (antarala), then the sanctum (garbhagriha), each roofed higher than the last [stated: Studio Matrx; Wikipedia "Kandariya Mahadeva Temple"]. Ours (`interior: true` on the temple, style `nagara`): the halls are hollow rooms with 0.7–0.8 m walls, doors cut through each party wall on the axis, the great hall lit by two window slots with stone bars (jali) on each long side and four pillars carrying a ceiling of stepped rings with a lotus pendant, the doorway from the porch left open; the figure bands omitted on faces that abut the next hall [inferred: dimensions from the exterior plan; the pillar count and ceiling].
- **The garbhagriha.** A small dark cell inside thick walls, reached by a narrow passage through the sanctum's east wall; "the sanctum is dark" and holds the image, here a linga [stated: general Nagara usage; inferred: cell size 4.8 m]. ## 15. Detail added (2026-11 L3 detail pass: sculpted walls, plinth procession, the great hall's images, the far village walked into, wayside things)

- **The wall's sculpture.** The wall carries "three rows of sculpture, all equal in size, bounded by two series of mouldings": the lower series a projecting band of kirttimukha (lion-faced) heads over a rosette frieze, the upper a fascia with stencilled scrolls [stated: mahavidya.ca, "The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple"]. Ours (`src/styles/nagara/index.js`, `wallFig`, `dentils`): in the three bands of every hall wall the old cone-shaped placeholders are replaced by modelled figures (six-sided lathes and rods, about 300 triangles each) on a small lotus, in the three-bend pose (tribhanga) with the hips swung one way and the bust the other, a tall crowned headdress, arms placed by kind — an apsara with an arm raised to her hair, one with a hand to the hip, a dancer with arms out and a leg lifted, a musician with a lute, a deity in a niche with four arms under a horseshoe hood (gavaksha), a standing pair side by side with hands joined, an attendant with a whisk — in a fixed order along each band that differs from tier to tier; along each band's lower ledge a row of small dark blocks for the kirttimukha heads. Only the decorum of the sources is modelled: apsaras, dancers, musicians, deities, divine couples standing; no scene beyond that [inferred: the kinds and their order; the figures' forms after the sculpture's well-known poses].
- **The plinth's procession.** "Beneath [the socle's five mouldings] is a recessed band with a processional frieze showing dancers, devotees, acrobats, musicians, warriors, hunters, horses, elephants" [stated: mahavidya.ca]. Ours: on the north, south and west faces of Kandariya's plinth a dark recessed band between the fillets with the same small figures of the jagati's frieze (elephant and rider, horseman, soldiers with shields, dancers, drummers, a pair, a porter) in the round, in place of the earlier row of blocks [inferred: figure kinds and order].
- **The great hall inside.** The mahamandapa has "atlantean brackets on the pilasters support[ing] the ceiling" and "paired nymphs … between the pilasters at the transept entrances"; the vestibule's pilasters show "a Siva door-keeper and a female chauri-bearer" [stated: mahavidya.ca]. Ours: on each pillar's inner face a nymph on a corbel under the beam; on the great hall's west and east walls four images either side of each door, each in a niche of two pilasters under a lintel; in the vestibule on its two side walls the four-armed door-keeper and the whisk-bearer [inferred: the placing and the number].

## 16. Detail added (2026-11 round 2: the landmark seen at the arrival and close)

Kandariya Mahadeva rebuilt where the arrival and the first metres of the walk meet it (`src/styles/nagara/index.js`; no new materials, one geometry per material):

- **The jagati and the plinth as moulded basements.** The temple stands "on a massive plinth" and its sides are "in three vertical layers" of carved bands under mouldings [stated: Wikipedia; mahavidya.ca: "a socle of five mouldings … beneath is a recessed band with a processional frieze"]. Ours (`basement`): on the jagati and again on the hall plinth, a base, a sloping khura, a swelling kumbha and a neck; fillets about the carved band; over it a field of niches with a small god in each, between projecting piers every 5–6 m with pale caps; a kapota eave flaring on a row of dark lion-head blocks; the coping. The earlier plain 4 m slab carried only a strip of figures [inferred: profile proportions and the pier spacing after the usual Nagara adhisthana].
- **The stairs' balustrades.** The flat wedge cheeks are now panelled parapets rising with the flight, pale coping, a newel with its pot at the head and a seated lion at the foot of the terrace stair, a stepped moonstone before it; the porch's flight has the same parapets [inferred: the lion (shardula) and the moonstone are the usual Nagara stair furniture; Kandariya's own are not in the sources I read].
- **Balconied windows.** The great hall's and sanctum hall's lateral windows (kakshasana) are now pavilions projecting 2.3 m: moulded floor, leaning seat-back with panels, pillars (square base, sixteen-sided shaft, pot capital), architrave, a three-tiered sloping roof with its amalaka, instead of a seat and a bare eave [stated: "lateral balcony windows" — Studio Matrx; inferred: dimensions].
- **The tower.** The shaft is a shade darker than the climbing spires so that they stand off it, pale ribs (lata) run up its convex corners following the curve, the dormers (gavaksha) stand deeper, the larger spires have ribbed corners [inferred].
- **The torana.** The seven-lobed garland is thicker, with a scalloped fringe beneath, bud pendants at the cusps, and makaras with a lower jaw, an eye and a curling tail [stated: a garland carved from a single stone — Wikipedia; inferred: forms].

## 17. Detail added (2026-11 round 4: sculpture and relief seen from two metres)

Kandariya Mahadeva's carved figures rebuilt as modelled sculpture (`src/styles/nagara/` — `form.js` the kit, `figures.js`, `relief.js`, `beasts.js`; no new materials, one geometry per figure, every difference between two pieces from a hash of the piece's own place):

- **The wall figures.** The wall carries "three rows of sculpture" [stated: mahavidya.ca]. Ours: twelve distinct figures standing in tribhaṅga (hips swung one way, bust the other, head back again) with rounded limbs, a torso, a head with brow, nose, lips and ear, hair in a chignon or matted knot or crown, a necklace, a girdle, armlets and bangles (swellings of the arm), a skirt falling in pleats that follow the legs: **six surasundaris** — applying kohl, wringing wet hair, holding a mirror (Darpana), removing a thorn (the foot drawn up and held), playing a flute, dancing — and the **gods and their company**: Viṣṇu with discus, mace, lotus and conch; Śiva with trident, drum, snake and a third eye; a whisk-bearer (Chamara); a lute-player; a drummer with the barrel drum (Mardala); a woman with a lotus (Padmagandha). The mirror, the whisk-bearer, the dancer, the drummer, the lotus, the thorn and the dripping hair are among the sixteen surasundaris and the usual maidens of Khajuraho [stated: Sahapedia, "Who are the 16 Surasundaris on Indian Temples?"]; kohl, flute and hair-wringing are among the temple maidens' commonest acts [inferred: not in the sources read]. All are clothed, standing, alone — no couples [decorum]. A dozen in rotation by the hash of the figure's place (a step of five through twelve, so neighbours differ and any twelve in a row are all different), the side of the swing and a few millimetres of height by hashes too [inferred: the poses and their forms].
- **Levels of detail.** Full figures (about 2,000 triangles, only their fronts) on the faces people come to first — the south, along which the terrace is walked, and the east, by the stair — in plans shown within 34 m (south) and 28 m (east) of the face (`sculpt.js`: `nagara_sculpture`, near and far plans); reduced figures (about 500 triangles: fewer sides, no face) on those faces beyond that distance and always on the north and west faces; the old lathe figures on the other temples [inferred: the walking order after the arrival (src/planet/arrivals.js) and the stair].
- **The basement's procession as continuous relief.** "Beneath [the socle's five mouldings] is a recessed band with a processional frieze showing dancers, devotees, acrobats, musicians, warriors, hunters, horses, elephants" [stated: mahavidya.ca]. Ours (`relief.js`): on the jagati and the plinth, round all four faces, groups of rounded solids standing 2–8 cm out of the dark ground, spread to fill each stretch between the piers, on a ridge of earth: an elephant with its mahout and rider, a horse and rider, a warrior with round shield and sword, a drummer, a dancer, a flute-player, a lute-player, a porter with his yoke; walking the way the run goes. - **The lions at the stair.** A seated lion (śārdūla) on each pier at the stair's foot [inferred: usual Nagara stair furniture, §16], modelled: haunches and loins, ribbed chest, forelegs with toes, a head with open jaws and fangs, brows, eyes and ears in a mane of locks in rings, the tail curled up the haunch; the two mirrored.

## 14. Detail added (2026-11 second deepening pass: Kandariya Mahadeva legible from the lane, the pilgrims' lane built up)

- **The temple from afar.** The temple stands "on a massive plinth of 4 metres (13 ft)", its exterior covered "in three vertical layers" of sculpture in horizontal ribbons of carved images, its halls "successively rise in height and width" with stepped pyramidal roofs over the mandapas, and the tower of 31 m carries 84 miniature spires [stated: Wikipedia, Kandariya Mahadeva Temple]. At the arrival (40–80 m) a single warm stone read as one blur, so the carved parts are told apart by tone, not by relief alone [inferred]: the sunk bands of the walls and of the jagati are a darker stone with the figures, pilasters and cornices pale; the dormers (gavaksha) of the tower are dark on a lighter shaft; the roof tiers of the porch, hall and great hall alternate dark slopes and pale ledges and each hall's apex stands higher than the one before (porch 0.32 H, mandapa 0.44 H, great hall 0.54 H, vestibule 0.59 H above the plinth) up to the sanctum's tower [inferred from "successively rise in height", proportions ours].
- **The jagati** of Kandariya now has the carved frieze Lakshmana's has (processions of elephants, horsemen, soldiers, dancers, drummers, couples, porters on a dark sunk band between pale mouldings) [inferred: the Western Group's platforms are carved; Kandariya's own sides are plainer in the sources]; the stair has cheek walls and a pad at its foot.
- **The light.** The temple faces east; the start hour is 11 (sun to the south-east, about 60° off the east front's normal, 65° high) so the front is raked and its ledges cast shadow; at 9.5 the sun stood behind the visitor and lit it flat [inferred].
- **The pilgrims' lane** (street `approach`, z −60, 5 m wide): 14 shop-houses and merchants' houses in continuous fronts from the forecourt (x −24) to the crossing (x 22), the shops all enterable, trades of what is carried up to the god — flowers (jasmine, oleander), ghee and oil for the lamps, sweets for the offering (prasad), clay lamps and pots, cloth [inferred: the trade of a temple approach; no source for Khajuraho in 1050]. Jasmine garlands strung across between the eaves, a brass bell at each shop door, clay lamps and plates of flowers laid on the platforms, the sweet-makers' fires smoking in the day, incense at the stair's foot, flower stalls with cloth awnings in the forecourt, stone lamp columns flanking the lane's end [inferred: lamp columns (dipastambha) are attested in later temples, not for Khajuraho].
- **A fix:** the earlier dressing of every front (goods, pots, sellers) had been placed by the sign of the lot's depth inside the houses, hidden in their walls; it now stands on the platforms and in the lane.
- **Life.** Pilgrims with flower baskets and ghee pots going up and others coming down, a woman carrying a child on her hip, a porter with a pole, a boy with his goat, dogs in the lane's dust, keepers sitting inside the open shops, buyers before them, women at the havelis' balconies; rock pigeons on the temples' porch roofs and about the tower, kites over the village [inferred]; more walkers by day (54).
