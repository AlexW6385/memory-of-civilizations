## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Kandariya Mahadeva temple, built about 1025–1050 under Vidyadhara, the largest of the Western Group. It is about 31 m long and 20 m wide, its tower about 31 m high [stated: Wikipedia]. Elsewhere: 116.5 ft above the ground and 88 ft above the terrace (jagati), which is about 4 m high [stated: slideshare (Sontakke); hindutemples-india]. It faces east [stated: hindutemples-india]. The tower is made of 84 miniature spires (urushringas) [stated: Wikipedia; esamskriti]. The temple shares its terrace with the Mahadeva shrine and the Jagadambi temple [stated: inditales]. Its sandstone is pink and yellow, from Panna on the Ken [stated: inditales].
- **The group:** the Lakshmana, Vishvanatha and Chitragupta temples belong to the Western Group, with the Varaha pavilion [stated: esamskriti]. Panchayatana plans set four corner shrines on the terrace [stated: hindutemples-india].
- **Moment shown:** about 1050.

## 4. The style

Nagara: a high terrace; halls in a row under stepped pyramidal roofs rising to the sanctum's curvilinear tower clustered with smaller towers, each ending in an amalaka and a pot; bands of sculpture on the walls. [stated]

**The tower, roofs and porch in detail (L2–L3, built).** The sanctum's tower is a *sekhari* śikhara: a main rekha tower with subsidiary towers (uruśṛṅgas) springing from its sides, engaged with it except at the top, each with its own amalaka and kalasha; Kandariya has 84 of them [stated: Wikipedia "Urushringa"], "clustered against its faces and flanks, and climbing them in tier upon tier", the tower "a tall curvilinear cone that swells outward and then draws in to a ribbed stone disc", capped by "a pot-shaped finial" [stated: Studio Matrx]. The amalaka is "a segmented or notched stone disk, usually with ridges on the rim", crowned with the kalasha [stated: Wikipedia "Amalaka"]; the tower's bands are "a mesh of gavaksha (dormer window) motifs" and its corner bands carry aedicules (bhumikhandas) [stated: Jio Institute, Neelkanth Mahadev latina śikhara]. The halls have "pyramidal, stepped phamsana roofs of diminishing tiers"; the porch "carries a low roof"; the great hall has "lateral balcony windows" [stated: Studio Matrx]; at the entrance is a torana, "a very intricately carved garland which is sculpted from a single stone", reached by steep steps [stated: Wikipedia "Kandariya Mahadeva Temple"].
Ours: every course of the tower on the stepped ratha plan (central bhadra, intermediate pratiratha, corner karna) [inferred: the Chandela plan type], the curve vertical at the foot and drawing in to the shoulder; ribbed bhumi-amalakas on the corners at every storey line [inferred: the Nagara corner division]; dormer motifs in relief on the bhadra and pratiratha of the larger towers; the crown of shoulder, neck, ribbed amalaka, a second small amalaka, the pot and its citron finial [inferred: the usual Nagara crown]. Round the main tower 28 towers instead of 84 (abridged): on the north, south and west faces a large one engaged against the face, a second further out and two pairs of small ones flanking; two on each corner; on the east the large one only, behind a gable (śukanāsa) with a great dormer medallion over the vestibule [inferred: placement after photographs]. The hall roofs carry rows of little bell-roofed pavilions (ghaṇṭā kūṭas) along each ledge and small spires at the corners, crowned by a great bell (ghaṇṭā) with amalaka and pot [inferred: the Khajuraho phamsana roof]. The porch is open: four pillars square below, then octagonal and sixteen-sided with a garland band, under a ribbed pot capital and cross brackets; the seat with its sloping back (kakṣāsana) along both sides; the architraves; the makara torana arching in seven lobes between the front pillars, a makara at each end. The halls now fit on the plinth (the porch overhung its east end before), and a steep flight climbs from the terrace up the plinth to the porch [stated: steep steps; inferred: dimensions].

## 10. Detail added (2026-11 pass: Lakshmana's platform, its corner shrines, the Varaha pavilion)

- **Lakshmana's platform.** The temple faces east, about 30 × 14 m, "constructed on high raised jagati (platform) of 10 feet height which is approached by a fleet of steps"; "the only temple which preserves the sculpture panel on the platform", depicting "everyday life: a royal hunt, battle, dance, musicians, erotic scenes, traders" [stated: hindutemples-india]; friezes along the plinth "depict images of daily life, love, and war" [stated: Smarthistory]. Ours: the platform enlarged to 43 × 26 m and lowered to 3.1 m (it was the size of the temple's own terrace, too small for its shrines, which hung over its edges), its sides moulded — a projecting base, a coping, and between two fillets a sunk band carved all round with processions of small figures in the round: elephants with riders, horsemen, foot soldiers with shields, dancers, drummers, couples, porters with their loads [inferred: the band's height and the figures' forms and order].
- **The corner shrines.** Four subsidiary shrines at the corners of the jagati (the panchayatana plan) [stated: arch-heritage; Smarthistory], "like miniature temples with their own vimanas" [stated: Smarthistory]; "each subsidiary shrine is like a separate temple with a sanctum and entrance porch. The subsidiary shrine at the back faces east and the subsidiary shrines at the front faces each other" [stated: hindutemples-india]. Ours: each on its moulded base, the sanctum under its curvilinear tower, an entrance porch of two pillars under a small stepped roof with its bells and spires, four steps down to the platform; the two at the back facing east, the two at the front facing each other across the way to the temple's stair, all now standing wholly on the platform. Vishvanatha's platform is enlarged likewise for its shrines [inferred].
- **The Varaha pavilion.** Of c. 900–925, opposite the Lakshmana temple; "an oblong pavilion with a pyramidal roof of receding tiers, resting on fourteen plain pillars", on "a lofty plinth", built of sandstone; the boar "2.6 m long and 1.7 high", colossal and monolithic, "carved with numerous figures on its entire body" [stated: Varaha Temple, Khajuraho — Wikipedia (IPFS mirror)]. Ours: 12 × 10 m on a 2.6 m moulded plinth east of Lakshmana's stair, its own stair on the side facing the temple; fourteen square pillars (five along each long side, two more on each short side), a ceiling slab and a five-tiered stepped roof; the boar on its pedestal facing north, rows of small carved figures over its flanks, short tusks [inferred: plinth height, plan size, the boar's facing].

## 11. Detail added (2026-11 L3 pass: the full tower, the things of the pūjā, the life of the temples)

- **Kandariya's 84 spires.** The tower is made of 84 miniature spires [stated: Wikipedia; esamskriti], "clustered against its faces and flanks, and climbing them in tier upon tier" [stated: Studio Matrx]. Ours: the 28 of the earlier pass (§4) and 56 more climbing the main tower — on each face a column of three smaller towers stepping up the bhadra (on the east two, above the śukanāsa, and a small one higher), a pair flanking it at two levels; on each corner three climbing the karna, two small ones at its foot and two on its flanks — 84 with the main tower [stated: the count; inferred: the placement, after photographs of the north and south faces]. The other temples keep the abridged cluster.
- **The doorway.** The porch now leads through a doorway into the halls: a dark opening framed by jambs in three bands (śākhās) rising to a stepped lintel with a small image at its middle, a half-round moonstone before the sill; the hall's sculpture bands stop either side of it [inferred: the Khajuraho doorway type].
- **The things of the pūjā.** Pūjā lays flowers before the image or drapes garlands round it, burns incense, waves a burning lamp before the god and offers food; temple officiants are usually Brahmins [stated: encyclopedia.com, "Pūjā"]. Ours (`life.js`, element `rite`, `src/styles/indian/rites.js`): in each porch a bronze bell hung from the ceiling on the axis; two bronze lamp stands with five lit wicks at the door; a clay lamp, flowers heaped on a leaf and a water pot (kalaśa) with its sprig set down before it; rows of little clay lamps along the seats; two garlands swagged between the front pillars behind the torana; before the Varaha a row of lamps, an offering and a garland on its pedestal [inferred: the placing]. The flowers are white (jasmine) and red (oleander, karavīra): no marigolds, which came from the Americas with Spanish and Portuguese traders [stated: National Geographic].
- **The life of the temples.** In each porch a priest at the sanctum's door — bare-chested in a white wrap, the hair in a top-knot — and a devotee kneeling with joined hands; pilgrims walking round each temple on its terrace with the temple on their right; at the foot of each stair two women selling jasmine and oleander from mats on the ground, a pilgrim coming with a basket of flowers; zebu cows grazing and lying on the lawns, a herdsman sitting by one; at the tank's north margin bathers at the water's edge, women filling pots, a priest at his prayers seated cross-legged [inferred: dress after the temple sculpture; the rest the usage of a temple town].
- **The tank's lotus; plants.** Drifts of lotus pads, with now and then a pink flower on its stalk, along the tank's margins [inferred]; oleander in clumps round the terraces; a pipal (a fig) and neem by the tank [inferred].
- **Weathering.** The place's climate is set damp (0.6): the materials' rain streaks darken the sandstone's faces and moss greens the shaded foot of the walls [inferred: the monsoon; the sandstone of Khajuraho weathers dark].
