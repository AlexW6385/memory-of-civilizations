## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** El Castillo (the Temple of Kukulcán), Chichén Itzá: a Mesoamerican step pyramid of limestone, 24 m high to the top platform, 30 m with its 6 m temple, a square base of 55.3 m [stated: Wikipedia; Designing Buildings]; nine levels, 91 steps on each of four sides [stated: amazingtemples; geographyworlds]; plumed serpents on the north balustrade [stated: Designing Buildings].
- **Moment shown:** c. 1000, the Maya–Toltec city at its height.

## 3. Orientation

The Castillo's main (north) stair faces the sacbe and the cenote. [stated: general]

## 4. The style

Limestone, lime plaster painted red with blue details; stepped terraces each a sloping base and a panel; stairs with balustrades ending in serpent heads; temples with doorways divided by serpent columns, a frieze and a crest; colonnades of square pillars under flat roofs of beams and plaster. [stated: general; inferred for colour]

## 5. The houses

Oval houses with a rubble footing, walls of wattle and daub, tall palm-thatched roofs; lots enclosed by dry-stone walls (albarradas). [inferred from Yucatec Maya houses]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat limestone plain, gently uneven; the plaza levelled; the cenote a sheer-walled sinkhole. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

Dry forest (acacia and neem stand in; a ceiba-like great tree), fruit trees in the lots, none on the plaza. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

The dry season: hot, bright, fair clouds. Latitude 20.7° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Clay flutes, the slit drum and turtle-shell drums (a flute and a struck wooden sound stand in). [inferred]

## 10. Detail (L2): serpents, masks, the chacmool

- **The serpent heads** at the foot of the balustrades (the Castillo's north stair, the Temple of the Warriors, the Platform of Venus): open-mouthed serpent heads top the balustrades flanking the Warriors' stair [stated: mayaruins]; plumed serpents on the Castillo's north balustrade [stated: Designing Buildings]. Ours carved after the type: the block ending the balustrade, the upper jaw with an upturned snout and heavy brows, the lower jaw open on the ground, fangs and a row of teeth, the tongue over the lip, ear spools, a crest of feathers; the mouth painted red [inferred: paint traces]. About 2.4 m long.
- **The serpent columns**: two giant serpent columns behind the chacmool, their rattlesnake tails rising to hold what was probably a wooden lintel [stated: mayaruins]; the Castillo's temple has the same pair in its north doorway [stated: general]. Ours: the head on the floor, jaws out; the body a square shaft banded with feathers (the bands painted red [inferred]); the tail bent out under the lintel with its rattles.
- **The chacmool** in front of the central entrance, holding an offering plate on its stomach [stated: mayaruins]: reclining on a low base, knees up, propped on its elbows, head turned to look down the stair.
- **The masks**: the frieze carries long-nosed rain-god masks on the corners and in the middle, alternating with vision serpents [stated: mayaruins]. Ours: masks on every corner (both faces) and in the middle of each side but the front; the mask after the Puuc mosaic type — framed eyes, heavy brows, the hooked nose curling up, the mouth with fangs, ear spools [inferred]; the vision serpents left for later.
- **Heights**: the Warriors' temple floor 45 ft (13.7 m) above the ground [stated: mayaruins] — ours 13 m (was 11). The colonnade before it moved 4 m west so the stair's serpents stand clear of it.
- **The warrior pillars**: some 64 square columns in four rows of sixteen, carved with Toltec warriors in battle dress with spears, shields and green quetzal-feather headdresses, once brightly painted, traces remaining [stated: mayaruins]. Ours: every pillar of the colonnades carved on its four faces with a framed warrior in low relief — legs and head, a quilted tunic with a red belt, a round shield, the spear, the green headdress — painted [inferred: the colours beyond the green]. The finer carving (faces, jewellery, the jaguar pelts) is left for L3.

## 11. Detail (L2, second pass, 2026-11-06): the stairs, the ball court, the Caracol, the market

- **The Castillo's stairs** now stand out of the pyramid's faces as solid masses: each tread clears the terrace moulding below it, so the terraces no longer cut through the treads and the upper flight is no longer buried. The stair line climbs at 45° (the foot 6.2 m further out than before) between solid balustrades a metre high [inferred: the angle; the projecting stairs are what every photograph shows]. The same builder gives the Temple of the Warriors and the platforms (Venus, the Tzompantli, the Eagles and Jaguars) stairs that project with real treads (about 0.27 m) instead of near-vertical ones [inferred].
- **The Great Ball Court:** 168 × 70 m overall; "the parallel platforms flanking the main playing area are each 95 meters long", their walls 8 m high; "set high up in the center of each of these walls are rings carved with intertwined feathered serpents"; "at the base of the high interior walls are slanted benches with sculpted panels of teams of ball players"; in one panel "one of the players has been decapitated; the wound emits streams of blood in the form of wriggling snakes" [stated: Wikipedia "Chichen Itza"]. Seven players appear on each side of a skull-shaped central motif; the first on one side is beheaded, the first on the other makes the sacrifice; the platforms have a wide stairway to ascend [stated: mayanpeninsula]. **Built:** the two platforms shortened to 95 m along the alley's middle (they ran the full 146 m before); each bench a low upright face, a slanted face and a ledge; six relief panels on each bench [inferred: the count], each 12 m long: the skull-ball with its speech scroll, the beheaded player kneeling toward it with six serpents and a flowering vine rising from his neck, the sacrificer holding the knife and the head, six more players behind each in yoke, knee pad and plumed headdress [inferred: the composition after the stated subject]; the rings 6.2 m up, now set on the wall (they stood 5 cm off it), each face carved with two strands twined round it, their heads meeting at the top [inferred: the carving's layout].
- **The Temples of the Jaguars:** the upper temple "has an entrance guarded by two large columns carved in the familiar feathered serpent motif" and looks toward the field; the lower temple has its façade facing outside the court, with a jaguar throne, and its outer columns and inner walls are covered with bas-reliefs [stated: Wikipedia; mayanpeninsula]. **Built:** the Upper Temple on the east platform's south end with its serpent columns facing the court, a steep stair of 34 steps up to it from the south [inferred: the stair's place]; under it the Lower Temple facing the plaza, two pillars carved with warriors dividing its doorway, the jaguar throne before it — a standing jaguar, its flat back the seat, its head turned out, its spots carved [inferred: the throne's form after the type of the Castillo's]. The Upper Temple's murals of battle are left for L3.
- **El Caracol:** a round building on a large square platform, named for the spiral stair inside, its doors and windows aligned to astronomical events [stated: Wikipedia]; the main platform 67 m north–south by 52 m east–west, 6 m high; a circular building 16 m across; an upper construction 11 m across and 3.70 m high [stated: mayanpeninsula]; three superimposed buildings; the lower tower's two concentric walls enclose a pair of circular chambers each with four doorways; above the doors a frieze with the mask of Chaac; the whole about 75 ft (23 m) high; the grand stair faces 27.5° north of west [stated: historyrise]; one smaller cylinder on a larger, three small irregular openings surviving in the upper tower, read as sighting shafts [stated: Exploratorium]. **Built** south-west of the Castillo (its real place is further south): the lower platform 52 × 67 × 6 m with a battered face and cornice and a broad west stair (facing due west); the upper platform 24 × 26 m, 4 m high, with its own stair [inferred: its size]; the round base 16 m across [our reading of the 16 m figure]; the tower 11 m across: the outer wall with four doorways to the cardinal points under timber lintels, the inner wall with its four doorways turned 45° [inferred: the offset, after published plans], the solid core holding the spiral stair; a red frieze between mouldings with the rain god's mask over each outer doorway; on the roof the small upper chamber with five narrow shafts [inferred: their number and bearings]. About 20 m high.
- **The market** on the plaza south of the Thousand Columns: the square structure at the south of the Warriors' complex was taken by early explorers for a market, though it is now thought ceremonial [stated: Wikipedia]. **Built:** some twenty sellers sitting behind palm mats with baskets of produce, cloth and dark cooking pots, buyers walking between, some with baskets or jars on their heads; men in loincloth and cape, women in long white cotton dresses [inferred: the market's place on the open plaza, and the dress after Maya figurines and murals]. The Thousand Columns' flat roofs of beams and plaster were already built.
