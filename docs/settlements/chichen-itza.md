## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** El Castillo (the Temple of Kukulcán), Chichén Itzá: a Mesoamerican step pyramid of limestone, 24 m high to the top platform, 30 m with its 6 m temple, a square base of 55.3 m [stated: Wikipedia; Designing Buildings]; nine levels, 91 steps on each of four sides [stated: amazingtemples; geographyworlds]; plumed serpents on the north balustrade [stated: Designing Buildings].
- **Moment shown:** c. 1000, the Maya–Toltec city at its height.

## 3. Orientation

The Castillo's main (north) stair faces the sacbe and the cenote. [stated: general]

## 4. The style

Limestone, lime plaster painted red with blue details; stepped terraces each a sloping base and a panel; stairs with balustrades ending in serpent heads; temples with doorways divided by serpent columns, a frieze and a crest; colonnades of square pillars under flat roofs of beams and plaster. [stated: general; inferred for colour]

## 5. The houses

Oval houses with a rubble footing, walls of wattle and daub, tall palm-thatched roofs; lots enclosed by dry-stone walls (albarradas). [inferred from Yucatec Maya houses]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat limestone plain, gently uneven; the plaza levelled; the cenote a sheer-walled sinkhole. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

Dry forest (acacia and neem stand in; a ceiba-like great tree), fruit trees in the lots, none on the plaza. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

The dry season: hot, bright, fair clouds. Latitude 20.7° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Clay flutes, the slit drum and turtle-shell drums (a flute and a struck wooden sound stand in). [inferred]

## 10. Detail (L2): serpents, masks, the chacmool

- **The serpent heads** at the foot of the balustrades (the Castillo's north stair, the Temple of the Warriors, the Platform of Venus): open-mouthed serpent heads top the balustrades flanking the Warriors' stair [stated: mayaruins]; plumed serpents on the Castillo's north balustrade [stated: Designing Buildings]. Ours carved after the type: the block ending the balustrade, the upper jaw with an upturned snout and heavy brows, the lower jaw open on the ground, fangs and a row of teeth, the tongue over the lip, ear spools, a crest of feathers; the mouth painted red [inferred: paint traces]. About 2.4 m long.
- **The serpent columns**: two giant serpent columns behind the chacmool, their rattlesnake tails rising to hold what was probably a wooden lintel [stated: mayaruins]; the Castillo's temple has the same pair in its north doorway [stated: general]. Ours: the head on the floor, jaws out; the body a square shaft banded with feathers (the bands painted red [inferred]); the tail bent out under the lintel with its rattles.
- **The chacmool** in front of the central entrance, holding an offering plate on its stomach [stated: mayaruins]: reclining on a low base, knees up, propped on its elbows, head turned to look down the stair.
- **The masks**: the frieze carries long-nosed rain-god masks on the corners and in the middle, alternating with vision serpents [stated: mayaruins]. Ours: masks on every corner (both faces) and in the middle of each side but the front; the mask after the Puuc mosaic type — framed eyes, heavy brows, the hooked nose curling up, the mouth with fangs, ear spools [inferred]; the vision serpents left for later.
- **Heights**: the Warriors' temple floor 45 ft (13.7 m) above the ground [stated: mayaruins] — ours 13 m (was 11). The colonnade before it moved 4 m west so the stair's serpents stand clear of it.
- **The warrior pillars**: some 64 square columns in four rows of sixteen, carved with Toltec warriors in battle dress with spears, shields and green quetzal-feather headdresses, once brightly painted, traces remaining [stated: mayaruins]. Ours: every pillar of the colonnades carved on its four faces with a framed warrior in low relief — legs and head, a quilted tunic with a red belt, a round shield, the spear, the green headdress — painted [inferred: the colours beyond the green]. The finer carving (faces, jewellery, the jaguar pelts) is left for L3.

## 11. Detail (L2, second pass, 2026-11-06): the stairs, the ball court, the Caracol, the market

- **The Castillo's stairs** now stand out of the pyramid's faces as solid masses: each tread clears the terrace moulding below it, so the terraces no longer cut through the treads and the upper flight is no longer buried. The stair line climbs at 45° (the foot 6.2 m further out than before) between solid balustrades a metre high [inferred: the angle; the projecting stairs are what every photograph shows]. The same builder gives the Temple of the Warriors and the platforms (Venus, the Tzompantli, the Eagles and Jaguars) stairs that project with real treads (about 0.27 m) instead of near-vertical ones [inferred].
- **The Great Ball Court:** 168 × 70 m overall; "the parallel platforms flanking the main playing area are each 95 meters long", their walls 8 m high; "set high up in the center of each of these walls are rings carved with intertwined feathered serpents"; "at the base of the high interior walls are slanted benches with sculpted panels of teams of ball players"; in one panel "one of the players has been decapitated; the wound emits streams of blood in the form of wriggling snakes" [stated: Wikipedia "Chichen Itza"]. Seven players appear on each side of a skull-shaped central motif; the first on one side is beheaded, the first on the other makes the sacrifice; the platforms have a wide stairway to ascend [stated: mayanpeninsula]. **Built:** the two platforms shortened to 95 m along the alley's middle (they ran the full 146 m before); each bench a low upright face, a slanted face and a ledge; six relief panels on each bench [inferred: the count], each 12 m long: the skull-ball with its speech scroll, the beheaded player kneeling toward it with six serpents and a flowering vine rising from his neck, the sacrificer holding the knife and the head, six more players behind each in yoke, knee pad and plumed headdress [inferred: the composition after the stated subject]; the rings 6.2 m up, now set on the wall (they stood 5 cm off it), each face carved with two strands twined round it, their heads meeting at the top [inferred: the carving's layout].
- **The Temples of the Jaguars:** the upper temple "has an entrance guarded by two large columns carved in the familiar feathered serpent motif" and looks toward the field; the lower temple has its façade facing outside the court, with a jaguar throne, and its outer columns and inner walls are covered with bas-reliefs [stated: Wikipedia; mayanpeninsula]. **Built:** the Upper Temple on the east platform's south end with its serpent columns facing the court, a steep stair of 34 steps up to it from the south [inferred: the stair's place]; under it the Lower Temple facing the plaza, two pillars carved with warriors dividing its doorway, the jaguar throne before it — a standing jaguar, its flat back the seat, its head turned out, its spots carved [inferred: the throne's form after the type of the Castillo's]. The Upper Temple's murals of battle are left for L3.
- **El Caracol:** a round building on a large square platform, named for the spiral stair inside, its doors and windows aligned to astronomical events [stated: Wikipedia]; the main platform 67 m north–south by 52 m east–west, 6 m high; a circular building 16 m across; an upper construction 11 m across and 3.70 m high [stated: mayanpeninsula]; three superimposed buildings; the lower tower's two concentric walls enclose a pair of circular chambers each with four doorways; above the doors a frieze with the mask of Chaac; the whole about 75 ft (23 m) high; the grand stair faces 27.5° north of west [stated: historyrise]; one smaller cylinder on a larger, three small irregular openings surviving in the upper tower, read as sighting shafts [stated: Exploratorium]. **Built** south-west of the Castillo (its real place is further south): the lower platform 52 × 67 × 6 m with a battered face and cornice and a broad west stair (facing due west); the upper platform 24 × 26 m, 4 m high, with its own stair [inferred: its size]; the round base 16 m across [our reading of the 16 m figure]; the tower 11 m across: the outer wall with four doorways to the cardinal points under timber lintels, the inner wall with its four doorways turned 45° [inferred: the offset, after published plans], the solid core holding the spiral stair; a red frieze between mouldings with the rain god's mask over each outer doorway; on the roof the small upper chamber with five narrow shafts [inferred: their number and bearings]. About 20 m high.
- **The market** on the plaza south of the Thousand Columns: the square structure at the south of the Warriors' complex was taken by early explorers for a market, though it is now thought ceremonial [stated: Wikipedia]. **Built:** some twenty sellers sitting behind palm mats with baskets of produce, cloth and dark cooking pots, buyers walking between, some with baskets or jars on their heads; men in loincloth and cape, women in long white cotton dresses [inferred: the market's place on the open plaza, and the dress after Maya figurines and murals]. The Thousand Columns' flat roofs of beams and plaster were already built.

## 12. Detail (L2, third pass, 2026-11-06): the Temple of the Warriors inside, the rites, people

- **The Temple of the Warriors opened.** Maudslay found the summit ruined: "the remains of square columns and the indications of partition-walls can just be made out", the back wall standing a few feet [stated: mayaruins]; the temple's floor was painted red [stated: Charlot, "The Dais of the Temple of the Warriors"]; in the restored temple "small Atlantian figures support an altar similar to that found in the Temple of the Tables" [stated: mayaruins], those being "small carved figures of men with upraised arms" [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of the Warriors"]; the murals, preserved in protected enclosures, show battle scenes [stated: Wikipedia], among them a "Procession of Victors and Captives" [stated: Morris, Charlot & Morris 1931, plate 139]. **Built:** the temple closed but at its west front (the side doorways removed); behind the serpent columns a front hall whose roof rests on a row of four square pillars carved with warriors (the colonnades' pillar); the floor painted red; a red dado 0.9 m high round the walls and above it, on a pale blue ground over a band of ochre earth, a painted procession repeated along both long walls — three warriors in quilted cotton armour with plumes, round shields and flint-tipped spears leading a bound captive [inferred: the composition and colours after the stated subject]; a partition wall with a doorway under a timber lintel into the sanctuary, where twelve small Atlanteans (0.8 m, loincloth, butterfly pectoral, feathered cap, arms raised) carry the stone altar table with its red-painted top against the back wall; on it censers with copal balls [inferred: the number of Atlanteans, the table's size, the offerings].
- **The rites at the Sacred Cenote.** The cenote received hundreds of jade objects; ceramic bowls with large quantities of copal resin into which jade beads were stuffed; gold disks reworked from imported blanks [stated: The Met, "Golden Kingdoms: the Sacred Cenote"]. **Built** at the rim west of the sacbe's end: two priests facing the water, one with a staff; three pilgrims kneeling behind them in prayer, a woman with a basket; the offerings — a ladle censer of fired clay burning copal on a stone, a basket of copal balls painted blue, jade beads and a gold disc on a mat, a bundle of darts laid down [inferred: the blue paint on the copal, the darts and their arrangement].
- **People of the rites:** pilgrims walking the sacbe both ways with baskets and jars, people crossing the plaza toward the Castillo, men climbing its south stair, two priests on its summit, two in the Temple of the Warriors (one at the altar, one in the hall) [inferred: their dress — white cotton, the priests in long cloaks and caps — after Maya murals and figurines].

## 13. Detail (L3, 2026-11-07): the Platform of Venus, the houses inside, the ball game, agave

- **The Platform of Venus.** "A base with a square floor plan more than 25 m on each side", 4 m high, with "four staircases bordered by balustrades ending in a cube, from which a snakehead is projected"; on the cornice board "a feathered serpent with fish among its undulations"; Venus represented "in its outer panels in the form of a Mayan Year Bundle"; a sunken space where Kukulcán or Venus emerges from a serpent's jaws [stated: mayanpeninsula, "Venus Temple"]. **Built** (`src/styles/maya/venus.js`): on the vertical panel of each face, either side of the stair, three framed reliefs 2.2 × 1.1 m alternating — Venus as a Year Bundle (the Venus sign, a ringed eye with four rays, on a blue ground beside the bundle of six sticks bound by two cords with knots) and the sunken panel of the god's face with its plumes and ear spool emerging from the open jaws of a feathered serpent's head (fangs, the eye under the brow, plumes behind) on a red ground; along the cornice on each side of each stair the serpent's body undulating, banded with blue and green feathers, a fish in each trough [inferred: the count and spacing of the panels, the motifs' drawing and colours].
- **The houses opened.** Yucatec houses: "single room, rectangular with rounded, apsidal ends, central doorway in front"; walls of vertical poles and sticks tied with vines, palm thatch [stated: Reed College, Labná huts]. Joya de Cerén: sleeping mats on the floor not yet rolled out; about 70 cooking vessels to a household; manos and metates made locally; hearths with fires lit in them; agave among the community's major crops; wattle-and-daub walls [stated: Wikipedia, "Joya de Cerén"]. **Built** (`maya_house`): the oval wall now a real wall of daub 0.16 m thick with its two doorways (1.0 × 1.85 m) in the middle of the long sides and a step stone before each; a beaten earth floor on the rubble footing; the thatch seen from inside (an inner skin), the wall's head run up into it; at one rounded end the hearth of three stones (k'oben) with the comal, two ollas, the metate, the water jar; at the other a sleeping mat with folded blankets, a stored jar and a basket; sometimes a woman kneeling at the metate [inferred: the arrangement].
- **A game in the Great Ball Court.** The basic outfit a loincloth, sometimes with leather hip guards; kneepads; elaborate headdresses likely only in ritual; teams of two to four; the Great Ballcourt's field 96.5 × 30 m, its rings 6 m up; scholars think it may never have been used for games [stated: Wikipedia, "Mesoamerican ballgame"]. **Built:** two teams of four in loincloths with hide hip belts (red and indigo), one player in a plumed cap, one kneeling, the solid rubber ball near the middle line; watchers sitting and standing along the inner edges of both platforms' tops and on the end temples' platforms [inferred: shown as a game despite the doubt; the numbers and the places of the watchers].
- **Agave.** Two to five agaves (the maguey solid of `plants.js`, smaller) along the walls of each house lot, kept clear of the houses and the gateway [inferred for Yucatán from Joya de Cerén's agave and the Yucatec henequen]. The ceiba (yaxche) of the plaza is wanted but the flora catalogue has no ceiba; the banyan stands in, as before.

## 14. Living systems (2026-11-11): walkers, birds, smoke

- **Walkers** (`movers.js`): men in the loincloth (ex) with a cotton cape, workmen in the loincloth alone, women in the long white cotton dress, bearers with jars on the head, porters with sacks, priests in long cloaks and caps, boys; barefoot, dark-skinned, long hair [inferred: dress after Maya murals and figurines, as §11–12]. Ways: the plaza (busiest), the sacbe, and lanes in the gaps of the 34 m lot grid beyond the plaza, stopping short of the cenote. No boats (the cenote lies 20 m down).
- **Birds** (`fauna.js`): swallows looping over and into the Sacred Cenote and over the plaza, vultures (as crows) high over the temples, sparrows on the plaster and sacbe, an egret on the cenote's south rim [inferred: Yucatán species; the swallows nesting in the cenote's walls are a well-known sight there].
- **Smoke** (`smoke.js`): hearth smoke from a scatter of the house lots' houses at the meals, through the thatch; copal incense at the Castillo's summit, the Temple of the Warriors' altar and the cenote's offering [stated: copal burnt in ceramic bowls at the cenote, The Met; inferred: the temple places].

## 15. Detail (deepening pass, 2026-11-11): the lots, the long houses, the plaza's wear, the cenote

- **The house lot's yard.** Kitchens were open thatched shelters over the hearth; Joya de Cerén kept some 70 cooking vessels to a household, hearths, manos and metates [stated: Wikipedia, "Joya de Cerén"]; maize cribs on posts, drying cobs and chile, hammocks and the logs of stingless bees (hobon, Melipona) are the Yucatec household [inferred: Maya ethnography]; turkeys and dogs were the domestic animals of the Maya [stated: general]; backstrap-loom weaving of banded cotton [inferred]. **Built** (`maya_yard`): per lot a thatched kitchen with its hearth, olla, jar, metate and a woman grinding; two to six of crib, rack, hammock, bee logs, firewood, jars, turkeys, a dog, a loom with a weaver — fewer toward the edge of the town (level 1, 0.6, 0.35). Lots are denser (two to four oval houses, a tenth left as forest).
- **The long houses.** Better families lived in stone-footed rooms on platforms with benches inside, besides the oval houses of the commoners [inferred: the residential groups of Chichén, e.g. the Casa Colorada's neighbourhood; benches are the stated furniture of Maya masonry rooms]. **Built** (`maya_range`): a rubble platform 0.9 m with a stair, a 12 × 5 m room of plaster-faced stone with three doors, a flat roof under a red and blue band, inside a masonry bench with sleeping mats, jars and a hearth outside; in the lots within 270 m of the Castillo, about half.
- **The market's awnings** (`maya_awning`): a thatched roof on poles over each stall, sloping to the back [inferred].
- **The plaza's wear** (`plaza_wear`): stains of damp, patches of new plaster, hair-cracks and weeds in the joints; **stelae** (a slab 3.2 m on a drum with a carved warrior, a painted top) and **small altars** (a low platform with a stair, an altar slab and a ladle censer burning copal, someone kneeling) set about the plaza [inferred: the stelae and altars of the Maya plazas; copal stated at §12].
- **Ceibas** (`maya_ceiba`): the world tree, a buttressed trunk and flat crown, on the plaza's edges and at the cenote's rim [stated: the ceiba (yaxche) as the Maya world tree].
- **The cenote's wall** (`maya_cenote_life`): roots of figs and lianas hanging down the wall toward the water, and rows of swallows' clay nests; **the sweat bath** (`maya_sweatbath`) near the rim: a low stone room under a mound of earth with a crawl door, hot stones and a bench inside, the firebox outside with its smoke [stated: sweat baths stand by the Sacred Cenote — general Chichén accounts; inferred: its form].
- **The Castillo's temple furnished** (`src/styles/maya/interior.js`): a red floor and dado, the battle procession on the east and west walls, the Red Jaguar throne (the inner temple's, found in the earlier pyramid, shown here in the summit chamber) with the chacmool before it, braziers at the corners [inferred: the placing]. Weathering (`maya_weather`) of the Castillo and the Warriors' pyramid: streaks of damp under the mouldings, grass on the ledges; the sacbe's kerb stones and weeds (`sacbe_edge`).
- **Left for later:** the Tzompantli's and the Eagles-and-Jaguars platform's carved panels, the Upper Temple of the Jaguars' murals, the Warriors' frieze vision serpents, the stair into the Castillo's core, the southern Old Chichén.

## 17. Detail pass (2026-11-16): the Tzompantli, the Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars, the serpent balustrades, the houses

Sources: [Chichén Itzá: the Tzompantli — mayanpeninsula](https://mayanpeninsula.com/en/?p=427); [Chichén Itzá: the Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars — mayanpeninsula](https://mayanpeninsula.com/en/?p=451); [Chichén Itzá's Shadows — Popular Archaeology](https://popular-archaeology.com/article/chichen-itzas-shadows/); [The traditional Maya house — Yucatán Today](https://yucatantoday.com/en/the-traditional-maya-house); [Other huts — Reed College, Uxmal and the Puuc](https://www.reed.edu/uxmal/galleries/Thumbnails/other/Other-Huts.htm); [Tzompantli — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzompantli) (the general form: posts with crossbeams, skulls threaded on them).

- **The Tzompantli (platform of skulls).** About 60 m long and 12 m wide, T-shaped with a projection in the middle; the base has a very small slope; three panels decorated with skulls divided by mouldings, the end panels with skulls in high relief, the wider centre panel with two rows, a slightly projecting top strip with one more row; more than 500 skulls found carved [stated: mayanpeninsula]. Pointed poles stood on the platform on which the heads of the enemies were threaded, four by four [stated: mayanpeninsula]; elsewhere posts joined by crossbeams carry the skulls [stated: Wikipedia, general]. **Built** (`cz_tzompantli`, replacing the plain 18 m platform with its row of knobs): a T of 44 × 11 m (shortened from 60 so that it stands clear of the ball court and the Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars) with a projection of 4 × 16 m facing the Castillo, 2.9 m high: a battered foot, a lower panel with one row of skulls, a moulding, the centre panel with two rows, a moulding, the projecting top strip with one row and a cornice — some 850 skulls in the stone, each a domed cranium with two dark sockets, the nasal hole, the jaw and a row of teeth; a stair of twelve steps up the projection's east face; on the top a rack of posts every 1.7 m with four crossbeams carrying small skulls threaded on them [inferred: the length, the height, the count of rows per register, the rack's form and place; the T's orientation, long axis north–south].
- **The Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars.** A square base with four staircases bordered by balustrades decorated with the bodies of feathered serpents, which change slope to form a cube from which the heads emerge; a sloping base, a vertical wall with projecting panels and a cornice; on the outer panels eagles eating human hearts, in the sunken spaces spotted jaguars also devouring hearts; eagles and warriors carrying human heads on the outer body; perhaps a stockade for the skulls of the slain [stated: mayanpeninsula]. **Built** (`cz_eagles`): the platform as before (14 m, 3.2 m, stairs on four sides with serpent heads) with, either side of each stair, a framed relief 2.3 × 1.0 m on a blue ground, an eagle in profile with its hooked beak, folded wing, tail feathers and talons about a red heart on the left (projecting frame), a spotted jaguar in profile, mouth open, a heart under its paw, on the right (deep frame, as a sunken panel) — eight panels; the serpent bodies undulating along the balustrades (below) [inferred: the drawing, the blue ground, the colours, which panel goes where; the warriors carrying heads and the stockade left out].
- **The serpent on the Castillo's balustrade.** At the equinoxes the sun's westward movement casts the pyramid's corner shadows onto the north stairway's balustrade, so that they look like an undulating serpent slowly descending toward its stone head at the foot [stated: Popular Archaeology]. The shadows come of themselves from the stepped mass in the sun of the game (the terraces and the balustrade are solid, the sun is real). **Built** (`cz_stair_serpent`): on the top of each balustrade of the north stair, from the head's block to the temple platform, the body of the plumed serpent undulating (a 0.4 m band wandering 0.17 m either way every 2.2 m) with painted feather bands in blue and red across it; the same on the Eagles platform's four stairs [inferred: the body's drawing, taken from the stated undulation].
- **The houses, by rule** (`cz_house`, replacing `maya_house` in the lots). The Yucatec house is oval with a high pyramidal roof; posts and beams, vertical rods and horizontal rods tied with vines, walls of bajareque round the house except the two doors; usually coated with red earth, grass and tree-bark juice (pak'lu'um); the roof of huano palm leaves in staggered rows; two doors in parallel on the sides for the air to pass; hammocks hung from the two beams; cooking inside, the smoke leaving a black layer on walls and roof [stated: Yucatán Today]. Walls of vertical poles and sticks, plastered and coated with local red clay, the plaster flaking off a neglected hut so that the sticks show; a footing of rubble; lashings of vine; one hut with an apsidal end, another with a rear extension, a storage hut [stated: Reed College]. **Built:** each house takes its front from its seed — the wall of red-earth daub, of lime wash over a red-earth dado, or of bare sticks (a daubed foot, the poles above, two vine lashings); the footing 0.35–0.6 m, the walls 2.0–2.15 m, the roof pitch 0.9–1.12, old or new thatch; a rolled mat above a door; some with a porch (ramada: a thatched awning on two posts over a door with a log bench and a water jar beside); some with an open lean-to at the rounded end holding firewood and jars [inferred: the proportions, the frequencies]; the interior by use — the hearth house as before, a weaver's house (the backstrap loom with the weaver, cloth, a mat), a store house (maize and bean jars, baskets, sacks, a mat) [inferred].
- **Left for later:** the Eagles platform's carved warriors with heads; the Castillo's inner temple and stair; the vision serpents of the Warriors' frieze; the Upper Temple's murals; the southern Old Chichén.

## 18. Landmark pass (2026-11-17): the terraces' panels, the temples' walls and friezes, the serpent columns, the Caracol, the ball court's rings

Sources: [El Castillo — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Kukulc%C3%A1n); [The Temple of the Warriors — mayaruins.com](https://mayaruins.com/chichen/1000Columns.html); [Chichén Itzá: the Observatory — mayanpeninsula](https://mayanpeninsula.com/en/?p=696); [Chichén Itzá: the Grand Ball Court — mayanpeninsula](https://mayanpeninsula.com/en/?p=194); [Mesoamerican ballgame — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_ballgame).

- **The Castillo's terraces** (`src/styles/maya/facade.js`, `terracePanelKit`): each terrace's vertical face carries a row of framed panels either side of the stair (the stair side left bare, the unstaired sides of the Warriors' pyramid filled across) — a two-step frame round a red-painted field with a pair of mirrored stepped frets (xicalcoliuhqui) in relief; before they were four flat bars. The panelled, stuccoed and painted terraces are the pyramid's known character [stated: the nine terraces; the Toltec stepped fret is a stated motif of Chichén's carving]; the fret pattern and the red field [inferred].

### 19.1 The visitor's walk (`walk.js`, `range.js`, `wares.js`, `house.js`)
