## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** El Castillo (the Temple of Kukulcán), Chichén Itzá: a Mesoamerican step pyramid of limestone, 24 m high to the top platform, 30 m with its 6 m temple, a square base of 55.3 m [stated: Wikipedia; Designing Buildings]; nine levels, 91 steps on each of four sides [stated: amazingtemples; geographyworlds]; plumed serpents on the north balustrade [stated: Designing Buildings].
- **Moment shown:** c. 1000, the Maya–Toltec city at its height.

## 3. Orientation

The Castillo's main (north) stair faces the sacbe and the cenote. [stated: general]

## 4. The style

Limestone, lime plaster painted red with blue details; stepped terraces each a sloping base and a panel; stairs with balustrades ending in serpent heads; temples with doorways divided by serpent columns, a frieze and a crest; colonnades of square pillars under flat roofs of beams and plaster. [stated: general; inferred for colour]

## 5. The houses

Oval houses with a rubble footing, walls of wattle and daub, tall palm-thatched roofs; lots enclosed by dry-stone walls (albarradas). [inferred from Yucatec Maya houses]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat limestone plain, gently uneven; the plaza levelled; the cenote a sheer-walled sinkhole. (`ground.js`)

## 7. Planting

Dry forest (acacia and neem stand in; a ceiba-like great tree), fruit trees in the lots, none on the plaza. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

The dry season: hot, bright, fair clouds. Latitude 20.7° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Clay flutes, the slit drum and turtle-shell drums (a flute and a struck wooden sound stand in). [inferred]
