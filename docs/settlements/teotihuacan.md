## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Pyramid of the Sun, about 65 m high on a base about 225 m square, c. 100–200 CE [stated: Studio Matrx]; the Avenue of the Dead about 40 m wide, 2.4 km long [stated: Britannica]; the Pyramid of the Moon at its north end, 43 m, 130 × 156 m at its base [stated: Britannica]; the Ciudadela with the six-level Feathered Serpent Pyramid [stated: Wikipedia]; some 2,000 single-storey apartment compounds [stated: Britannica Kids; Live Science].
- **Moment shown:** about 400, the city at its height, the Adosada built before the Feathered Serpent Pyramid (4th century) [stated: Wikipedia].

## 3. Orientation

The avenue runs nearly north–south (a little east of north, toward Cerro Gordo) [stated: Britannica]; ours due north.

## 4. The style

Talud-tablero: a sloping apron with a framed vertical panel above [stated: Studio Matrx]; adobe-and-rubble cores faced in stone and stucco, painted (red dominant) [stated: Studio Matrx; inferred: the colours].

## 5. The town

Single-storey walled compounds of plastered stone and adobe, rooms round several patios, flat roofs. [stated: Britannica Kids; ancientcivilizations] Walls plastered with lime and painted, most often red; columns standing before a room to shade a terrace; almenas on the roofs [stated: cookjmex]. Ours: a blank outer wall on a talud with one doorway under a painted lintel; a sunken main patio with a small talud-tablero altar in its middle; the four rooms on it raised on low red platforms with stairs down, each opened to the patio by a portico of two square pillars under the roof; the principal room on the north side higher, crested with almenas; a second, plain patio; the other rooms closed blocks with a red dado [inferred: the plan after the excavated compounds' type, simplified to a 3 × 3 grid].

## 6. Ground and water

The level valley floor, rising a little to the north; dry. (`ground.js`) [inferred]

## 7. Planting

Maguey, nopal and grass; fields between the compounds at the edge; few trees. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

High valley: clear dry season, thunderclouds in the rains. Latitude 19.7° N. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Clay flutes, drums and conch trumpets (the ney, a plucked string and a drone stand in). [inferred]

## 10. Detail (L2): the avenue, the tableros, the Feathered Serpent

**The avenue's plazas.** Between the Ciudadela and the Moon the avenue is not a flat road but a run of long rectangular plazas, each raised a little above the one before toward the Moon, lined on both sides by small temple platforms and elite compounds; broad staircases close their ends (Plaza B, 3,723 m², linked to Plaza C and to the plaza north of it by broad stairs; Plaza C, 7,595 m², with a central temple base and platforms on its sides) [stated: cookjmex]; "ramps, stepped elevations and wide plazas" [stated: pilgrimaps]. Ours: platforms 2.4 m high across the avenue (at z −140, −10, 110, 240) and along both its sides out to 52 m, so that each stretch is a sunken plaza enclosed on four sides; a stair 24 m wide up and down each cross platform; on the side platforms the temples, each with its own stair up from the plaza; a low altar platform in the middle of the longer plazas [stated: the central altar of the West Plaza and the temple base of Plaza C — cookjmex]. Where the Sun's and the Ciudadela's fronts face the avenue the side platform is left out. The rise from plaza to plaza is now modelled (§11).

**Talud-tablero, painted.** Each tablero an inset panel framed by projecting mouldings over an inward-sloping talud [stated: Met]. Plaster walls painted red with specular hematite [stated: cookjmex]; the stair of the West Plaza's temple painted with green circles (chalchihuites) outlined in black over red [stated: cookjmex] — ours paints a row of these on every tablero of the avenue's platforms and temples, the frame a darker red [inferred: the motif extended from the one stair to the panels]. The small temples' taluds stuccoed red, the great pyramids left in their stone facing [inferred]. Stairs between plain sloping balustrades (alfardas) [inferred: general Teotihuacan form]. The temples on the platforms with a doorway over the stair and a crest of almenas; almenas "with water-droplet motifs" [stated: cookjmex] — ours plain stepped merlons with a red inset [inferred]. Platform tops plastered; the plaza floors of lime plaster, re-laid in patches [inferred].

**The Temple of the Feathered Serpent.** On the taluds feathered serpents in profile move among shells and conches; on the tableros the same scene larger, the serpent heads projecting through flowers (mirrors), each serpent's body carrying a second head — a geometric reptile, Cipactli or the fire serpent, without a lower jaw, read as a headdress; the two alternate; some 300–400 in all, each about 0.9 m tall and tenoned to stand about 0.9 m out; the bodies in shallow relief ending in rattles [stated: Met]. About a century after it was built, its west front was buried behind the Adosada; the sculptures of the other three sides were later damaged and the burials emptied [stated: Met]. Ours, at 400: heads on the north, south and east tableros, one every 3 m alternating serpent and headdress (about 300), the serpent bodies undulating along the taluds with a shell in each bend; the west face behind the Adosada; the sculpture intact [inferred: the damage dated later]. Obsidian eyes [inferred].

## 11. Detail (L2+): the avenue's rise, the Palace of Quetzalpapalotl, the plazas' altars

**The avenue rises.** Each plaza "raised a little above the one before" toward the Moon [stated: cookjmex]. Ours: the plaza floors at 1.8, 0.9, 0, −0.45 and −0.9 m from the one under the Moon Plaza's platform southward (the arrival's plaza between the Sun's and the Ciudadela's fronts the datum); the step is taken inside each platform across the avenue, so its stair up from the south is taller than the one down to the north; the platforms' taluds carried down at their slope to the lower ground; beyond the avenue's edge the ground eases back to the valley floor over 9 m where no side platform hides it [inferred: the heights, compressed with the avenue].

**The Palace of Quetzalpapalotl.** Main entrance toward the Avenue of the Dead, south-west of the Pyramid of the Moon; existing structures c. 450–500 over earlier ones of 250–300; the name from the mythological birds on the courtyard pillars [stated: Wikipedia]. Configured round a square courtyard with four porticoed vestibules leading to rooms on three sides, its east side backing on the pyramidal temple base at the Moon Plaza's south-west; large painted tablero-style lintels; the pillars carved and inlaid with obsidian and mica: four circular eye motifs that held obsidian, the owl's head with a green quetzal-feather headdress and a feather crest over the eye, sitting on a throne inlaid with three obsidian stones over three obsidian mirrors, a flower where the body would be, a zig-zag band at the top [stated: Uncovered History]. From the plaza a broad stair rises to a roofed precinct with a door at its back into the palace; the patio's walls painted with stepped frets framed by hooks, the west wall with red stepped figures (xicalcoliuhqui); the pillars' reliefs with butterfly-like wings; the roof's merlons on the patio's east side as the year sign (trapeze and ray); mica discs as mirrors [stated: México Desconocido]. The vestibule a white colonnade, its lower wall painted with rippling water; the patio's square edge raised; a feathered serpent's head at the top of the front stair, on the right only [stated: endow.world]. Ours (`tt_palace`, src/styles/teotihuacano/palace.js): on a talud-tablero base 4.2 m high west of the side platform of the avenue's highest plaza, level with that platform's top, reached by a broad stair 11 m wide up from the plaza (the serpent's head at its head on the right); the vestibule open east through five white pillars, its lower walls painted with blue and green ripples on pale blue; a patio 15 m square, its edge raised 0.4 m with a red kerb and a step down in the middle of each side; twelve andesite pillars round it (corners and two between on each side) each carved on its patio faces with the bird panel (obsidian discs in double rings, mica mirrors, the green headdress), under a white lintel band painted red with white stepped frets and hooks; the portico walls with a red dado of white stepped frets (the west wall white with red xicalcoliuhqui); behind the north, west and south porticoes rooms entered by doorways, a red bench and reed mats in them; a flat roof, red cornice outside, year-sign merlons along the patio's edges and the vestibule's front [inferred: the plan's dimensions, the merlons' extent, the count of pillars, the rooms' furniture]. Not built: the buried Temple of the Feathered Conches below.

**The Jaguars' patio.** A flat square courtyard; a central staircase on its east side to the temple above (over the Feathered Conches); three identical colonnades, each with a central doorway and two jaguars painted on each side of it; a jaguar statue at the foot of the stair, its top third the eyes, the middle the upper jaw, the bottom the lower jaw [stated: endow.world]; jaguars in profile with feathered headdresses, holding a conch and quetzal feathers, speech glyphs from the trumpet's mouth [stated: ontheroadin]; plumed felines holding conch shells [stated: Wikipedia]. Ours (`tt_jaguars`): south of the palace on a low base; the patio 14 m square, porticoes of two pillars on its north, west and south before walls with a central door, on each wall four murals of a netted, plumed jaguar on a red ground in an ochre border blowing a plumed conch from which blue drops fall, walking toward the door; the temple platform on the east with its stair into the patio and the carved jaguar beside its foot [inferred: dimensions, mural size and composition].

**The plazas' altars and people.** The central altar of the West Plaza, the temple base of Plaza C [stated: cookjmex]. Ours (`tt_plaza_altars`): in the arrival's plaza a talud-tablero altar 9 m square and 1.6 m high with a stair on each side, two theatre censers and offerings (a bowl, flowers) on its top, a small altar at each corner with a bowl on it; a lone small altar with its censer nearer the arrival; a priest at the censers (red cape, feathered cap), worshippers kneeling at the stairs' feet, pilgrims and bearers with baskets and pots about [inferred: the quincunx of altars, the censers' form after the theatre-type censers, the dress (loincloth, cape)]. Figures also people the plaza and the palaces.

**The Moon Plaza.** It "contains a central altar and an original construction with internal divisions … four rectangular and diagonal bodies that formed what is known as the 'Teotihuacan Cross'"; the complex had small pyramidal structures, rooms, porticoes, patios and low platforms [stated: Wikipedia, Pyramid of the Moon]. A 20-ton monolithic water goddess (Chalchiuhtlicue) was excavated from the plaza forecourt of the Pyramid of the Moon [stated: Wikipedia, Chalchiuhtlicue]; folds over the abdomen, a rectangular opening at mid-chest, a necklace of three strands of square beads [stated: Waymarking]. Ours: the Cross as a square talud-tablero platform 11 m across with four long red bodies on its diagonals and a stair on the south (`tt_cross_altar`); the goddess 3.2 m on a low base beside the central altar facing south, block-like, with the wide tasselled headdress and ear spools, hands before the chest (`tt_goddess`) [inferred: the Cross's arrangement, the statue's height and details beyond the stated].

## 12. Detail (L3): the compounds' rooms, weathering, maguey and nopal

**The rooms and their things.** At Oztoyahualco, a three-household commoner compound, analysis of the floors identified kitchen residues and food remains; the residents made tortillas and cooked squash and maize; households were self-contained and conducted their own domestic rituals; residents made pottery, obsidian tools and fired clay figurines (moulds and kilns found) [stated: Manzanilla, Berkeley Review of Latin American Studies 2015]. A housebuilder installing a hearth and a smoke hole; the comal for tortillas; the metate worked beside the hearth; reed mats (petlatl) to sleep on with a pillow; pots and baskets for stores [stated, for the Mexica house of the Florentine Codex: ancient-history-sites, "Aztec Housing and Living Conditions"] — taken for Teotihuacan too [inferred: the same highland household]. **Built** (`rooms.js`, `../maya/household.js`): behind each portico on the main patio a row of two or three rooms, each with its own doorway (threshold stone, wall carried over it) on the portico and a back room through a doorway in a cross wall; plastered floors, a red dado 0.9 m high with lime plaster above, the flat roof on beams. The middle room behind the principal (north) portico is the household shrine: a talud-tablero altar against its back wall with a theatre censer, cups, a mat before it, the ceiling smoked over it (its back room closed). One room off the west portico is always the kitchen: the hearth of three stones with the comal, ash and sticks in a back corner, a plume of soot up the wall over it and a stain on the ceiling, two ollas, the metate with its mano and often a woman kneeling at it, the great water jar by the door, a stack of firewood, a stored jar. The other rooms are sleeping rooms (reed mats along the side walls with folded cotton blankets with a woven border and a rolled one, a jar, a basket, a stack of cups) or store rooms (two rows of jars stopped with cloth along a wall, a basket of pigment, an olla) [inferred: the rooms' sizes, the division, which room holds what].

**Weathering.** The compounds' outer walls: the plaster re-laid in patches (whiter lime) and here and there fallen away to show the rubble core near the foot [inferred]. Soot on the ceiling of the Sun's temple over the censers [inferred]. (The engine adds the darker foot of walls, rain streaks and damp on the shaded side to every plastered wall.)

**Maguey and nopal.** "Murals in Teotihuacan depict agave plants, which are also known as maguey plants"; pulque is made from maguey sap; maguey withstands frost and drought better than maize, whose growing the low rainfall made risky [stated: Live Science, "Ancient People of Teotihuacan Drank Milky Alcohol"]. **Built** (`../maya/plants.js`): the pulque maguey as a rosette of some 27 thick, channelled, spine-tipped leaves in three whorls, 1–1.6 m high, in rows a metre and a half off the compounds' outer walls and in small plots in the open ground between them; a nopal now and then (a woody trunk, jointed pads in four tiers, red or yellow fruit on the topmost rims) [inferred: the nopal at Teotihuacan; the rows and plots]. The flora catalogue has neither; they are built as instanced solids.

## 13. Detail (L3, batch): the painted room, the obsidian workshop, the market

**Murals in the compounds.** Murals covered many of the city's buildings, most survivors in apartment compounds; subjects deities, rulers or priests, carnivorous animals; wealthy homes had painted walls and floors, modest ones plastered but less decorated [stated: Met essay; popular-archaeology]. The Tepantitla "Paradise of Tlaloc" (Tlalocan): a large central mountain pouring water with fish into rivers and canals that irrigate fields; above it the Great Goddess (hawk headdress, masked eyes, ear-flares, nose-bar with pendants), a flowering tree growing from her head, drops falling from her hands; human figures in red, yellow and blue swimming in the water, ball-players, a catch game with a small blue ball, butterfly hunters with branches and nets, priests sowing and offering; commoners barefoot in a loincloth (maxtlatl), unadorned [stated: uncoveredhistory (Tepantitla Mural 3); popular-archaeology]. Ours (`mural.js`, role `mural` in `rooms.js`): about one compound in five has a painted room off its principal (south) portico, closed at the back: on the back wall the Tlalocan as a red field in a darker frame — the stream along the foot with fish and swimmers, the four-step green mountain with its cave and cascades, maize in the fields, ball-players and a butterfly hunter on the flanks, the Goddess over the mountain with her green headdress, falling drops and the blossoming tree; on each side wall a procession of plumed priests in profile under a white stepped-fret band, water-drop glyphs between them [inferred: the composition and its proportions, the colours (a red ground), the figures' drawing, the room's use]. Paint is flat shapes in layers 1.4 cm apart on the wall's face.

**Obsidian workshops.** Compound 17 in the Tlajinga district was "a locus of large-scale obsidian craft production" identified from surface concentrations of debris and confirmed by excavation [stated: Ancient Mesoamerica, excavation of an obsidian craft workshop at Teotihuacan]; obsidian from Cerro Soletepec made tools, knives, spearheads and blades, and lined war clubs; skilled craftspeople lived where they worked [stated: popular-archaeology; Met essay]; residents of a commoner compound made obsidian tools and fired figurines [stated: Manzanilla 2015, §12]. Ours (`market.js`, role `workshop`; about one compound in five, mostly near the Ciudadela and the Moon): a room off the west portico with a heap of raw nodules and a row of prismatic cores against the back wall, two knappers sitting face to face on a reed mat with an anvil stone, hammerstones and a drift of flakes between them, a basket of finished blades, blades laid in rows on a cloth by the door, flake drifts in the corners; and in the second patio the midden of flakes and spent cores [inferred: the arrangement, the sitting posture, the stones' form].

**The Great Compound's market.** The Great Compound stood on the west side of the avenue across from the Ciudadela, "the marketplace", seat of traders [stated: popular-archaeology]; a monumental low-lying structure opposite the Ciudadela that possibly served as a centralized market, more excavation needed [stated: Met essay]. Ours (`tt_market`, layout `MARKET`): a plastered plaza 124 × 174 m behind a wall with a gate in each side, a small talud-tablero platform with a temple at the crossing of two broad lanes, stalls in back-to-back rows in the quadrants — a mat on the ground, the wares laid on it (pots and jars, bowls, obsidian blades and cores, baskets of produce, folded cotton blankets, agave-fibre bundles) under a reed-mat shade on four poles, a seller kneeling or squatting at the back — and bargainers and bearers in the lanes [inferred: the plan, the stalls (ground mats rather than tables: the later Mexica market's usage), the wares after §12's household things and the stated goods: mica, cotton blankets, obsidian].

**Fixed.** The compounds' footing was a solid frustum whose top roofed the whole compound at 0.9 m, burying the patios and the rooms on the 0.5 m platforms; now a ring (the courts and the rooms' floors are seen and walked at their own heights).

## 14. Life: people, birds, smoke

Dress: commoners barefoot in a loincloth, a cloak of ixtle (agave fibre); thousands of elites in cotton blankets [stated: popular-archaeology]; priests in feathered caps and red capes (§11). Ours (`movers.js`): men and women (wrapped skirt, shawl), bearers with baskets and jars on the head, traders with sacks, priests, boys, about 52 by day near the visitor, on the avenue, the grid streets between the compounds and the market's lanes [inferred: the numbers, the cloth colours]. Smoke (`smoke.js`): kitchen smoke from some three in five compounds at the morning, noon and evening meals; incense on the plaza altars' censers [inferred]. Birds (`fauna.js`): swallows over the plazas and the market, crows over the pyramids, sparrows on the paving [inferred].

## 15. Detail (deepening): the streets' faces, the crafts, the avenue's life, weathering

**Streets and the faces on them.** "Streets and lanes connected homes to markets, workshop zones, temples", and "streets also required continuous repair"; walls "often plastered and, in wealthier or more ritually important spaces, painted"; "roofs may also have served as work and drying areas" [stated: dailylifehistory, Daily life in Teotihuacan during the 5th century CE]. Protein from "turkeys, dogs, rabbits, deer, fish, waterfowl" [stated: same] — so turkeys and dogs about the doors. Ours (`street.js`, called from `compound`): on every outer wall a red band under the roof's edge with white stepped frets (instanced), a crest of plastered merlons, roof drains with a streak of damp below each, the doorway's stone step and painted jambs; and set out by the wall in slots by chance — water jars, firewood, a plastered bench with someone sitting, a mat with goods and a seller, trays of maize drying, turkeys, a dog, sacks and baskets, a woman grinding at her door, a ladder to the roof, washing on a line, a little shrine in a niche with offerings, cobs hung by the door, pairs talking, children [inferred: all of it, after the stated household things of §12; the ladder and the niche are ours]. The grid's streets are beaten earth without grass; the empty lots are plots of maguey with a nopal hedge; the city's edge is milpa (maize in plots with fallow), with huizache (acacia), oak and tejocote (apple stand-in) in the outer lots [inferred].

**Craft compounds.** Residents of a commoner compound made pottery, obsidian tools and fired figurines (moulds and kilns found) [stated: Manzanilla 2015]; potters and textile workers among the city's workers; spinning, weaving, tying and repair largely at home; cotton scarcer and costlier than maguey fibre, perhaps got by trade or tribute [stated: dailylifehistory]. Ours (`craft.js`, about a third of the compounds that are neither painted nor obsidian workers): a weaver's room — a backstrap loom hung from the roof beam with its warp and woven stripes and a woman kneeling at it, a spinner squatting with spindle and baskets of raw cotton, skeins on a pole, dye vats, folded cloths — with dye vats and a washing line in the second patio; a potter's room — heaps of clay, a man kneeling at the turntable slab, unfired pots on a mat, polishing stones, stacks of orange bowls, sherds — with a kiln (a rubble dome with its fire-hole, wood stacked beside it, a man squatting at it, pots waiting) in the second patio [inferred: the forms and their arrangement; the kiln as a dome].

**The market.** More stalls, dressed: a cloth or reed shade, strings of chilies and gourds hung from its front, food stalls (a hearth with the comal, a stack of tortillas, ollas and a woman kneeling at it), feather stalls (green and red feathers standing in a jar), children, turkeys and dogs in the lanes, two watchers inside each gate and braziers at the lanes' heads [inferred: after the Mexica market's usage; the goods after §13 and the stated trade in feathers and cotton].

**The avenue and the courts.** The avenue's plazas were laid with lime plaster and repaired as the city's streets were; ours shows the masons at it (`dress.js`, `tt_avenue`): a lime trough, two plasterers kneeling at a whiter fresh patch, a man carrying lime; incense braziers (stone bowls on three stones) along the plazas' sides and about the Cross, the smoke rising from those at the arrival plaza, the Moon Plaza and the Ciudadela's court; drain slabs let into the floor; a worn way up the middle; husks and petals on the floor; offerings of bowls at the altars; sweepers; bearers resting with their loads; women selling on mats at the edges; groups talking, boys at a ball game; dogs and turkeys [inferred: all; the drains after the city's stone-lined drainage, not dated]. The Ciudadela's court has its plaster floor, re-laid in patches, and the same life about the Feathered Serpent Pyramid [inferred].

**Puma mural.** The Puma mural on the avenue, east side, just before the Moon Plaza [stated: Uncovered History, W2-0023]. Ours (`tt_puma`): on the panel of the east platform before the Moon Plaza, on each side of the temple stair, a row of pumas in profile, spotted, ochre on the red ground in a border, crested with feathers, mouth open with drops of water; every fourth turned the other way [inferred: the drawing after the subject; the platform as its wall].

**Weathering.** On the Sun, the Moon, the Feathered Serpent and the Adosadas: patches where the lime plaster has fallen away to the rubble core with a whiter new patch beside some, flakes of the old red paint on the lime, and long streaks of damp running down from the ledges, more on the Sun [inferred]. On the compounds the streaks under the drains.

**Temples entered.** Besides the Sun's chamber (§12): the chambers of the four platform temples round the Moon Plaza, of the big temple on each avenue side platform, of the Feathered Serpent Pyramid's temple, and of the temples facing in or out on the Ciudadela's east and west banks — each a room within thick walls with the painted dado, the band of chalchihuites, the altar with its censers and a priest kneeling [inferred: the same chamber as the Sun's, smaller]. The Pyramid of the Moon's own temple stays closed (its stair faces south; the chamber builder takes a door on an east or west face).

**Sound.** The market with a drum and the flute; the households quieter; the palace, the Jaguars' patio and the Sun's chamber as halls [inferred].
