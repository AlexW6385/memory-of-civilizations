## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the abbey church of Sainte-Foy, built about 1050–1120, a pilgrimage church on the road to Santiago [stated: `docs/buildings/sainte-foy-conques.md` and its sources].
- **The village:** Conques stands on a steep hillside above the confluence of the Dourdou and the Ouche. Its houses are of schist and limestone with roofs of schist slabs (lauzes) [stated: Wikipedia "Conques"].
- **The bridge:** the "Roman bridge" over the Dourdou, a medieval bridge used by pilgrims [stated: Wikipedia "Conques"].
- **Moment shown:** about 1120, the church completed.

## 1b. The bridge over the Dourdou (built: `pont` in `src/styles/romanesque/index.js`)

- A semicircular-arch masonry bridge of the 14th century, five spans of 2.00, 3.80, 6.50, 10.30 and 6.60 m, its deck 2.30 m wide [stated: Structurae "Conques Bridge"]. The pilgrims to Santiago arrived in Conques by this bridge [stated: Callahan, Illinois Wesleyan].
- **Date:** the standing bridge is later than our moment (c. 1120); we build it as the type of the crossing the pilgrims used, an earlier bridge on the same site being assumed [inferred].
- **Built:** the five round arches in that order across the river, the great one over the stream; piers of 2.0–2.4 m [inferred]; the body of schist rubble with the arch rings in dressed stone standing a little proud; pointed cutwaters on both sides of each pier, capped with lauze; a paved deck humped over the great arch, 2.3 m between low parapets with a coping [inferred: the parapets and the cutwaters, after medieval bridges of the Rouergue]. Replaces the generic flat stone bridge.

## 1a. The tympanum of the Last Judgement (built: `tympanum` in `src/styles/romanesque/index.js`)

- About 6.70 m wide and 3.60 m high, a wide half-circle set in the deep arch of the west portal; first half of the 12th century, probably under Abbot Boniface (1107–25); at least 124 figures; three registers parted by bands bearing inscriptions; once brightly painted, blue dominating Paradise and red Hell [stated: tourisme-conques.fr "The tympanum"].
- **Top:** the cross with the inscription, the sun and moon, angels holding the nail and the lance, angels sounding horns at the corners [stated: christianiconography.info]. Built: the gilt cross, two angels, a gold sun and a pale moon, two angels with horns.
- **Middle:** Christ in majesty in an almond-shaped glory set with stars, one hand raised to the elect, one lowered to the damned; angels with candles at his feet [stated: tourisme-conques.fr]. On his right the procession of the elect: the Virgin, Saint Peter with the keys, the hermit Dadon, an abbot leading Charlemagne, saints; on his left four angels with a book, a censer, a lance and a sword, then the damned and their devils [stated: christianiconography.info].
- **Between the registers:** Saint Michael weighing a soul while a devil presses on the scale; the dead rising from their tombs; Sainte Foy kneeling before the hand of God [stated: both sources].
- **Lowest register:** on the left Abraham enthroned with souls in his lap among the just under the arcades of the heavenly Jerusalem, the gate of Paradise; on the right the mouth of Hell receiving the damned and Satan enthroned among the torments [stated: christianiconography.info; art-roman-conques.fr: the compartments as houses with gabled roofs, gates, walls and arcades].
- **Archivolts:** three rings, the little "curious" heads peering over the inner one [stated: tourisme-conques.fr names them; their placing inferred].
- Figures are composed as simple carved and painted masses in the manner of the sources, not copied from photographs [inferred].

## 1c. The capitals (2026-11 L3; `pier` and `column` in `src/buildings/sainte-foy-conques/style.js`)

- The church has 212 columns with decorated capitals, of palm leaves, symbols, biblical monsters and scenes from the life of Sainte Foy; they "functioned as didactic picture books for both monks and pilgrims" [stated: Wikipedia "Abbey Church of Sainte-Foy"].
- **Built:** every capital of the piers' half-columns, the hemicycle, the tribunes' colonnettes and the cloister carved instead of the plain block: an astragal, a bell flaring to the square abacus with its chamfer, and on it either foliage — a lower tier of eight leaves and an upper tier of four rising at the corners into volutes under the abacus, in three patterns (broad leaves; with a rosette on the abacus; curling palms) — or figures: a figure on the middle of each face between the corner leaves (two sizes). The pattern of each capital is chosen by its place, about two in five figured [inferred: the proportion and the compositions, in the manner of the period's Corinthianising and historiated capitals; no single capital is copied].

## 1d. The altar and the treasure (2026-11 L3; `foy_treasure`)

- The treasure was "kept in the abbey-church until 1875" [stated: tourisme-conques "The treasure"]. Its pieces of our moment: the Majesty (built before); the "A of Charlemagne", ordered by Abbot Bégon III (1087–1107), of filigree and enamelled settings with an antique carnelian carved with a winged Victory; the "shrine of Pepin", a small reliquary of the 9th–11th century with Carolingian enamels, gems, filigree and an antique carnelian; Bégon's portable altar, a porphyry plaque framed in silver engraved and nielloed, with busts of Christ, the Virgin, Sainte Foy and saints on its sides; Bégon's "lantern", a reliquary in the form of an ancient mausoleum with an inscription round the base of its roof; the reliquary sent with Pascal II's relics in 1100, with the Crucifixion; the hexagonal reliquary of gold and silver plates with cabochons [stated: tourisme-conques "The treasure"; Callahan, Illinois Wesleyan, "A of Charlemagne", "reliquary of Pepin", "Begon's lantern"].
- **Built:** the high altar under a linen cloth with a red frontal banded in gold, Bégon's portable altar in its middle (about 26 × 16 cm of porphyry), a gilt cross set with stones and two candlesticks with burning candles; on two stands under red cloths either side of the Majesty the A (0.42 m), the lantern (a hexagonal arcaded body under a pointed roof, 0.4 m) and the hexagonal reliquary on one, the shrine of Pepin (a little house-shaped casket, 0.19 m) and the Pascal II reliquary on the other, a candle before each [inferred: the sizes approximate, the stands and their place by the Majesty, the frontal].

## 1e. Wear and the pilgrims inside (2026-11 L3)

- **The threshold** of the west portal hollowed by feet in a shallow trough across the middle of the way (2 cm) [inferred: the pilgrims' traffic — Conques a stage of the Via Podiensis].
- **Pilgrims inside:** four kneeling at the grilles of the choir in the aisles, two kneeling in the ambulatory behind the Majesty, one standing with his staff; the grilles let pilgrims see into the sanctuary, and seeing the reliquary "was the main goal of the pilgrims who came to Conques" [stated: Wikipedia "Abbey Church of Sainte-Foy"].
- **Damp:** the climate set damp 0.75 — moss on the north feet of the walls and the north slopes of the lauze roofs, rain streaks (the renderer's weathering) [inferred: the misty gorge, §7].
- **Plants:** willows and reeds at the Dourdou, poplars on the bank, elms and more apple trees among the houses [inferred: the gorge's vegetation, §6].

## 1f. The village rebuilt (2026-11, batch; `maison`, `calade`, `stairs`, `retain`, `jardin`, `fontaine`, `porte` in `src/styles/romanesque/village.js`; `layout.js`, `town.js`)

- **Site and walls:** houses adapt to the slope and face south; most have two entrances, one at ground level on the lower street and one above on a garden or the upper street; cellars are cut into the rock, with a load-bearing arch against the back wall [stated: tourisme-conques.fr "The village of Conques"]. Conques was walled early, a rectangle of about 250 x 150 m oriented NW/SE, with the Porte de Fer, the Porte du Barry (barrel-vaulted, a semicircular arch with a discharging arch above) and the Porte de la Vinzelle [stated: same]. Built: the Rue Charlemagne's gate as a barrel-vaulted passage with a round arch of dressed stone [inferred: its form after the description; the rest of the enclosure is not built].
- **Materials:** schist is the main material, quarried locally for walls, the roof covering (lauze) and the street cobbles; roofs high and steep with a silvery sheen; door and window frames of cut sandstone or granite; yellow limestone (Rousset) in some buildings [stated: same]. Built: walls of schist rubble (a fifth of the houses of yellow limestone), lauze roofs of 43–50 degrees, cobbled lanes of schist.
- **Period:** "the oldest dwellings date only from the end of the Middle Ages", and the half-timbered, corbelled fronts are a 15th-century technique [stated: same]. For c. 1120 the houses are therefore built as the Romanesque houses of the Rouergue: a ground floor of rubble with a round-headed door, small round-headed windows and, on the shops, a wide round arch over a stone counter; an upper floor of stone with round-headed windows, some twin under one arch with a colonnette; a string course of dressed stone [inferred: after the Romanesque houses of the region, e.g. Saint-Antonin-Noble-Val, not checked on site]. A timber gallery on the upper floor's front of about a fifth of the two-floor houses [inferred].
- **Terraces:** the hill is cut into 14 shelves 22 m apart down the slope, each a cobbled lane 3 m wide with a row of houses on either side, held on its downhill edge by a rubble retaining wall (2.9–4.9 m) with a coping; four stair streets of 2.2 m climb across them, the middle one (x = -80) being the pilgrims' way up from the bridge (Rue Charlemagne), with landings at the lanes and cheeks of rubble [inferred: after the site's slope and the sources above; the shelves' regularity is a modelling choice]. Houses 5–9 m wide, 7–8.4 m deep, one to three floors; continuous rows within 100 m of the church, thinning to walled yards and orchards (about 160) toward 160 m; about 530 houses [inferred: more than the village's real count, which we did not source; to be thinned if the frame needs it].
- **Fountains:** the Romanesque fountains of Conques share one layout: spring water runs through a stone conduit into a barrel-vaulted tank, then out at street level through a semicircular opening. The Plò, beside the church square ("in front of the doors of the basilica runs an excellent source", the 12th-century pilgrims' guide); the Fumouze with its coping to rest buckets; the Barry down the Rue Charlemagne near the lower suburb's workshops (mills, tannery) [stated: same]. Built: all three as a wall of yellow limestone with a round-headed opening, a trough with the water and a coping; the Plò at the terrace's west edge, the Fumouze on the lane east of the church, the Barry beside the road to the bridge.
- **Bread ovens** stood outside the ramparts for fear of fire [stated: same]; built: a baker's steam from some shops' chimneys in the morning only [inferred].
- **The pilgrims' road:** pilgrims came to Conques by the Via Podiensis and crossed the Dourdou by the bridge [stated: §1b]; the way up the stair street and along the lane to the church's square is that route [inferred].

## 1g. The pilgrims' hostel and the shops (built: `maison` with `hostel`, `shop`; interiors of three kinds)

- A pilgrimage abbey received its guests: the Rule of Saint Benedict (ch. 53) says that all guests be received as Christ [stated: Rule of Saint Benedict]. A hostel at the foot of the church's terrace on the pilgrims' lane is [inferred], the building itself after no source: a hall of two floors, 15 x 8.4 m, a wide round-headed door, a hearth at one end, straw beds along the back wall, two long tables, water barrels; the upper floor closed.
- The pilgrim's outfit: a long plain coat, a leather bag at the waist, a stout staff with a metal point, a wide-brimmed hat [stated: Travel Signposts, a 2010 walking blog, to be checked against scholarly sources]; the scallop shell as the pilgrim's sign [stated: same, without a date; the shell sewn on the cape is the common later attribute, so for 1120 [inferred]].
- Shops (55, along the pilgrims' way within 36 m): a counter of stone under a wide arch with a hinged board let down over it, goods on it by trade (shells, staffs, bread, cloth, pots), a bundle of staffs stood against the pier, a banner on a bracket, sacks and a barrel; two of them can be entered, with shelves of jars, barrels and sacks [inferred: the trades are those a pilgrim would need; the arrangement after medieval shop arcades].
- Two ordinary houses can be entered (hearth under a stone hood, table, bench, chest, bed of boards with straw, shelf of pots) [inferred].

## 1h. Life (movers.js, fauna.js, smoke.js)

- Walkers (34 by day within 150 m of the visitor, 3 at night): pilgrims in coats and cloaks with the wide hat or a cap, staff, scrip, gourd and shell; Benedictine monks in black; villagers in tunics, women in gowns and veils with baskets, peasants with sacks, children; on the lanes, the stairs, the square and the road to the bridge, the pilgrims' way the busiest [inferred]. Hearth smoke from the chimneys at the three meals, incense before the Majesty, mist in the gorge at dawn. Swallows over the roofs, crows about the church at twilight, sparrows on the cobbles, a heron and an egret on the Dourdou's bank, trout in the river [inferred: the fauna of the gorge].
- Still below the Suzhou standard: the church interior's pilgrims are few; no cloister life; the rampart is not built; the village is not thinned to a sourced house count.

## 1i. Deepening pass (2026-11)

- **The tribune stair:** the tribunes were "reached by stairs in the thickness of the walls", pilgrims keeping vigil there [stated: `docs/buildings/sainte-foy-conques.md` §1, from POP]. Built: a straight stair of 57 risers (0.19 m) up the north aisle, 1.2 m wide, the aisle vault left off over its last 4.6 m and replaced by a flat floor pierced over the stair's head; the north tribune's floor (0.4 m thick) walkable, with colliders where the carved work is open; five pilgrims kneeling or standing at its breast [inferred: the stair's place and form; the real ones are spiral stairs in the walls].
- **Fronts at eye level** [inferred: after the region's Romanesque houses and medieval life in general]: a corbel table (modillons) under every eave, strap hinges on shut doors, stone benches by doors, herb pots on sills, garlic braids and herb bundles hung by doors, washing hung out of upper windows and across the lanes, tie-rod plates between the floors, spouts of stone at the roof corners, a hung bush at the tavern, a horseshoe at the smith, brackets with boards at the other shops; about 37 wide houses of the north rows have an outside stone stair (perron) along the front to a door in the upper wall, with a parapet [stated: the houses have two entrances, one on the lower street and one above, tourisme-conques.fr "The village of Conques"].
- **Interiors:** 25 houses can be entered (was 5): the hostel, 12–14 shops (the trades: pilgrims' staffs and shells, bread, cloth, pots, tavern, smith, weaver) and 10 homes of three kinds. Baker: oven of rubble under a dome with its mouth glowing, kneading trough, loaves, wood. Smith: forge under a hood, bellows, anvil on its stump, tub, bars and tools. Tavern: long tables with benches, casks on a counter, hams from the beams, hearth. Weaver: an upright loom with warp and cloth, baskets of wool. Homes: a spinner's with a second bed, cradle and ladder to the loft; a farmer's with sacks, a pen of straw and tools [inferred].
- **Lanes:** a gutter of dressed stone along each lane's middle and weeds at the foot of the walls; lanterns by the shops; each house's front kept with a ladder, barrow, sacks, stool and basket, crates or pots; cats on the copings of the retaining walls, dogs and pigs at doors, donkeys at the hostel [inferred].
- **The square before the church:** a stone cross on three steps, five stalls along the west edge (bread, pottery, baskets, cloth, fruit) with sellers and buyers, benches along the terrace edge, women with jars at the Plò, children at play, pigeons, a mule with its panniers [inferred: the pilgrims' market at the church door].
- **The cloister:** monks walking the galleries, two in talk, one reading, one kneeling at the garth's beds [inferred: the Benedictine day].
- **The Dourdou:** women kneeling at the washing-place by the bridge with linen spread on the bank, a fisher and a boy, geese and ducks, a mule drinking at the ford [inferred]; the stream is too shallow for boats, so no boats are shown.
- **Gardens:** four kinds by the seed: vegetable beds with cabbages; vines on posts and wires [inferred: "vines" in the terraces below the village]; an orchard yard with straw skeps on a stone bench and a stone hut; a pen with straw, a trough and a hay rick, with sheep or goats.
- **Planting:** maquis scrub on the outer slopes, elms among the houses (chestnut is not in the plant list; oak stands in).
- **Life:** walkers now include water carriers and wood gatherers; baker's steam and the smith's forge smoke at the open shops; crows on the nave's ridge; sparrows on the lanes every 21 m.
- **Still below Suzhou:** the south tribune and the transept arms' tribunes are shut; the rampart is not built; no mill or tannery at the lower suburb; shop goods inside are boxes and turned pots, not individually modelled; the church's interior has few figures beyond the vigil and the choir's kneelers.
