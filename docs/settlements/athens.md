## 1. The real example

- **The Acropolis:** a limestone rock whose flat summit is about 270 m east–west by about 150 m north–south (the 5th-century works widened it from 85 m) [stated: areq/Wikipedia-fr; Larousse; SNL]. It stands at 156 m above sea level, 90–100 m above the lower town [stated: Larousse; SNL]. It can be climbed only from the west, where the slope ends at the Propylaia [stated: SNL; areq].
- **The buildings:** the Parthenon (447–432), the Propylaia by Mnesikles (437–432), the Temple of Athena Nike (427–424) and the Erechtheion (421–406) [stated: UNESCO text via medium]. The Nike temple is a small Ionic temple on a bastion south of the Propylaia [stated: SNL]. The Erechtheion is Ionic, asymmetrical, with six Caryatids carrying its south porch [stated: mycityhunt]. The pedestal of the Athena Promachos stands between the Erechtheion and the Parthenon [stated: wikiloc].
- **The Hephaisteion:** 13.7 × 31.8 m, 8.8 m high, 447–413 BC [stated: imaginoso].
- **Moment shown:** about 410 BC, with the Erechtheion nearly finished (shown complete).

## 3. Orientation

The temples face east; the Propylaia faces west down the ramp. [stated]

## 4. The style

Pentelic marble temples in the Doric order, and the Ionic for the Nike temple and the Erechtheion. Limestone stoas with Doric colonnades and tiled roofs; the round Tholos; the summit wall of limestone. [stated: orders; inferred: details]

## 4a. Sculpture and paint (added 2026-11-06)

**Parthenon pediments.** East: the birth of Athena; west: the contest of Athena and Poseidon [stated: ancient-greece.org east pediment page]. East, from the south corner: the horses of Helios rising (heads only, about to rise above the horizon), Dionysos, Demeter and Kore, Artemis, … the horses of Selene sinking in the north corner [stated: ancient-greece.org]; the corner figures recline, the centre is lost (Zeus, Athena, Hephaistos and Hera conjectured) [stated: same]. West: Athena and Poseidon at the centre, their chariot groups flanking them, figures of Athenian myth out to the corners [stated: same]. The statues were finished on all sides [stated: same]. Egyptian blue was found on the water under EP A, beneath the peplos of EP G and in the background between EP E and F, on the mantle of EP L, on the serpent of WP B and the belt of WP N; garments widely painted [stated: Verri et al., *Antiquity* 2023]. **Built:** about twenty figures in the round to each pediment, sized to the raking cornice above them (≈ 2.4–3.7 m), the corner figures reclining, Helios and Selene rising from and sinking into the floor with their horses' heads, the olive between Athena and Poseidon, rearing chariot horses; the tympana painted blue [inferred: the composition after Carrey's drawings as usually reconstructed; the blue ground after the pigment finds]. The olive now a gnarled trunk forking low, five boughs and clumps of foliage painted green on the marble (it was three bare blobs) [inferred: the tree's carving; green paint on it inferred, no pigment reported for it].
**Metopes.** 92, east the gigantomachy, west the amazonomachy, north the Trojan War, south the centauromachy, in deep relief [stated: ancient-greece.org metopes page]. Red and red-orange pigment found on the ground, blue on the bands above and below, Egyptian blue on the top band [stated: same]. **Built:** every metope with two figures in high relief (combatants, a centaur rearing, a rider on a rearing horse, a fallen figure), on a red ground under a band of Egyptian blue (kept red, as the pigment found, rather than the blue grounds of some reconstructions); each long side pairs two left and two right figures in turn so the same carved figure repeats (drawn once, instanced), each end varies through nine pairings [inferred].
**Paint (2026-11-06):** every blue of the temples — triglyphs, the metopes' band, the Ionic friezes' grounds, the tympana, the Erechtheion porch's coffers, the Pinakotheke's panels — is now the palette's Egyptian blue: "the only blue pigment used in Greece and Rome" [stated: King's College London, 2023], found on the frieze "on some of the background spaces" and on eleven pedimental sculptures [stated: The Past, Science Notes, after Verri et al.]. The Agora's horos and the Altar of the Twelve Gods' bolsters, single stones, are no longer drawn in the jointed marble [inferred]. **Triglyphs** painted dark blue [inferred: the usual reconstruction, after Vitruvius 4.2.2 on blue-painted beam ends; applied to the Parthenon and the Propylaia].
**Ionic frieze.** About 1 m high, projecting at most 5.6 cm, 160 m long; 378 figures and 245 animals; horsemen make 46% of it (west, north, south), ten chariots on the south and eleven on the north, cattle and sheep for the sacrifice, the twelve seated gods and the peplos scene on the east [stated: Wikipedia "Parthenon Frieze" via cavac mirror]. "Perhaps the background was blue" (Hephaisteion frieze, grave stelae) [stated: same]. **Built:** a relief 5.6 cm deep on a blue ground round the top of the cella walls and over the porches (13.34–14.34 m): the west the cavalcade setting out; north and south (both moving east) horsemen, chariots, elders, bearers, heifers; the east maidens and marshals converging on the seated gods [inferred: proportions from the figures above].
**Acroteria:** a floral palmette over each pediment [inferred: the Parthenon's central acroteria are lost; floral after fragments as usually restored]. **Antefixes** as palmettes on every temple roof [inferred].

## 4b. The Propylaia, the Nike temple, the Erechtheion, the Promachos (added 2026-11-06)

- **Propylaia** (Mnesikles, 437–431, never finished): a central gate building and two wings; six Doric columns on each façade; the central passage flanked by three pairs of Ionic columns; five doors in the cross wall, the middle one wider for the procession and its animals; the north wing the Pinakotheke, named from the paintings Pausanias saw there [stated: Acropolis Museum; ancient-greece.org]. The roof of marble slabs with coffers painted with floral motifs and stars; a ramp up the middle as an alternative to the four steps [stated: ancient-greece.org]. **Built:** the central building on four steps (0.24 m) with the roadway cut through as a ramp; west and east fronts of six Doric columns (8.81 m, Ø 1.56), the central bay 5.43 m, the others 3.18 m [inferred: proportions after published plans, not measured here]; the west hall with its six Ionic columns and coffered ceiling of blue and gold stars; the cross wall with doors 4.18, 2.9 and 1.5 m wide [inferred]; the Pinakotheke with three Doric columns between antae, a door and two windows, painted panels on its back wall [inferred: three columns in antis after plans]; the south wing's matching front [inferred].
- **Temple of Athena Nike:** amphiprostyle, four Ionic columns at each end (4.07 m, Ø 0.53), a continuous frieze; the bastion's marble **parapet** of about 410 BC with Victories in relief [inferred: dimensions after common figures; the parapet's date and subject are standard].
- **Erechtheion:** six maidens of the same height and build carry the south porch, the three on the left resting on one knee, the three on the right on the other; heavy braided hair [stated: Wikipedia "Erechtheion" via IPFS mirror]; a caryatid is about 2.28 m, wears a peplos pinned on each shoulder, her hair braided in a thick rope down her back, the head surmounted by a capital with bead-and-reel and egg-and-dart [stated: British Museum 1816,0610.128]. The friezes are of black Eleusinian limestone with white marble figures attached; a north porch of six Ionic columns [stated: Wikipedia via IPFS]. **Built:** the cella under one roof with the east porch of six Ionic columns (6.6 m), the north porch (four in front, one behind each corner) with its own north-facing pediment and blue-painted ceiling, the great north door, the east door between two windows, windows high in the west wall; the porch of the maidens: a 1.77 m parapet, six korai 2.31 m with their capitals, architrave of three fasciae with rosettes, dentils, cornice [inferred: dimensions]. **Since the second wave:** "the entire temple is on a slope, so the west and north sides are about 3 m (9 ft) lower than the south and east sides" [stated: Wikipedia "Erechtheion" via IPFS]; the north porch now stands in a paved court 3 m below the summit, its columns 7.63 m (the tallest of the building) on two steps, its pediment under the temple's eaves; the north wall goes down to the court on a foundation wall, the great door opens at the porch's floor (its leaves shut, with bronze bosses, under a moulded lintel with gilt rosettes); the court is walled where the summit stands above it and reached by a stair of twenty steps from the east [inferred: the stair's place after plans of the site, the court's extent ours; the column height as commonly given, not checked against a survey].
- **Athena Promachos:** a bronze about 7.4 m standing armed, the spear upright, the shield resting at her side, on a stepped base [inferred: the height and pose after ancient descriptions as commonly given].

The facts are those of `docs/buildings/parthenon.md` §4, §7, §8 (about 11.5 m, gold and ivory, the pedestal 8.043 × 4.096 m, the birth of Pandora on the pedestal, the Nike, the shield, the serpent) [stated there]. **Built:** the goddess as a figure (10.6 m without her crests), her weight on her right leg, the peplos with its overfold, the scaly aegis with an ivory gorgoneion, the Attic helmet with raised cheek-pieces and three crests; the Nike, winged, on her outstretched right hand over its supporting column; her left hand on the rim of the shield at her left side, the gorgon boss, the serpent inside it; the spear at her left shoulder; drapery and arms in gold, face, forearms and feet in ivory; twenty gilt figures in relief across the front of the pedestal for the birth of Pandora [inferred: the pose and the column after the reduced copies (the Varvakeion statuette); the earlier massing had the Nike and the shield on the wrong sides].

## 4d. The Agora about 410 BC (added in the second wave)

The moment shown excludes the later buildings (the Stoa of Attalos is of 159–138 BC, the porch of the Tholos Augustan, the garden round the Hephaisteion 3rd century BC); the stoas built are those standing by 410.
- **Hephaisteion** (449–415): 13.71 × 31.78 m, six by thirteen columns; an inner Doric colonnade of five columns on each side and three across the end; only 18 of the 68 metopes sculpted: the ten of the east front (the labours of Herakles) and the four easternmost of each flank (the deeds of Theseus); continuous Ionic friezes over the pronaos (Theseus and the Pallantids before seated gods) and the opisthodomos (Lapiths and centaurs); the pronaos antae in line with the third flank columns; the bronze cult statues of Hephaistos and Athena paid for in 421–415; the east pediment the birth of Athena, the west the return of Hephaistos [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of Hephaestus" via IPFS]. **Built:** the krepis of three steps, 34 columns (5.71 m, Ø 1.02, spacing 2.548 × 2.51 m [inferred: as usually published, not checked against a survey]); the entablature with blue triglyphs; the sculpted metopes on a red ground under a blue band (the others plain); pediment groups of nine figures each on blue tympana; floral acroteria [inferred: the west acroteria were Nereids and Nikai — shown floral for now]; the cella with two Doric columns in antis at each porch, the pronaos frieze carried across the pteron to the peristyle on its own architrave, the opisthodomos frieze over the porch only [inferred]; marble coffers over the pteron and porches; inside, the two-storeyed colonnade, a timber ceiling, the two bronzes side by side on a base of dark stone, the doors open [inferred: pose and base].
- **Stoa Poikile** (about 460, by Peisianax): Doric outside, narrow Ionic columns inside, the earliest known building at Athens to combine the two; intercolumniation 1.998 m; triglyphs 0.384 m, metopes 0.615, frieze 0.718 m high; west wall 12.6 m long; probably at least 46 m long; four panel paintings (Marathon, the Amazonomachy, the sack of Troy, Oinoe) by Polygnotos, Mikon and perhaps Panainos, probably on wooden boards on the back wall; bronze shields from Sphakteria (425) and Skione (421) displayed [stated: Wikipedia "Stoa Poikile"]. **Built:** 48 × 12.6 m facing south onto the Way, fluted Doric columns 4.9 m [inferred height], the inner Ionic row, a timber coffered ceiling, gabled tile roof with pediments; the four panels in cedar frames on the back wall with painted figures (horsemen, fighters, women) on a light ground; shields of bronze hung above them [inferred: arrangement].
- **Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios** (about 430–410): 43.56 × 10.73 m, wings projecting 5.86 m, 10.6 m wide; outer Doric columns Ø 0.79 at 2.01 m (wings) and 3.02 m (centre); inner Ionic Ø 0.69 at 6.04 m; open to the east [stated: Perseus "Athens, Stoa of Zeus"]; two marble Nikai on the roof; a statue of Zeus Eleutherios before it; shields of the fallen inside [stated: Harry's Greece guide]. **Built:** to those dimensions, the colonnade running round the wings' fronts and inner sides, the wings with their own pediments and a Victory on each, the bronze Zeus on a stepped base before it, shields on the back wall [inferred: column height 5.0 m; the Nikai on the wings' front pediments].
- **South Stoa I** (end of the 5th century): 80.45 × 14.89 m; fifteen rooms about 4.86 m square with off-centre doors 1.2 m wide, so that seven couches fit round the walls (dining rooms); outer Doric colonnade of 45 unfluted poros columns Ø 0.53 at 1.745 m, inner colonnade of 22 at 3.49 m; columns stuccoed white; walls of mudbrick, the back wall stone; clay floors [stated: Wikipedia "South Stoa I (Athens)"]. **Built:** shortened to 43 m with eight rooms (the Agora is compressed and the Panathenaic Way passes its east end) [inferred], the double colonnade to those dimensions (outer columns 3.6 m [inferred]), the rooms with their off-centre doors, seven couches with cushions and three tables with cups in each.
- **Tholos** (about 470–465): more than 18 m across; six interior columns carried the roof; diamond-shaped terracotta roof tiles with simple antefixes; two small rooms on the north probably kitchen and pantry; the porch was added under Augustus [stated: Harry's Greece guide; FHW "Athens — Tholos"]; the prytaneis dined there, the official measures were kept there, a central altar may have stood inside [stated: FHW]. **Built:** 18.32 m across on its stone socle, plastered walls, the door on the east with a marble frame, six unfluted columns on an oval clear of the door's axis carrying a timber ring beam [inferred: arrangement], the conical roof of lozenge-laid tiles (two families of cover-ridges crossing) with antefixes and a finial, rafters inside; a bench round the wall, the hearth-altar, a marble table with bronze measures and lead weights; the kitchen and pantry on the north with a cooking bench, pots and amphorae.
- **Altar of the Twelve Gods** in its low enclosure of marble parapet slabs between posts by the Way, open west and east [inferred: about 9.35 × 9.8 m as commonly given]; **the Herms** where the Way enters the Agora at its north-west corner [inferred: placing; the Herms were mutilated in 415]; **the boundary stone** ("I am the boundary of the Agora") by the Tholos [inferred: the stone is c. 500 BC].
- **The market:** booths of wicker screens (gerra) with a cloth over the front, a trestle table and goods — painted and black-glazed pottery, amphorae of oil and wine, fish on marble slabs, bread, myrtle, a banker's table with coins and a balance; a seller behind and buyers in the aisles [inferred: after the usual accounts of the Agora as a market with its "circles" of trades; not separately sourced here].

## 4e. The south slope about 410 (added in the third wave)

- **Theatre of Dionysos.** In the sanctuary of Dionysos Eleuthereus on the south slope; before the stone theatre of Lycurgus (the 330s) the spectators sat on the hillside and on wooden benches (ikria), the first row of stone reserved for officials [stated: ancientathens3d "The south slope"; Wikipedia "Theatre of Dionysus"]. The orchestra was a circular terrace on polygonal masonry, an altar (thymele) probably at its centre; in the Periclean phase the orchestra was shifted a little north, the auditorium banked up, retaining walls added to the west, east and north [stated: Wikipedia]. Dinsmoor put the early orchestra at about 26 m across, Dörpfeld at about 27 [stated: THEATRON, King's College "The theatre of the 5th century BC"]. The Periclean stage building was of wood, semi-permanent, usually two storeys, standing for palaces, houses or temples, gods appearing above its roof; it stood back to back with the new stoa [stated: THEATRON]; probably at most two floors with a roof and three doors [stated: Wikipedia]. A long hall lay south of the skene, its breccia foundations suggesting the late 5th century [stated: Wikipedia]. The wooden classical theatre was probably rectangular rather than semicircular [stated: ancientathens3d]; Carlo Anti proposed a polygonal auditorium [stated: Wikipedia]. **Built:** the orchestra 26 m across on its terrace of beaten earth, a stone kerb round the dancing circle, the polygonal terrace wall on the downhill side, the square altar in the middle; the theatron in three straight facets (the middle one facing the stage, the wings turned 40° in) of twenty rows 0.8 m deep and 0.42 m high, each row an earth step with a bench of planks over its front and a board down its face; the front row of the middle facet of stone with low backs; half-steps up the two joints of the facets; retaining walls on the end lines stepped with the rows, a walk behind the top row and its wall against the hill [inferred: the polygonal plan after Anti and the rectilinear view; row sizes as in later Greek theatres; the size of the 5th-century auditorium is not known — kept small]. The hill is cut back above the theatre, steeper than the real slope because our Acropolis is compressed [inferred]. The skene: a timber hall 28 × 6 m, 7.2 m to its flat roof with a railing (where gods appear), three doors in its front (the middle one the widest), posts and a storey beam standing proud, painted scenery between the posts — a red dado, painted columns with white bases and capitals, a blue architrave with red regulae — and a pediment of boards over the middle door [inferred: the painting after the tradition of skenographia; no 5th-century scenery survives]; the stage terrace on a rubble wall. Behind it the stoa, 52 × 7.5 m, a Doric front of limestone toward the sanctuary [inferred: dimensions].
- **Older Temple of Dionysos Eleuthereus** (6th century), which housed the wooden image (xoanon) of the god [stated: Wikipedia]; the new temple of about 340 (21.95 × 10.5 m) is later and not shown [stated: ancientathens3d]. **Built:** a small Doric temple of limestone in antis (13.4 × 8 m, two columns 4.6 m), facing east, the bearded wooden image with his cup and an ivy wreath inside, the altar before it to the east [inferred: dimensions and the image's form].
- **Odeion of Pericles**: at the south-eastern foot of the Acropolis beside the entrance to the theatre; foundations 62.40 × 68.60 m; the roof carried by 90 internal supports in nine rows of ten; square and pyramid-like, like a tent (Pausanias: a copy of Xerxes' tent); many seats and many pillars (Plutarch); timber, by tradition, from captured Persian ships [stated: Wikipedia "Odeon of Pericles"; ancientathens3d: "the biggest roofed building of Greek antiquity"]. **Built:** the hall on a levelled platform cut into the slope, limestone walls 1.4 m thick and 8 m high, a great door in the south wall and two in the west toward the theatre, small high windows; ninety columns on a 6.24 m grid whose heights rise toward the middle (from about 7 to 19 m) to carry a hipped roof of tiles rising 12.6 m from all four walls to a short ridge, its underside of boards, purlins and rafters running from column-head to column-head and out to the walls; wooden benches facing south over the northern two-thirds of the floor; a platform for the musicians [inferred: the column heights, the walls, the doors, windows and the seating arrangement are ours; the Odeion is shown east of the theatre as on the site].

## 4f. The summit's smaller sanctuaries and offerings (added in the third wave)

- **Brauronion**: in the south-west of the summit between the Propylaia and the Chalkotheke; no temple but a stoa facing north before the south wall, about 38 × 6.8 m, with two wings about 9.3 m long at its corners facing each other; its western part stood on the remains of the Mycenaean wall; the final form commonly dated about 430; a wooden cult image of Artemis Brauronia to which women dedicated clothing; limestone members [stated: Wikipedia "Brauroneion"]. **Built:** the stoa of limestone with its Doric front and its two wings, the wooden image in the west wing with clothes laid about her and more on a rail beside her, the precinct's low north and east walls, the entrance at the north-east [inferred: the wings' width and the image's place; our summit is level, so the precinct's seven rock-cut steps are not shown].
- **The bronze Trojan horse** of Strongylion: an over-life-sized bronze horse in the sanctuary, dedicated by Chairedemos, the base inscribed with his name and the sculptor's [stated: Wikipedia "Brauroneion"]; "the Trojan horse with the Greek heroes inside it and looking forth", set up late in the 5th century, its inscribed base found (IG I³ 895) [stated: Wikipedia "Strongylion"]; mentioned before 415/414 [stated: Wikipedia "Brauroneion"]; "colossal" [stated: Lefkowitz, *Hesperia* 89 (2020), abstract]. **Built:** a bronze horse 2.7 m at the withers standing on a marble base 3.5 m long with the dedication cut along its crown, bands across the barrel, a hatch in its flank with two heroes' heads looking out [inferred: size and the hatch].
- **Chalkotheke**: about 43 × 14 m, east of the Brauronion and south-west of the Parthenon, before the south wall; the main building roughly contemporary with the Parthenon, its north portico an addition of the early 4th century; a "bronze store" [stated: Wikipedia "Chalkotheke"]. **Built:** the hall without its later portico, limestone walls under a gabled tile roof, a middle door and two side doors on the north, two columns on its axis carrying the ridge beam under a coffered ceiling of cedar; racks of shelves with helmets, bowls and water jars, shields hung above, spears stacked; an inscribed stele before the door [inferred: contents after the building's function].
- **Offerings along the way**: the Lemnian Athena of Pheidias stood on the Acropolis (Pausanias 1.28.2), reconstructed holding her helmet in her outstretched right hand and an upright spear in her left [stated: Wikipedia "Lemnian Athena"]. **Built:** in bronze, bareheaded, the helmet in her hand, the aegis across her breast, on a marble pedestal; a bronze tripod on a column; inscribed stelae of decrees on stepped bases [inferred: material of the Athena (bronze, as usually held), the stelae and the tripod as generic offerings; their places ours].
- **Not shown on purpose:** the archaic korai and the other sculptures wrecked by the Persians in 480–479 were buried by the Athenians afterwards [stated: Wikipedia "Perserschutt"], so none stands on the summit in 410.
- **Mycenaean wall**: a stretch south of the Propylaia under the Brauronion's west end [stated: the Brauronion stood on it, Wikipedia "Brauroneion"]: huge rough blocks of limestone in irregular courses, chinked with small stones, about 4.2 m thick and 3 m standing [inferred: dimensions].

## 5a. The houses (third wave)

Herms stood outside houses in Athens as protective charms, rubbed with oil and hung with garlands (Thucydides 6.27) [stated: Wikipedia "Herma"]; the andron held an odd number of couches to leave room for the door [stated: Wikipedia "Andron (architecture)"]. **Built:** every house now shuts its court from the street by a wall coped with tiles (or a low range of rooms), the street door in a timber frame with its leaf shut and a stone threshold, small shuttered windows high in the outer walls, a third of the houses whitewashed; the houses along the ways, round the Agora and below the theatre also have the herm beside the door, the altar of Zeus Herkeios, a well-head and a storage jar in the court [inferred: the court plan, the altar and the well after the usual Greek courtyard house].

## 5b. A house walked into: Simon's house and shop (L3 pass)

- **Simon the shoemaker:** the remains of a shop near the Tholos in the south-west corner of the Agora yielded quantities of hobnails and the base of a 5th-century drinking cup inscribed ΣΙΜΟΝΟΣ ("Simon's"); Socrates frequented the workshops round the Agora to talk, Simon among them; it cannot be certain the shop was his [stated: Wikipedia, Simon the Shoemaker]. Small iron tacks and bone rings thought to be attachments for leather boots, and a worn stone suggesting knife-sharpening, were found; workshops were usually set up "in one of the rooms or the yard of the craftsman's house", small family businesses of five to eight with the craftsman, his family and his slaves [stated: fhw.gr, craft workshops].
- **The andron:** often identified by its square shape, raised cement borders that held the couches, an offset door, pebble mosaic designs sometimes in the middle of the floor; wine mixed with water in a krater; the room also used for weaving, as loom weights at Olynthus show [stated: Houses and Households in Ancient Greece, "The Andron"].
- **Built:** a house of 13 × 14 m by the Agora's south-west corner, its street on the north: rubble socles and mud-plaster walls, tiled roofs over the two ranges, worn stone thresholds and timber lintels at every door, the herm by the street door. On the street the shop with its wide opening: the bench with leather pieces, four lasts, the iron knife, the low stool, the whetstone, sandals and boots on a shelf, hides on pegs, a basket. The passage to the court (pebbled): the altar of Zeus Herkeios with its burnt top, the well-head, a storage jar, a bench. The andron (5.1 × 4.8 m) with its door offset into the court: the raised white border 0.9 m wide, a pebble floor in the middle, the walls red to a metre; seven couches on the border with mattresses, covers and cushions, a three-legged table before each with a black kylix or two and a dish of bread; the bell krater in the middle with its ladle; a bronze lampstand burning. Across the court: the loom room (the warp-weighted loom against the wall, its weights in a row, a basket of wool), the kitchen (the hearth of a ring of stones with its ash, the cooking pot on an iron stand, the soot on the wall above, the water jar), the store (amphorae leaning, a pithos) [inferred: the plan, the sizes, seven couches as the common andron, the red dado, the furnishings].

## 10. Life (L3 pass)

- **Built:** Simon at his bench and a bearded citizen in his himation talking with him in the shop; the woman of the house at the loom, the slave at the hearth; citizens in chiton and himation, workmen in short tunics and women with water jars in the Agora, kept to the aisles between the booths; men talking in the Painted Stoa; a donkey with panniers led in along the Way [inferred: numbers and places, §5 of DESIGN].
- **Plane trees:** Kimon "adorned the agora with plane trees" (Plutarch, *Life of Cimon* 13.8) [stated: Plutarch]. **Built:** plane trees along the Agora's edges, clear of its stoas, the Tholos, the booths and the Way; the middle open [inferred: the rows' places].

## 5. The town

About 1,200 courtyard houses of mudbrick on stone socles with tiled roofs, packed in blocks. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

Grey-white limestone, thin red soil, dry grass; olives and cypresses; a few plane trees in the Agora. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

The clear Attic light; a little cloud sometimes. [inferred]

Along the Panathenaic Way through the Agora, up the ramp, through the Propylaia onto the summit, to the Parthenon's east front. [inferred]

## 11. The fourth wave: the Kerameikos, the Academy, the fountain, the shops (added 2026-11-11)

**11a. The potters' quarter.** Potters' quarters lay between the Dipylon Gate and the Sacred Gate, in the Kerameikos (others at Kolonos-in-the-Agora, Melite, Kollytos); a water supply was a basic criterion for the site; workshops were usually at the edge of residential areas, in a room or the yard of the craftsman's house, small family concerns of five to eight with the craftsman, his family and slaves [stated: FHW, craft workshops]. Attic clay is rich in iron and fires orange-red; it was purified in settling basins (the middle layer drawn off from one into the next), then matured damp for weeks; the wheel was hand-turned; big vessels thrown in sections, pithoi and monumental kraters coiled; pieces dried leather-hard before decoration; black-figure (Corinth, 7th c.) and red-figure (Athens, about 530–520); workshops of a proprietor-potter, one or two more potters, painters and assistants, slaves and metics beside citizens; a cup cost one to three obols; firing in an updraught kiln in three stages (oxidising to about 800–920 °C over 8–9 hours, reducing at about 945 °C with the vents sealed and damp green wood added, re-oxidising at 800–850 °C), more than 24 hours by eye [stated: Gadsden, "How the Greeks made their vases"]. **Built (`potter_works`, `src/styles/greek/quarter.js`):** eight workshops of 17 × 15 m in two rows on either side of a lane (`quarters.js`): the workroom on the lane under a tiled range with its wide opening, shelves of black- and red-figure vases (amphora, krater, hydria, oinochoe, psykter, lekythos, kylix), then by trade the potter's wheel with a stool and a half-risen pot, the clay bin and leather-hard ware; the vase-painter's bench with a krater on its stand, slip pots and brushes; the tile-maker's stacks of pan and cover tiles and moulds; the coroplast's figurines and moulds; a store with pithoi; behind, the walled yard with the updraught kiln (a domed chamber of plastered brick on a rubble ring, the vent and peep-hole, the stoking tunnel with its sooted mouth, firewood, ash), two settling pits (clay, then water), racks of unfired pots drying in the sun, a lean-to shed with wood, clay and tiles, pithoi of water, finished ware stacked; the kilns smoke through the day, two glow [inferred: the plan, sizes and fittings after the sources above; the kiln's dimensions (about 2.5 m across, 2.1 m high) are ours, the source gives its structure only; the number of workshops is a compression].

**11b. The South-East Fountain House.** Built about 530–520 BC under the Peisistratids, about 7 × 18 m, of cut yellow limestone blocks; a larger central room with two smaller reservoirs opening off it; the reservoirs had terracotta spouts, terracotta supply and overflow pipes were found; Pausanias took it for the Enneakrounos [stated: Harry's Greece guide, "Agora fountain house"]. Black-figure hydriai show women filling their jars at lion-head spouts under a Doric colonnade [inferred: the usual reading of those vases; not in the fetched source]. **Built (`fountain_house`):** 16 × 8 m (shortened), a Doric front of five limestone columns on two steps, the back wall of the hall with four bronze lion-head spouts and a thin stream from each into a trough of dark stone, a ledge before it with four hydriai waiting, the closed ends the cisterns' houses [inferred]. Women with hydriai queue at it (`life.js`).

**11c. The Academy.** A sacred grove of olives dedicated to Athena outside the walls, named from the hero Akademos; Cimon enclosed the precincts with a wall; a torchlit night race ran from altars in the city to the altar of Prometheus in the Akademeia; the cult of Athena there from the Bronze Age; Plato's school, later (about 387), took its name from the gymnasium; most Athenian gymnasia lay in the suburbs, with more level ground and cooler air [stated: Wikiwand "Akademia"; Wikipedia "Gymnasium (ancient Greece)"]. Gymnasia held spaces for each kind of exercise, a palaestra for wrestling, boxing and ball games, covered porticoes where philosophers talked; athletes undressed in apodyteria and oiled and scraped themselves with a strigil [stated: Wikipedia "Gymnasium"]. Socrates, back from Potidaea in 429, went to the palaestra of Taureas [stated: Plato, *Charmides*, from memory of the text; not re-fetched]. **Built (`gymnasium`, `academy_grounds`, west edge, `quarters.js`):** a 68 × 74 m precinct with a wall (rubble socle, plaster, tiled coping) and a gate of two limestone posts on the east; a grove planted with olives and plane trees clear of the buildings and paths (`ground.js`); the palaestra 34 × 31 m — a peristyle of 28 unfluted Doric columns of stuccoed limestone with blue triglyphs and pent roofs falling to a court of sand, five rooms behind its north portico: the apodyterion (stone benches, pegs with himatia, sandals), the loutron (four basins on pedestals with bronze spouts and a drain), the elaiothesion (oil jars, shelf of aryballoi and strigils), the konisterion (a dust floor and heap), an exedra room (a ring of seats round a herm); in the court two herms, a jumping pit with its weights, a rack of javelins, a punch-bag (korykos), an altar; a covered track (xystos, a plain stoa of 54 × 7.5 m) with the open track (paradromis) before it; the altar of Prometheus (three steps, burnt top, two torches), a hero-shrine of Akademos (rubble enclosure, stele, offering table), the philosophers' exedra (a half-ring of limestone seats), herms and benches along the way from the gate [inferred: all dimensions and the room list, after the general descriptions above and the usual palaestra plan; the Academy gymnasium's own 5th-century plan is not known]. The date shown is before Plato's school; the men on the exedra are an old man in the himation with his companions [inferred]. Athletes are drawn in a loincloth, a compromise for the nude of the real palaestra [inferred].

**11d. The shops under the south side.** Along the south edge of the Agora, east of South Stoa I, a double-colonnaded row of five workshop-shops (the stoa element with `rooms.trades`): the bronze-smith (hearth with glow, anvil on its stump, bronze ware and helmets hung up), the perfumer (shelves of lekythoi and alabastra), the cloth-seller (folded stacks and hung pieces, a rail), the wine and oil merchant (amphorae, pithoi, kylikes, a krater on the counter), the carpenter (bench, planks, tools on the wall, a half-made couch frame); each with its counter and a shopkeeper behind it [inferred: trades after those the craft-workshop sources list for the Agora; Simon the shoemaker's shop of §5b is the sourced one].

## 12. Life, smoke, animals (fourth wave)

Walkers (`movers.js`, 60 by day, 5 by night): citizens in chiton and himation, philosophers in the himation alone over the bare chest and barefoot, young men in the short chiton with the chlamys and the broad petasos, women in the peplos with shawl or veil, women carrying a hydria on the head, slaves and workmen in the short belted tunic with the felt cap carrying sacks and baskets, boys; on the Panathenaic Way and the ramp (as links), in the Agora's aisles and middle, the Kerameikos lane, the Academy's way and tracks, the summit [inferred: dress after vase paintings as usually described; no source fetched this wave]. Still figures: potters, painters and slaves at work in the workshops, shopkeepers and buyers, women at the fountain, the old man and his companions on the exedra, the trainer, wrestlers and runners. Smoke (`smoke.js`): kilns, house kitchens at the meals, the bronze-smith's hearth, the altars. Animals (`fauna.js`): crows over the rock, swallows over the Agora and the Kerameikos, sparrows on the paving and in the workshop yards [inferred].

## 13. The wards (deepening pass, added 2026-11-11)

**13a. What the sources give.** Houses were mostly mudbrick and wood; walls weathered so that houses needed repair or rebuilding about every year; windows in poorer houses small, unglazed, high in the façade and shuttered; most houses built round a central court where women cooked, animals were kept and shrines stood; some houses had two storeys, with the women, children and perhaps slaves upstairs; houses rarely had chimneys (slats in the roof let the smoke out); some had flat roofs; urban houses were fairly alike in size because land was scarce [stated: History Hit, "What were houses like in ancient Greece?"]. Workshops were attached to the craftsman's house, in a room or the yard (§5b) [stated: fhw.gr]. That the lanes were narrow and crooked, the houses wall to wall with shared party walls, the streets unpaved beaten earth with a gutter, and the shop openings closed by hinged shutters that fold up as awnings are the usual picture of the Greek town and not read from a fetched source here [inferred].

**13b. Built: the layout.** The blocks are bands 27.4 m deep: two rows of houses back to back (the northern row faces the lane on its north, the southern the lane on its south), lanes 3.6 m wide between the bands; along a band the houses stand wall to wall in runs of three to five with a cross-lane of 3.4 m between runs; along the Way and the ramp's foot the houses face the road itself across 7 m (`layout.js` `lots()`). About 680 houses, each 10.5–14.5 m wide, in place of the 1,100 small isolated ones of the third wave [inferred: sizes]. The lanes are ground of beaten earth and gravel (the ground's cover), cleared of trees within 9 m of the houses.

**13c. Built: the house front (`athens_house`, style `greek`, `styles/greek/ward.js`).** A range of rooms on the street with the door in its front wall, or a coped wall with the door; the court behind between two wings and the range at the back; one storey or two (a third), the upper floor with shuttered windows, one leaf swung open, and sometimes a small timber gallery over the door; a quarter of the houses near the visitor are shops: a wide opening framed in timber, a dark cell behind it, a counter board, the shutter propped up as an awning on two poles, a painted sign board; the walls in natural mud plaster, whitewash, ochre or grey wash; the dado grey with damp and splash, rain streaks under the eaves, patches of bare mudbrick where the plaster has fallen, terracotta rain spouts, ridge tiles, chimney pots (kapnodoke) on some roofs [inferred: the pots are a later Greek feature, kept as the smoke vents the sources describe], a fig tree over the court wall. Detail is by tier: all of it within about 35 m of the ways, the Agora's edge and the theatre road; walls, roofs, door and windows only in the outer town.

**13d. Built: walk-in houses.** Twenty-six houses near the arrival, the Way, the Agora and the road under the theatre are Simon's plan (§5b) walked into, each with another trade in the shop on the street, fitted as the Agora's shops (§11d): the bronze-smith, the shoemaker, the cloth-seller, the wine and oil merchant, the perfumer, the carpenter; and the andron with its couches, the loom room, the kitchen with its hearth, the store, the court. The keeper stands at his counter (or the shoemaker sits at his bench), the woman at the loom, the slave at the hearth, in some an old man in his himation on the couch [inferred]. The kitchens smoke at the meals (`smoke.js`).

**13e. Built: the street.** At each front near the visitor: the jar by the door, a bench of stone or timber (sometimes with a man or a woman sitting on it), a stack of firewood, baskets, hens, a dog, a line of washing, a handcart with its load, an amphora or two, a woman on a low stool with her basket; the shops' goods on the counter board; herms at the ends of some runs and wells at some crossings [inferred: the Hermai stood at doors and crossroads (Thucydides 6.27, §5a)]. Walkers on the lanes as well as the Way (`movers.js`, 80 by day); sparrows on the lanes and swallows skimming the longest lanes between the roofs (`fauna.js`).
