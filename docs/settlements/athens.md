## 1. The real example

- **The Acropolis:** a limestone rock whose flat summit is about 270 m east–west by about 150 m north–south (the 5th-century works widened it from 85 m) [stated: areq/Wikipedia-fr; Larousse; SNL]. It stands at 156 m above sea level, 90–100 m above the lower town [stated: Larousse; SNL]. It can be climbed only from the west, where the slope ends at the Propylaia [stated: SNL; areq].
- **The buildings:** the Parthenon (447–432), the Propylaia by Mnesikles (437–432), the Temple of Athena Nike (427–424) and the Erechtheion (421–406) [stated: UNESCO text via medium]. The Nike temple is a small Ionic temple on a bastion south of the Propylaia [stated: SNL]. The Erechtheion is Ionic, asymmetrical, with six Caryatids carrying its south porch [stated: mycityhunt]. The pedestal of the Athena Promachos stands between the Erechtheion and the Parthenon [stated: wikiloc].
- **The Hephaisteion:** 13.7 × 31.8 m, 8.8 m high, 447–413 BC [stated: imaginoso].
- **Moment shown:** about 410 BC, with the Erechtheion nearly finished (shown complete).

## 3. Orientation

The temples face east; the Propylaia faces west down the ramp. [stated]

## 4. The style

Pentelic marble temples in the Doric order, and the Ionic for the Nike temple and the Erechtheion. Limestone stoas with Doric colonnades and tiled roofs; the round Tholos; the summit wall of limestone. [stated: orders; inferred: details]

## 4a. Sculpture and paint (added 2026-11-06)

**Parthenon pediments.** East: the birth of Athena; west: the contest of Athena and Poseidon [stated: ancient-greece.org east pediment page]. East, from the south corner: the horses of Helios rising (heads only, about to rise above the horizon), Dionysos, Demeter and Kore, Artemis, … the horses of Selene sinking in the north corner [stated: ancient-greece.org]; the corner figures recline, the centre is lost (Zeus, Athena, Hephaistos and Hera conjectured) [stated: same]. West: Athena and Poseidon at the centre, their chariot groups flanking them, figures of Athenian myth out to the corners [stated: same]. The statues were finished on all sides [stated: same]. Egyptian blue was found on the water under EP A, beneath the peplos of EP G and in the background between EP E and F, on the mantle of EP L, on the serpent of WP B and the belt of WP N; garments widely painted [stated: Verri et al., *Antiquity* 2023]. **Built:** about twenty figures in the round to each pediment, sized to the raking cornice above them (≈ 2.4–3.7 m), the corner figures reclining, Helios and Selene rising from and sinking into the floor with their horses' heads, the olive between Athena and Poseidon, rearing chariot horses; the tympana painted blue [inferred: the composition after Carrey's drawings as usually reconstructed; the blue ground after the pigment finds].
**Metopes.** 92, east the gigantomachy, west the amazonomachy, north the Trojan War, south the centauromachy, in deep relief [stated: ancient-greece.org metopes page]. Red and red-orange pigment found on the ground, blue on the bands above and below, Egyptian blue on the top band [stated: same]. **Built:** every metope with two figures in high relief (combatants, a centaur rearing, a rider on a rearing horse, a fallen figure), on a red ground under a blue band; each long side pairs two left and two right figures in turn so the same carved figure repeats (drawn once, instanced), each end varies through nine pairings [inferred].
**Triglyphs** painted dark blue [inferred: the usual reconstruction, after Vitruvius 4.2.2 on blue-painted beam ends; applied to the Parthenon and the Propylaia].
**Ionic frieze.** About 1 m high, projecting at most 5.6 cm, 160 m long; 378 figures and 245 animals; horsemen make 46% of it (west, north, south), ten chariots on the south and eleven on the north, cattle and sheep for the sacrifice, the twelve seated gods and the peplos scene on the east [stated: Wikipedia "Parthenon Frieze" via cavac mirror]. "Perhaps the background was blue" (Hephaisteion frieze, grave stelae) [stated: same]. **Built:** a relief 5.6 cm deep on a blue ground round the top of the cella walls and over the porches (13.34–14.34 m): the west the cavalcade setting out; north and south (both moving east) horsemen, chariots, elders, bearers, heifers; the east maidens and marshals converging on the seated gods [inferred: proportions from the figures above].
**Acroteria:** a floral palmette over each pediment [inferred: the Parthenon's central acroteria are lost; floral after fragments as usually restored]. **Antefixes** as palmettes on every temple roof [inferred].

## 4b. The Propylaia, the Nike temple, the Erechtheion, the Promachos (added 2026-11-06)

- **Propylaia** (Mnesikles, 437–431, never finished): a central gate building and two wings; six Doric columns on each façade; the central passage flanked by three pairs of Ionic columns; five doors in the cross wall, the middle one wider for the procession and its animals; the north wing the Pinakotheke, named from the paintings Pausanias saw there [stated: Acropolis Museum; ancient-greece.org]. The roof of marble slabs with coffers painted with floral motifs and stars; a ramp up the middle as an alternative to the four steps [stated: ancient-greece.org]. **Built:** the central building on four steps (0.24 m) with the roadway cut through as a ramp; west and east fronts of six Doric columns (8.81 m, Ø 1.56), the central bay 5.43 m, the others 3.18 m [inferred: proportions after published plans, not measured here]; the west hall with its six Ionic columns and coffered ceiling of blue and gold stars; the cross wall with doors 4.18, 2.9 and 1.5 m wide [inferred]; the Pinakotheke with three Doric columns between antae, a door and two windows, painted panels on its back wall [inferred: three columns in antis after plans]; the south wing's matching front [inferred].
- **Temple of Athena Nike:** amphiprostyle, four Ionic columns at each end (4.07 m, Ø 0.53), a continuous frieze; the bastion's marble **parapet** of about 410 BC with Victories in relief [inferred: dimensions after common figures; the parapet's date and subject are standard].
- **Erechtheion:** six maidens of the same height and build carry the south porch, the three on the left resting on one knee, the three on the right on the other; heavy braided hair [stated: Wikipedia "Erechtheion" via IPFS mirror]; a caryatid is about 2.28 m, wears a peplos pinned on each shoulder, her hair braided in a thick rope down her back, the head surmounted by a capital with bead-and-reel and egg-and-dart [stated: British Museum 1816,0610.128]. The friezes are of black Eleusinian limestone with white marble figures attached; a north porch of six Ionic columns [stated: Wikipedia via IPFS]. **Built:** the cella under one roof with the east porch of six Ionic columns (6.6 m), the north porch (four in front, one behind each corner) with its own north-facing pediment and blue-painted ceiling, the great north door, the east door between two windows, windows high in the west wall; the porch of the maidens: a 1.77 m parapet, six korai 2.31 m with their capitals, architrave of three fasciae with rosettes, dentils, cornice [inferred: dimensions]. **Since the second wave:** "the entire temple is on a slope, so the west and north sides are about 3 m (9 ft) lower than the south and east sides" [stated: Wikipedia "Erechtheion" via IPFS]; the north porch now stands in a paved court 3 m below the summit, its columns 7.63 m (the tallest of the building) on two steps, its pediment under the temple's eaves; the north wall goes down to the court on a foundation wall, the great door opens at the porch's floor (its leaves shut, with bronze bosses, under a moulded lintel with gilt rosettes); the court is walled where the summit stands above it and reached by a stair of twenty steps from the east [inferred: the stair's place after plans of the site, the court's extent ours; the column height as commonly given, not checked against a survey].
- **Athena Promachos:** a bronze about 7.4 m standing armed, the spear upright, the shield resting at her side, on a stepped base [inferred: the height and pose after ancient descriptions as commonly given].

## 4c. The Athena Parthenos redrawn (added 2026-11-06)

The facts are those of `docs/buildings/parthenon.md` §4, §7, §8 (about 11.5 m, gold and ivory, the pedestal 8.043 × 4.096 m, the birth of Pandora on the pedestal, the Nike, the shield, the serpent) [stated there]. **Built:** the goddess as a figure (10.6 m without her crests), her weight on her right leg, the peplos with its overfold, the scaly aegis with an ivory gorgoneion, the Attic helmet with raised cheek-pieces and three crests; the Nike, winged, on her outstretched right hand over its supporting column; her left hand on the rim of the shield at her left side, the gorgon boss, the serpent inside it; the spear at her left shoulder; drapery and arms in gold, face, forearms and feet in ivory; twenty gilt figures in relief across the front of the pedestal for the birth of Pandora [inferred: the pose and the column after the reduced copies (the Varvakeion statuette); the earlier massing had the Nike and the shield on the wrong sides].

## 4d. The Agora about 410 BC (added in the second wave)

The moment shown excludes the later buildings (the Stoa of Attalos is of 159–138 BC, the porch of the Tholos Augustan, the garden round the Hephaisteion 3rd century BC); the stoas built are those standing by 410.
- **Hephaisteion** (449–415): 13.71 × 31.78 m, six by thirteen columns; an inner Doric colonnade of five columns on each side and three across the end; only 18 of the 68 metopes sculpted: the ten of the east front (the labours of Herakles) and the four easternmost of each flank (the deeds of Theseus); continuous Ionic friezes over the pronaos (Theseus and the Pallantids before seated gods) and the opisthodomos (Lapiths and centaurs); the pronaos antae in line with the third flank columns; the bronze cult statues of Hephaistos and Athena paid for in 421–415; the east pediment the birth of Athena, the west the return of Hephaistos [stated: Wikipedia "Temple of Hephaestus" via IPFS]. **Built:** the krepis of three steps, 34 columns (5.71 m, Ø 1.02, spacing 2.548 × 2.51 m [inferred: as usually published, not checked against a survey]); the entablature with blue triglyphs; the sculpted metopes on a red ground under a blue band (the others plain); pediment groups of nine figures each on blue tympana; floral acroteria [inferred: the west acroteria were Nereids and Nikai — shown floral for now]; the cella with two Doric columns in antis at each porch, the pronaos frieze carried across the pteron to the peristyle on its own architrave, the opisthodomos frieze over the porch only [inferred]; marble coffers over the pteron and porches; inside, the two-storeyed colonnade, a timber ceiling, the two bronzes side by side on a base of dark stone, the doors open [inferred: pose and base].
- **Stoa Poikile** (about 460, by Peisianax): Doric outside, narrow Ionic columns inside, the earliest known building at Athens to combine the two; intercolumniation 1.998 m; triglyphs 0.384 m, metopes 0.615, frieze 0.718 m high; west wall 12.6 m long; probably at least 46 m long; four panel paintings (Marathon, the Amazonomachy, the sack of Troy, Oinoe) by Polygnotos, Mikon and perhaps Panainos, probably on wooden boards on the back wall; bronze shields from Sphakteria (425) and Skione (421) displayed [stated: Wikipedia "Stoa Poikile"]. **Built:** 48 × 12.6 m facing south onto the Way, fluted Doric columns 4.9 m [inferred height], the inner Ionic row, a timber coffered ceiling, gabled tile roof with pediments; the four panels in cedar frames on the back wall with painted figures (horsemen, fighters, women) on a light ground; shields of bronze hung above them [inferred: arrangement].
- **Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios** (about 430–410): 43.56 × 10.73 m, wings projecting 5.86 m, 10.6 m wide; outer Doric columns Ø 0.79 at 2.01 m (wings) and 3.02 m (centre); inner Ionic Ø 0.69 at 6.04 m; open to the east [stated: Perseus "Athens, Stoa of Zeus"]; two marble Nikai on the roof; a statue of Zeus Eleutherios before it; shields of the fallen inside [stated: Harry's Greece guide]. **Built:** to those dimensions, the colonnade running round the wings' fronts and inner sides, the wings with their own pediments and a Victory on each, the bronze Zeus on a stepped base before it, shields on the back wall [inferred: column height 5.0 m; the Nikai on the wings' front pediments].
- **South Stoa I** (end of the 5th century): 80.45 × 14.89 m; fifteen rooms about 4.86 m square with off-centre doors 1.2 m wide, so that seven couches fit round the walls (dining rooms); outer Doric colonnade of 45 unfluted poros columns Ø 0.53 at 1.745 m, inner colonnade of 22 at 3.49 m; columns stuccoed white; walls of mudbrick, the back wall stone; clay floors [stated: Wikipedia "South Stoa I (Athens)"]. **Built:** shortened to 43 m with eight rooms (the Agora is compressed and the Panathenaic Way passes its east end) [inferred], the double colonnade to those dimensions (outer columns 3.6 m [inferred]), the rooms with their off-centre doors, seven couches with cushions and three tables with cups in each.
- **Tholos** (about 470–465): more than 18 m across; six interior columns carried the roof; diamond-shaped terracotta roof tiles with simple antefixes; two small rooms on the north probably kitchen and pantry; the porch was added under Augustus [stated: Harry's Greece guide; FHW "Athens — Tholos"]; the prytaneis dined there, the official measures were kept there, a central altar may have stood inside [stated: FHW]. **Built:** 18.32 m across on its stone socle, plastered walls, the door on the east with a marble frame, six unfluted columns on an oval clear of the door's axis carrying a timber ring beam [inferred: arrangement], the conical roof of lozenge-laid tiles (two families of cover-ridges crossing) with antefixes and a finial, rafters inside; a bench round the wall, the hearth-altar, a marble table with bronze measures and lead weights; the kitchen and pantry on the north with a cooking bench, pots and amphorae.
- **Altar of the Twelve Gods** in its low enclosure of marble parapet slabs between posts by the Way, open west and east [inferred: about 9.35 × 9.8 m as commonly given]; **the Herms** where the Way enters the Agora at its north-west corner [inferred: placing; the Herms were mutilated in 415]; **the boundary stone** ("I am the boundary of the Agora") by the Tholos [inferred: the stone is c. 500 BC].
- **The market:** booths of wicker screens (gerra) with a cloth over the front, a trestle table and goods — painted and black-glazed pottery, amphorae of oil and wine, fish on marble slabs, bread, myrtle, a banker's table with coins and a balance; a seller behind and buyers in the aisles [inferred: after the usual accounts of the Agora as a market with its "circles" of trades; not separately sourced here].

## 5. The town

About 1,200 courtyard houses of mudbrick on stone socles with tiled roofs, packed in blocks. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

Grey-white limestone, thin red soil, dry grass; olives and cypresses; a few plane trees in the Agora. [inferred]

## 7. Sky and climate

The clear Attic light; a little cloud sometimes. [inferred]

## 8. Paths

Along the Panathenaic Way through the Agora, up the ramp, through the Propylaia onto the summit, to the Parthenon's east front. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

As for the Parthenon (`docs/buildings/parthenon.md` §9): the aulos and the kithara.
