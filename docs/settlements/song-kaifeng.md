# Northern Song Bianjing (Kaifeng), c. 1120 — dossier

Settlement id: `song-kaifeng` · Style: `song` · Region: the Central Plain (the Bian River) · Code: `src/settlements/song-kaifeng/`
Status of this dossier: L1–L2 (the Iron Pagoda of the Kaibao (Youguo) monastery with its halls; the Bian River with the Rainbow Bridge and grain boats; the east wall with its water gate and gate tower; streets of shops, inns and houses along the river as in the Qingming scroll; ground, planting, weather, music). L3 built (§10): the Rainbow Bridge's woven arch, the grain boats, the city gate, the wine shops' colour gates, sedan chairs, the camel train, the crowd on the bridge, the pagoda's bells; the monastery's gate hall; the Sun Yang main shop's signs, lamps and wine jars; Physician Zhao's signboards; fortune tellers' stands; ox carts, the donkey-drawn barrow, a cask dray; trackers on the towpath; the wheat fields and earth banks of the suburb. L3 built since: the Iron Pagoda's glazed skin of moulded bricks, the gate hall's two guardians, the shops' name boards, two tea houses by the bridge. L3 built (third pass): a grain boat unloading at the south bank, the tax office by the gate, damp weathering. L3 recorded for later: the imperial city and the Zhou Bridge, the Xiangguo monastery, the glazed bricks' carved Buddhas.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** the Iron Pagoda, completed 17 June 1049, an octagonal solid-core brick tower of 13 storeys, 56.88 m (other sources 55.08 or 55.88 m), with an inner spiral stair [stated: Wikipedia; China Daily; architecturasinica]; faced with brown glazed bricks, hence its name [stated: Wikipedia (Youguo Temple)]; each side 4.16 m at the base [stated: Kaifeng old town (slideshare)]; densely set brackets in its eaves [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Setting:** the Bian River as painted by Zhang Zeduan in *Along the River during the Qingming Festival*, with the Rainbow Bridge, a timber arch [stated: slideshare (Qingming garden)]; the boulevard's stone Zhou Bridge over the Bian [stated: Wikipedia (Song architecture)].
- **Moment shown:** about 1120, under Huizong, before the fall of 1127.

The river east–west, the bridge north–south. [inferred]

## 4. The style

Northern Song timber building — tiled hip-and-gable halls, shops open to the street, many houses thatched in the suburbs; the pagoda of brown glazed brick imitating a timber pagoda, with eaves on brackets at every storey. [stated: the pagoda; inferred: the rest]

## 5. The town

Two-storey shops and restaurants on the bridge's approaches and along the river, single-storey houses behind, warehouses at the landings. [inferred from the scroll]

## 6. Ground and water

The flat Yellow River plain; the river's banks low and lined with stone near the bridge. (`ground.js`) [inferred]

## 7. Planting

## 10. The scroll in detail (L3)

- **Rainbow Bridge.** A woven timber arch (*bianmu gong*): two systems of logs alternating across the width, locked by transverse timbers [stated: happypontist; Yang (Nagasaki)]. Estimated from the painting: 18.5–19.2 m long, 8–9.5 m wide, logs about 40 cm across; **21 groups of ribs** across the width — 11 of the outer system (two long and two short members) and 10 of the inner (three members of equal length) — and **five transverse logs**; abutments of hard rock [stated: Yang; Fuzhou arch-bridges paper]. "Over 20 m" span, 8 m wide [stated: thepaper]. Ours: 20 m between the stone abutments, 8.6 m wide, 21 ribs of 0.38 m logs (11 four-member, 10 three-member), five transverse beams with rope lashings; the heights of the nodes follow from each system's logs passing under the other's beam [inferred: the weave as described]; short posts on the ribs carry cross-beams, a deck of planks and railings with balusters [inferred from the scroll]; the four tall posts at the bridge ends with crossed boards and a crane on top [inferred: as painted; not found in a text source]. Stalls with parasols along the bridge, a crowd at the railing gesturing at the boat below [stated: smarthistory (onlookers gesture at the approaching ship); inferred: the parasols, as painted].
- **Boats.** 28 boats in the scroll [stated: smarthistory]; the Bian boats were grain and cargo boats from the Huai and Yangzi [stated: rujiazg 29200]; near the city they unshipped their sails, laid down their masts and went by tow or scull; the boat at the bridge lowers its mast, which turned on a pivot at its foot (*kemian wei*) [stated: rujiazg 11895]. Ours: flat-bottomed hulls with square raised bow and stern and wales, a long house of boards with lattice windows under a vaulted mat roof, the bipod mast pivoting in a tabernacle (laid back on a crutch on most, being lowered on the boat before the bridge, up on two downstream), a stay, a great rudder and a sculling oar [inferred from the scroll for the bipod form, the house and the rudder].
- **City gate and walls.** The scroll's outer wall is of rammed earth, without battlements, partly fallen and overgrown; the gate stands open, without barbican [stated: rujiazg 29200]; camels come out of the gate [stated: aisixiang; smarthistory]. Ours: the wall in battered rammed earth, the gate platform faced in brick over the road with a trapezoidal passage lined with timber posts, struts and lintels (the Song timber-framed passage, before brick arches) [inferred], a single-eaved hip-roofed tower [inferred]; a train of five camels with drivers coming out.
- **Wine shops.** The colour gate (*cailou huanmen*), a gateway of bamboo and timber bound with coloured silk, the grander the richer the house; the Sun Yang main shop's two or three storeys high; five in the scroll; wine flags of breadths of blue and white cloth; lantern-box signs ("Sun Yang", "main shop", "fine wine") before the door [stated: rujiazg 3505]; colour balls, and cranes bound in an S as decoration [stated: thepaper; aisixiang]. Ours: five colour gates at the two-storey shops nearest the bridge, the tallest 8.6 m — posts and rails of poles in two planes, crossed bracing, a pointed lintel over the door, a crest of pointed frames with pennants, festoons of red, green and ochre silk with balls, an S-necked crane; a blue-and-white flag on its pole; lantern boxes.
- **Sedan chairs.** Nine in the scroll [stated: smarthistory]. Ours: five on the street, each with its two bearers [inferred: the cabin, curtains and poles as painted].
- **Iron Pagoda.** Wind bells at the corners of its eaves [inferred: as it stands; the count not checked].
- **The monastery's gate hall (shanmen).** A Song/Liao gate hall as at Dule Monastery (984): three bays wide and two deep, single-eaved hip (wudian) roof, about 10 m tall, bracket sets on the columns and between them, the bracket sets about half the columns' height; two guardian statues inside [stated: architecturasinica 000024b; Wikipedia (Dule Temple)]. Ours: bays 4.4 + 6.1 + 4.4 m by 2 × 4.4 m, standing in the precinct's south wall, columns 4.4 m on stone bases, the middle line of columns carrying the doors (red studded leaves turned back inward) and red walls with a grey-brick dado in the side bays (the fen xin cao plan) [inferred: the Song gate-hall plan], puzuo of bearing block, cross arms, two tiers of arms stepping out and a ling'gong on every column and between each pair, a coffered ceiling, a hip roof; an upright plaque under the south eaves in the middle bay [inferred]. The guardians recorded for later. (This replaces the precinct wall's own gate, whose tiled coping read as a solid roof over the walker in the collision field and shut the monastery.)
- **Sun Yang main shop (Sun Yang zhengdian).** Its colour gate two or three storeys high; three lantern-box signs before the door, "Sun Yang", "main shop", "fine wine", candles inside [stated: rujiazg 3505]; a large sign "Sun Yang house", red gardenia lamps (zhizi deng) at the entrance; "wine jars heaped like a hill" in the back yard [stated: aboluowang 614740]. Ours: the first of the five colour gates (8.6 m) with three lantern boxes, two red gardenia lamps hung from the door's pointed lintel, a tall white standing sign at its corner, and behind the house a heap of jars of dark glazed and unglazed stoneware lying in three stepped rows, back to back, mouths out, with three upright before it [inferred: the stacking]. Characters on signs are abstract strokes, not legible text [inferred: no lettering in the world].
- **Physician Zhao's (Zhao taicheng jia).** A clinic and pharmacy at the scroll's end; a vertical signboard outside: "true formula of gathered-fragrance pills, cures what wine has harmed" [stated: aboluowang 614740; rmzxw 2977442], and one for "the five exhaustions and seven injuries" [stated: rmzxw]; two women with children come to be seen, an old woman receiving them [stated: rmzxw]. Ours: a shop on the gate street with two standing signboards (white boards in black frames on braced feet under a little tiled hood; nine and seven characters), the two women with two children at the door, an old woman inside [inferred: the signboards' form, as painted].
- **Fortune tellers.** A fortune-telling stall with three signs, "divine lessons", "reading fates", "resolving doubts" [stated: aboluowang 614740]; "fortune tellers and seers" among the scroll's people [stated: Wikipedia (Along the River)]; a seer with a fan before a group of young men, the examinations two weeks after Qingming [stated: rmzxw]. Ours: two stands (gate street; the bridge's north approach) — an awning of matting on four bamboo poles, a table and cloth, inkstone and a tube of lots, the seer seated on a stool, five young men in white and blue round the table; two signs tied to the front poles, one behind the seer [inferred: the stand's form].
- **Carts.** In the scroll: ox carts boarded at sides and ends under a reed-mat cover, pulled by three oxen, and a lady's ox carriage with a palm-fibre canopy; a dray (ping tou che) with one driver and two donkeys carrying two wine casks; a large cart (tai ping che) with four donkeys; the chuan che, a one-wheeled cart pushed from behind and steered at the shafts, a donkey pulling in front; a man-pushed wheelbarrow [stated: qlwb 2022-01-21 A12]; 46 donkeys and mules and 20 horses [stated: rujiazg 14532]. Ours: two ox carts (two tall 16-spoked wheels with iron tyres, a box of planks on its frame, the vaulted reed-mat cover on bent hoops, shafts and yoke; an ox in the shafts and two ahead in traces, the drover) [inferred: the arrangement of the three oxen]; two chuan che (the barrow of the objects, a donkey ahead in traces, a man at the handles and one steering at the front); a two-wheeled dray with two casks and two donkeys [inferred: the dray's form is the generic two-wheeled cart].
- **Trackers.** Trackers, heads down, hauled a boat right up to the Rainbow Bridge [stated: rmzxw]; "men at the river bank, pulling the larger ships" [stated: Wikipedia (Along the River)]. Ours: a 16 m boat with its mast up on the north side of the river east of the bridge, heading upstream (west) toward it; five trackers in single file on the earth bank of the towpath, the line from the masthead over their shoulders [inferred: the line from the masthead, as Chinese tow lines were made fast high to clear the bank].
- **Suburbs.** The scroll opens in the countryside: fields, farmers, herders [stated: Wikipedia (Along the River)]. Ours: east of the houses, strips of winter wheat 17 m wide between grass balks with a willow here and there, every fourth strip fallow; at Qingming (early April) the wheat is green and knee-high, rendered with the flora's green crop tuft [inferred: the season; the stand-in plant]; farmers hoeing, two straw stacks, a train of five donkeys with panniers coming in by the road; willows on the earth banks (thick on the south bank, a few on the towpath side).

- **The Iron Pagoda's glazed bricks.** The whole exterior is of glazed bricks, "more than 50 distinct patterns", with "figures of Buddha and Bodhisattva, different flowers, and lions and kylins" [stated: travelchinaguide]; "standing and seated Buddhas, monks, singers, flying dancers, flowers, lions, dragons, and other legendary beasts", some 1,600 carved interlocking elements, doors, windows, columns, brackets and eaves built of 28 standard glazed-brick components, doors on four sides, 104 bells under the eaves [stated: ancient-history-sites 133351]. Ours (`src/styles/song/iron.js`): on every face of every storey, rows of moulded bricks at a 0.42 m pitch [inferred: the size] — a seated Buddha in a niche (mandorla, head halo, lotus seat, crossed legs, hands in the lap, topknot), every fourth row a rosette in a round frame, under the brackets a frieze of flying figures over clouds on double bricks with a seated lion at each end [inferred: the arrangement]; the openings arched, in glazed-brick surrounds (doors on the four faces to the compass points, windows on the diagonals) [stated: doors on four sides; inferred: the arch, after the pagoda as it stands]; engaged corner columns; a row of glazed bracket sets at 0.7 of a timber set's size under each eave [inferred]. The bricks are a few shapes placed thousands of times as instances.
- **The gate hall's guardians.** At the Dule Monastery's gate hall (the model of ours) two guardians (jingang lishi) "more than 5 m tall" stand under the eaves, one west and one east, leaning a little forward over the one who passes between them; the west one glares with mouth open, the east one's lips are shut; each holds a vajra weapon [stated: thepaper 29735734]. Ours (`src/styles/song/guardian.js`): 4.6 m figures on low stone plinths in the front halves of the side bays, turned in toward the way through; bare-chested and muscled, a short skirt with a knotted sash, a scarf in a hoop over the head and falling past the arms, hair in a topknot, a wide stance on a rock; the west one raises a clenched fist, the east one (mirrored) plants a vajra staff; painted clay, red-brown flesh, green and red cloth, gilt jewellery [inferred: the dress and the colours, after Liao and Song guardians in general; the mouths are not modelled].
- **Shop name boards.** The scroll's shops carry their names on boards — the inn's "Jiuzhu Wang yuanwai jia" (lodgings of the Wang family) [stated: rujiazg 6273]; the "Shiqian" wine shop's lantern box [stated: rujiazg 6273; digitaling 58792]. Ours: on every shop but the five wine shops a horizontal board hung on iron hooks from the beam over its opening, two to five characters (abstract strokes), black with gilt, white with black, or red with gilt, in a moulded frame [inferred: the form and colours]. Instanced.
- **Tea houses.** *Dongjing menghua lu*: tea houses lit their lamps at the fifth watch and traded till dawn; the better ones "clean and elegant, every vessel of the first quality, chairs and tables neat"; "flowers of the four seasons set out, paintings by famous hands hung up"; "flower racks with rare pines and junipers" [stated: rmzxw 3904620 (the last two after *Mengliang lu*, of Lin'an)]. Ours: two one-storey shops on the north riverside street nearest the bridge are tea houses (the `tea_house` of the Hangzhou dossier §10: open front between posts under a lattice valance, hanging landscapes and plum vases on the back wall, the counter with brazier, kettle and tea bowls, a rack of potted pines), each with two tea tables and their benches, customers seated, the host by the counter, a two-character name board on the front beam.

- **Landing (third pass).** In the scroll the boats moored by the bank are the "ten-thousand-picul boats" (over 500 t); "the ropes staked into the bank and the boards for going on and off the boats are clearly visible"; "some boats are moored at the bank, unloading"; at Qingming the canal transport from Huainan to the capital reopened [stated: aisixiang 93358; thepaper 1391144]. Ours (`src/styles/song/landing.js`): the moored grain boat east of the bridge on the south side unloading — two gangplanks with cleats from its foredeck's gunwale up to the bank, a mooring line from bow and stern to stakes driven into the bank, sacks heaped on the foredeck and in two stacks on the bank; porters, some bare to the waist, carrying sacks on the shoulder up one plank and coming back down the other, one stooping to the heap on deck, one setting his sack down on the bank; a clerk in a blue robe and cap tallying; the boatman with his pole on the stern [inferred: the cargo as grain in sacks, the porters and their work].
- **Tax office (third pass).** By the city gate "goods waiting to be taxed stand at the door, and inside the tax official sits at a table registering them" [stated: aisixiang 93358; thepaper 1391144]. Ours: in the first shop inside the wall on the north side of the gate road, sacks at its door, the official in a green robe and black cap seated at a table under the eaves with the register, inkstone and brush, two merchants waiting before him, a porter squatting by his load [inferred: the table under the eaves rather than inside, so it can be seen; the house is the street's usual shop].
- **Weathering.** The climate's dampness set to 0.4 (the spring rains of Qingming) [inferred].

## 11. Street life, interiors and sound (B1, life part)

Code: `src/settlements/song-kaifeng/` movers.js, fauna.js, smoke.js, trades.js, shops.js, props.js, interiors.js, music.js; all derived from `layout.js` (lots, streets, river, bridge).

### Townscape: street life

- **Dress.** Officials and scholars wore the *futou*, "a soft cap", and a long loose-sleeved robe with a round collar; commoners wore plainer cloth, the regulations on colour changing over the dynasty (white, later black permitted for civilians and clerks); labourers a short coarse jacket (*duanhe*) or trousers; women the *ru* (jacket) with a *qun* (skirt), trousers under it, and the *beizi*, a loose outer coat worn by women and men; a veil (*gaitou*) when riding [stated: ancient-history-sites 174079]. Ours (`movers.js` WALKERS): scholars and merchants in robes with a black soft cap over a topknot, women in long gowns with a crossed collar in pale green, rose and white, labourers in trousers; porters with the carrying pole and two baskets, porters bare to the waist with a sack on the shoulder (as the scroll's landing), drovers with staff and straw hat, monks shaven in grey-brown and ochre; in rain umbrellas of oiled paper for the robed and palm-fibre capes with straw hats for the porters, drovers and boatmen [inferred: the colours, the trades' dress after the scroll; the cape from the Suzhou method]. Women with parasols in the dry (a shallow silk cone on bamboo ribs) [inferred: the scroll's stalls carry parasols; held by ladies is our reading]. Numbers: 46 walkers by day, 7 at night, more on the main street, the riverside streets and the bridge (weights from the share of shops along each street) [inferred].
- **Boats on the Bian.** Ten-thousand-picul grain boats and cargo boats from the Huai and Yangzi; near the city they laid down their masts and went by tow or scull [stated: rujiazg 29200, 11895; aisixiang 93358, see §10]. Ours (`movers.js` BOAT, boatShapes): the song style's grain boat (§10) at 16 m, its mast laid back on the crutch, rudder left out, a long stern oar (the *yuloh*) worked by a boatman standing on the stern deck; two kinds: the passenger boat with a mat awning on bamboo hoops over the foredeck and a passenger seated cross-legged, and the grain boat with sacks heaped on the foredeck and in the waist; they keep to the right-hand lane, pass the Rainbow Bridge's 20 m arch one at a time, and keep clear of the boats moored along the banks (read from plan.js and town.js) and of the bridge's abutments [inferred: the sculling oar and awning; the passenger boats are ours: the scroll's boats are cargo boats].
- **Animals.** Carp in the river and red carp near the landings; swallows over the river and the main street; tree sparrows on the paving and the monastery's walls; crows at twilight over the monastery; egrets and a heron on the earth banks [inferred: common species of the Yellow River plain; none of them in a text source of the period for this exact place].
- **Smoke.** Kitchen smoke from a share of houses at the three meals (all inns, wine shops, tea houses and cook-shops burn), steam from tea houses and cook-shops (tea houses opened at the fifth watch and traded till dawn [stated: rmzxw 3904620, §10]), incense at the monastery's censer, a dawn mist over the Bian [inferred].
- **The trades of the scroll** (`trades.js`, `shops.js`): wine (the five colour-gate shops, §10) with the blue-and-white flag and the counter of jars with red-bound mouths; tea (lantern box, jars and cakes of tea: Song tea was pressed into cakes, dried, ground and whisked [stated: ancient-history-sites 174077]); the physician (gourd, mortar and packets; Physician Zhao, §10); cook-shops with steamers; cloth; grain (the measure, sacks, bin); books; lacquer; pottery; iron; incense and joss paper; saddlery (the scroll has saddle shops [inferred: as painted]); butchers with hung joints; inns (lantern and gardenia lamps, §10). Dongjing's taverns: the licensed *zheng dian* (72 in Dongjing) and the lesser *jiao dian* that bought their wine from them; the Fan Lou of three storeys, open through the night; courtesans waiting along the corridors, wandering singers coming in uninvited for tips [stated: rujiazg 23479, after Dongjing menghua lu and Zhou Mi]. Each trade's sign hangs from a bamboo pole on a stone foot, the goods on the apron and on trestles; the trade of each lot follows from where it stands (the finer trades by the bridge, inns, saddlers and smiths by the gate, grain and wine at the landings) [inferred].

### Interiors (`interiors.js`, `props.js`)

- **Furniture.** Chairs, benches, stools and higher tables had become common, though mats and low seating remained; the folding chair with crossed legs; plain polished wood widely used, lacquer for the wealthy; raised beds and platforms; ceramic pillows; ewers pouring hot water into bowls for tea; dark conical Jian bowls [stated: ancient-history-sites 174077]; the canopy bed and screens [inferred: forms after Song paintings and tombs; not checked against a museum object]. Ours: plain tables with set-in legs and stretchers, long benches with splayed legs, stools, the back chair with its top rail turned out beyond the posts, the couch/platform, the canopy bed with a white curtain, chests with iron fittings, open shelves, the three-panel screen with an ink landscape, hanging scrolls, a plum vase with a blossoming branch, lamp stands with oil dishes, red paper lanterns.
- **Shop** (41 of the lots within 110 m of the bridge): the counter behind the open front with a gap for the door, a ledger and a steelyard on it, shelves along the back stocked by trade (jars, bolts, volumes, boxes, packets, pans, bridles), a chest, a stool, a lantern, a scroll. **Tea house** (the tea lots within 160 m; the two by the bridge keep the style's `tea_house`): tables with benches and a tea set, the counter with brazier and kettle, three scrolls, a plum vase, a stepped rack of potted pines [stated: the tea house's flowers, paintings and pines, §10; inferred: the arrangement]. **Tavern** (the wine shops): the counter with jars, bowls and a dipper, shelves of jars, four or six tables with stools and a wine pot, a screen, red lanterns. **Inn**: a long table with benches, the keeper's desk, beds with canopy behind plank partitions, chests and lamps. **House**: the main room's table between two armchairs under a hanging scroll, a side table, the bed, chest, lamp, screen; the kitchen's brick stove with an iron pot and chimney, a water vat and firewood [inferred]. 93 lots in all; their keepers, customers and hosts as figures (`interiorLife`).

### Sound (`music.js`)

- The score's zones are the plans' room kinds: **open** (street, river): the pipa of wandering singers and a dizi above, a breath of *sheng* below (the engine's reed organ); **hall** (monastery): a chant over a drone, wooden fish, bowl-bell [inferred: the practice as in the Tang Foguang score]; **tea**: pipa and a sung voice, a clapper keeping time; **tavern**: the singing girls' pipa and voice over a hand drum, brighter [stated: courtesans singing in the taverns; a mural of a young woman with a waist drum singing for guests, rujiazg 23479]; **inn**: low drone, a few notes; **shop**: little but a clapper; **house**: the guqin, slow and sparse [inferred].
