Class P (point), radius 90 m.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** a Dogon village at the foot of the Bandiagara Escarpment (Sanga, Ireli, Banani and their neighbours are the type), listed by UNESCO in 1989 (ref. 516, criteria v and vii) as a cultural and natural landscape of about 400,000 ha with some 250 villages [stated: focusongeography.org "Mali"; Wikipedia "Bandiagara Escarpment"]. Ours is a composite village, not one of them [inferred].
- **The cliff:** a sandstone escarpment about 150 km long (other passages: over 200 km), about 500 m above the sandy flats of the Seno-Gondo plain to the south [stated: Wikipedia "Bandiagara Escarpment"; focusongeography.org]. Our disc shows only its foot and the first 55 m of it; the rest is above the view [inferred].
- **Moment shown:** about 1930, the end of the rains (October–November): millet standing ripe in the fields and the first stalks cut and bundled, the granaries filling [inferred; stated: millet is planted after the first June or July rains and harvested in October, focusongeography.org]. Before the onion gardens (Griaule is said to have encouraged them near Sangha later, Wikipedia "Dogon people"), so none are shown [inferred]. The Tellem cliff houses stand empty, as now [stated: focusongeography.org: the Tellem left the escarpment; "abandoned Tellem dwellings are carved into the sheer cliff face", 5cense.com].
- **History used:** Toloy settlers c. 3rd c. BC, the Tellem built their cliff dwellings from the 11th century, the Dogon arrived in the 14th and displaced them; Islam reached the Dogon only in the early 19th century [stated: focusongeography.org; Wikipedia]. The Dogon took the Tellem's stone-and-clay houses as the pattern for their own [stated: habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu].
- **Caveat on cosmology:** the well-known cosmological readings of village form (Griaule's account) are disputed; we use only what is plain to see [stated: DESIGN §7 note].

## 2. Layout of the settlement

- Villages sit "at the foot of the cliffs, not in the cliffs", packed tightly, often sharing walls, around the head man's house (*ginna*); an average of about 44 houses [stated: habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu; Wikipedia "Dogon people"]. Narrow winding paths run between mud-covered houses and granaries [stated: focusongeography.org].
- Farmers' high-status houses near the centre; blacksmiths, hunters, leatherworkers, woodcarvers and the menstruation huts (*punulu*, round) on the outskirts [stated: focusongeography.org; Wikipedia: built outside the village, of lower quality].
- The toguna is the first building of a village; in cliff villages it overlooks the plain, and doubles as a lookout; one per neighbourhood in larger villages [stated: dogon-lobi.ch; focusongeography.org].
- Our disc (radius 90): the cliff in the north (-z), its foot at about z = -46; the village on the talus slope below it in three terraced rows of compounds with stepped lanes between; the toguna and a small open place at the village's lower middle where the lanes meet; the plain to the south with millet fields, a baobab and scattered acacias; the Tellem dwellings on two ledges of the cliff face [inferred: composite from the sources above; orientation arbitrary].
- Altars at the lanes' ends: conical mud altars of the Lébé kind, and the *binu* shrine like a small house with a door [stated: Wikipedia "Dogon people"].

## 3. Orientation

- The cliff runs along the north of the disc; the village and its fronts face south down the slope to the plain [inferred: the real escarpment runs north-east to south-west with the plain to the south-east, Wikipedia: "lower sandy flats to the south"]. Sun from the south in the afternoon lights the facades; the cliff face is in shade in the morning, lit at midday [inferred].

## 4. The landmarks

### 4.1 The toguna (men's house, "house of words")
- Open on all sides, held up by eight posts of wood (here *Prosopis africana*) on the Seno plain, or of stone; many carved with ancestors, masks and sexual symbols, older posts in an archaic style [stated: focusongeography.org; dogon-lobi.ch; Wikipedia "Dogon people"].
- The roof is a thick mat of millet stalks in eight layers (another source: three, for cliff, plain and plateau); so low that one cannot stand upright, which keeps a quarrel from turning to blows and gives shade [stated: Wikipedia "Dogon people"; focusongeography.org; dogon-lobi.ch; 5cense.com].
- Only men enter; they rest through the dry-season heat, settle the village's affairs, and make rope and baskets there [stated: Wikipedia; dogon-lobi.ch].
- **Our build:** 8 forked posts of 0.2–0.26 m on low stone footings, carved with ancestor figures, zig-zags and a pair of breasts on the post nearest the visitor (Seno-style figures) [inferred]; 8 layers of millet-stalk bundles, alternating direction, about 1.1 m thick in all, bundle ends showing at the edges, a few stones on top as weights [inferred]; a beaten earth floor with a low stone-and-mud bench along the back [inferred]. The roof stands 1.95 m clear above the floor so that a visitor can walk in [inferred: the real one is too low to stand in; we raise it to make it enterable — written here, not hidden].
- Plan about 7.2 × 5.4 m, roof about 8.2 × 6.4 m [inferred: sizes of photographed togunas run from 4 to 8 m; no measured source read].

### 4.2 The family compound and its ginna (enterable)
- The *ginna* is the house of the senior man of the lineage; it forms one side of a courtyard with a cylindrical kitchen at the far end [stated: focusongeography.org].
- Rectangular, flat-roofed; walls of mud, cow dung and millet straw (adobe bricks of mud, straw and dung), new coats added regularly; stone hard to find, used for footings; plaster of twigs, baobab bark and mud; roof slab of forked wooden posts and beams [stated: focusongeography.org; habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu].
- The facade is windowless and decorated with rows of rectangular niches (the page counts eighty) standing for the first ancestors and their descendants; two doors, carved with rows of male and female figures; the raised living part reached by a ladder cut from a tree trunk; altars and a small shrine inside [stated: eartharchitecture.org "Ginna House"].
- Inside: a central room where the family eats and sleeps, a few small storerooms, a round kitchen; the roof terrace is reached through the kitchen; strangers go no further than the entrance hall [stated: habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu; focusongeography.org].
- **Our build:** the ginna 7.0 × 5.0 m, the facade 4.2 m high with pilasters and three rows of niches either side of the carved door; entrance hall (the strangers' limit, which we allow the visitor past), central room with hearth stones, a mat, jars and a forked post under the beams; a storeroom with grain jars; the kitchen as a round room against the courtyard with a notched-log ladder to the roof terrace [inferred from the above]. Interior height 2.1 m; walls 0.45 m thick.
- Granaries: cylinders of mud, dung and straw on a raised platform of stones against rodents; square wooden doors carved with the family's history (a crocodile and a turtle in one photographed); male granaries (millet, sorghum) with a high door, a second male type with a door at ground level used as a shelter by old men; female granaries, square (belongings) and round (beans, baobab leaves) [stated: focusongeography.org; 5cense.com]. Roofs a pointed cap of millet straw; the tall roofs mark a rich village [stated: focusongeography.org; Wikipedia]. Our granaries: 1.4–2.0 m across, wall 1.8–2.4 m, hat 1.1–1.6 m, the hat overhanging 0.25 m and standing on a ring of stones [inferred sizes].
- Outer walls often decorated with geometric designs [stated: focusongeography.org]: bands of triangles and zig-zags in a lighter or darker wash on some facades [inferred].

### 4.3 The Tellem dwellings in the cliff
- Built from the 11th century in recesses of the cliff for protection, cylindrical granaries in the rock recesses; tombs about two thousand years old high in the face; the Tellem carved caves for the dead high up, away from flash floods [stated: focusongeography.org; Wikipedia].
- **Our build:** two ledges of the cliff face, at about 26 and 40 m, under overhangs of the sandstone; on them small flat-roofed rectangular mud-and-stone rooms and round granaries, doors tiny and square, several fallen in, none reachable on foot [inferred].

## 5. The townscape

- Compound: ginna on the slope side, courtyard wall of mud about 1.8 m, granaries (2–5), a kitchen, a low shelter, stacked millet stalks, firewood; walls shared with the next compound [stated: packed tightly, sharing walls, Wikipedia; inferred: forms].
- Terracing: the village is cut into the talus in rows; dry-stone retaining walls at the downhill edges of the pads; the lanes climb between them [inferred; stated for stone lines and terraces in the fields: focusongeography.org].
- Public: the toguna; a small open place with the Lébé altar (conical); the *punulu* round huts at the village's edge; the shrine of the *binu* [stated: Wikipedia].

## 7. People

- Men in tunics and wide trousers of indigo or white cotton and a cap [inferred: the usual dress of the region; no source read]; the Hogon in white, later a red fez [stated: Wikipedia]. Women in wraps of indigo-dyed or white cotton, bare-headed or with a cloth, jars on the head [inferred].
- Work: women pound millet with large mallets while singing, carry water, gather wild fruits, tubers, nuts and honey; men weave rope and baskets in the toguna, farm, hunt, forge [stated: 5cense.com; Wikipedia; dogon-lobi.ch].
- Masks (kanaga, sirige) belong to the *dama* funeral; not shown in motion [stated: Wikipedia].

## 8. Plants and animals

- Millet is the staple, with sorghum, groundnuts, calabash, rice [stated: focusongeography.org; Wikipedia]; planted as `wheat` in the engine (no millet species yet) [inferred].
- Baobab (leaves eaten, bark in plaster) [stated: focusongeography.org]; acacia and other thorn scrub, dry grass, on the plain [inferred: Sahelian].
- Cattle, goats, chickens; totem animals (snake, crocodile, tortoise) [stated: 5cense.com]; kites, swallows, doves, hornbills [inferred].

## 9. Climate

- Near the southern edge of the Sahara; wet season June to mid-October, dry January to May, up to 47 C in February–March; Harmattan dust [stated: focusongeography.org]. At the moment shown (end of the rains) the air is clearer than in the dry season; we keep a light haze [inferred]. Surface water scarce: no stream is drawn [stated].

## 10. Sound

- Millet pounded in wooden mortars with work songs, call-and-response greetings, goats, chickens, a dog, doves, the wind in the thatch [stated: 5cense.com; inferred].
- Music: the rattle of a wooden rod with calabashes of the circumcised boys; drums; a pentatonic voice line [stated: Wikipedia; inferred].

## 12. Deepening pass (what was added, and why)

- **The escarpment is built, not terrain.** The terrain's rim clamp (a height of at most 0.9 m per metre from the rim, layout.js `height`) and the rim check (no 1.5 m/m slope at r ≥ 60) cannot hold a 36 m cliff inside a 90 m disc, and the old mound was ~35 m only for |x| < 10 and ~12 m elsewhere, with three of its four Tellem ledges floating in the air or buried in the rock. The terrain keeps only a low shelf (`CLIFF_H` 9); the escarpment is a built wall of strata (cliff.js): lattice 2 m × 1.2 m, hard bands proud and soft ones recessed, a pale capping layer, 34–40 m above the plateau at the middle, sinking into the ground toward |x| = 66 [inferred: the real cliff is ~500 m high and 150 km long, so any wall in a disc is a cut-out of it; the strata and colours are those of the Bandiagara sandstone, red-brown with a paler cap, as in the photographs of focusongeography.org and 5cense.com].
- **Tellem alcoves** are cut in the face (holes in the lattice, floor, back wall blackened by smoke, overhang): six, 8–12 m wide, 3.4 m deep, 6 m high, 22–38 m above the ground, with small flat-roofed rooms (some with a second small room, some ruined), round granaries with hats, burial niches in the back wall, a notched log left leaning [stated: Tellem dwellings and granaries in recesses of the cliff, tombs high in the face, Wikipedia; focusongeography.org].
- **Ten of the eighteen ginnas are enterable** (all of row C and B within 34 m of the middle, two of row A): the same plan as the first (entrance hall, central room with hearth and forked post, storeroom, round kitchen with its ladder, roof terrace), each with its own seed's niches, shelves of the ancestors' vessels, pegs, cord of cloth, sacks, firewood [stated: ginna plan, eartharchitecture.org; habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu; inferred: furnishings].
- **Fronts at eye level:** rubble plinth (stone is used for footings, focusongeography.org), mud bench with a mat between the doors, water jars by the doors, firewood or a mortar, gourds and bunches of millet heads hung on pegs, soot over the doors; weathering of mud: rain streaks from the cornice, patches of new plaster (the walls are re-plastered regularly, focusongeography.org), cracks; thorn laid along the tops of the courtyard walls against the goats, patches, stones, upturned pots [inferred].
- **The smith's forge** at the village's edge: a sober shelter with a thatched roof on a piled stone wall [stated: dogon-lobi.ch "The smithy"]; the smiths are an endogamous caste living at the fringe (Jèmè-na, Irine), making agricultural tools [stated: dogon-lobi.ch; encyclopedia.com "Dogon": ironworking restricted to a craft caste]. Ours: open to the south, furnace of clay, two goatskin bellows, an anvil stone on a stump, hammers, charcoal, a quench trough, hoe blades and axe heads on a bench, tongs on the wall [inferred: the usual African smith's kit; no Dogon source read for the details].
- **Weaving:** men weave (with basketry and rope) [stated: encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia]; the strip loom stands in the toguna, the warp laid out along the ground to a stone weight, a weaver on a low stool [inferred: the strip loom of West Africa].
- **Indigo dyeing:** four lined pits with cloths of indigo, blue and white drying on a cord, balls of indigo in a basket [inferred: West African indigo dyeing; the dyers are women in much of Mali; no Dogon source read].
- **Threshing floors, goat pens:** beaten-earth floors with a heap of millet heads, sheaves stood round, mortars and pestles; stone-and-thorn pens with goats and sheep (men tend the livestock; goats, sheep, donkeys, dogs and poultry are the domestic animals) [stated: encyclopedia.com; inferred: forms].
- **The place:** two fig/tamarind shade trees by the toguna and two more at the edges, mats with goods and sellers, benches, women at the mortars; lane litter (flat stones, chaff, dung, stalks, sherds) [inferred].
- **People and animals near the visitor:** weaver, smith and boy at the bellows, dyers, pounding women, sellers, seated women and men at the doors with children by them, hens, goats and sheep at the walls and in the pens, donkeys [inferred].
- **Planting:** millet (planted as `wheat` with tall `maize` among it, no millet species yet), halfa, thorn acacia and a fig rooted in the rocks at the cliff's foot, boulders fallen from the face [inferred].
- Houses are separated by stone walls and so close that a floor may begin where the neighbour's roof ends [stated: encyclopedia.com "Dogon"]; our compounds keep a 3.4 m lane between them so that a walker can pass [inferred].
