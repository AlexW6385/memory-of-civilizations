## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Wat Phra Si Sanphet, the royal temple within the old palace grounds. Ramathibodi II had two chedis built in 1492 for the ashes of his father Borommatrailokkanat and his brother Borommaracha III; Borommaracha IV built a third in 1529 for Ramathibodi II [stated: Wikipedia, Wat Phra Si Sanphet; ayutthaya-history.com, Architecture of Wat Phra Sri Sanphet]. The site had been the palace of Ramathibodi I (1350–51); in 1448 Borommatrailokkanat built a new palace to the north and made the old grounds holy [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Moment shown:** about 1687, the end of King Narai's reign (1657–1688), when Simon de La Loubère came with the second French embassy (from September 1687) and described the city in *Du Royaume de Siam* (1691) [stated: Wikipedia, Du royaume de Siam; ayutthaya-history.com]. [inferred: the chedis are shown as the chronicles have them, finished 1529; a late-17th-c. dating of the present form is argued by Piriya Krairiksh (ayutthaya-history.com), and the chronicles record a rebuild by Borommakot in 1742; either way the three bell chedis in a row are what stood at the temple in Narai's reign.]
- **Why here:** Ayutthaya was Siam's capital for 417 years, Narai's court traded with France, the Dutch, English, Persians, Chinese and Japanese, and La Loubère compared its canal-laced streets to Venice [stated: ayutthaya-history.com, Boathouses].

## 3. Orientation

North–south long axis of the island; the chedis' row and the royal wihan east–west, the wihan's front to the east, so the visitor from the river road looks east to the three spires and the main hall [stated: ayutthaya-history.com, Architecture]. The sun climbs high (lat 14.4° N): shadows short at noon.

## 4. The landmark: Wat Phra Si Sanphet

### 4.1 The three chedis [stated unless marked]
- Identical bell-shaped chedis on one rectangular platform, in the Sukhothai bell type derived from the Srivijayan stupa, set on superimposed pedestals [ayutthaya-history.com].
- Each has four porches jutting out toward the cardinal points, each porch crowned by a small chedi (stupika) identical to the main one; in the porch niches a standing Buddha on three sides; the east porch leads to the small chamber holding the king's ashes [ayutthaya-history.com].
- Vertical pillars (sao han) round the shaft support the spire above the harmika [ayutthaya-history.com].
- Heights are not given by the sources found. [inferred: about 36 m to the finial, from the bell's ratio to the rows of the pedestals in the plates and photographs of the restored chedis; the bell about 9 m across on a pedestal of three square tiers about 14 m to a side.]
- Finish: lime-plaster stucco, whitewashed [inferred: surviving stucco; the finials and the top of the spire gilded, as the temple's gilded fittings were looted in 1767 (Wikipedia)].
- The four corner chedis (Phra Aggiya) of the gallery match the main chedis in style [ayutthaya-history.com].
- Between the chedis stand three mandapa on the square base with spired tops [ayutthaya-history.com] [inferred in plan: small square halls with a stepped spire].

### 4.2 The royal wihan (Vihara Luang) with the gilded image [stated unless marked]
- Built 1499 under Ramathibodi II, isolated from the chedis, east of them; originally eleven sections of about 4.6 m, about 50 m in all [ayutthaya-history.com]; the Phra Si Sanphet image cast around then.
- Front and back porches with two entries each; no windows, only vertical slits; a gabled roof with two interior and two exterior rows of pillars forming colonnades on each side; capitals in places stylised lotuses; wooden gables and tiled roofs [ayutthaya-history.com].
- Inside a pedestal where the golden Buddha stood [ayutthaya-history.com]. The image: 16 m high, bronze core of about 64 tons, gilded with about 343 kg of gold, the pedestal 8 m long [Wikipedia].
- [inferred in the model: a hall of seven sections (32 m) rather than eleven, so that it fits the compressed plan; the image a standing Buddha 16 m high would reach the roof, so the figure is shown as the hall's image at reduced height (about 10 m) on its long pedestal, gilded; the hall's roof three-tiered with the central tier highest, terracotta tiles, gilded finials (chofa), the gables wood, carved, gilt on red.]
- Interior [inferred]: columns lacquered red with gilt, the ceiling red with gold stars, walls plastered white with a band of painted figures low down.

### 4.3 The rest of the compound [stated]
- A cloister (Phra Rabieng) round the chedi and mandapa terrace, Subduing-Mara Buddhas facing outward, built under Prasat Thong (1629–56) [ayutthaya-history.com; Wikipedia].
- A cross-shaped prasat west of the platform added in Narai's reign (the Prasat Phra Narai), Greek-cross plan, a slender prang above, pointed-arch windows (European and Indo-Persian influence) [ayutthaya-history.com; Wikipedia].
- A bell tower before the royal wihan, brick columns, a five-tier roof; the ordination hall (ubosot) 33 × 15 m, brick, wooden roof of unglazed terracotta tiles, with sema stones; the sala of the Phra Chom Thong with its tiered gabled roof and seven bays; 26 small chapels each with a bell chedi along the outer wall; a high thick brick wall with battlements and four gates [ayutthaya-history.com].

## 5. The townscape

### 5.1 Houses [inferred from the Thai tradition and from La Loubère and Kaempfer's drawings unless marked]
- The common house on posts 2–2.5 m above the ground against floods, a floor of plank or split bamboo, walls of woven bamboo (fak) or plank, a steep gable roof of palm thatch (atap) or terracotta tile with the barge-board ends crossed as horns (kalae); a verandah and a ladder or steep stair; the space under the house for boats, tools and animals.
- The great houses of officials and the Chinese and foreign merchants: timber on posts with tiled roofs; brick shophouses of the Chinese along the canals [inferred].
- Floating houses (rueanae): a bamboo raft carrying a small thatched house, moored to posts by rattan, with a platform in front for goods; the shop is the boat or the raft [stated: La Loubère and Kaempfer describe them; here as inferred].

### 5.2 Canals and bridges
- The streets are water: boats are the road [stated: La Loubère, via ayutthaya-history.com]. Brick-faced banks with steps down; the ways on foot run along the banks as raised earth paths.
- Small bridges of hurdles (woven bamboo, on posts) and some taller arched brick bridges [stated: La Loubère, ayutthaya-history.com].

### 5.3 The floating market
- Four floating markets are listed in the "Description of Ayutthaya" [stated: ayutthaya-history.com]. [inferred: the vendors sit in small boats under bamboo-matting awnings, selling fruit, fish, rice, pots, cloth; buyers paddle alongside.]

## 6. Materials
Whitewashed lime stucco; fired brick (the walls and bridges); terracotta tile, unglazed [stated: ubosot roof]; thatch (atap) of nipa palm; teak and other timber; woven bamboo; gold leaf on the finials and the image; red lacquer for the columns; laterite for the plinths.

## 7. People and dress [inferred from La Loubère and the Dutch and French drawings unless marked]
Men and women alike in a length of cloth drawn between the legs and knotted at the waist (the panung), the chest bare or covered by a sash worn over one shoulder, the feet bare; the hair cropped short on top; women's cloth wrapped as a skirt with the sash across the breast; boatmen in a loincloth and a hat of palm leaf or straw; monks in saffron robes with an alms bowl; mandarins in a long-sleeved gown with a tall conical hat (lomphok) at court; the farmers of the Chao Phraya plain with a bamboo yoke and baskets.

## 8. Plants [inferred]
Coconut and areca palms, sugar palms, banyan and bodhi (pho) trees at the wats, tamarind, mango, bamboo clumps, lotus in the ponds, water hyacinth is not yet there (it came late, 19th–20th c.).

## 9. Sound [inferred]
The temple bell and the gong; the chant of monks; the long-necked fiddle (saw sam sai), the xylophone (ranat), the gong circle and the oboe (pi) of the piphat ensemble; boats' paddles and punt poles; cicadas, doves, the chop of the market's vendors.

## 10. Climate
Tropical monsoon: hot and humid, the sky hazy; rainy season May–October, floods in September–November [stated: Wikipedia: flooded each year]; algae and black mildew on plaster [inferred].

## 11. Deepening pass: the houses and the temple's furnishing (facts and what the model does with them)

### 11.1 Houses [inferred from the Thai house tradition and the plates of La Loubère and Kaempfer unless marked]
- The timber house on posts: floor 1.9–2.5 m up against the floods [stated: flooded each year, §10], a terrace (chan) open before the door at the floor's level, walls of plank or woven bamboo, a steep gable roof of palm thatch or terracotta tile with crossed bargeboard horns (kalae) at the ends. Model: `thai_house` in `src/styles/siamese/houses.js`, four wall finishes, tile on the larger houses, one room (two in the large house) entered by the stair and the terrace.
- The household altar: a shelf on the wall with a Buddha image, flowers and a candle; the spirit house (san phra phum) on a post at the corner of the yard, with its offerings [inferred: widespread Thai custom; its antiquity in the 17th c. is not documented in the sources read]. Model: both are built at every house.
- The space under the house in use [inferred]: boat drawn up, loom, fish drying, net, mortar and pestle, hammock, water jars at the stair, firewood.
- Chinese shophouses: brick on the ground floor with the front open to the lane, a counter, shelves of goods; timber upper floor; Chinese merchants settled along the canals and kept shops [inferred from the Chinese community's weight in the trade, see Wikipedia "Ayutthaya Kingdom"]. Model: `kind: 'shop'`, about a third of the houses fronting a canal or the river road; stair inside to a sleeping floor with a balcony, a vertical sign board, red lanterns, wares hung from the lintel.
- Floating shops: the shop is the boat or the raft [stated, §5.1]; the model's `floating_shop` is a raft with an open counter, goods, hung wares and a hipped roof, moored among the market's boats.

### 11.2 The temple and the palace [inferred unless marked]
- The compound's wall had battlements [stated: ayutthaya-history.com, §4.3]; the model crowns it with a row of leaf-shaped merlons and a coping.
- Boundary stones (sema) stand round the ordination hall, eight in little plastered bases [stated in general: a Thai ubosot is bounded by eight sema and a ninth buried in the floor]; pennants of white cloth (tung) on tall bamboo before the halls; offering tables with lotus and incense; candle stands.
- Inside the halls: a painted band on the walls, hanging lamps, mats before the dais, the preacher's seat, rows of small gilded images given as merit; the cloister's Buddhas face the platform on three sides and outward on the south.
- The throne in the palace hall under the multi-tiered umbrella (nine tiers is the king's) [stated for the Thai court in general; inferred for this hall].
- The palace gate: a pair of brick pylons with a three-tiered tiled roof over the way, a gilt finial [inferred]; the court's brick way, rows of palms and the stands of the royal umbrellas.
- The bell tower: stair to the bell's floor [inferred; Ayutthayan bell towers were of brick with a wooden floor, §4.3].
