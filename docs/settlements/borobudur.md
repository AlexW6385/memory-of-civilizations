# Sailendra Java (Borobudur), c. 825 — dossier

Settlement id: `borobudur` · Style: `javanese` · Region: Java (the Kedu plain) · Code: `src/settlements/borobudur/`
Status of this dossier: L2 (Borobudur at true size — the base, five square terraces with their galleries, balustrades, niches and small stupas, the stairs on the four sides, the three circular terraces with 72 latticed stupas, the crowning stupa; the hill it encloses; a monastery compound of timber halls; villages of houses on posts among palms, bamboo and fields; ground, planting, weather, music). Detail of §10: the pierced stupas with their Buddhas, the crowning stupa's finial, the kala–makara gateways, the makara spouts, pilgrims on the galleries; and §12: the niche Buddhas with the mudra of each direction, the relief panels with composed scenes on the main walls and the balustrades, devotees, monks and the monastery's life. L3 recorded for later: the 2,672 relief panels told one by one (each its own subject), the 32 lions at the gates, Mendut and Pawon, the volcanoes and rivers, processions.

## 1. The real example

- **Landmark:** Borobudur, built by the Sailendra dynasty c. 800–825 of about two million andesite blocks [stated: Studio Matrx]; it encloses a small hill [stated: Britannica].
- **Moment shown:** about 825, newly finished.

## 2. Layout of the settlement (radius 380 m)

| Part | Where (m, +x east, +z south) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 123 × 123 m, 4 m walls on a plinth 1.5 m high and 3 m across | [stated: skylineaparthistory (Wikipedia text); abhijna-emuseum]; the square itself 113 m with projections [stated: abhijna] |
| Square terraces | five, set back from the base (the first 7 m, then the others) | [stated: Wikipedia text]; walled galleries with reliefs and Buddhas in niches [stated: Studio Matrx] |
| Circular terraces | three, with 72 bell-shaped latticed stupas, a Buddha in each | [stated: molon; Studio Matrx] |
| Crowning stupa | at the middle, about 35 m above the ground, 11 m across | [stated: Britannica; Bananas for Scale] |
| Stairs | in the middle of each side, to the summit | [stated: Studio Matrx] |
| Monastery | timber halls in a walled compound north-west of the monument | [inferred: remains of a monastery on the hill's west, recorded by excavation — L3 to confirm] |
| Villages | houses on posts among palms and fields round about | [inferred: houses in the reliefs] |

Ours: the terraces' setbacks of 4 m (gallery and wall) after the first, so that the circular terraces (about 51, 38 and 26 m across) sit on the top plateau; the step heights chosen to reach 35 m. [inferred]

## 3. Orientation

Square to the compass, a stair on each side; the main approach from the east. [stated: Studio Matrx for the alignment; inferred: the east approach]

## 4. The style

Grey andesite, dry-laid [stated]; redented squares in plan [stated: Studio Matrx]; bell stupas with octagonal and square harmika, the upper ones pierced with diamond openings (ours: a lattice) [stated: architectureofbuddhism; Studio Matrx]. Houses: raised timber floors on posts, walls of woven bamboo, steep saddle roofs of palm thatch flaring at the ridge ends, as in the reliefs. [inferred]

## 5. The town

Hamlets of houses on posts with granaries, round open yards, in groves of palms and bamboo; fields between. [inferred]

## 6. Ground and water

The Kedu plain, the monument's hill rising a few metres under it. (`ground.js`) [inferred]

## 7. Planting

Palms, bamboo, banyan and teak near the hamlets; fields and grass on the plain. [inferred]

## 8. Sky and weather

Tropical: morning mist, bright midday, afternoon cloud and showers. Latitude 7.6° S. [inferred]

## 9. Sound

Bronze gongs and metallophones in an interlocking pattern, a bamboo flute (a plucked string, the ney and a reed organ stand in), slendro-like pentatonic. [inferred]

## 10. Detail (L2)

| Feature | What we build | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Pierced stupas | 72 stupas, 32, 24 and 16 on the three round terraces, each on a double lotus cushion; the bell's middle an open stone lattice — diamond holes in staggered rows on the two lower terraces, square holes on the top one — with a solid band below and a solid crown above, the inside surface and the holes' sides built so that the lattice has its thickness; inside, seen through the holes, the Buddha seated on his lotus; a harmika and an octagonal spire on top | 32/24/16; the bottom two tiers with rhombus openings, the top tier "slightly smaller" with square openings; a Buddha in each behind the openwork [stated: arisguide]; the double lotus cushion, square harmika below and octagonal on the top terrace, the number of rows and holes [inferred from photographs] |
| Crowning stupa | Solid, on a double lotus cushion with a moulded band round its foot; a square harmika in three steps; an octagonal shaft (yasti) carrying three parasols (chattra), the top 42 m above the ground | the main stupa "plain without any holes", about 35 m high, 42 m originally with the three-tiered chattra [stated: arisguide]; Van Erp's reconstruction of a tiered pinnacle with parasols from octagonal fragments, later dismantled as too uncertain [stated: Buddhistdoor] — we show it, the monument being new c. 825 [inferred] |

| Spouts | A makara spout at each outer corner of the base and of each square terrace, below the balustrade: a channel block, brow, hanging jaw, eyes and rising snout, pointing out along the corner's bisector | 100 spouts in the form of makaras at the corners of the tiers, draining the galleries [stated: arisguide; skylineaparthistory]; ours one per outer corner (72) [inferred] |
| Pilgrims | Monks in ochre and red robes and lay pilgrims in wraps, some carrying offerings, walking each gallery clockwise (the monument on their right), now and then in pairs | pilgrims walk the path round the monument and climb terrace by terrace past the reliefs [stated: skylineaparthistory]; the clockwise circumambulation and the dress [inferred] |

## 11. The round terraces' spacing (2026-11-06)

The 72 stupas, 32, 24 and 16 on the three round terraces, the top tier's "slightly smaller" [stated: arisguide]; the stairs run up through the round terraces on the four axes [stated: arisguide]. Ours: in each quarter of a terrace the stupas spaced evenly between the axes, leaving 2.2 m between the cushions where the stairs (2 m wide) come up; every stupa set back so that its lotus cushion stands 0.25 m clear of its terrace's edge; the bells 1.45 m in radius on the two lower terraces and 1.35 m on the top one [inferred from photographs].

## 12. Third pass: the Buddhas, the panels, the people (2026-11-06)

| Feature | What we build | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Niche Buddhas by direction | In the upper part of each terrace's face (the outer face of the balustrade above, where the niches are) a niche every 4.5 m, on the straight runs and on the projections: a dark recess between pilasters on a sill, its head stepped in twice as a corbelled arch, a lintel with the kala's mask (face plate, brows, bulging eyes, fangs over the arch); in it a seated Buddha in padmasana on a double lotus, distinguished only by his hands — east *bhumisparsa* (right hand over the knee touching the earth), south *vara* (right hand on the knee, palm out), west *dhyana* (both hands in the lap), north *abhaya* (right hand raised, palm out); on the last terrace's face (the fifth balustrade) *vitarka* on all four sides | four lower rows of niches about 92 to each facade, the fifth row 64 in all; east bhumisparsamudra (Aksobhya), south varamudra (Ratnasambhava), west dhyanimudra (Amitabha), north abhayamudra (Amoghasiddha), fifth row vitarkamudra (Vairocana); "all seated in padmasana and only differentiated by the gestures of the hands" [stated: Foucher, in photodharma]; the niche's frame, the kala on its lintel and the corbelled head [inferred from photographs]; the figure's proportions (about 1.05 m seated at full size, smaller here where the face is low) [inferred] |
| The Buddhas of the latticed stupas | Inside each of the 72 latticed stupas a seated Buddha turning the wheel (*dharmacakra*: both hands before the chest), each facing out by the quarter of the terrace he sits in | the 72 cupolas' Buddhas in dharmacakramudra [stated: Foucher, in photodharma]; their facing [inferred] |
| Relief panels of the main walls | Below the niches one register (two where the face is tall) of framed panels 2.5 m wide, each holding two or three composed scenes in relief: an audience (a lord seated in a pavilion on a platform under a steep roof, attendants standing, a figure kneeling), a procession of standing figures in tall headdresses and ankle-length kain beneath a wishing-tree, a hermitage (a seated figure between trees) — figures standing out 8–9 cm from the ground, inside a raised frame | 2,672 relief panels in all [stated: arisguidetour]; the subjects (Lalitavistara, Jataka, Avadana, Gandavyuha) and the house, pavilion and dress types [stated in outline: skylineaparthistory; arisguide]; our three compositions stand for them [inferred] |
| The balustrades' own register | On the inner face of every balustrade (toward the gallery it encloses) its own row of framed relief panels 2 m wide, as high as the balustrade allows | the balustrade of the first gallery carries its own upper series (the first 135 panels the 34 Jatakamala legends, then 237 from other sources) and the second gallery's balustrade more stories [stated: arisguidetour]; one register here, the balustrade being lower than the real one [inferred] |
| Pilgrims with offerings | Besides those walking the galleries clockwise: devotees kneeling at the main wall with palms joined, a basket of flowers or a water pot set down before them; monks walking round the round terraces between the stupas and the wall above, one seated in meditation by the crowning stupa; a party of pilgrims coming up the way from the east with offerings on their heads and slung from carrying poles | pradakshina, the pilgrim's walk round the monument terrace by terrace past the reliefs [stated: skylineaparthistory]; offerings and dress [inferred from the reliefs] |
| The monastery's life | In the great pavilion fifteen monks seated in rows before a teacher; two monks with a book in the small pavilion; at the east gate lay people bringing rice and water, a monk receiving them; a well, water jars, firewood and baskets in the kitchen yard by the monks' houses | a monastery by the monument [inferred, §2]; its daily round [inferred] |

**The hidden foot.** The original base, carved with the 160 Karmavibhangga panels, was encased in the broad added base (the 3 m plinth here) during construction; by c. 825 it was already hidden, so the encasement is what we show, plain [stated: the encasement — skylineaparthistory; inferred: its date relative to ours].
