## 1. The real example and the moment

- **Place:** Tikal, Petén, Guatemala, a Classic Maya city whose Great Plaza has Temple I (Temple of the Great Jaguar) on the east and Temple II (Temple of the Mask) on the west, the North Acropolis to the north and the Central Acropolis to the south [stated: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikal].
- **Moment shown:** c. 750, a generation after the burial of Jasaw Chan K'awiil (entombed AD 734; Temple I completed c. 740–750) [stated: Wikipedia, Tikal], when the city was at its height: growth peaked in the Late Classic, about AD 700–830 [stated: Wikipedia, Tikal]; the city counted over 3,000 structures on about 15 km² [stated: https://www.worldhistory.org/Tikal/].
- **People:** estimates of 10,000 to 90,000 for the city, 120,000 within 12 km of the core at the peak [stated: Wikipedia, Tikal]. [inferred] At 330 m we show the monumental core and the nearest residential groups at the density of the core's edge, thinning into forest.

## 4. The landmark

### 4.1 Temples I and II
- Temple I: a nine-tier pyramid of limestone rubble faced with stucco painted red; each tier a battered face under a moulding; one stair, on the west, steep and narrow, between sloped balustrades; at the top a terrace and the shrine with three chambers one behind the other, entered by a single door, each with its corbel vault braced by sapodilla beams, lintels of sapodilla over the inner doors; above the shrine the roof comb, a tall slab of stepped, niched masonry that once carried the king's enthroned figure [stated: Temple I sources above; mayaruins].
- Temple II: three broad tiers, a wide stair; a shrine of three consecutive chambers (only the middle lintel carved [stated]), roof comb 12.3 m [stated].
- Colour: red stucco over the whole [stated: Maya architecture: bright painted stucco; red as the dominant colour is general knowledge of Tikal's temples and tombs, tomb offerings rubbed with vermilion, [stated: Wikipedia, Tikal]]; trim in white lime and a little Maya blue [inferred].
- Stelae and altars: a smooth stela with a carved ruler and its round altar at the stair's foot of each temple [stated: Stela P-83 at Temple II].

### 4.2 North Acropolis
- A platform of 100 × 80 m with its broad stair facing the plaza; on it temple pyramids with roof combs, the tallest (33 m, [stated], we set about that) in the middle; the front of the platform lined with stelae and altars [stated: 43 and 30 by the 9th century].

### 4.3 Central Acropolis
- A maze of palaces on a terraced platform, six courts at different levels, vaulted ranges of rooms entered from the courts, rooms with masonry benches along the walls, walls plastered and painted; a five-storey palace [stated: worldhistory.org]. We build a front stair from the plaza, ranges round two courts, the south range with its rooms opened and furnished (benches, mats, jars, a hearth), and a stepped palace of four storeys.

### 4.4 Ball court, causeways, reservoirs
- The small ball court; the causeways of packed, plastered limestone [stated: Wikipedia, Tikal]; reservoirs lined with plaster (water level below the ground, [inferred] 1.5 m).
