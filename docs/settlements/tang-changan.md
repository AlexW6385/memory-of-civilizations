## 1. The real example

- **Moment shown:** about 750, the later years of Xuanzong's Tianbao era, when Chang'an held some 800 000–1 000 000 people inside its walls (the 742 census gives 1 960 188 for Jingzhao, the capital's province, in 362 921 households) [stated: Wikipedia "Chang'an"].
- **The city:** a walled rectangle about 8 km by 6 miles, its outer wall about 5.5 m high with three gates a side (three gateways each), laid out on a grid of long avenues; 108 residential wards and two markets, 110 walled enclosures [stated: Wikipedia "Chang'an"; Lin 2015]. The Imperial City lay in the north-centre; the Daming Palace on higher ground north-east.
- **Zhuque (Vermilion Bird) Avenue:** the central axis from the palace area to the south gate, Mingde Gate; about 150 m wide, not counting the ditches (the Imperial Way's 150 m in the sources; the avenues to the gates 100 m, the narrowest streets 25 m) [stated: Lin 2015; Wikipedia "Chang'an"]. Drainage ditches 3 m wide ran along both sides of it [stated: Lin 2015]. Locust (huai, *Sophora japonica*) trees were planted along the avenues, as in all Tang capital streets [inferred: standard in the poetry and later accounts; the sources fetched do not say].
- **Wards (fang):** walled, 50–100 families each; walls on average 3 m high and 2.5–3 m thick; the wards flanking the central axis measured about 500–590 m by 580–700 m, those beside the palaces up to 600–883 m by 1020–1125 m [stated: Lin 2015 (Twitchett 1968; Xi'an excavation report 1961); Wikipedia]. The wards were closed at night by the ward headman (fangzheng); only temples, monasteries and officials of rank 3b and above could have gates opening onto the street [stated: Lin 2015]. The walls were of rammed earth [inferred: the usual Tang wall, and what the excavations found; Lin 2015 says only that the roads were of beaten earth].
- **Curfew drums:** the street drums (jiegu) sounded at dusk and the ward gates were shut after them; at the fifth watch the drums began again from the palace, passed on street to street, and the gates opened [stated: Tang huiyao, Tanglü shuyi as cited in Lin 2015 for the ward headman and closing; the beats (about 800 then 600 at dusk) are [inferred] from the Tang huiyao as usually quoted and not verified in the pages fetched].
- **Markets:** the West Market (Liren) and East Market (Duhui) each about two wards in area, walled and gated, divided into nine blocks by a well-shaped (井) cross of streets, a government office block at the centre; open from noon at the stroke of 300 drum beats, closed some 1¾ hours before dusk at 300 gong beats [stated: Wikipedia "Chang'an"]. The West Market held a Persian bazaar, many wine shops, taverns and drink sellers, a safe-deposit firm, caravanserais on its western edge and was the centre of foreign trade with acrobats, magicians and foreign plays [stated: dragonsarmory "Chang An part 6"; Wikipedia].
- **Foreigners:** Sogdians from the Samarkand region, Persians, Arabs, Indians, Koreans, Uighurs; Sogdians kept wine shops and Persian shops stood beside a slave market; Sogdian and Persian merchants rode Bactrian camels, Chang'an was the eastern end of the Silk Road; Zoroastrian fire temples (at least five or six, four round the West Market), Manichaean and Nestorian (Church of the East) presence, the mosque c. 742 [stated: Wikipedia; dragonsarmory].
- **Landmark: the Big Wild Goose Pagoda (Dayan ta)** of Daci'en Temple. Xuanzang had it built from 652 to keep the sutras and images he brought from India; rebuilt in the Wu Zetian years (the "Chang'an" era, 701–704) as a seven-storey tower; modern form from the repair of 930–933 [stated: Wikipedia "Giant Wild Goose Pagoda"]. Seven storeys, about 58 m [stated: Britannica "Chinese architecture"]; the present tower is 64.1 m with a base about 4.2 m high and 48.8 m wide, the lowest storey 25 m on a side, brick with an earth core, the upper levels tapering [stated: Wikipedia]. Timber forms imitated in brick (flat columns, struts, capitals, corbelled cornices) [stated: Britannica]; a brick archway on each face of each storey; bluestone door lintels and frames carved with Buddha figures on the ground storey; two tablets by Chu Suiliang (texts of Taizong, 648, and Gaozong) at the south entrance; hidden columns per side, 9 bays on floors 1–2, 7 on 3–4, 5 on 5–7 [stated: Wikipedia]. The pagoda stands in the northern part of the temple [stated: Wikipedia].
- **Daci'en Temple:** founded 648 in the ward of Jinchang by the crown prince Li Zhi for his mother's memory, a huge compound (some ten courtyards) in which Xuanzang worked on his translations [stated: Wikipedia, Giant Wild Goose Pagoda page: the translation work; founding 648 and the courtyards are [inferred] from the standard accounts, not in the page fetched].
- **Daoist temple:** Xuandu Guan, in Chongye ward on the east of the avenue, famed for its peach blossoms [inferred: Liu Yuxi's poem, from memory].

## 3. Orientation

North–south axis, everything facing south: halls open to the south, gates to the south or the lane [stated: the Chinese rule; Lin 2015 for the grid]. The sun therefore falls on the front of the halls.

## 4. The style (`src/styles/tang/`)

Tang timber building as the Foguang East Hall (857) and the Nara halls show it, and the murals (Dunhuang) and tomb paintings (the Yide, Yongtai, Zhanghuai tombs) show the city's houses [inferred from the surviving examples; Britannica says the Tang timber buildings survive mainly at Nara and Foguang, that the northern Tang eaves were still straight, the upturned corner coming later with the Song]:
- **Massive bracket sets** (dougong) at the column heads, large for the column's height (the set about half the column) [stated: Foguang East Hall, dossier tang-wutai §4]; sets between the columns only on the larger halls.
- **Broad low roofs**: pitch about 1:4–1:5, eaves very deep (about 3 m on large halls), corners not turned up (a slight lift only), grey tiles; the ridge ended by the **owl tail** (chiwei) curling inward [stated: tang-wutai dossier, from the Foguang hall and Tang murals].
- **Red columns, white walls**, plain plank doors and barred windows (zhiling), a ceiling of red boards; the buildings on a **platform of rammed earth** faced with brick or stone.
- **Rammed-earth walls** for wards, courts, houses, capped with tiles and battered [stated: Lin 2015 for the ward walls' size].
- **Houses:** courtyard houses of one or two courts with a gate building (wuyi) or a plain gate with a tiled roof, a main hall at the back, side wings, covered corridors; shops open-fronted under deep eaves with a counter; thatch and earth huts at the poor edge [inferred from the murals and from Heian-kyo and Nara, which copied Chang'an].

## 5. Ward gates and the curfew

Each ward gate: a rammed-earth gate platform in the wall's line with a timber gate building of three bays (the central passage 6 m), a tile roof with chiwei, two-leaf plank doors; a small bell-less guardhouse; the street drum on a drum tower at the crossings of the avenues [inferred]. By evening the gates are drawn shut (the model's doors stay open; the visitor is not locked out).

## 6. Ground and water

Beaten earth everywhere (the streets), no paving in the roadway; at the temple gates and in the temple courts brick paving; the ditches of the avenue hold shallow standing water [inferred]. The plain is flat; the avenue's ditches 1.6 m deep.

## 7. Planting

Locust (huai: `pagoda_tree`) along the avenue (two rows) and the cross streets; elm and willow in the wards' gaps, jujube, persimmon and poplar in the house courts; cypress and Chinese pine in the temple courts; peach trees in the Daoist temple's front court (stand-in: `apple`) [inferred]. Wheat fields only at the far edge.

## 8. Sky and weather

Latitude 34.3° N; dry continental, warm; the brief season is the early autumn morning-afternoon. Haze and loess dust give a pale horizon [inferred]. Weather: clear, hazy, a short rain.

## 9. Sound

Open streets: the pipa and a flute, Central Asian modes (the Tang court used Hu music), sparse; temple halls: chant, wooden fish, bowl bell; the wine shop: pipa and drum, brisker (Sogdian whirl music); a house: the zither (qin) alone. Camel bells, hooves, cart wheels, the market drum, the temple bells [inferred].

## 10. People and dress

- **Officials:** round-collar robes (yuanling pao) belted, black soft caps with two "legs" (futou), boots; colour by rank (purple, red, green, blue) [stated: the standard accounts, culturechina; inferred in detail].
- **Women:** the high-waisted skirt (ruqun) tied under the breast, a short jacket with narrow sleeves, a shawl (pibo), hair in high buns; some in Hu dress with turned-down lapels; at Xuanzong's time fuller, some in men's dress [stated: Wikipedia "Women's clothing in China"; culturechina].
- **Sogdian and Persian merchants:** tight-sleeved belted caftans with lapels, trousers and boots, a felt or conical cap, beards [stated: Garment Atlas; inferred].
- **Hu dancers:** the Sogdian whirl and the Hu twirl on a small round carpet, with the pipa, the flute and the drum [inferred: Tang poetry; the tomb figurines].
- **Commoners:** short tunic and trousers, a cloth cap or a bound topknot, straw sandals; **monks** in grey and ochre robes; **camel drivers** in Hu dress with the Bactrian camel [stated: dragonsarmory: tomb figurines of Hu camel drivers and grooms].

## 11. Interiors

- **A Hu wine shop (jiusi)** in the West Market: an open front with a wine flag; inside a counter built up of earth (lu) with jars, low platform seating, small tables, hu chuang folding stools, a round carpet where a Hu dancer whirls, a lute player; a Hu girl serving [inferred: Tang poetry (Li Bai's "Hu girls"), tomb figurines].
- **The main hall of Daci'en:** red columns, the red-board ceiling, a raised altar with the seated Buddha and two disciples, bodhisattvas, lamps, banners, kneeling mats; the temple's own `temple_interior` of the style.
- **A house:** the main hall of an official's house: a raised wooden platform (ta) with a low table and mats, a screen behind, a lamp stand, a chest, a zither.

## 12. Smoke and the day

Kitchen smoke at the morning and midday meals, steam from the food stalls, incense at the temples; a faint dust haze at noon [inferred].

## 13. Moving life

Walkers by trade and hour: officials in procession, women, Sogdian merchants, camel drivers, porters, monks, soldiers of the ward guard; the camel trains on the avenue and into the West Market; horses; fauna: sparrows, crows, pigeons round the pagoda, magpies, dogs, and a cat or two.
