# Decisions log

The record of decisions behind `DESIGN.md` (its §11 points here). **Agents do not read this file as part of a task**; grep it when you need the reason for one specific thing. Append a line only for a decision that constrains future work: date, decision, reason — one or two sentences. Anything a decision changes in the design itself is written into `DESIGN.md` where it belongs, not only here. The log of the Memory Palace era (2026-10) is in `docs/history.md`.

- 2026-11-03 — **Renamed to Memory of Civilizations / 文明的记忆;** repository `memory-of-civilizations`. Reason: the project is about civilizations, not one palace or a memory technique.
- 2026-11-03 — **The world is a small planet** (r = 4 km, see below for 8 km) with Earth's continents and latitudes; regions at true positions, pushed apart where crowded; open country between them. Supersedes the compact city of 2026-10-28. Reason: author's vision — every civilization where it stood; sun, climate and the globe map come for free; sparsity helps frame times.
- 2026-11-03 — **A civilization is a place at a time;** one region holds several civilizations of different eras, standing near each other in order of age. Reason: author's instruction (Southern Song and Ming Jiangnan are two civilizations).
- 2026-11-03 — **A settlement is a landmark plus its townscape,** both to the dossier; a bare landmark is unfinished. Reason: the author wants the feeling of a civilization, not a collection of monuments.
- 2026-11-03 — **Detail first** (§5 standard), picture quality second, smoothness third and reached by engineering; budgets in §8 are targets met by streaming, LOD and baking, and detail is thinned only as the last resort. Reason: author's instruction — the current level of detail is far too low; the author's machine is an M2 with 8 GB, so engineering must carry the detail.
- 2026-11-03 — The target list of ~55 civilizations (§7), balancing well-known landmarks with the places and eras history needs. Reason: author's instruction.
- 2026-11-03 — v7's world code is rebuilt; landmarks, styles, renderer, movement, clock, UI and tools are kept (§10). Reason: the author asked to keep only the main mechanisms and rebuild the rest.

- 2026-11-03 — **A lean interface:** the small map (top left; a click opens the globe), the clock-and-speed card (top right) and one language button; the settings panel is gone. Quality is always the highest; music level and look sensitivity are fixed. The maps shade night (the globe's terminator, the settlements glowing at night). A click on or near a settlement on the map (within about 1 km of its rim) lands beside its landmark, facing it, not at its far gate. Reason: author's instruction.
- 2026-11-03 — **Roofs can be stood on:** every built part has a height field of its upper surfaces (`topField`, 0.5 m cells); one lands on it from the air and walks up and down its slopes; from inside, below the roof, it is out of reach. Reason: author's instruction (flying up to a building, one should be able to stand on it).

- 2026-11-03 — **One corner card:** the small map with the clock and the speed under it, top left; the language button alone top right (there during the loading too). The loading screen is the title, the bar and the percentage; the bar's stages are weighted by how long each took on this machine last time, and it never goes back. Reason: author's instruction.
- 2026-11-03 — **No arriving inside things:** after any travel, once the place is built, the visitor is moved to the nearest free spot (ground or floor, not water, nothing built overhead, no wall in the body); the walker is never left below the open ground; the near plane follows the height over the ground, not over the sea (it had grown to ~0.5 m on high ground and cut close walls away). Reason: author's report of falling through the ground on travel.
- 2026-11-03 — **Settlement modules:** a settlement of its own is `src/settlements/<id>/` — `layout.js` (pure data), `ground.js` (height, cover, water, planting, climate; pure, used by workers), `plan.js` (the landmark), `town.js` (the townscape as several plans), `music.js`, `index.js`; registered in `src/settlements/grounds.js`. Its frame's origin is the disc's centre. First: Ming Suzhou (radius 215 m), style `ming` (`src/styles/ming/`).

- 2026-11-04 — **Smoothness by engineering, measured on the author's machine** (the owner's report that turning the view was sluggish; ~21 fps turning, ~27 still, 1,160–1,190 draw calls and 3.2 M triangles at Edfu in the desktop app's browser): (1) a settlement let go of while it was still being built no longer goes on adding parts to its emptied group (a load generation, bumped by `unload()`); unloading also frees its instanced meshes and its baked light volumes (`GIVolume.release`); at most `MAX_BUILT` = 4 settlements are built at once, the nearest — from the air the reach grows, the count does not. (2) A lower resolution while moving was tried and dropped: changing the pixel ratio re-makes every pass's render targets and stalled frames for up to a second; the pixel ratio stays 1.5. (3) Shadows on "high": three cascades of 2048² (§8 asks for three; it was four of 4096²); the far cascades' shadows are a little softer. The small things of the streets (`life` tiles) cast shadows only within ~120 m. (4) Held still for a second, the loop draws ~24 frames a second instead of 60 (wind, water and flames still move). Measured after (same place, the browser pane hidden so absolute rates are low): turning ~27 fps instead of ~21 with fewer hitches; 710–770 draw calls instead of 1,160–1,190 (within §8's 800); 2.8–3.1 M triangles instead of 3.2 M. Picture quality changes, recorded per §14: softer far shadows; small objects beyond ~120 m without shadows.

- 2026-11-05 — **Measured before engineered** (the owner's performance plan, phase 0): `?bench` (a fixed route of three 15 s legs on the author's machine) and `?perf` (a panel of switches); A/B switches for the benchmark only — `?logdepth=0` (no logarithmic depth buffer) and `?plain=1` (flat materials). `tools/geo-budget.mjs` replaces the headless `tools/perf.mjs` of §8, and `npm test` holds every settlement within its limits (now 45 MB of vertex data, 1,500 meshes). Reason: headless frame rates are meaningless; vertex counts are exact, and memory is the author's machine's bottleneck (8 GB shared, swapping).

- 2026-11-05 — **Vertex arrays let go of once on the GPU; fewer shadow casters** (phase 1): a built part's position, normal and index arrays are emptied after every one of them has been uploaded (memory is shared on the author's machine, so they were held twice); window voids (`opening`), clear glass, water and lamps cast no shadow; merging copies and drops each piece in turn; building yields every 4 ms (§8), not 6. Picture unchanged.

- 2026-11-05 — **Repeated pieces are instanced automatically** (phase 2): `GeoBag` (src/core/geom.js) matches the pieces of a material within one plan by shape — the same piece moved, or, when all its faces face along the axes (every box), moved and stretched — and a shape repeated ≥ 12 times (saving ≥ 2,400 vertices) becomes one indexed prototype drawn as an `InstancedMesh`. No style code changes; the tools (bake, impostors, checks) still build without instancing, and the top field one stands on expands the instances (`flatParts`). Instance matrices are always vertex attributes (the uniform path writes the instance count into the shader: one pipeline per count). Measured: vertex data on the GPU 3,026 → 1,688 MB over all settlements; Florence 184 → 72 MB, London 177 → 29, Athens 157 → 72, Beijing 142 → 63; screenshots unchanged (Florence: 0.06% of pixels differ, clouds). Stand-off of stretched pieces: their box grows by the material's rank (≤ 6 mm) instead of each face moving out — not visible.

- 2026-11-05 — **The life of the streets instanced settlement-wide** (phase 3): every object of use, animal and figure is built at its own origin and placed as an instance of each piece's shape (`GeoBag.addInstance`, src/objects/index.js `placer`); cloth, skin and hide are each one material tinted per copy. The viewer gathers a settlement's life tiles by shape (src/engine/life-instances.js): a shape with ≥ 16 copies in the settlement is one instanced mesh (two: the copies within 120 m cast shadows, those beyond do not), and its copies are chosen four times a second by distance; rarer shapes are merged back into their tiles. Each kind of object has its reach (figures 170 m, animals 190 m, pots and baskets 110–120 m, chickens 90 m, the rest 230 m as before). To share pieces, figures are built at one height and scaled to their own, their irregularities (skirt flare, hands, sash) come in four variants, pots and baskets in four sizes, and hens face eight ways. Measured: vertex data on the GPU over all settlements 1,688 → 618 MB (from 3,026 before phase 2); the largest settlement 34 MB (Beijing), Florence 24 MB, Athens 21 MB. Picture changes (§14): small objects and figures are no longer drawn as far (pots beyond ~120 m, figures beyond ~170 m — a few pixels there); figures and pots vary a little less.

- 2026-11-05 — **Settlements are laid out and built in workers** (phase 4): `src/engine/builder.js` keeps two workers (`src/engine/build-worker.js`); a settlement's townscape (`town()`, up to 1.7 s at Athens) and every plan of it (with its top field) are made there, and the page receives the geometry as transferable arrays and only makes the meshes. The builders (src/core, src/objects, src/styles) import three.js through `src/core/three.js`, never by name, since a worker has no import map (`tests/worker.test.mjs`). If the workers cannot start or a job fails, the page builds as before (`?worker=0` forces it).

- 2026-11-05 — **Fine detail drawn near; indoor shadows** (phase 5): the viewer's bags put the pieces of a material under 0.4 m (and instanced shapes under 0.4 m) into meshes of their own, drawn within 160 m of their part's bounds and casting shadows within 50 m; indoors in a landmark, the other parts of the settlement cast no shadows (their light comes in through the building's own openings). Finer chunks of the town meshes were not made: the town is already in 100 m tiles with near and far versions, and smaller chunks would add draw calls (§8: ≤ 800) for little culling. Picture changes (§14): pieces under 0.4 m are not drawn beyond ~160 m from their building (3–5 pixels there) and cast no shadow beyond ~50 m.

- 2026-11-05 — **Quieter audio thread** (phase 6): the 12 s convolution (great domed spaces) is fed only in zones that ask for it and cut off 14 s after leaving them; the scheduler does nothing while the sound is muted. The shader work of phase 6 (fading procedural detail with distance, merging material variants) waits for `?bench&plain=1` on the author's machine: only if flat materials are markedly faster is it worth the risk to the picture.

- 2026-11-05 — **One form of each material** (the owner: loading had become slow): instancing had made most materials appear both as plain and as instanced meshes, and as instanced meshes with and without instance colours — each form its own shader and pipeline, so the start-up's compiling grew by about a quarter (measured headless: 41 → 55 s of compiling; real GPUs compile faster, in proportion). Every building mesh is now an instanced mesh with instance colours (a merged part is one white copy at its place), and the life meshes always carry colours: back to 44 s with today's added detail. Picture unchanged.

- 2026-11-05 — **The frame, measured on the author's machine** (Chrome, 1710×985 at pixel ratio 1.5, Edfu, turning): ~120 ms a frame (8 fps), GPU-bound — the time fell with the pixel count (67 ms at ratio 1.0) while JavaScript stayed ~20 ms. Changes, picture unchanged unless said: (1) **reversed float depth instead of logarithmic** (`?depth=log|reversed|standard`): the logarithmic buffer writes depth from every fragment, which disables the GPU's early depth test, so every hidden surface was fully shaded — 139 → 36 ms at the same view; the sky's vertex now puts it on the far plane of either buffer (at z = w it was the near plane of a reversed one and covered the ground drawn before it). (2) **Shadow cascades**: the middle one redrawn every second frame, the farthest every third (each map keeps the matrix it was drawn with); ground tiles cast shadows only within ~420 m (beyond, they were drawn into every cascade along the sun's direction — ~400 draws a frame); far views cast none. (3) **The ground shader** computes each cover's noise only where that cover is present (grass's Worley noise, rock strata and banding, canopy, roads, sand ripples, puddles only when wet, fine noise only near) — with flat ground the frame was ~45% faster. (4) **Ground tiles**: the finest level carries 64 samples a side and the level below it is the last — the same 2 m grid near the visitor in a quarter of the tiles. (5) A module whose download fails is fetched again (one failed fetch had stopped the loading at its percentage). Measured after (1)–(3): 33–42 ms a frame at ratio 1.5 (~25–30 fps), ~20 ms at ratio 1.0; `render()` itself ~12–20 ms of CPU for ~700 draws.

- 2026-11-05 — **The loading bar names its step** (the owner: the phone stopped at 3% with no way to tell where): the text reads "37% · buildings 12/44 · ground 60%", adds the seconds once the bar has not moved for five, and shows any error with the step it stopped at. The start fetched every settlement's module (each with its styles: most of the site's code) one after another before the first frame — seconds on a fast machine, far longer on a phone; now only the first settlement's, the rest when the page is idle or when the map asks for one.
- 2026-11-05 — **Every settlement arrives at a chosen view** (§4.4): `src/planet/arrivals.js` holds one pose per settlement, chosen by looking — the screenshot tool's viewpoint where it was good, otherwise composed on the spot (Heian Uji from the pond's east shore, Sydney from the cove's east side, Cairo from the Rumayla square, Kilwa inside the Great Mosque, Stonehenge clear of the Heel Stone, Barcelona from the street corner, Persepolis on the terrace before the Apadana, Machu Picchu over the town to Huayna Picchu); `goNear` uses it and falls back to `viewpoint()` only without one; tests/arrivals.test.mjs checks that every settlement has one. Weak still, for the models more than the views: Lalibela (the church reads poorly from the rim), Florence (the dome hidden behind the front from the piazza), Conques (seen up a lane). Reason: the owner's instruction that arriving from the map should land at the best viewing angle and place, and the automatic choice often stood too close, behind something or facing a plain wall.
- 2026-11-05 — **Flight has momentum; cruising** (the owner): the height above the ground sets the top vertical speed, the same rising and falling (6 m/s at the ground, +0.4 m/s per metre, at most 250 m/s; first +0.25 and 160, raised at the owner's word; falling at 0.8 of it). Horizontal aloft: 12 m/s +0.3 per metre up to 160 (24 +0.6 up to 300 with Shift), raised from 9/18 at the owner's word; held, one rises toward it, let go, one falls toward it, the acceleration only smoothing the start (the top speed reached in about 0.6 s, first 1.2 s) (`flyVy` in `src/core/walk.js`; first tried as an acceleration that grew with the time held, then replaced at the owner's word). Aloft the horizontal velocity eases toward what the keys ask; without them it slows by a small fixed part plus a part in proportion to the speed, to a full stop within 3 s flying and 5 s cruising even from the top speed (first an exponential decay, which kept one sliding; then a steady one, which felt mechanical). Cruise holds one aloft while the speed dies away (vertical and, with no keys, horizontal): Space twice within 0.32 s on a desktop; on touch, a small cruise button shows beside the fly button while it is held, let go on it (at first after a second's hold; the owner asked for it at once). Flying again, or landing, ends it. The gait reads "Cruising".
- 2026-11-05 — **The occlusion is averaged** (the owner: black specks everywhere on the ground): GTAO turns its sample directions by a 5×5 noise pattern and leaves the averaging to a denoiser, which we never had; under the logarithmic depth buffer its depth was misread and it barely darkened anything, so the speckle only showed once the reversed buffer made it right. The occlusion is now averaged in the final pass over 5×5 taps one of its half-resolution texels apart (its noise repeats every 5 texels; a first 3×3 left blotches), each weighted by how close its depth lies to the pixel's, so it does not bleed across edges (`aoBlur` in `src/engine/renderer.js`; three's DenoiseNode was rejected as about 200 texture reads a pixel). Beyond 25–70 m it fades out (first 50–140 m, which left mottled ground below the horizon): its 0.9 m radius is under a pixel there, and all it found was the depth's error darkening open fields. Quality: the occlusion is a little softer, and none far off.
- 2026-11-05 — **Shaders are compiled before a part shows** (the owner: it still catches now and then): measured in his Chrome, the worst catches were frames that compiled new pipelines (14 at once, a second's frame) the first time a kind of mesh came into view; nothing was ever compiled ahead. Each built part now compiles, before it joins the scene, one mesh of each kind (material, attributes, instancing) not seen before, through three's `compileAsync` with the scene pass's own target, outputs and context, set up and put back synchronously so no frame sees the change (`Renderer.precompile`). The sun's shadow pass cannot be compiled this way (three compiles each object after the pass's shared depth material is reset, so the result never matches; tried and measured), so its small depth shaders still compile on first use. A tree species new to the open country is likewise held hidden until its shaders are compiled.
- 2026-11-05 — **A page outlives a deploy** (found testing: after a deploy, an open page could no longer travel, silently): the code is served under a folder named for its commit and each deploy dropped the last one, so every module a page fetched later (a settlement, a worker) failed. The deploy now also keeps the code of the last 12 commits (`git archive` into their folders, about 8 MB each); and should a module still fail to come, the notice says the site was updated and asks for a reload, and stays.
- 2026-11-05 — **A neighbour's street life is drawn only near too** (measured: of 961 visible meshes at Edfu, 244 were the rare street things of Lalibela, a neighbour kilometres off): the tiles of street life were shown by distance (their plan's `near`/`far`) only in the settlement one is in or nearest to; a neighbour built in the background drew all of its tiles, a draw call per piece and per material. Now every built settlement's tiles are shown by distance.
- 2026-11-05 — **Small things cast into the first cascade only; ground shadows to 340 m** (measured at Edfu: about 280 of a frame's ~720 draws were the shadow pass, the street life alone 60): street life (instanced and rare) and fine detail are on a layer of their own (`src/engine/layers.js`), seen by the camera and by the first cascade's shadow camera, not by the outer two, where their shadows are a texel or two. The ground tiles cast shadows within 340 m (was 420; the cascades end at 320). Quality: no shadows of street life and detail beyond the first cascade.
- 2026-11-09 — **Plants' shadows do not sway** (measured on the M2: shadows about 11 ms of a 49 ms frame at Suzhou, 2× pixels): plants cast their shadows from their resting shape (no sway in the shadow), so that they are static casters kept in the cascades' caches; the first cascade waits for the sun to turn three times as far before it is drawn again. The cost of the shadows measured on the M2 (Suzhou, 2× pixels): about 11 ms of a 49 ms frame.
- 2026-11-09 — **Every instanced mesh precompiled**: every instanced mesh is precompiled (three keys node builds per instanced mesh), not one per material: the builds move off the frame into the loading.
- 2026-11-09 — **Fewer shapes, fewer draws** (measured on the M2 at Suzhou: about 1000 draw calls a frame, 320 of them plants, 146 street life): a street-life shape is instanced only if its copies hold over 10,000 triangles, the rest merged by material (about 6 MB more at Suzhou, about 90 fewer draws); the far level of detail of plants uses two shapes per species instead of four (quality: fewer distinct far tree shapes; yaw, size and tint still vary).
- 2026-11-09 — **Shadow cascades do not follow every turn of the view** (measured on the M2 at Suzhou: turning on the spot, about 400 of 1100 draws a frame were the cascades drawn again): each cascade is fitted to the view's orientation when last fitted and kept until the view has turned 12° from it, its box widened by a fifth to cover that. Quality: shadow texels about a fifth larger.
- 2026-11-09 — **Precompile at the scene pass's depth** (found on the M2 at Suzhou: about 500 shader builds, 3–4 s, in the first turn round after arriving, every one already precompiled): three keys a build by its render context, and a context by the renderer's nesting depth (the scene pass draws at depth 3 inside the pipeline, compileAsync at 0); the precompile now asks for the scene pass's own context.
- 2026-11-10 — **Settlements built within 350 m of their rim** (was 700): from Suzhou, Kaifeng and Hangzhou (rims about 550 m away) were built in full — thousands of meshes to compile, about 50 draws a frame, tens of MB — where their far views read the same beyond the horizon; unloaded beyond 800 m.
- 2026-11-10 — **Fine pieces culled by view, copy by copy** (measured on the M2 at Suzhou: looking straight up still drew 330 draws and 2.6 M triangles, the merged copies' bounds spanning the settlement): a fine piece's copies more than 80° off the view's direction are left out of the drawn sets, chosen again every 10° of turning; their shadow set is unchanged. The post passes measured at about 1 ms each (AO; bloom and edge smoothing nothing visible).
- 2026-11-10 Dynamic resolution on medium too, down to 0.85 (0.75 on 2× screens): measured on an M2 at 60 Hz, Suzhou's garden missed every other refresh at 1.0 with the GPU the limit (draw submission ~11 ms, GPU ~20 ms); a softer picture beats a halting one.
- 2026-11-10 Global uniforms (ENV, baked-light bounds and volumes) in the render's shared group: each object's own group is checked at each of its draws (~500 a frame).
- 2026-11-10 Draw calls are Suzhou's limit, not shading (flat materials −13%, ¼ pixels −20%, half the draws −40%): fewer draws first; baked material textures stay deferred.
- 2026-11-10 Build-worker watchdog 180 s (was 45): a busy worker cannot answer, and the false alarm moved all building to the main thread, freezing it for a minute per settlement.
- 2026-11-10 Build workers restarted (and the job sent again) when one falls silent, at most three times, before the page builds itself: falling back to the main thread froze it for minutes.
- 2026-11-10 Ming halls always odd bays (a doorway in the middle bay, never a post on the axis); a top-field cell whose top is a downward-shaded face is not a shell (an arch's soffit over a passage), kept as a flag so nothing else changes.
- 2026-11-10 Living additions (movers, fauna, smoke, catkins) are settlement systems (src/engine/systems.js), attached when a settlement is ready, each a few instanced draws moved on the GPU, data per city in src/settlements/<id>/.
