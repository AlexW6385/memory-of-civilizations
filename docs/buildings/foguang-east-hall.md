# The Great East Hall of Foguang Monastery, Mount Wutai — dossier

Building id: `foguang-east-hall` · Style: `chinese` · District: Tang China (Mount Wutai)

## 1. The real example

- **What:** the Great East Hall (Dongda dian, 东大殿) of Foguang Monastery (佛光寺) on Mount Wutai, Shanxi — the grandest surviving Tang timber building, and the earliest surviving hall of the palatial type (*diantang*). [stated: architecturasinica; Wikipedia]
- **Date:** 857, the 11th year of Dazhong, recorded in ink on a beam and on a dhāraṇī pillar of the same year; found by Liang Sicheng's team on 5 July 1937. [stated: Wikipedia]
- **State shown:** as completed in 857, with its Tang sculptures; without the later Manjuśrī Hall (1137) of the lower court and the Ming murals. [stated: dates — Wikipedia]

## 2. Type and typology

A Buddhist hall of the palatial type: seven bays by four, two rings of columns of equal height (an outer ring and an inner ring — the *jinxiang doudi cao* layout of the *Yingzao fashi*), each column carrying a bracket set (*puzuo*) of the seven-*puzuo* rank, crescent beams tying the sets into an inner and an outer ring, a latticed ceiling (*ping'an*) of small square coffers hiding the roof frame, a single-eaved hipped roof (*wudian*); the statues on a long altar platform in the inner zone. [stated: architecturasinica; Wikipedia; bilibili; cnyes]

## 3. Orientation and site

- The hall faces **west**, unusually (most Chinese temples face south), built against the eastern hillside at the highest point of the monastery, on the western slope of Foguang Mountain, enclosed by hills on three sides and open to a valley on the west. Three terraces rise from west to east; the highest, about 13 m above the courtyard, carries the hall and is reached by stairs. [stated: Wikipedia]
- In our world: in a new region, Tang China, north of Francia over a pass: loess hills and terraced fields, the monastery's terraces climbing a hillside facing west over a valley toward the river (docs/world.md). [inferred]

## 5. Section and structure

A timber frame of the palatial type: the two column rings carry the bracket sets, which carry the beams and the ceiling, on which rest the roof frame and its purlins; inverted-V struts (*chashou*) under the ridge; tamped-earth walls between the columns at the back and sides; the frame on a stone-faced terrace. [stated: cnyes; Wikipedia]

## 6. Sequence of spaces and use

1. **The approach up the terraces** from the gate at the bottom of the valley, by stairs, to the highest terrace. [stated: three terraces, stairs]
2. **The front:** the five doors under the deep eaves, the bracket sets overhead.
3. **The outer aisle** walked round the inner zone (circumambulation), **the inner zone** with the altar and its statues facing west, lit through the doors. [stated: layout; use inferred]

## 7. Materials and finishes

Timber (columns, brackets, beams, doors) painted red ochre; walls plastered and red; architraves with the "seven red, eight white" band; grey tiles on the roof; the platform faced in stone. Painted clay statues, gilded faces and hands [inferred: Tang practice]. [stated: cnyes]

## 8. Decorative programme (L3)

The murals (the Tang mural in the panels between the bracket sets, later murals on the walls), the thirty-five sculptures in detail, the inscriptions on beam and pillar.

## 9. Lighting

Daylight only through the five doors and the two slatted windows, falling deep into the hall when the doors face the setting sun (the hall faces west); the statues in a gold-red half-light. Here: the region's late-afternoon sun from the west-south-west. [stated: doors; light inferred]

## 10. Surroundings

Loess hills, terraced fields, Chinese pines and arborvitae on the slopes, elms and poplars in the valley; the monastery's terraces, its wall and gate, the Tang pillar and the old pagoda; a village of courtyard houses of grey brick and earth with tiled roofs below. **In the city (2026-10-28):** the terraces stand in the Tang district, climbing east toward a wooded slope (18 m) that stands for Foguang Mountain; grey-brick courtyard houses fill the district's blocks; the loess hills and terraced fields are not shown.

## 11. Music

Tang Buddhist liturgy: chanted sutras (*fanbai*) on a pentatonic scale, marked by the wooden fish (*muyu*) and the bowl-bell (*qing*); outside, the court and banquet music of the Tang (*yanyue*) with the pipa (plucked lute), the vertical flute (*xiao*) and the mouth organ (*sheng*). [general musicology, e.g. L. Picken (ed.), *Music from the Tang Court*] Our synthesis (`src/buildings/foguang-east-hall/music.js`): inside, a chanting voice on the *gong* pentatonic mode over a drone, the wooden fish keeping a steady beat, the bell now and then; outside, the pipa and the flute.
